# *Dalbergia librevillensis* Pellegr. Planche 42

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Bot. France 92 : 91 (1945)

## Description

Liane reaching 10 m in height, with an aromatic fenugreek-like odor generally perceptible on dried material, unarmed but sometimes with hooked prehensile branchlets; bark brown ± longitudinally striate, sometimes with paler lenticels; young branchlets with short appressed hairs, sometimes quickly glabrescent. Stipules generally falling off fairly early, lanceolate to narrowly ovate, ± asymmetric, 3–10 × 1–2 mm, acute, with short appressed and dense hairs. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.7–2.5 cm; rachis (1–)1.6–12.2 cm, with short appressed to semi-erect hairs, sometimes quickly glabrescent; leaflets (3–)5 to 13, alternate and slightly unequal, with petiolule of (2–)3–6 mm, blade elliptic, often fairly narrow, symmetric or nearly so (the lower leaflets sometimes slightly unequal toward the base), 4.5–11 × 1.3–5.5 cm in the upper leaflets and 1.1–4.3 × 0.8–3.2 cm in the lower ones, rounded or acute at the base, long and gradually acuminate at the apex, coriaceous, becoming brown and scarcely discolorous on dried material, with very scattered appressed hairs on both faces or sometimes glabrous on the upper face; lateral veins 8 to 13, very slightly marked and scarcely distinct from the veinlets, veinlets in fairly dense network but little apparent on the lower face. Inflorescence in a panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary and terminal on the leafy branchlets, 1.5–3.5 cm in length, with dense spreading hairs, golden brown and slightly crisped; bracts persistent, broadly ovate, 0.8–1 × 0.5–0.8 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1.5–3 mm; bracteoles persistent at flowering, but falling in fruit, elliptic, 1–1.3 × 0.6 mm, pubescent; calyx pale brownish green, with very dense golden brown and ± appressed hairs, tube 2–3 × 2.3–3 mm, teeth valvate ± equal and triangular 1–1.8 mm in length, acute or obtuse; corolla white, petals with claw ± equaling the calyx, standard bent at a right angle, with claw 2 mm and blade ± cordate 4–4.5 × 4.5–5 mm, with apex bilobed, wings slightly exceeding the keel, with claw 2–3 mm and blade ± rectangular 4.5 × 2–2.3 mm, smooth externally and bearing a hooked auricle at the base, keel strongly curved, with claw 2.5–3 mm and blade fairly broad 3–3.5 × 2–2.3 mm, with base shortly auriculate; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5 (or exceptionally all fused), filaments ± 6 mm, fused for ¾ of their length, straight at the base and strongly curved in their free part; ovary 1.7–2 × 0.8–1 mm, pubescent on the lower margin and sometimes also on the upper margin, with 1 or 2 ovules and straight stipe 3.5 mm; style linear, 1–1.5 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 2–3.5 mm and stipe 4–7 mm, slightly woody, flattened and ellipsoid, 3.9–12.5 × 2.1–2.9 cm, obtuse at both ends, with faint reticulation, glabrous, glaucous, with 1 seed; pericarp 0.3–0.5 mm thick. Seed reniform, flattened, ± 13 × 8 mm (seen only in young state).

Antonio Fernandez, Meise Botanical Garden (©).

## Distribution

Gabon and southwest of the Republic of the Congo; infrequent, but fairly widely distributed in Gabon, recorded from Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

Secondary forests and forest edges, forest galleries, often in flooded terrain; 0–300 m altitude in Gabon. Flowers in July and August; fruits in September and October

## Source note

This species was known, until now, only from the types (Pellegrin 1949; Sosef et al. 2006). It is, in reality, fairly widespread in Gabon; we also report it for the first time from the Republic of the Congo, based on the specimen M'Boungou 446 (P, WAG). The pods are described here for the first time. Although very flat, they are clearly woody, which distinguishes D. librevillensis from related Gabonese species. Their shape is moreover fairly variable, as illustrated in the figure. A specimen from the Makokou region, Hladik 1539 (P), resembles D. librevillensis closely in the shape of the leaflets and in the standard bent at a right angle, but has more discolorous leaves and the keel less curved and narrower, which brings it closer to D. adamii; moreover the ovary is entirely glabrous. Additional collections, notably in fruit, would be necessary to clarify its status. b: Pellegrin (1949). Bibliography
