# *Urera trinervis* (Hochst.) Friis & Immelman Planche 34

**Genus**: [[Urera]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Nordic J. Bot. 7 : 126 (1987)

## Description

U. cameroonensis Wedd. in DC., Prodr. 16(1): 97 (1869). U. acuminata (Poir.) Decne. var. cameroonensis (Wedd.) Léandri, in Humbert, Fl. Madag. 56, Urticaceae: 24 (1965), excl. spec. cit. Robust woody liana, dioecious, reaching 20–40 m into the canopy and attaining 60 m in length (according to Letouzey 1968); stems in large curves anchored to the substrate by adventitious roots, sometimes epiphytic; bark longitudinally striate on old stems, greyish-black brownish, glabrous, rarely pubescent, with large leaf scars on young branches and sometimes with insignificant protuberances; sap abundant, clear; heartwood large, spongy or stems with hollow centre; stems weakly woody, circular or elliptic in section, up to 10 cm diameter at base, with ± concentric growth rings and a well-developed pith; cystoliths punctiform to somewhat elongate. Stipules fused almost to apex, lanceolate, 6–10 mm long, brown, pubescent on the median nerves and somewhat silky externally, glabrous internally. Leaf: petiole 2.5–6(–10) cm, glabrous to pubescent, rarely with a few sessile stinging hairs or prominent on protuberances reaching 0.5 mm in height, epidermis with numerous cystoliths; blade elliptic to ovate, rarely obovate, (4–)6–12(–18) × 3.5–5(–9) cm, base cuneate, truncate or rounded, rarely weakly emarginate, apex acuminate to caudate, margin entire, coriaceous to weakly fleshy, upper surface green to bright green, glabrous or rarely with a few scattered stiff hairs, with numerous cystoliths, lower surface pale green, glabrous, sometimes sparsely puberulous or pubescent or with scattered stiff hairs and perhaps stinging, particularly on the lateral nerves, rarely with stinging hairs borne on protuberances reaching 0.8 mm in height; basal pair of lateral nerves extending near the margin and to the upper third (or quarter), lateral nerves above 1(–2) pairs above the basal pair, all connected by the tertiary venation scalariform to reticulate. Inflorescence in loose panicle, on a (poorly defined) peduncle of 2–5 cm; male inflorescence arising in leafy or naked axils, ± 8 × 5 cm, with flowers densely and regularly grouped along the axes or at the tip of the branches; peduncle generally with a few stinging hairs; female inflorescence generally in defoliated axils, ± 5(–15) × 5(–15) cm, with small floral glomules with stinging hairs; peduncle with numerous stinging hairs. Male flower 4-merous; pedicel up to 1 mm, glabrous; perianth 1–1.75 mm diameter in bud, tepals with a few stiff hairs, perhaps sometimes stinging and with a tiny apical pectinate appendage. Female flower sessile; perianth cylindric, covering ⅓ to ¾ of the ovary, 1–1.5 mm long, narrowed at apex, with 4 blunt but clearly distinct teeth; ovary 1–1.5 mm long; stigma large, penicillate. Fruit 1.5–2 mm long, glabrous, enveloped in the orange perianth.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical Africa, from Ghana to Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola, south-west to Ethiopia, across East Africa, including Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Mozambique, south to South Africa (see notes for its presence in Madagascar and islands of the Indian Ocean); in Gabon, it is widespread and common, in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

humid evergreen forest, riparian or swamp forest, particularly along forest edges and clearings, sometimes running over rocks or scree; in Gabon at 0–1000 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m

## Source note

See under the genus. Letouzey (1967) discussed, like many earlier authors, the possible identity of U. acuminata (Poir.) Decne. – widely distributed in Madagascar and islands of the Indian Ocean – and U. cameroonensis Wedd. (now synonym of U. trinervis), widespread in continental tropical Africa. He concluded that the two taxa represented distinct species, based on a consistent difference in male flowers (4-merous in U. trinervis, 5-merous in U. acuminata) and less consistent characters in leaf blade morphology, inflorescence form and male bud. The differences in male flowers appear to hold and the distinction is maintained here. For the distinction between U. trinervis and U. thonneri, see under that latter species.
