# *Urera trinervis* (Hochst.) Friis & Immelman Planche 34

**Genus**: [[Urera]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Nordic J. Bot. 7 : 126 (1987)

## Synonyms

- Elatostema trinerve Hochst., Flora 28 : 88 (1845).

## Description

U. cameroonensis Wedd. in DC., Prodr . 16(1) : 97 (1869). U. acuminata (Poir.) Decne. var. cameroonensis (Wedd.) Léandri, in Humbert, Fl. Madag. 56, Urticaceae : 24 (1965), excl. spec. cit . Robust woody liane, dioecious, reaching 20–40 m in the canopy, and attaining 60 m in length (according to Letouzey 1968) ; trunks in large curves anchored to the substrate by adventitious roots, sometimes epiphytic ; bark longitudinally striated on old trunks, greyish-black brownish, glabrous, rarely pubescent, with large leaf scars on young branches and sometimes with insignificant protuberances ; sap abundant, clear ; core broad, spongy or trunks with hollow centre ; stems weakly woody, with circular or elliptical section, up to 10 cm in diameter at the base, with ± concentric growth rings and well-developed pith ; cystoliths punctiform to somewhat elongate. Stipules fused almost to the apex, lanceolate, 6–10 mm in length, brown, pubescent on the median nerves and somewhat silky externally, glabrous internally. Leaf : petiole 2.5–6(–10) cm, glabrous to pubescent, rarely with a few sessile stinging hairs or in relief on protuberances reaching 0.5 mm in height, epidermis with numerous cystoliths ; lamina elliptic to ovate, rarely obovate, (4–)6–12(–18) × 3.5–5(–9) cm, base cuneate, truncate or rounded, rarely weakly emarginate, with acuminate to caudate apex, entire margin, coriaceous to weakly fleshy, upper surface green to glossy green, glabrous or rarely with a few scattered stiff hairs, with numerous cystoliths, lower surface pale green, glabrous, sometimes sparsely puberulent or pubescent or with scattered stiff hairs and perhaps stinging, particularly on the lateral nerves, rarely with stinging hairs borne by protuberances reaching 0.8 mm in height ; basal pair of lateral nerves extending near the margin and to the upper third (or quarter), upper lateral nerves 1(–2) pairs above the basal pair , all connected by scalariform to reticulate tertiary network . Inflorescence in loose panicle, on a (poorly defined) peduncle of 2–5 cm ; the male arising from leafy or naked axils, ± 8 × 5 cm, with flowers densely and regularly clustered along the axes or at the tips of the branches ; peduncle generally with a few stinging hairs ; the female generally from defoliate axils, ± 5(–15) × 5(–15) cm, with small floral glomérules with stinging hairs ; peduncle with numerous stinging hairs. Male flower 4-merous ; pedicel up to 1 mm, glabrous ; perianth 1–1.75 mm in diameter in bud, tepals with a few stiff hairs, perhaps sometimes stinging and with a tiny pectinate apical appendage. Female flower sessile ; perianth cylindric, covering ⅓ to ¾ of the ovary, 1–1.5 mm in length, narrowed at the apex, with 4 blunt teeth, but clearly distinct ; ovary 1–1.5 mm in length ; stigma large, penicillate. Fruit 1.5–2 mm in length, glabrous, enveloped in the orange perianth.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical Africa, from Ghana to Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola, south-west to Ethiopia, across East Africa, including Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Mozambique, south to South Africa (see notes for its presence in Madagascar and islands of the Indian Ocean) ; in Gabon, it is widespread and common, in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

evergreen humid forest, riparian forest or swamp forest, particularly along forest edges and clearings, sometimes trailing over rocks or scree ; in Gabon at 0–1000 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m

## Vernacular names

avom, sas (fang) ; bakayisa (banzabi) ; batsi-tsi-durumbu (bavungu, bapunu) ; dibumaï, magunga (éshira) ; édjanga, ntsaga-kangé (galoa) ; ewogago (nkomi, orungu) ; gébomaï (apindji) ; kamfoula (aux plateaux Batéké) ; oèngu-a-mongèngè (mitsogo). Usage : Les feuilles fournissent un légume. La fibre de l’écorce est utilisée pour faire des cordes et des lignes de pêche. La tige fournit de l’eau potable lorsqu’on la coupe. Cette eau et les feuilles ont plusieurs applications dans la médecine traditionnelle

## Source note

See under the genus. Letouzey (1967) discussed, as did many previous authors, the possible identity of U. acuminata (Poir.) Decne. – widely distributed in Madagascar and islands of the Indian Ocean – and U. cameroonensis Wedd. (currently a synonym of U. trinervis ), well distributed in tropical continental Africa. He concluded that the two taxa represented distinct species, based on a constant difference in male flowers (4-merous in U. trinervis , 5-merous in U. acuminata ) and less constant characters in leaf lamina morphology, inflorescence form and male bud. The differences in male flowers appear to hold and the distinction is thus maintained here. For the distinction between U. trinervis and U. thonneri, see under that latter species.
