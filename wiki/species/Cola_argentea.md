# *Cola argentea* Masters.

**Genus**: [[Cola]]
**Family**: [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mast., F. T. A. I: 224 (1868), descr. orig

## Description

Small tree reaching 5 m in height. Stipules linear-lanceolate persistent. Petioles 24-40 cm long, cylindrical, clothed with a tomentum of rusty hairs then glabrescent. Leaves digitate with 5-9 leaflets entirely sessile, entire or pinnatifid in the main leaflets, 30-60 cm long, by 12-29 cm wide, with narrowly attenuate base and sublanceolate apex; the outer leaflets, much shorter, are asymmetrical. Blade glabrous, and olivaceous above when dry, pruinose whitish below; lower face with very prominent veins, rusty hairs at once stellate and pedicellate, and reticulate venules very apparent.

Inflorescences in multiflorous cauline glomerules. Flowers subsessile or with pedicel 2-3 mm long, furnished toward the base with 2-4 small imbricate obtuse bracteoles. Calyx 7-12 mm long, campanulate, coriaceous, puberulent without, purple and papilliferous within, ornamented at the base with salient radiating ribs. 5 ovate acute lobes, somewhat shorter than the tube. Male flower with slender androphore bearing a uniseriate crown of 10 pollen sacs (the original description indicates 10 anthers of 2 sacs each). Female flower with ovary girt at the base with a crown of pollen sacs, tricarpellate, covered with ochraceus stellate hairs; carpels containing 8 ovules in two series (according to Pierre), each surmounted by a broad fleshy reflexed stigma.

Fruit unknown.

Type of the species: Mann 1798 (K., isotypes P.). This single known material bears as locality indication: lat. 1° N; the place of collection is thus situated toward Cocobeach or on the banks of the Rio Mouni, either in Gabon or in Spanish Guinea.

By its calyx, by the tint and pubescence of its leaves, this rare species comes very close to the Ficicola and particularly to Cola duparquetiana.

Small tree reaching 10-16 m in height for a trunk of ca. 20 cm in diameter (Letouzey), with sinuous bole bearing floriferous bossulations more or less developed; branchlets glabrescent ochraceous; extremities squamiferous; terminal buds ovate acute with enveloping squamiform scales. Stipules lanceolate squamiferous, ca. 12 mm long, rapidly caducous. Petioles 5-60 cm long, cylindrical, squamiferous. Leaves trifoliolate digitate; base of leaflets acute, apex sublanceolate; petiolules 2-15 mm long. Median leaflet elliptic 15-45 cm long by 6-15 cm wide; lateral leaflets somewhat shorter, inequilateral, enlarged toward the lower third on the outer side. Blade coriaceous, glabrous above, clothed below with gray or silvery-brown squamiform hairs. Lateral veins pinnate ca. 10 pairs on the median leaflet.

Inflorescences on the bossulations of old wood, in fascicles of small cymes entirely silvery squamiferous, 20-40 mm long, more or less ramified. Bracteoles lanceolate 2-3 mm long, caducous. Pedicels reaching 5-13 mm long, articulate toward the lower third. Bud ovate. Calyx reaching 10-16 mm long, campanulate, coriaceous, squamiferous externally, divided to about the middle into 5 lobes with developed purple margins and strongly undulate. Interior of calyx dark purple, glabrous, ornamented toward the base with salient micropapilliferous ribs.

Male flower with slender glabrous androphore 4-7 mm long, surrounded at the base with golden scale-like hairs and supporting a uniseriate crown of 20 narrow pollen sacs. Female flower with golden squamiferous ovary, crowned at the base with pollen sacs arranged in pairs, with (4) 5 adherent attenuate carpels, with purple papilliferous convex stigmas. Ovules 6-8 in two series per carpel.

Fruit with subcylindric follicles reaching 20 × 5 cm, briefly acuminate, probably indehiscent, moderately squamiferous glabrescent. Large seeds with thick very fleshy cotyledons.

Type of the species: Preuss 43, probably destroyed; specimen conformable to the type and determined by the author of the species: Zenker 549 (P.).

This Cola is a small tree with soft wood which grows in the understory, in humid forests; it can be encountered in inundable low grounds with Uapaca (Letouzey); it is distributed from southern Nigeria to Gabon.
