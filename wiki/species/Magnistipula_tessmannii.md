# *Magnistipula tessmannii* (Engler) Prance

**Genus**: [[Magnistipula]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bol. Soc. Brot., ser. 2, 40 : 185 (1966); Mendes, Consp. Fl. Angol. 4 : 19 (1970); White, BJBB 46 : 292 (1976)

## Description

— Parinari tessmannii Engl., Bot. Jahrb. 46 : 139 (1911); Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 303 (1915); Haum., BJBB 21 : 189 (1951).

— P. ingangense PELLEGRIN, Bull. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat. Paris 29 : 267 (1923); Fl. Mayombe 1 : 113 (1924); Exell, J. of Bot. 66, Suppl. Polypet. 161 (1928), « Parinari nyangense » per err.

— P. sp. De Saint-Aubin, Forêt Gabon : 169 (1963).

Large tree reaching 40 m in height and 1(-1.20) m in diameter at the base, the latter almost always flared with large ascending flanges giving a trunk ± fluted, cut by deep furrows and higher up presenting flattened areas and sinuosities, short, with oblique branches very ramified and very leafy. Rhytidome yellowish-greenish tinged with greyish, smooth but with large lenticels (approximately 5 mm) arranged in short vertical lines. Bark compact, 2 cm thick; oblique section orange with red fibres, these very dense towards the interior, fugacious scent of sugar cane. Sapwood thin, yellowish, passing into dark red heartwood, quite hard. Branchlets lenticellate. Buds of young shoots surrounded by scales rapidly caducous, oblong concave, with densely villous margin. Young branchlets puberulent and hispidulous with appressed hairs then obliquely erect-directed upward, these hairs found again on the stipules, petioles and beneath the median nerves, all these organs subsequently glabrescent. Stipules triangular elongate, 1 × 8 mm, extremely rapidly caducous. Petiole 10–12 mm, flattened on the upper face and bearing 2–4(–5) elliptic lateral glands ± distinct, the upper ones in contact with the base of the lamina. Lamina elliptic oblong, often slightly or quite distinctly glaucescent, 3–7(–11) × 8–16(–30) cm (for leaves of young shoots up to 9 × 24 cm) exceptionally very oblong (4 × 14 cm); base acute, sometimes obtuse, the lamina narrowing progressively or abruptly on the upper part of the petiole; apex obtuse, sometimes broadly acute, abruptly acuminate, acumen often inclined curved, ± broadly triangular, approximately 0.5 × 1 cm, obtuse at the apex; median nerve glabrescent beneath; lateral nerves 8–10–12 pairs, generally forming a very open angle (approximately 60°) with the median nerve, rectilinear and parallel from the start (especially for leaves of young shoots bearing 16–18 pairs of nerves); lamina glabrous; apart from the petiolar glands, sometimes very small glands beneath the lamina towards the base, on either side and away from the median nerve.

Terminal and axillary subapical cymose panicles, reaching 12(–18) cm in height with peduncle of 2–3 cm, 2–4 times ramified ± regularly dichotomously, the ultimate cymules grouping 3 flowers with pedicel of the central flower not exceeding 1.5 mm; inflorescences sometimes puberulent on the first axes, generally tomentellose to densely hirtellose, ash-coloured, extending to the exterior of the bracts, bracteoles and sepals. Bracts and bracteoles extremely rapidly caducous, ovate-concave to linear, the bracteoles not exceeding 1 mm in length. Flowers early caducous, 6 mm long with, beneath, a pedicel not exceeding 1.5 mm between the articulation and the base of the receptacle; receptacle obliquely campanulate, slightly gibbous at the base, 2.5–3 mm high on one side, 4–5 mm on the other and 3–3.5 mm wide at the apex obliquely surmounted by the cone of sepals, furnished internally at the throat and especially beneath the ovary with long dense hairs directed downward; sepals triangular acute, 1.5–3 mm, the upper one curved, sometimes 4 mm; petals white, oblong, 3.5–4 mm, acute at base and apex, with villous margin; stamens (7–)8–10, united at the base, curved, 2.5–3 mm high; staminodial rim 0.5 mm high, with 8–9 triangular points; ovary ovoid, 1 mm high, densely villous with ferruginous erect hairs reaching 1 mm in height, this ovary being divided into 2 locules by a median partition; style curved, 3.5 mm long, villous towards the base.

Infructescence bearing some fruits, with persistent sepals. Drupe globose but flattened, 35–50 × 45–60 × 50–70 mm, dark brown speckled with white then cracked in all directions; pericarp lignified; interior of endocarp and partition tomentose. — PL. 27, p. 91.

## Discussion

ENGLER (l.c. : 139) placed this species in the section Petrocarya (Schreb.) Oliv. of Parinari but, by its glabrous leaves, set it apart from all other species in this section. PELLEGRIN (l.c. : 267) considered that Parinari ingangense Pellegr. and Parinari tessmannii Engl. had great affinities but could not belong to the same section; he brought the first species closer to the genus Magnistipula Engl. whereas, later, DE WILDEMAN (Pl. Bequaert. 5 : 278, 1931) placed it, with doubt, in the section Neocarya DC. of the genus Parinari Aubl., while maintaining Parinari tessmannii Engl. in the section Petrocarya (Schreb.) Oliv. HAUMAN (l.c. : 189) subsequently found himself led to place Parinari ingangense Pellegr. in synonymy with Parinari tessmannii Engl. and to establish for this species, on account of its peculiarities, the subgenus Pellegriniella Haum. (alongside the subgenera Sarcostegia (Benth.) Haum., Neocarya (DC.) Haum. and Euparinari Haum.) within the genus Parinari Aubl. PRANCE (l.c. : 185) finally places the species in question in the subgenus Pellegriniella (Haum.) Prance of the genus Magnistipula Engl. and characterized this subgenus by the bilocular ovary and the thick, hard and smooth endocarp.

Magnistipula tessmannii (Engl.) Prance is known from Nigeria (Calabar), Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Congo (Mvouti) and Cabinda, almost always, it seems, in primary-type evergreen forest, often on hilly terrain, rarely near watercourses and, while not very frequent, this species does not appear in the end very rare compared to other Magnistipula. Its presence in "Congo", reported by MENDES (l.c. : 19), does not seem confirmed by specimens from the Congolese Mayombe (where it apparently exists) in the Paris herbarium, nor by HAUMAN (cf. FCB 3 : 52, 1952) for Zaïre.
