# *Dalbergia gilletii* De Wild. Planche 39

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: D. djalonensis A.Chev., Expl. Bot. Afr. Occ. Fr. 1 : 210 (1920), nom. nud

## Description

Studies fl. Bas- Moyen-Congo 2(2): 265 (1906). D. djalonensis A.Chev., Expl. Bot. Afr. Occ. Fr. 1: 210 (1920), nom. nud. Liane or shrub reaching 20 m in length, 5 m in height and 10 cm in diameter, sometimes ± leafless at flowering, inodorous, usually provided with strong spines (0.7–4 cm in length) on the trunk and old branches, often also provided with prehensile hooked branchlets; bark dark brown to reddish-brown, with pale lenticels not always very prominent; young branchlets glabrous or shortly pubescent. Stipules unknown, very early caducous. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole (0.6–)0.9–2.7 cm; rachis (1.3–)2.3–13.5 cm, glabrous to densely pubescent; leaflets (7–)11 to 32, alternate and slightly unequal, with petiolule 0.5–3 mm, blade elliptic to obovate, symmetric or nearly so, 0.9–5 × 0.4–2.5 cm in the upper leaflets and 0.4–3.4 × 0.2–2 cm in the lower ones, acute or rounded at base, rounded or emarginate at apex, rather coriaceous, olive-green to dark brown when dry, entirely glabrous to densely pubescent on both faces, with hairs either short and crispy, or (in non-Gabonese forms) long and strigillose; lateral veins 6 to 8, poorly marked and scarcely distinct from the veinlets, the latter in a network generally little apparent (at least in Gabonese specimens). Inflorescence in panicle often leafy at base and branched into contracted cymes, with very numerous flowers, terminal or axillary and sometimes on leafless branchlets, (2.5–)4–45 cm in length, with dense golden-brown hairs; bracts caducous or persistent, shortly elliptic, 1–1.2 × 0.3–0.5 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 0–2 mm; bracteoles caducous or persistent, orbicular or widely elliptic, 0.5–1 × 0.5–1 mm, rounded at apex, pubescent; calyx pale green, glabrescens or sparsely pubescent, tube 1–2.5 × 1.5–2 mm, imbricate teeth 0.8–1.7 mm, ± equal and rounded or the lower one sometimes slightly longer and acute, the upper ones fused at base; corolla white or rarely violet, petals with claw shorter than calyx, standard 3–6 × 2.2–4 mm, slightly and very obliquely bent and ± folded lengthwise, obovate and gradually narrowed toward base, with claw absent or very short (< 0.8 mm), wings ± equalling the keel, with claw 0.5–1 mm and blade ± rectangular 2–3.3 × 0.8–1.2 mm, truncate at base, smooth externally and provided with an internal thickening at the upper edge, keel almost straight, with claw 0.7–1 mm and blade 2–3.3 × 1–1.7 mm, slightly auriculate at base and provided with a pouch at the upper edge; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments 2–4 mm, fused for ⅔ to ¾ of their length, straight at base and curved in their free part; ovary 1.5–2 × 0.5 mm, glabrous or pubescent only on the stipe 1–1.2 mm, with 4 to 6 ovules; style short and thick, sometimes almost absent, < 1 mm, with oblique laminar stigma. Legume with pedicel 1–2 mm and stipe 2–4 mm, papyraceous, flattened and narrowly ellipsoid, (2.4–)2.8–7 × 0.7–1.9 cm, acute or rounded at base, rounded or obtuse at apex, with prominent reticulation over the entire surface and without differentiated zone around the seeds, glabrous, never glaucous, with 1 or 2 seeds. Seed reniform and flattened, ± 5 × 3 mm.

## Distribution

Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Togo, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, present in Moyen-Ogooué (east of Ndjolé), Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

forest galleries, shrubby savannas, forest regrowth and inselberg thickets; in Gabon, at 100–285 m altitude, elsewhere, at 0–1290 m. In Gabon, fruiting in October. Uses: No uses reported in Gabon. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the juice of the taproot is used in internal enema against syphilis. In Guinea, the decoction of the leaves which forms a transparent gel, is given to cattle against wasting

## Source note

Old stems generally have spines, but it is not certain that these are always present. This species has long been confused with D. hostilis, from which it differs however by numerous characters (Lachenaud & Maesen 2016; see key). It is very variable in the number, size and pubescence of the leaflets. The typical form, which is not known from Gabon, has small and numerous leaflets, with long straight and sparse hairs on both faces. Specimens from Gabon, and most from Cameroon and West Africa, have larger and fewer leaflets, glabrous or with fairly short crispy hairs. There are intermediates between these two forms, notably in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. b: Lachenaud & Maesen (2016). Bibliography
