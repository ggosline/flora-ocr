# *Phyllanthus muellerianus* (O.Ktze) Exell

**Genus**: [[Phyllanthus]]
**Family**: [[Phyllanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Cat. Vasc. Pl. S. Tomé : 290 (1944)

## Description

Lianescent shrub with woody lianas, up to 10 m tall; branches glabrous; cataphylls and stipules of orthotropic axes spinescent. Stipules 1.2–1.7 mm long. Leaf: petiole 2.5–3 mm; lamina ovate to lanceolate, 4–7 × 1.8–3.5 cm, cuneate or obtuse to subcordate at base, acute to acuminate or obtuse and mucronate at apex, with (5–)7–11 pairs of lateral veins. Inflorescence bisexual. Flowers monoecious. Male flower: pedicel 2–2.5 mm; sepals 5, elliptic, ± 1 mm long; disk with 5 free glands; stamens 5, free, with filaments 0.7–1 mm long. Female flower: pedicel ± 1 mm; sepals 5, ± 1 mm long, caducous early; disk ± annular; ovary 3–4 locular, smooth; styles 3–4, bifid. Fruit 3–5 mm in diameter, fleshy, reddish. Seeds with spherical arillode, 1.2–1.5 mm long, reddish, with reticulate testa when dry.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa from Guinea to Sudan in the east, and in Angola, Zambia and Malawi in the south; in Gabon, present in all provinces.

## Ecology

Secondary and secondarized forest; in Gabon, up to ± 500 m altitude. Uses: P. muellerianus is commonly used to treat various intestinal disorders. The leaves and branches have numerous other medicinal uses (see Ben-Bala 2008). The fruits are edible. Bibliography: Ben-Bala (2008), Brunel (1987), Keay (1958), Radcliffe-Smith (1996b), Smith (1987).
