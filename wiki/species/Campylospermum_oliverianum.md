# *Campylospermum oliverianum* (Gilg) Farron, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 35 : 400

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]

## Description

(1965). Tree reaching 9 m in height; branches with whitish-brown bark. Stipules persistent, triangular, 4–13 mm long. Leaf: petiole 3–6(–7) mm; lamina narrowly elliptic to narrowly elliptic-obovate or sometimes elliptic-obovate, (6–)9–17(–21) × (2–)3–6(–7) cm, ratio 2.3–4.2, narrowly cuneate to attenuate at base, acuminate at apex, coriaceous to papyraceous, distinctly to slightly bullate, margin serrulate to entire; primary vein strongly prominent on upper surface, prominent on lower surface, lateral veins in 10 to 16 pairs, generally canaliculate above, prominent or sometimes slightly canaliculate below, tertiary veins scalariform, perpendicular to primary vein, distinct above, slightly distinct below. Inflorescence terminal, non-branched or rarely with a raceme, compact, main axis 1–5(–8) cm; gemmate scales persistent, often numerous, narrowly triangular, reaching 12 mm long; cymules distant by 3–7 mm, 1- to 7-flowered; bracts persistent, triangular, 2–3(–5) mm long. Flower: pedicel 7–13 mm, articulate at 2–3 mm from base; sepals oval, 8–10 × 3–4 mm in flower, 10–12 × 5–6 mm in fruit, covering the drupels, coriaceous; petals elliptic to obovate, 10–13 × 3–8 mm, truncate at base, rounded to emarginate at apex; stamens: anthers 4–7 mm long; style 4–7 mm. Fruit: receptacle 3–6 mm in diameter; drupels 1 or 2 well-developed per receptacle, ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, 7–9 × 4–7 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, southern part of the Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, widespread throughout the country

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, forest gallery, swamp forest, along rivers and streams, on clay and sandy soil; in Gabon and elsewhere, up to 960 m elevation. Use: The species is considered an ornamental plant (Burkill 1997). Taxonomic note: C. sulcatum is well distinguished by its short, compact inflorescence and its bullate leaves. Therefore, this species presents traits resembling those of C. excavatum. But the latter is distinguished from it by its inflorescence which is often branched, its narrow sepals and numerous more pronounced tertiary veins.
