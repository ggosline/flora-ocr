# *Artabotrys le-* Testui Pellegrin (P1. 24, p. 135)

**Genus**: [[Artabotrys]]
**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull.Soc.Bot.Fr. 94 : 255 (1947)

## Description

Vine with branches densely pubescent-ferruginous. Leaves with petiole approximately 5 mm long, canaliculate above, densely rusty pubescent. Blade coriaceous, elliptic to elliptic-oblong, 9–14 cm long, 4.5–6.5 cm wide, rounded at base, very slightly asymmetric, apex obtuse, rounded or slightly acuminate; upper face glabrous except at the base of the midvein, lower face hirsute, with rusty and short hairs. Midvein prominent beneath; 8–10 pairs of lateral veins oblique and very ascending, prominent beneath.

Inflorescence multiflorous with axis and peduncle curved hook-like, 1–2 cm long, densely rusty pubescent. Flowers with short pedicel, 2–3 mm long. Bracts broadly triangular-ovate, densely rusty pubescent. Sepals deltoid, 2–3 mm long, rusty pubescent on the outside, glabrous on the inside. Outer petals lanceolate, 17–18 mm long, 4–5 mm wide, narrowed toward the base itself dilated, concave, subspatulate, covered with long appressed and silky hairs on the outside, and minute hairs on the inside; inner ones much narrower, 2 mm wide, linear, costulate, covered with minute hairs on both faces, with dilated base forming a trigone cavity applied to the reproductive organs. Stamens 3–4-seriate, sessile, small, anthers extrorse, connectives dilated peltate, glabrous. Carpels 6–10, exceeding the stamens, approximately 2 mm long, ovaries ovoid, densely pubescent, stigmas very developed, bilobed. 2 basal ovules. Receptacle rusty pubescent.

Fruit with thick pedicel, 1–1.5 cm long; mericarps green, sessile, obovoid, 16–20 mm long, 8–10 mm wide, rounded at apex, glabrous.

## Discussion

This species has only been collected in Gabon, but very near the Cameroonian border.

It is very close to A. Thomsonii Oliver, but the lower face of the leaves is densely hairy instead of glabrous, and the floral pedicels are much shorter, 2–3 mm instead of 10–20 mm.

Species of dense humid forest and forest galleries widespread in southern Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo-Kinshasa and Central African Republic.

This species known currently in Central Africa and Gabon is related to A. crassipetalus Pellegrin; although we do not know the fruits of this latter species, A. rhopalocarpus differs from it by its larger leaves, longer petiole, and triangular-acute sepals 5–8 mm long and not semi-orbicular 3 mm long only.
