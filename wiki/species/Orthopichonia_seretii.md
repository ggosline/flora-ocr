Leaf: petiole 5–13 mm; limb elliptic to obovate, sometimes elliptic-oblong, 0.8–1.3 times as long as wide, 7–19 × 5–16 cm, cuneate at the base, apex acuminate for 6–14 mm, glabrous on both faces; secondary nerves 14 to 35 pairs, alternating with less apparent nerves. Inflorescences axillary, often apparently terminal, solitary or 2 or 3 together, rather condensed, long of 12–45 mm with 3–6 flowers, pedicel reaching 1 mm; pedicel and main axis glabrous, sometimes weakly pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1–4 mm; calyx with lobes 0.7–1.6 mm long; corolla with pale green to cream tube, with white lobes, 12.7–28 mm long in mature bud, glabrous outside, tube 7.5–16 mm long, with maximum width at 2–2.8 mm above the base, 1–1.5 mm in diameter, then contracted just above the anthers, inside with a pilose band of 0.4–0.7 mm above the base up to 0.5–1 mm above the apex of the anthers or of the throat, lobes narrowly ovate to narrowly triangular, 0.5–1.3 times as long as the tube, 1.8–3.5 times as long as wide, 4–9 × 0.8–2 mm, outside glabrous, ± ciliate, inside glabrous or sometimes slightly hairy at the base; stamens inserted at 1.6–2.4 mm from the base of the tube, anther 0.7–1 mm long; pistil 1.5–3 mm long, with stigma situated 1.4–1.8 mm below the apex of the anthers, ovary glabrous or with a hirsute band of 0.5 mm above the base up to the apex of the ovary. Fruit ± globose to ellipsoid, at least 5 cm in diameter, with several seeds and white pulp. Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, and Gabon; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ogooué, Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: Primary and secondary forest in Gabon; 0–450 m altitude; flowering up to 700 m.

In Vonk's revision (1989), O. longituba is treated as a synonym of O. indeniensis (A.Chev.) H.Huber; we treat it here rather as a synonym of O. viscifflua. The latter (including O. longituba) and O. indeniensis differ by the shape of the corolla; that of O. viscifflua is more fleshy, with rigid and upturned lobes, whereas that of O. indeniensis has spreading and more reflexed lobes. The size of the corolla changes gradually from Nigeria (region where the type of O. longituba originates) towards the south-east, and it is not possible to separate O. longituba from O. viscifflua. O. indeniensis is now known only from western Nigeria. B : Vonk (1989). BIBLIOGRAPHY.
