# *Pentaloncha humilis* Hook. f.

**Genus**: [[Pentaloncha]]
**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bentham et Hook., Gen. Pl. 2 : 73 (1873). Hicern, Oliver F.T.A. 3 : 79 (1877). Oliver, Hook. 1c. Pl. 24, pl. 2326 (1895)

## Description

Subherbaceous erect, about 15-30 cm tall, with the lower part of the stem ± prostrate with rooting nodes; young extremities with very small hairs. Internodes of (8) 10-40 (60) mm, glabrescent, compressed longitudinally when dry. Interpetiolar stipules attenuate-acuminate, sometimes bifid, 14-16 mm long by 2.2-5 mm wide at the base, glabrescent to glabrous. Leaves sometimes weakly, sometimes ± or very strongly anisophyllous; the most reduced of a pair may be only 7-10 mm long with a narrow lanceolate lamina; when anisophylly is very strong, the developed leaves appear alternate. Petiole 5-20 (25) mm, ± puberulent. Young leaves and stipules violaceous in vivo. Lamina chartaceous in vivo, dry to the touch and discolorous, olive-green ± yellowish when dry, obovate, 7-18 (25) × 3.5-6 (12) cm, glabrous on both faces; base cuneate fairly long-attenuate, more rarely obtuse; apex shortly rounded surmounted by a short acute acumen. Main veins beneath ± puberulent, the midvein finely canaliculate above; 10-14 (17) pairs of secondaries.

Inflorescences axillary with peduncle 2-6 (20) mm, bearing a very contracted corymbiform cyme of about 5-20 flowers, and above its mid-length a pair of opposite prefoliate bracts; bractlets elongate and narrow. Pedicels slender, 1-3 mm long. Bud with subacute violet corolla. Calyx with 5 slightly unequal lobes, pedicels and spathulate-lanceolate, 5-7 mm long, whitish in vivo, yellowish to light green when dry, with short arcuate marginal hairs. Corolla about 6 mm long, pale violaceous (or whitish?), with cylindrical tube 1 mm wide at the base, with 5 (6) lobes puberulent externally; pubescence dense inside the tube except the lower third which is glabrous. 5 anthers with short filament, inserted at the middle of the tube (long-style form) or at the top (short-style form), about 1.5 × 0.4 mm, arcuate apiculate. Pollen simple tricolporate. Ovary puberulent, pale yellow in vivo, with 2 (3) locules; disc light yellow. Style slender, glabrous, with narrowly bilobed stigma over 2 mm.

Fruits spherical indehiscent bearing the persistent sepals and with lamina somewhat accrescent. Seeds spherical, reddish-brown, about 1/4 mm in diam., scrobiculate, about 160 in an average fruit, inserted in fossules on two peltate placentae.

## Type

Mann 1734 (K!), Monts de Cristal

## Discussion

This species is endemic to the Cristal Mountains. It grows in intact undergrowth, on slopes or embankments, often near ± torrential streams. Tending to be gregarious, individual plants are encountered in small groups. The roots are orange. The fruits persist long on the mother plant. A viviparous infructescence has been observed.

The original description is erroneous on several points, notably concerning the number of ovary locules (the type has been reviewed); 22 years later, the figure of Miss Smith in Oliver requires completion: the internal piloseness of the corolla extends a little below the anthers (fig. 2); moreover the placental folds of the two locules in fig. 5 are inaccurate. The approximation suggested by Hiern with the genus Schizostigma Arn., from Ceylon, appears to us to be based on a resemblance of habit and not on truly related structures.
