# *Landolphia ligustrifolia* (Stapf) Pichon Planche 48(7-17) ; Figure 8(A, B)

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 154 (1953)

## Description

Large liana; branchlets with small light lenticels, glabrous or pilose. Leaf: petiole of 3–9 cm, blade elliptic or obovate, rarely ovate, 4.2–15 × 1.3–7.5 cm, rounded or less often retuse or cuneate at the base, generally acuminate at the apex, glabrous or weakly pilose towards the base; secondary veins 7 to 12 pairs, curved, not prominent beneath. Inflorescence axillary. Flower fragrant; dichasial and calyx attaining mm; bracteoles, glossy, distichous; sepals globose, 1.6–3.5 × 1.7–3.2 mm, ciliate and often membranous at the margin, pilose on the outside; corolla white, yellowish and greenish, with tube 12–30 mm long, 1.1–2.4 mm wide at the base, 2.1–4 mm at the level of the anthers and 2–4 mm at the throat, glabrous on the outside or pilose on and around the auricles, partially pilose on the inside, with lobes narrowly ovate to elliptic, 0.4–0.5 times the length of the tube, 0.5–0.9 times as long as wide, 2–5 mm, partially pilose and pubescent, with ciliate or glabrous margin; stamens at the throat level or included 2 mm, inserted at 0.81–0.9 of the length of the corolla tube, anther 1.5–2.5 mm long; pistil 16–24 mm long, ovary pilose; style 11.5–18 mm, pilose, more sparsely towards the apex. Fruit of variable shape, generally rounded or retuse at the base, 3.8–17 × 2.7–13 cm, shiny, red, violet, yellow or orange, sometimes with dark violet longitudinal bands, with 5 to 100 seeds; wall 0.2–1.7 cm thick. Distribution: Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, recorded in all provinces except Ogooué-Lolo. Ecology: primary and secondary forests, often at the forest edge; in Gabon up to m altitude, elsewhere up to m. Use: The latex is sometimes used as glue. The fruits are edible. Notes: This species is sometimes fertile, sometimes difficult to separate from L. violacea. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
