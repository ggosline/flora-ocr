Small monocaulous tree 10 cm in diameter, rarely branched, 3–10 m tall, with brownish pubérulent apex. Petiolar base thick, subtended by a stout rib bordered by 2 grooves. Leaf rachis reaching 85 cm in length, terminated by a slender, pubescent appendage, approximately 7 mm; 9–12 pairs of leaflets sometimes alternate, with brown-reddish or olive blade when dry; the upper ones longer, reaching 35 × 7.5 cm; midvein glabrous above, flattened or depressed; up to 20–25 pairs of secondary veins.

Spiciform inflorescences, cauline, surrounding the base or inserted on the lower half-metre of the trunk, unisexual or first producing male flowers, then pistillate flowers; reddish or garnet-pink pubérulent channelled axis (at least in the typical form), 15–40 (–60) cm long, unbranched except exceptionally, with very numerous flowers¹; lower internodes non-floriferous extended over 5–10 cm; bracts 2–3 mm; numerous lateral cymes few- or many-flowered tending to curve all to the same side of the raceme; they are pedunculate for 1–3 mm and bear 1 or 2 branches in the most proliferous forms, 2–5 (–20) mm. Flower bud ovoid; pedicel 2–4 mm. Flower with conical appearance at anthesis in vivo, of variable color depending on the forms, from incarnate red to greenish white, generally cream-white, with garnet-pink calyx base; calyx 10 × 7 mm, split for 5 mm into 5 acute lobes, sometimes slightly adhering to each other in pairs; 4–5 whitish petals exserted for 1.5–2 mm, pubescent, with divergent hairs on the median line of the outer face, 9–11 × 2–2.5 mm, with blade denticulate at the apex, with a long ligule, sometimes emarginate and often refracted at the apex; appendicule absent or salient, ± elongate, at the apex of the fusion line of the ligule; disk pale yellow subpentagonal, 4 mm; 7–8 stamens with pubescent filament; glabrous anther 1.9–2.5 × 0.9–1.2 mm with connective lacking a hump or wart; sometimes a few isolated hairs at one of the extremities; trilocular ovary, hirsute, aborted in the ♂ flower. ♀ Flowers most often simultaneous and numerous, succeeding ♂ flowers, either on the same inflores-

Young fruit garnet-violet densely clothed hispid, with reddish ± variegated hairs, approximately 0.5–1.5 mm in length, with 6 ribs of which only 3 meet at the apex, the less prominent ones laterally. Mildbraed 3032 (ou 3082) et 3231, région de Mawambi, Ituri, Zaïre (BR)

## Discussion

From Sierra Leone (Ch. villiger, in FWTA) to Central Africa (Tisserant 1492, 2164, 2631), Zaïre (Yangambi) and Angola (Welwitsch 1690), this species is the most widely distributed in Africa.

Uses: The fruits would be edible according to WALKER, rather than toxic, according to HAUMAN. It is not specified whether it is the seeds or the mesocarp that is consumed.

Breteler 2025, Nguélémdouka near Bamo river, alt. 700 m (fr., Nov.).

Letouzey 1972, Simbane near Nanga Eboko (fl., May); 3971, Mayos near Abong Mbang (fl., May).

Villiers 634, 25 km N Moloundou (fl., Apr.).

DOUBTFUL SPECIMEN: Annet 463, Déhane (fl., June; the anther is pubescent but connective, ligule, appendicule etc. are indeed those of C. carneus). The problem arises anew of a possibility of hybridization.
