# *Flagellaria guineensis* Schumacher

**Family**: [[Flagellariaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Flagellaria]]

In Schumacher & Thonning, *Beskrivelse af Guineiske Planter*: 181 (1827).

## Synonymy

- *Flagellaria indica* sensu Engl., *Pflanzenwelt Ost-Afrikas* C: 133 (1896).
- *Flagellaria indica* L. var. *guineensis* (Schumach.) Engl., *Vegetation der
  Erde* 2: 257, tab. 174 (1908).

## Description

**Habit**: Climbing herb. Stem surrounded by leaf sheaths 3–3.5 cm long,
split for a variable proportion of their length and auriculate at the apex;
edges of the split papery and paler.

**Leaves**: Blade glabrous and green on both surfaces, narrowly lanceolate,
9.5–25 × 0.8–3 cm; base abruptly attenuate; apex terminating in a spiralled
tendril. Midrib prominent on both surfaces. Secondary veins very numerous,
parallel to one another and to the midrib, prominent on both surfaces.

**Inflorescence**: A many-flowered terminal panicle.

**Flowers**: Hermaphrodite, trimerous, actinomorphic and greenish-white.
Tepals 6 in two whorls of 3, elliptic, 2.6 × 1 mm, papery, apex obtuse.
Stamens 6, free, gynobasic and epitepalous, 2.5 mm long; filament 0.75 mm;
anthers introrse, ellipsoid, 2.5 mm long, opening by longitudinal slits.
Pistil superior, 2.75 mm long; ovary ellipsoid and triangular in cross-section;
style ending in 3 stigmas.

**Fruit**: A one-seeded drupe, bright red when fresh, globose, 6 mm in
diameter, apex apiculate. Endocarp woody.

## Type

Ghana, *Thonning s.n.* (holotype C).

## Distribution

**Range**: Along all tropical African coasts; particularly abundant in Natal.

**Gabon**: Cap Lopez, Libreville and its surroundings (Komo, Cap Esterias and
south of Libreville), Port-Gentil, and the Maliba River; several specimens lack
a locality.

## Habitat and ecology

Frequent along forest rivers. Flowering material was collected in March,
April, October and December; fruiting material in April, June and July, with
some collections lacking a month.

## Notes

Napper reported long stamen filaments in the *Flora of Tropical East Africa*,
while noting that short-filament specimens also occur. Villiers examined only
the short-filament condition.
