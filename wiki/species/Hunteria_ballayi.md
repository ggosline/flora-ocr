# *Hunteria ballayi* Hua Planche 37 ; Figure 6(C)

**Genus**: [[Hunteria]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Mus. Hist. Paris 7 : 281 (1902)

## Description

Pleuranthemum ballayi (Hua) Pichon, Bull. Soc. Brot., sér. 2, 27: 113 (1953). Shrub, sometimes lianescent (?), reaching 5 m in height; wood very hard. Leaf: petiole of ±1.5–2 mm, pubescent to glabrous; lamina obovate to oblong, 1–2 × 2.2–8.1 cm, cuneate at base, acuminate at apex; secondary veins straight, in 20 to 30 pairs forming an angle of ±45° with the midvein, united in a submarginal vein. Inflorescence mainly axillary, sometimes ramiflorous, sometimes terminal, fasciculate, of 3–10 flowers; peduncle null or up to 2 mm. Flower: pedicel of ±1 mm; calyx with lobes 5, sometimes also as long as wide, of ±2.5 × ±1.5 mm, with rows of colleters on the interior up to the two-fifths of their length; corolla long of 7–11 mm in the mature bud, off-white, pale yellow in the bud, with a relatively small ovoid head of ±1.5 mm of the length of the bud, apex obtuse to rounded, furnished with a pubescent band 1 mm wide on the interior just below the insertion of the stamens, tube long of 5–7.2 mm, enlarged at the level of the anthers to 1.3–1.8 mm, with lobes not twisted in the bud, ±2.5 times as long as the tube, ±0.8 times as wide, of ±3 × 1.3–1.5 mm; stamens with apex at 0.2–0.3 mm below the throat, inserted at 0.7–0.8 of the length of the tube, anther long of 0.9–1.4 mm; pistil long of 4.6–5.6 mm, with 2 ovules in each carpel. Fruit: mericarps obovoid to ellipsoid, of 15–28 × 9–15 × 6–11 mm, yellow to orange, warty to smooth, with a beak of 2–6 mm at the apex, with 2 seeds, with wall of ±2 mm in thickness, pulp smooth or slightly fibrous. Seed ellipsoid to subglobose, of 8–13 × 5–9 × 3–7 mm. Distribution: Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, and Central African Republic; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime, and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: understory of primary and secondary forest, inselbergs in Gabon, up to 800 m in altitude; elsewhere at 350–500 m. B: Omino (1996). BIBLIOGRAPHY
