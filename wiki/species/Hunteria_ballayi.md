# *Hunteria ballayi* Hua Planche 37 ; Figure 6(C)

**Genus**: [[Hunteria]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Mus. Hist. Paris 7 : 281 (1902)

## Synonyms

- Pleuranthemum ballayi (Hua) Pichon, Bull. Soc. Brot. , sér. 2, 27 : 113 (1953).

## Description

Shrub, sometimes lianescent (?), reaching 5 m in height; wood very hard. Leaf: petiole of ±mm, pubescent; blade obovate to oblong-obovate ± times as long as broad ± × 2.2–8.1 cm, cuneate at base, acuminate at apex; secondary nerves straight, in 20 to 30 pairs forming an angle of ± with the median nerve; reunited in a submarginal nerve. Inflorescence principally axillary, sometimes ramiflorous, sometimes terminal, fasciculate; peduncle null or up to 2 mm. Flower: pedicel of ±mm; calyx with ± lobes ± times as long as broad, of ± × ±mm with rows of colleters on the interior up to the two-fifths of their length; corolla long of 7–11 mm in mature bud, off-white, pale yellow in the bud, with a relatively small ovoid head of ± of the length of the bud from apex, obtuse to rounded, provided with a pubescent band 1 mm broad on the interior just beneath the insertion of the stamens, tube long of 5–7.2 mm, enlarged at the level of the anthers to 1.3–1.8 mm, with lobes not twisted in the bud ± times as long as the tube ± times as long as broad, of ± × 1.3–1.5 mm; stamens with apex 0.2–0.3 mm below the throat, inserted at 0.7–0.8 of the length of the tube, anther long of 0.9–1.4 mm; pistil long of 4.6–5.6 mm, with 2 ovules in each carpel. Fruit: mericarps obovoid to ellipsoid, of 15–28 × 9–15 × 6–11 mm, yellow to orange, verrucose to smooth, with beak of 2–6 mm at apex, with 2 seeds, with wall of ±mm thick; pulp smooth or slightly fibrous. Seed ellipsoid to subglobose, of 8–13 × 5–9 × 3–7 mm. Distribution: Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, and Central African Republic; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ogooué, Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime, and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: understory of primary and secondary forest, island-isolated in Gabon, up to m of altitude; elsewhere at 350–500 m. B: Omino (1996). BIBLIOGRAPHY
