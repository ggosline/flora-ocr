# *Lippia multiflora* Moldenke Planche 38

**Genus**: [[Lippia]]
**Family**: [[Verbenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Phytologia 3 : 168 (1949)

## Description

Shrub 0.9–4 m tall, aromatic; stem striated, with scattered appressed hairs rigid in manner and others glandular. Leaves generally in whorls of 3; petiole 2–10 mm; blade narrowly elliptic to oblong-elliptic-ovate, 5.5–17 × 1–5.5 cm, cuneate at base, acute at apex, broadly to very narrowly serrulate, upper surface with hairs tuberculate at base, ± smooth to the touch, lower surface glabrous or with dense appressed hairs and others ± resinous glandular, longer on the nerves; venation impressed above in herbarium material. Inflorescence terminal, with a few axillary in the upper nodes, 3–20 cm long, with numerous subglobose to cylindric-oblong spikes, 0.5–2 × 0.7–0.8 cm, densely hairy; primary bracts leaf-like; secondary bracts elliptic-ovate to suborbicular, 3–6 × 1–3 mm, obtuse to shortly mucronate. Flower fragrant; calyx subglobose, ± 1 mm long, densely pubescent with erect hairs, with very short lobes; corolla white or pale yellow, throat yellow, tube funnel-shaped from a narrow base, ± 2 mm long, densely hairy outside, lower lip oblong, ± 1.5 × 1.5 mm. Seed semiovoid, 1–1.5 × 0.8 mm.

## Distribution

Native to West African tropical Africa to Sudan and northern Angola, including Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Central African Republic; in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Grasslands, savannas; in Gabon 0–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Use: The plant is used for medicinal purposes throughout its range. In Gabon, it is sometimes cultivated in gardens and sold in markets. The leaves are added to coffee to improve the taste or steeped in tea, while the roots are sometimes used as a vegetable salt. According to Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), the leaves serve to prepare a sudorific infusion, it is also used against stomach pain and as a substitute for kinkéliba. The Mpongwé often use it in steam baths against fever

## Vernacular names

afi (fang) ; bogolo (benga) ; dikadumba di kumu (eshira, bavarama) ; ewuma (nkomi) ; muvérèvèsè (bavili, baduma) ; muvèvèsè (banzabi) ; ndjindji-mbani (mpongwé)

## Source note

It is highly probable that L. adoensis Hochst. ex Walp. treated by Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961) in fact represents L. multiflora, which is why their data are reproduced above.
