# *Lantana ukambensis* (Vatke) Verdc. Planche 37

**Genus**: [[Lantana]]
**Family**: [[Verbenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. E. Afr., Verbenac . : 43 (1992)

## Description

Herb or sometimes shrub 0.2–1.5 m tall, aromatic; stem quadrangular, unarmed, pilose with long whitish hairs with tuberculate base, interspersed with very short varnished bristles and scattered sessile glands. Leaves in whorls of 3 or sometimes opposite; petiole 1–5(–8) mm; lamina ovate to ovate-elliptic, 1.7–10 × 0.5–5 cm, broadly cuneate to attenuate at base, acute to rounded at apex, denticulate-crenate or crenate, above pilose with appressed hairs, rugose or plane, below coarsely and densely velvety with hairs and glands yellowish-grey-green. Inflorescence in solitary or paired spikes, conical, 0.3–5 cm long; peduncle short, generally not exceeding the leaf base, but elongating and becoming oblong in fruit; bracts elliptic-ovate or triangular-ovate, 8–20 × 5–13 mm, acute to cuspidate at apex, often violaceous. Flower: calyx 1–2.5 mm long, fine; corolla scarcely longer than the bracts, dark purple, crimson or magenta, sometimes with a white or yellow centre, with tube 2–5 mm long, pilose on the outside, with limb 1–3 mm in diameter, with rounded lobes, 0.7–1.5 mm long. Fruit subglobose, 2–5 mm in diameter, blue or violet at maturity.

## Distribution

Native to tropical Africa, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic and Angola; in Gabon, known from Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

Woodland grasslands, grasslands, former plantations; in Gabon at approximately 550 m altitude, elsewhere at 75–2070 m

## Source note

This species had been incorporated into L. rhodesiensis Moldenke by Huber et al. (1963) and Verdcourt (1992). However, this was questioned by Fernandes (1989) and Fernandes & Diniz (2005), who stated that specimens from West Africa that they had seen were different from those in the Flora Zambesiaca region and those from the Democratic Republic of Congo and they therefore reinstated this species as a distinct taxon.
