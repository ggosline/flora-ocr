# *Ekebergia capensis* Sparrm. Planche 2

**Genus**: [[Ekebergia]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kongl. Vetensk. Acad. Handl. 40 : 282, t. 9 (1779)

## Description

Tree reaching 30(–35) m in height; trunk up to 1 m in diameter; bark greyish-brown, squamose; inner surface brown, odorless, without exudate; branchlets puberulent, becoming glabrous. Leaves clustered in a tuft towards the apex of the branches; petiole 7–11 cm, planoconvex, glabrous or finely pubescent; rachis 14–24 cm; petiolule very short or absent; leaflets 9–15, ovate to elliptic or rectangular, 3.5–10(–13) × 2–3.5(–6) cm, obtuse or cuneiform and sometimes slightly asymmetric at the base, with subacuminate to acute or subrounded apex; lateral veins 13–15 pairs, inconspicuous. Inflorescence in panicles 10–20 cm long, shortly branched. Flower: pedicel articulated, 1.5–2 mm; calyx cupuliform, 1–2 mm long, shortly 4(–5)-dentate, puberulent on the outside; petals 4(–5), narrowly rectangular to elliptic, 4–7 × 2–2.5 mm, tomentose; staminal tube broadly cupuliform, 2–3 mm long, with obscurely crenulate margin, slightly fused to the disk at the base and glabrescant externally, densely white-hairy internally and externally towards the apex, with 8(–10) anthers 1 mm long; gynoecium 4–5 mm long, glabrescant, surrounded by an annular disk 0.5 mm high, ovary broadly ovoid, 1–2 mm in diameter, 4–5-locular; style thick, 3 mm, stigma capitate. Fruit subglobose, 15–25 mm in diameter, reddish at maturity, containing (3–)4–5 pyrenes.

## Distribution

species with wide African distribution, from Senegal to Ethiopia and south to South Africa; rare and very localized in the Atlantic zone of dense forest in Central Africa, from Cameroon to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, to be sought in Gabon

## Ecology

limited to mountainous zones; up to 2600 m altitude. Taxonomic notes: 1 - We follow here Styles & White (1991) who consider that the Ekebergia of West Africa belong to a single variable taxon with varied ecology and wide distribution. 2 - The only Gabonese specimen previously attributed to the genus Ekebergia and preserved at the National Herbarium of Paris, namely Soyaux 103, belongs to Pseudospondias (Anacardiaceae).
