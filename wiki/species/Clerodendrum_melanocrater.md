# *Clerodendrum melanocrater* Gürke

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 18 : 180 (1893)

## Description

Vine or ± climbing shrub, 1.8–4.5 m tall or large shrub with long slender branches climbing up to 8 m in height; stem quadrangular, brown to black, with pale carinate angles, striate when dry, with ± mixed pubescence, pubescent and appressed when young, sometimes with persistent recurved pseudospines 6 mm long. Leaves opposite; petiole (0.6–)3–6 cm; lamina ovate to elliptic or oblong-elliptic, (1.5–)5–16 × 0.7–9.5 cm, cordate to rounded or cuneate at base, acuminate and mucronate or with a ± rounded but mucronate apex, entire, ± finely pubescent above, glabrous below, black or black-violet when dry. Inflorescence terminal, thyrsoid, lax, 9–25 × 8–15 cm, with up to 20 lateral dichasia with numerous flowers; peduncle reaching 4 cm, with appressed grey or pale ferruginous pubescence. Flower: pedicel 5–23 mm; calyx with cupulate tube, 2–4 mm long, white or white-mauve, with ovate lobes, 1.5–2 × 2 mm, acute at apex; corolla cream becoming yellowish or brownish-yellow, ink-blue when damaged, fading and blackening on the living plant, with tube 7–10 mm, with ovate lobes, 2–3 × 1–2 mm; stamens exserted 0.8 cm, anthers dark blue. Fruit 5–10 × 5–8 mm, black.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko), Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and tropical eastern Africa; in Gabon: Estuaire. Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961: 422) report it as very common in Gabon, although only a single herbarium specimen has been located by the author. Conservation status: preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC) by Pollard in Cheek et al. (2004).

## Ecology

cultivated in family vegetable gardens, flowering towards the middle of the dry season; in Gabon at ± 30 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2100 m. Uses: Leaves crushed and macerated in egg white are a remedy for headaches. The Fang consider this plant a talisman for protection against evil spells.

## Vernacular names

dikapa (éshira) ; ebelbeleyem, étsag’eyèn (fang) ; ndjana (mpongwè) ; nondjango (apindji) ; pólè-misu (bakèlè). B : Cheek et al. (2004), Huber et al. (1963), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Verdcourt i B liographie (1992)
