# *Hannoa klaineana* Pierre ex Engl.

**Family**: [[Simaroubaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Hannoa]]

## Synonymy

- *Hannoa gabonensis* Pierre

## Description

Tree. Leaves glabrous, imparipinnate, with 3–7 pairs of leaflets; rachis 15–25 cm, petiole broadened and channelled at the base. Leaflets oblong-obovate or obovate-elliptic, apex rounded, sometimes slightly emarginate or very shortly blunt-acuminate, base cuneate, 7–17 × 3.5–7.5 cm; blade coriaceous and matt on both faces; midrib prominent on both faces, especially beneath; lateral veins slightly impressed and very faint, veinlets invisible.

Panicles large, terminal and lax, finely pubescent or nearly glabrous. Flowers male or bisexual. Pedicels about 2 mm, finely pubescent. Calyx enclosing the bud, then splitting into three unequal thin translucent segments, finely pubescent outside. Petals 5, yellow, oblong-lanceolate, about 4 mm, finely pubescent outside and hairy inside. Stamens 10; filaments flat, 3–4 mm, with a hairy basal ligule. Disc hollow; five rudimentary carpels free but contiguous, with five short united styles.

Fruits violet, ovoid drupes 2–3 cm, about the size of a small plum, glabrous and one-stoned, solitary or grouped 2–5 at the end of a short common peduncle in pendent clusters.

## Distribution

Widespread through the Guineo-Congolian forest from Guinea to Angola, the Congo Basin and Ubangi. Apparently uncommon in Gabon.

## Habitat and ecology

A fast-growing secondary-forest tree, without large buttresses. Young plants have a straight slender stem crowned by large pinnate leaves. Bark yellowish and smooth, flaking in circular scales; slash thin and whitish. Wood white and soft.

## Specimens examined

**Gabon** — Klaine 407, 1333 (holotype), 2306, 3127; Le Testu 6322 (Ndingui); S.R.F. 47 (Gabon estuary).

## Vernacular names

*ikondéré* (Orungu).
