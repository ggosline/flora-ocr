# *Passiflora edulis* Sims

**Genus**: [[Passiflora]]
**Family**: [[Passifloraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Mag. 45 : tab. 1989 (1818)

## Description

Herbaceous liane, reaching 15(25) m in height, entirely glabrous (except the ovary). Stipules subulate, 1–7 mm long. Leaf with petiole 1–4 cm; blade deeply 3-lobed, incised to three-quarters, rarely entire, 5–13 × 6–15 cm, rounded to subcordate at the base, lobes acute to acuminate at the apex, with serrate margins, laminar glands absent. Inflorescence uniflorous, with a simple tendril 5–20 cm; peduncle 2.5–6 cm, terminated by an involucre of 3 leafy bracts; bracts ovate to elliptic, 1–2 cm long, acute at the apex, persistent in fruit. Flower with pedicel approximately 5 mm; hypanthium cupuliform, approximately 1 × 1–1.5 cm; sepals 5, free, oblong, 2–3 cm long, horned dorsally towards the apex, white; petals 5, oblong, shorter and narrower than the sepals, the inner ones much shorter, white; operculum membranous, incurved; stamens hypogynous, filaments robust, 6–8 mm, curved, anthers 8–10 mm long, pendulous; androgynophore 6–10 mm, in fruit up to 15 mm; ovary subglobose to ellipsoid, 3–5 mm long, finely and densely silky-tomentellous to glabrous; styles 3, 10–12 mm, spreading, stigmas in two clubs. Fruit globose to ellipsoid, 3–5 cm long, with coriaceous pericarp, greenish to violet or yellow, glabrous. Seeds: several dozen per fruit, elliptic, 5 × 6 mm, with pulpy orange aril, finely pitted with approximately 50 small holes per side.

## Distribution

native to tropical America, cultivated in all tropical regions; in Gabon, collected from Ogooué-Maritime, Woleu-Ntem and Haut-Ogooué, but probably present throughout the country

## Ecology

cultivated and naturalized in fallows, secondary scrub, roadsides, degraded forests; in Gabon, up to 500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2500 m. Flowers and fruits year-round, mainly from August to December. Use: The pulpy aril and sweet juice are consumed

## Vernacular names

fleur de la passion, fruit de la passion, fruits de la pansi, grenadille (fr.), maracuja (port.), purple granadilla, purple passion flower (angl.), Purpurgrenadille (allem.). Note taxonomique : Des formes avec des fruits jaunes sont en culture et parfois distinguées comme P. edulis forma flavicarpa O.Deg. b : De Wilde (1975), Fernandes & Fernandes (1978), Gurnah (1991), Killip ibliographie (1938), Robyns (1995), Sinsin (2006)
