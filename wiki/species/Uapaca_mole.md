# *Uapaca mole* Pax Planche 33A-D

**Genus**: [[Uapaca]]
**Family**: [[Phyllanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 19 : 79 (1894)

## Description

Tree at least up to 35 m in height; trunk up to 75 cm in diameter, with buttress roots at least up to 2.5 m in height; branchlets puberulent, glabrescent. Stipules ovate-spatulate, very often slightly falciform, (3–)4–20 mm, up to 8 × 3 cm on young individuals, often acuminate at the apex, pubescent, with midvein generally well visible and with unequal sides. Leaf: petiole cylindric, (1.5–)3–6(–9) cm, puberulent, glabrescent; lamina obovate-elliptic, (5–)13–26(–48) × (3–)7–16(–28) cm, obtuse to rounded, sometimes subcordate or ± cuneate at the base, obtuse to truncate or retuse at the apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, densely puberulent on both surfaces at the young stage, sparsely so later; lateral veins (8–)9–12(–19) pairs, very prominent beneath, ± obscure and often impressed above, tertiary veins ± parallel, apparent beneath. Inflorescence: peduncle often with a few small bracts, glabrous, the male one 10–25 mm, the female one 8–12 mm; involucral bracts 9 or 10, concave to cucullate, 7–9 × 3–7 mm. Male flower ± 2 mm in length; calyx gamosepalous or with sepals almost free, ± 1.5 mm in length, pubescent outside; petals sometimes present, filiform, 1–1.5 mm in length; stamens 4–6, glabrous, anthers ± 0.7 mm in length, with connective sometimes pilose; pistillode ± cyathiform, pubescent. Female flower: calyx 1 mm in length, lobed or not, pubescent, glabrescent in fruit; pistil ± globose, 3–3.5 mm in length, with some scattered hairs; stigmas palmatifid, glabrous or with some hairs. Fruit ± globose, 22–31 × 17–28 mm, yellowish, glabrous or sparsely hairy, lenticellate, with yellow pulp.

## Distribution

from Liberia to Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Angola and the Democratic Republic of the Congo towards the south and Uganda and Tanzania towards the east; in Gabon, collected in all provinces

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, on firm and swampy soil and along river margins; in Gabon, at 0–600 m altitude. Uses: An infusion of the roots treats headaches, nasal obstruction and pulmonary affections. A decoction of root or stem bark is drunk to treat female infertility, dysentery and food poisonings. The fruits are edible. The wood is used for general woodworking, crates, kitchen utensils and as firewood.

## Vernacular names

rikio des marais (fr.). b : Breteler (2012a, b), De Wildeman (1936), Pax & Hoffmann (1922), ibliographie Schmelzer (2008d)
