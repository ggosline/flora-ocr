# *Vigna racemosa* (G.Don) Hutch. & Dalziel

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: J. Bot. 66, suppl . 1 : 117 (1928)

## Description

V. racemosa (G.Don) Hutch. & Dalziel f. glabrescens Baker f., Legum. trop. Afr. 2 : 409 (1929). Climbing herb, hirsute, tawny; stem with ferruginous pubescence. Stipules ovate-lanceolate, 5–9 × 1.5 mm, with sagittate base in two unequal lobes, hirsute. Leaf: petiole 1.5–9 cm; rachis 3–16 mm; stipelles 1–2 mm long; leaflets with petiolule 1.5–4 mm, limb ovate to lanceolate, 3–12 × 0.8–8 cm, rounded or cuneate at base, acuminate to acute, mucronate at apex, hirsute on both surfaces, ciliate on margins. Inflorescence: peduncle 5–18 cm, puberulent; rachis 1–12 cm, tomentellous; bracts ovate-lanceolate, 3–5 mm long. Flower: bracteoles 1.2–2.2 mm long; calyx pubescent to glabrescent, with tube 2 mm long, lobes ovate-lanceolate, 1.5–2 mm long, acute; corolla 8–15 mm long, blue, standard 10–16 mm wide, with emarginate apex, furnished on inner face with four parallel appendices, wings obovate, 5–12 mm long, keel curved, 5–12 mm long, with short and acute rostrum; ovary with 6 to 9 ovules; style prolonged beyond stigma by a short beak. Pod pendulous, 2–4.5 cm × 3–4 mm, puberulent-hirsute, terminated by a curved beak. Seed 3–4.5 × 2–3 mm; hilum and strophiole excentric, 1–2.5 mm long.

## Distribution

from Senegal to Sudan, southward to Zambia and Angola; in Gabon, known from Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

savannas and forest edges, river margins, among grasses and on trees; in Gabon at 485–635 m altitude, elsewhere from low altitude to 1650 m. Flowers in May

## Vernacular names

haricot sauvage (fr)

## Source note

Very close to V. gracilis, but generally with long, spreading, ferruginous pubescence, with much larger stipules and different standard calluses. Some sparsely pubescent specimens exist, distinguished by Baker (1929) as f. glabrescens Baker f., but the variation appears to be continuous. B : Pasquet (2001), Pellegrin (1949), Verdcourt (1971c), Wilczek (1954). Bibliography
