# *Cyperus imbricatus* Retz. Planche 34

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Obs. bot. 5 : 12 (1788)

## Description

Robust herb, perennial, with few stems arising from a short woody rhizome; stems 40–120 cm × 2–5 mm, trigonous, almost smooth, the basal part covered by fairly thick sheaths. Leaf: sheath light brown and purple at least below; basal blades few, the largest 20–40 cm × 4–8 mm, plane, scabrous at least on the margin and central nerve. Inflorescence in a large open anthela, 6–15 × 8–15 cm, composed of a few sessile or shortly pedunculate spikes and 3–8 fascicles of spikes on rays 1–12 cm long, each fascicle formed of 2–12 tightly packed spikes; primary involucral bracts foliaceous, the largest 20–50 cm × 5–8 mm, scabrous on the margin and central nerve, erect or spreading; spikes 1–3.5 cm × 3–8 mm, with 10–80 spikelets spreading and very tightly packed. Spikelet linear–lanceolate, 2–5 × 0.8–1.5 mm, flattened, with 8–16 flowers; glumes ovate, 0.9–1.2 mm long, pale brown or golden, with or without purplish striae, the central nerve green and prolonged by a short mucro. Flower: style with 3 branches. Achene elliptic, 0.5–0.6 × 0.4 mm, trigonous, reddish-brown; surface with irregular alveoles or almost smooth.

## Distribution

pantropical, found mainly in the tropical regions of Africa and Asia; in Gabon apparently rare, found only in Ogooué-Ivindo (2 collections)

## Ecology

in marshes, on river banks or in open water; in Gabon, probably between 300 and 600 m elevation, elsewhere at 0–1200 m. Taxonomic note: close to C. exaltatus Retz. and C. alopecuroides Rottb. (not yet found in Gabon), but differing from them by shorter glumes and achenes. C. exaltatus is a larger plant, with spikes having far fewer spikelets. C. alopecuroides usually has a style with 2 branches, a flattened achene and glumes with margins rolled around the achenes. B: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Bibliography Lye (1997)
