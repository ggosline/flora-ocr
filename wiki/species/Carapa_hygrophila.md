# *Carapa hygrophila* Harms ex Engl.

**Genus**: [[Carapa]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: C. palustris (G.C.C.Gilbert) Kenfack, Harvard Pap. Bot. 16(2) : 229 (2011), syn. nov

## Description

in Engler & Drude, Veg. Erde, 9, Pflanzenw. Afr. 3(1): 811 (1915). C. procera DC. var. palustre G.C.C.Gilbert, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 28: 383 (1958). C. palustris (G.C.C.Gilbert) Kenfack, Harvard Pap. Bot. 16(2): 229 (2011), syn. nov. Small sparsely branched tree, 5–20(–30) m tall; trunk reaching 60 cm in diameter; heartwood pink-violet, odorless. Leaf paripinnate; petiole 11–21 cm, narrow, 5–6 mm in diameter above the pulvinus, densely lenticellate, brownish, glabrous; rachis 40–90 cm, glabrous; petiolule robust, 0.5–1.3 cm; leaflets 8–12(–20), mostly opposite or subopposite, narrowly rectangular to elliptic or narrowly ovate, (4.5–)10–40 × (3.5–)5.5–7.5(–15) cm, base obtuse or cuneate, apex acuminate and terminated by a slender or acute point, discolorous, coriaceous to papyraceous, scrofulous, glabrous; primary vein prominent below, secondary veins 7–11 pairs. Inflorescence with pelliculate rachis, reaching 60 cm in length, loosely branched, branches reaching 19 cm in length, flowers in small spaced clusters. Flower: pedicel (1–)2–3 mm, glabrous; calyx patelliform, 0.5–0.8 mm tall, deeply 5-lobed, lobes rounded, 1–1.5 mm in diameter, glabrous; petals 5, obovate, 4–5 × 2.5–3 mm, glabrous, color unknown; staminal tube urceolate, 3 mm long, margin 10(–11)-lobed, glabrous, with 10(–11) ovate anthers of 0.6 × 0.3 mm; annular disk, 1 mm long, margin slightly lobed; ovary cylindric-ovoid, 1–1.5 mm long, glabrous, 5-locular with 4 ovules per locule; style 1 mm, glabrous. Fruit subglobose to ovoid, reaching 10 cm in diameter. Seed unknown.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Moyen-Ogooué

## Ecology

Rainforests, humid places; in Gabon, at low altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m. Taxonomic notes: 1- It should be noted that Harms (1917) remarks that C. hygrophila is very close to C. batesii. Lacking more detailed information on the variability of these two entities, they are here kept as distinct species. 2- The discriminating characters for distinguishing C. hygrophila and C. palustris are found to be variable and weak, for which reason C. palustris sensu Kenfack is considered here as a synonym. B: Harms (1917), Kenfack (2011), Staner & Gilbert (1958). Bibliography
