# *Maranthes kerstingii* (Engler) Prance

**Genus**: [[Maranthes]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Nota : Parinari kerstingii sensu AUBR., FFCI ed. 1, 1 : 148, tab. 51 (1936) est en réalité Maranthes glabra (Oliv.) Prance (cf. AUBR., FFSG : 203, 1950)

Trees 1 : 314 (1960); Bouquet, Féticheurs et médecines traditionnelles du Congo-Brazzaville : 204 (1969).

is actually Maranthes glabra (Oliv.) Prance (cf. AUBR., FFSG : 203, 1950).

Tree reaching 80 cm in diameter and 30 m in total height, with a trunk often straight and base sometimes swollen; bark fairly smooth, greyish-red; wood reddish, very hard; branches erect supporting dense foliage of dark green tint; branchlets brown. Young branchlets and petioles furnished with very short ascending hispid pubescence. Intrapetiolar stipules fused into an oblong piece (6 mm), bifid and bicarinate with ascending hispid hairs on the 2 nerves. Petiole 8–10 (–12 mm) long, frequently bearing at the apex, at the decurrent base of the lamina, 2 small circular glands with margins, these glands sometimes only rudimentary or sometimes absent. Lamina coriaceous, elliptic-oblong (3–8 × 6–11 cm), more rarely elliptic (7–9 × 9–16 cm, particularly for young stems and young leaves), with obtuse-rounded base and somewhat decurrent, with apex also obtuse-rounded furnished with an acumen, 5–8(–10) mm, with apex acute or obtuse; 6–8 pairs of arched lateral nerves, not very prominent; surfaces glabrous, dark green above, pale green below.

Inflorescences often very abundant on the crown, terminal, at the end of robust branchlets, in corymbiform panicles with curved axis, reaching 10 × 20 cm in width and 6–8 cm in height, ± leafy towards the base, sometimes grouping nearly a hundred flowers; axes fairly long (15 to 5 mm), glabrous. Bracts and bracteoles ovate acute to lanceolate, up to 3 mm long, early caducous. Pedicel 3–5(–8) mm long and receptacle pale green obconic curved (8–10 mm) glabrous, as are the parts exposed externally of the pale green sepals, these orbicular or ovate with broad base, concave, 4–6 mm long, tomentellous otherwise; petals white, obovate, 4–5 × 6–10 mm, silky externally, with flabellate venation; 30–40 white stamens with filaments 10–15 mm long, a few dentate staminodes; (2–)3 hirsute carpels, style 15–20 mm long.

Fruit formed of (1–2–)3 obovoid flattened drupes, 15 × 20 × 25 (–40) mm, acute at the base, obtuse at the apex, glabrous and smooth, with persistent calyx and androecium. — PL. 33, p. 113.

## Discussion

Maranthes kerstingii (Engl.) Prance is very close to M. glabra (Oliv.) Prance but differs from it by its elliptic-oblong leaf (and not elliptic), with obtuse-rounded base (and not acute-obtuse), by its flower of larger dimensions with 3 carpels (and not 1 alone), finally by its smaller drupes. Intermediate forms between these two species seem however to exist in eastern Central African Republic and in the Ubangi-Uele of Zaïre.

This species appears to be known from Liberia (cf. DALZIEL, Us. Pl. W. Trop. Afr. : 169, 1937) and is present in Ghana, Togo, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic and Zaïre (Ubangi-Uele, cf. above); it appears to exist in Congo, only in the region of wooded savannas and thickets of Fort Rousset (cf. BOUQUET, l.c.). It frequents exclusively the southern margins of the Sudano-Zambezian region and in Cameroon is thus localized within a narrow band between 6° and 7° degrees of latitude, and somewhat further south in the region of Bétaré Oya; it occurs essentially in the forest valleys of the south-facing slope of the Adamaoua plateau, very often in company with Aubrevillea kerstingii, Eriocaelum kerstingii, Beilschmiedia jacques-felixii, Erythrophleum suaveolens, Khaya grandifoliola, Vitex cienkowski, ...; these forest formations represent, it seems, a primitive formation of the Adamaoua plateau (cf. LETOUZEY, Et. phytogéogr. du Cameroun : 306, 1968) and thus one can encounter this species isolated in savanna, on flat ground away from watercourses, apparently as a result of agricultural clearing in the forest; similarly it constitutes an important element of the relict forest islet of Gaui (25 km NNW Banyo), unfortunately cleared for corn cultivation.

## Uses

In the Fort Rousset region in Congo, according to BOUQUET (l.c.), used to treat various bronchopneumonic ailments and febrile body aches; the decoction with added rock salt is prescribed as a drink, in baths and in steam baths; it would also have emetic and purgative properties
