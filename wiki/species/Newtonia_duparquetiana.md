# *Newtonia duparquetiana* (Baillon) Keay

**Genus**: [[Newtonia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 8 : 488 (1954) ; KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 489 (1958) ; AUBRÉVILLE, FFCI, ed. 2, 1 : 224, pl. 70 (1959) ; IRVINE, Woody Pl. Ghana : 347 (1961) ; RAPONDA-WALKER & SILLANS, Pl. ut. Gabon : 243, pl. 34 (1961) ; KEAY, ONOCHIE & STANFIELD, Niger. Trees 2 : 93 (1964)

– Piptadenia unijuga PiERRE ex A. CHEV., Bois Gabon : 189 (1917). Syn. nov.

Tree 20-30 m tall. Buttresses well developed at the base of the trunk. Bark smooth, greyish to whitish; thin in section, pale brown, exuding some gum; Branches dark brown with lighter lenticels. Petiole glabrous, 3-5.5 cm long, cylindrical. Rachis glabrous, 0-7.5 cm long, flat to channelled above and bearing prominent glands between each pair of pinnae; apex terminated by a caducous mucro. Pinnae 1-2 pairs. Pinna axis glabrous, 0.6-4.5 cm long (for leaves with 2 pairs of pinnae, rachis of lower pinnae shorter than that of upper pinnae), channelled above with glands between the leaflets. Leaflets, one pair per pinna, sessile. Blade glabrous, coppery brown to blackish grey on both faces, ± shiny above and dull below when dry; asymmetric: proximal half elliptic, to broadly elliptic to lanceolate, distal half narrowly elliptic, elliptic to ± oblanceolate, 5.5-18.5 (-20) × 2.5-8 (-9.5) cm, base asymmetric: proximal margin attenuate to cuneate and distal margin very clearly cuneate; apex acuminate. Midvein, prominent on both faces. Secondary veins, 7-12 pairs, ascending, arched, prominent on both faces. Veinlets in fairly dense network, prominent on both faces.

Inflorescence in a panicle of spikes, terminal or subterminal. Rachis shortly densely pubescent to subtomentellous. Bract caducous. Floral bud fusiform. Sepals fused into a cupule, tomentellous, 1 mm long. Petals very shortly pubescent externally and glabrous internally, narrowly elliptic to narrowly lanceolate, 3-4.5 mm long, apex acute. Stamens 10, fused together and to the petals, 4-6 mm long; anthers oblong-ellipsoid, 0.5 mm long, connective prolonged by a short stipitate gland. Pistil with stipe 0.75 mm long; ovary with densely hirsute apex, 1-2 mm long, with 6 ovules; style hirsute at the base, ± 2 mm long, stigma cupular.

Pod yellowish greyish to brown, greyish blackish, black when dry, glabrous, oblong (6-) 9-20 (-24) × 1.4-3 cm; base with stipe 0.7-1.2 cm long; sutural margins with prominent ridges; apex obtuse to subacute; transverse venation oblique, prominent. Winged seed 5-10 × 1.6-2.5 cm.

Hypogeal germination; cotyledons remaining in the seed. Epicotyl 3 cm long, keeled. First leaves opposite pinnately compound, with a single pair of opposite leaflets. Petiole pubescent at the apex, 3-4 cm long, channelled and gland between the leaflets above. Leaflet elliptic, 2 × 1.1 cm, base asymmetric obtuse, apex acute. (After G. de la Mensbruge, GPEACI, 1966). – Pl. 11, p. 47.

## Discussion

briefly described in French Piptadenia unijuga Pierre ex A. Chev. with Klaine 1189 bis, collected at Libreville, as reference material. Despite the absence of a Latin diagnosis and having been published before 1935, this taxon is valid. The type specimen has not been found; however the description is sufficiently precise to establish that P. unijuga and N. duparquetiana are synonyms. Due to the priority of its basionym, N. duparquetiana remains the only valid name.

This tree has rather variable foliage. Young leaves appear to be those with 2 pairs of pinnae. The shape of adult leaflets is subject to fairly significant variation.

The distribution of this plant extends from Sierra Leone to the Congolese Mayombe. It is encountered mainly on sandy soils. In Gabon, it is present only in the western part of the country. Its flowering extends from February to July and fruiting from May to December.
