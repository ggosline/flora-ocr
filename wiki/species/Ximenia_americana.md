## Description

**Habit**: ± spiny shrub 6–8 m tall, much branched. Bark longitudinally
fissured, striate and transversely cracked. Cork often abundant.

**Leaves**: petiole 0–5 mm long, pubescent above, striate beneath. Blade
glabrous on both faces, grey-green to blackish-brown, elliptic-lanceolate to
oblanceolate, 2.5–7(–9) × 1–4 cm; base cuneate, apex obtuse to rounded, often
mucronate, sometimes retuse. Midrib concave above, prominent and striate
beneath. Secondary nerves ascending, arcuate, anastomosing in arches a few mm
from the margin, ± prominent above, prominent beneath. Nervules forming an
almost indistinct reticulum on both faces.

**Inflorescence**: axillary umbelliform or corymbiform racemes, solitary or
fascicled in 2s–3s. Flowers 2–8 per inflorescence. Common rachis 6–18 mm long.
Bracteole lanceolate, ciliate on the margin, quadrangular in section. Flower
bud ellipsoid.

**Flowers**: tetramerous, greenish-white to cream-white, fragrant, 7–12 ×
5–10 mm. Calyx small, the sepals lanceolate, 2 mm long, ciliate on the margin.
Petals glabrous outside, pubescent within with white-russet hairs,
oblong-linear, 6–11 × 1–2 mm, valvate, the apex reflexed and bearing a hooked
apicule. Stamens 8, hypogynous, free; filament 2–4 mm long, ± spiralled near
the anthers; anthers basifixed, 3–5 mm long, with longitudinal dehiscence
slits. Ovary ovoid, 4-locular, the locules uniovulate, 1–3 mm long. Style
quadrangular in section.

**Fruit**: drupe ellipsoid to globose, 1–3 × 1–2 cm, yellow at maturity.
Fruiting calyx persistent at the base of the fruit. Mesocarp fleshy. Endocarp
hard.

**Seed**: albumen oily. Embryo apical, small.

## Types

HOLOTYPE: *Hort. Cliff. 483*, tropical America (BM).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Senegal to Uganda and Angola; on the seashore from Senegal to
Angola.
**Gabon**: coastal — Estuaire (Libreville, Baie de la Mondah, Pointe Denis),
Ogooué-Maritime (Cap Lopez).

## Habitat and ecology

Chiefly a coastal shrub, but also in savanna and low vegetation. Ecologically
very tolerant: laterite, *bowal*, riverbanks. Ascends to 1400 m (Fouta Djalon).

## Properties and uses

The yellow to reddish-yellow or light brown wood is very hard and dense, and
can be used as a substitute for sandalwood. The leaves are applied to snakebite
and to the bites of other venomous animals. Accounts of the fruit pulp
disagree: it is reported both as edible and as purgative, and the seed likewise.
The seed contains 60–70% of a slightly thick, viscous yellow oil containing
ximenic acid, which is much sought after. The oil-cake is rich in protein (43%)
and carbohydrate (20%).

## Notes

Villiers records that Lanessan, in *Plantes utiles des colonies*, mentioned a
second Gabonese *Ximenia* — ***Ximenia gabonensis* Lanessan**, with vernacular
names *elozy* and *zégué*, said to be abundant on the banks of the Gabon
estuary. It was described as a shrub with alternate, exstipulate leaves,
flowers in axillary racemes, a calyx of 4–5 sepals, petals absent, 8–10 free
stamens, a free 3–5-locular ovary with incomplete uniovulate locules, and a
yellow one-seeded drupe with fleshy albumen; the seed was said to be purgative
and to yield 6–7% oil. No type was indicated, and in the absence of any
specimen Villiers declines to confirm the validity of the binomial.
