# *Radlkofera calodendron* Gilg

**Genus**: [[Radlkofera]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]

## Description

1.c. (1897); in Engl. & Prantl., Nat. Pflanzenfam., Nachtr. 1 : 228 (1897); Radlk., in Engl., Pflanzenreich, Sapindacex : 778 tab. 16 A-F (1932); Hauman, Fl. Congo 9 : 360 (1960); Pellegrin, Bull. Soc. Bot. France 102 : 69 (1955); N. & F. Hallé, Biol. Gabon. 1 : (3) 247-255 (1965); N. Hallé, Biol. Gabon. 1 (4) : 343 (1965).

Small monocaulous tree, sometimes forked, 10-15 m tall, with vertical bole reaching 20 cm in diameter, orange wood; stem apex robust, clothed in fine brownish pubescence. Approximately 25 leaves grouped in a crown 3.5 m in diameter; leaf rachis brownish, 1-1.5 m long, 0.7-1 cm wide, robust, with 2 prominent lateral ridges; first leaflets at 21-42 cm above the base; the latter is thickened with a subaxillary groove; petioles 7-18 mm, puberulent to glabrescent; 15-27(-36) leaflets opposite or nearly alternate often subopposite, reaching (20-) 30-40(-50) × (6-)9-12(-14) cm, the longest not being the widest; blade entire, elliptic or oblong, with broadly rounded base sometimes briefly cuneate, acuminate apex, subcoriaceous, glabrous above, puberulent to glabrescent below; approximately 15 pairs of secondary nerves for a leaflet 32 cm long; fine nerves well-apparent on both faces of blade when dry; median with a sulcus above.

Inflorescences in narrow racemes arranged in a ladder-like pattern below the cyme from the axils of lower leaves up to mid-height of the bole, ascending, at least until flowering (120 young inflorescences were counted, with a total weight of nearly 300 g, on a single tree 10 m tall, Hallé 3172). The inflorescence measures 5-20(-35) cm and the numerous bracts are arranged

along its entire length; rachis brownish or reddish-brown, puberulent; floriferous bracts linear 7-13 × 1 mm, greenish, deflexed. Buds crowded, globose and brownish. Male flower with pedicel 2-3 mm inserted almost laterally; calyx 6-9.5 × 5-7 mm, opening barely, with 5 quincuncial obtuse lobes 2-3 mm; 4 pale yellow petals 5-6 mm, hidden, with pubescent claw, rounded blade 3-3.5 mm wide, with 2 superposed ligules on the inner face; crescent-shaped fleshy disc, orange or red, 3.2 × 2 mm, weakly lobed, between the claws of the petals which it separates from the androecium; 6-8 stamens 7-9 mm, unequal, introrse, with filament somewhat swollen towards the base, pale yellow pubescent, with brown ascending hairs in the upper part, white and deflexed in the lower part; anther salmon-coloured somewhat pilose 2 × 0.9 mm; pollen white ochraceous; flower filled with sweet nectar; aborted ovary clothed with brown hairs containing 6 uniovulate locules alternating with the stamens. Female flowers (according to HAUMAN) few, scattered without order among the male flowers, with 5 staminodes 3 mm long, bulbous and pilose towards the base, with ovary 5 mm in diameter, with robust style 3 mm long, hairy at the base.

Fruit berry-like with appearance of a pear or very large subglobose fig, not ridged reaching (according to HAUMAN) 13 cm in length and 14 cm in diameter, tomentose then glabrescent and orange, with mesocarp approximately 1 cm thick, with orange integument, smooth and shiny. Hypogeal germination with cotyledons remaining in the seed; seedling with very robust root, with epicotyl 18-20 cm and glabrous; 5-6 first simple leaves, the 6th or 7th, 4-jugate (according to HAUMAN). PL. 16, p. 81.

## Discussion

The flower is turned towards the ground and appears at once very little attractive and very little accessible to insects despite the abundance of nectar observed. The 2 or 4 longest stamens alone have slightly apparent anthers at anthesis. The species is distributed from Nigeria, Benin province, to the Congo, Yangambi; it is known from Central Africa (Tisserant 1471, 1512), the Congo (Bouquet 670, 795) and Cabinda. In primary rainforest, a small stand was observed at Bélinga around 700 m altitude.
