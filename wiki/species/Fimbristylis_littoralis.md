# *Fimbristylis littoralis* Gaudich. Planche 95, Figure 11b

**Genus**: [[Fimbristylis]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Voy. Uranie 10 : 413 (1829)

## Description

& auct. pl., non Scirpus miliaceus L., nom rej. Annual herb, tufted, with a superficial root system and numerous basal leaves; stems 10–60 cm × 1–2 mm, each stem bearing at its base a prophyll shortly bifid, followed by two sheaths with short blade; the prophyll may successively sheath several stems. Leaf: blade distichous, 10–40 cm × 1.5–2 mm, plane or canaliculate, gradually narrowed at the apex, their axils subtending new leafy shoots (as in F. quinquangularis). Inflorescence in a composed, loose, open anthela; involucral bracts much shorter than the inflorescence. Spikelet globose or shortly cylindric, 2–3(–5 at fruiting) × 1.5–2 mm, pale brown to

## Distribution

very widespread in tropical Asia, probably introduced to Africa with rice cultivation; in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime (12 collections)

## Ecology

in humid or seasonally humid habitats, often in disturbed areas such as cultivated land and particularly rice fields and roadside ditches; in Gabon, at 0–300 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2000 m

## Vernacular names

grass-like fimbristylis, lesser fimbristylis (angl.) ; barba de fraile (esp.)

## Discussion

Taxonomic note: very close to F. quinquangularis. It differs from it by its more rounded spikelets, its glumes rounded at the apex (not mucronate) and by its leaves with a less prominent midvein. B: Goetghebeur & Coudijzer (1984a), Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972).
