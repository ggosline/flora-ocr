# *Dalbergia louisii* Cronquist Planche 43

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État 22 : 217 (1952)

## Description

D. ecastaphyllum (L.) Taub. var. cuprea Pellegr., Lég. Gabon: 193 (1949), nom. nud. Vine or sarmentose shrub reaching 7(–15) m in height, inodorous, without spines, but sometimes furnished with hook-like prehensile branchlets; bark dark brownish, with lenticels somewhat lighter; young branchlets with short, patent or appressed, dense or sparse pubescence. Stipules quickly caducous, lanceolate to narrowly elliptic, ± falciform, 2.5–7 × 1–2 mm, shortly pubescent. Leaf unifoliolate; petiole 0.4–1.7 cm, pubescent like the branchlets; leaflet with petiolule of 0.2–0.5 cm, limb elliptic, 4.3–16.5(–22) × 2.3–9(–13) cm, rounded to slightly cordate at base, acuminate or rarely obtuse at apex, coriaceous, browning when dry, glabrous or with very scattered appressed hairs on upper surface, lower surface with variably erect or appressed hairs, sometimes golden and covering the entire limb, or more rarely glabrescent (in certain Congolese specimens); lateral veins 10 to 14(–17) pairs, very prominent, strongly ascending except the lower ones more spreading, veinlets in dense and generally very prominent reticulum on lower surface, rarely ± hidden by hairs if these are very dense. Inflorescence in short panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary and sometimes also terminal, 2–5.5 cm in length, with dense and ± crisped golden-brown pubescence; bracts generally persistent, ovate to lanceolate, 0.7–1.2 × 0.4–0.7 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1.5–4 mm; bracteoles generally persistent, ovate to narrowly elliptic, 0.7–1.3 × 0.3–0.5 mm, pubescent; calyx pale yellow to bronze-green, with fairly dense appressed hairs, tube 1.7–2.7 × 1.7–2.3 mm, teeth valvate ± equal and shortly triangular, < 0.5 mm, acute or obtuse at apex, sometimes almost absent; corolla white, often persistent at base of pod, petals with claw exceeding calyx, standard obliquely bent, with claw 2–2.5 mm and suborbicular limb 4–4.5 × 4–5 mm, with rounded base and bilobed apex, wings exceeding keel somewhat, with claw 2.3–2.7 mm and ± rectangular limb 4.2–4.8 × 1.8–2 mm, smooth on outside, with truncate base or barely auriculate, keel distinctly curved, with claw 2.3–2.5 mm and limb 3.3–4 × 1.5–1.8 mm, with auriculate base; stamens in 2 bundles of 5, filaments 5–6.5 mm, united for ¾ of their length, straight at base and curved in their free part; ovary 1.7–2 × 0.6–0.7 mm, pubescent at least on margins, with 2 ovules and straight stipe 3–3.3 mm; style linear, 1.3–1.5 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 2–4 mm and stipe 3–4 mm, woody, very thick and shortly ellipsoid, 1.2–2.7 × 0.8–1.8 cm × 0.4–0.8 cm, rounded at base, acute or obtuse at apex, with almost smooth to strongly tuberculate surface, with dense and short ± crisped hairs, 1-seeded; pericarp 1.5–3 mm thick, with outer layer formed of fairly soft cork tissue, with wooden fibers absent or very sparse. Seed reniform and ± flattened, 13.5–17 × 7–8 × 2.5–3.5 mm.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; widely distributed in Gabon, recorded in most provinces: Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué (locally common in the lake region), Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime, Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Inundated forests of lake or river margins; in Gabon at 0–430 m altitude, elsewhere up to 470 m. In Gabon, flowering from January to May, fruiting from February to May, and in October and November

## Source note

D. louisii is very close to D. ecastaphyllum, from which it differs especially in the fruits. In the flowering state, separation of the two species is sometimes difficult; the risk of confusion exists only on the coast, however, since only D. louisii is present in the interior. D. louisii is a very variable species. Gabonese specimens have rather small and almost smooth pods, and leaves with lower surface more or less pubescent; in certain specimens (described as D. ecastaphyllum var. cuprea Pellegr.), the pubescence forms a very dense golden layer on the lower surface. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, there are forms with very tuberculate pods and almost glabrous leaves, represented notably by the type. The dimensions in parentheses in the above description concern non-Gabonese material. The fruits of D. louisii are sometimes deformed by galls, they are then shorter and thicker than normal fruits and usually show a hole on the side. b: Cronquist (1954). ibliography
