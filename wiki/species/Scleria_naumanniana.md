# *Scleria naumanniana* Boeckeler Planche 139, Figure 28b

**Genus**: [[Scleria]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 5 : 94 (1883)

## Description

? Scleria buettneri Boeckeler, Beitr. Cyper. 1 : 36 (1888). Robust, perennial herb, in tufts; rhizome short, with 4–10 stems; stem 60–150 cm × 4–5 mm, triangular, pilose on the faces, scabrous on the angles especially above. Leaves numerous, the lower ones reduced to pubescent sheaths; ligule as wide as long; blade 20–40 cm × 6–9 mm, plane or incurved, with 3 principal veins and hyaline hairs on both faces. Inflorescence formed of a terminal panicle and 2–4 lateral ones of 3–10 × 2–4 cm, arranged regularly along the stem; peduncles up to 3 cm, erect, solitary in each sheath of the upper leaves; rachis pilose; secondary bracts filiform. Male spikelet generally terminal, 3–4 mm long, formed of 3 sterile basal glumes and numerous glumes bearing either 3 staminodes or 3 stamens; glumes light brown to dark brown, pilose at least on the margin. Female spikelet 4–5 mm long, composed of 3 brown or dark brown glumes as long as or shorter than the corresponding achene. Achene broadly ovoid or ellipsoid, 2–3 mm (disc excluded) × 2–2.5 mm, smooth, glabrous, ivory to grey at maturity; hypogynium brownish with 3 distinct lobes with reflexed margins.

## Distribution

widespread in central and western tropical Africa, from Senegal to Angola; replaced by S. iostephana in East Africa; fairly widespread in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Nyanga, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (12 collections).

## Ecology

wet or flooded places in forests or on their margins, also on the margins of mangroves and marshes; in Gabon, at 0–400 m altitude. Taxonomic note: very close to S. iostephana, but the fruit is not bluish, and the spikelets, glumes and achenes are smaller (achene 2–2.5 mm wide compared to 2.5–3 mm for S. iostephana). Bibliography: Hooper & Napper (1972), Piérart (1953).
