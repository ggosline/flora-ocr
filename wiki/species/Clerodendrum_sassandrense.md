# *Clerodendrum sassandrense* Jongkind Planche 8 ; Figure 2(E-G)

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Syst. Geogr. Pl . 72 : 240 (2002)

## Description

Terrestrial vine; main stem of ± 3 m, arising from a taproot, extending over the soil or beneath the litter, with several subsidiary axes emerging at the base, at ± 5 cm depth, ascending or erect, generally distant 0.2–1 m apart, 0.15–0.3(–0.45) m tall, solid or with a narrow and hollow central part, horizontal, without pseudospines, young axes densely and shortly pubescent, rooting at the nodes, sometimes at the internodes, with (1 to)4 to 10(to 20) lateral floriferous nodes. Leaves opposite; petiole 0.7–3.5(–4.7) cm, finely puberulent; blade oval to oval-elliptic or elliptic, 3.5–11(–14.5) × 2.2–6(–7.5) cm, broadly cuneate to rounded at the base, sometimes asymmetric, acuminate over up to 1.1 cm at the apex, entire, dark and punctate below, generally 3-nerved. Inflorescence axillary, appearing superficially terminal, ± 5 × 5–12 cm; peduncle ascending, 2.3–3.6 cm; cymes bearing 2 to 4(or 5) flowers, with axes densely and shortly pubescent; bracts discrete, narrowly oval, ± 4 × 2 mm. Flower: pedicel 5–14 mm; calyx campanulate, accrescent, red-green or reddish pink at maturity, glabrous, with obconical tube, 2–3 mm long, up to 5 mm in fruit, with oval lobes, 8–10 × 5–6 mm, ± 12 × 7 mm in fruit, apiculate, shortly ciliate; corolla white, with tube 10–17 × 0.5–1 mm, with lobes ± elliptic to slightly obovate, 5–6 × 3–4 mm; stamens exserted over ± 1.2 cm, at first reflexed, then spreading. Fruit ± 10 × 10 mm, greenish violet to black, sometimes with a metallic green sheen when drying.

Seed. Scale bars: dotted bar: 0.5 mm; single bar: 1 mm; single partitioned bar: 3 mm; double bar: 1 cm; double partitioned bar: 5 cm; thick bar: 10 cm. (1, 3, 10: M'Bourga 702; 2, 4-9: photographs of M'Bourga 702; 11-14: Mpamzou 1435; 15, 16: Koubamba 157). Drawing by Andrew P. Brown, Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (©), reproduced with permission.

## Distribution

disjunct, Côte d'Ivoire, Gabon and Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, discovered in 2017 in the Ogooué-Lolo. Conservation status: Preliminarily assigned to Endangered (EN); with five localities for the entirety of its geographical range

## Ecology

evergreen tropical forests, secondarized forests dominated by Aucoumea klaineana, with a canopy at ± 35 m; in Gabon at 275 m altitude, elsewhere up to 620 m

## Source note

This species appears to be unique within the genus, as it is a partly subterranean vine, the main stem generally not being visible, as it grows beneath the upper layer of litter in the evergreen tropical forest. It often has the deceptive appearance of being a group of shrubs or gregarious erect herbs, but it is in reality a single plant. A specimen from the Republic of the Congo, Mpandzou 465, mentions 20 "plants" seen in a plot of 5 × 2 m, which are in fact flowering shoots arising from a single main axis. The only known collection from Gabon reports that it is "fairly abundant" in a small area. Due to the particular habit of this species, it is hardly surprising that it has not developed pseudospines, otherwise so common in the climbing species of Clerodendrum of tropical Africa. The species was originally published in 2002 based on two collections from Côte d'Ivoire. A series of collections was subsequently made in 2010/2011, far from the others, to the southeast, in the Chaillu Massif, in the Republic of the Congo, near the border with Gabon. Jongkind (2014) stated that it was likely to be found in Liberia, but no specimen was known to the author. The author suspected that this species could well also be present in Gabon and a specimen was discovered there in 2017. The protologue illustrates this species from herbarium material pressed where it can be difficult to decipher the symmetry/asymmetry of the corolla lobes. A series of photographs of this species taken in the Congo during fieldwork by staff of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew in 2010 and 2011, clearly shows that the corolla is zygomorphic, with four lower lobes and one reflexed upper lobe. This appears to be confirmed by the photographs of the Gabonese specimen (Texier 944). B: Jongkind (2002, 2015). Bibliography
