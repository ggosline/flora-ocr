# *Campylospermum umbricola* (Tiegh.) Farron

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 35 : 403 (1965)

## Description

Ouratea umbricola (Tiegh.) Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 259 (1903). Tree reaching 15 m in height; trunk up to 20 cm in diameter; branches with whitish-brown bark. Stipules caducous, leaving a scar at the base of the petiole, ± 10 mm long. Leaf: petiole 10–13(–15) mm, stout, canaliculate; lamina obovate to narrowly obovate, (24–)28–45(–53) × (8–)11–14(–17) cm, ratio 2.5–3.9, cuneate at base, acute to shortly acuminate at apex, coriaceous, smooth or slightly bullate, margin regularly serrulate, entire toward base; primary vein plane on upper surface, prominent on lower surface, 2–3 mm wide toward petiole, lateral veins in (16–)23 to 26 pairs, slightly prominent, sometimes raised above, slightly prominent below, tertiary veins scalariform, perpendicular, oblique to primary vein, slightly distinct above, indistinct below. Inflorescence terminal, branched, lax, main axis (19–)22–40(–48) cm, cylindric; bud scales absent; racemes (3–)5 to 9, (9–)12–26(–30) cm; cymules distant 0.5–3.5 cm apart, 1- to 12-flowered; bracts caducous, rounded, 1 mm long. Flowers: pedicel (8–)10–15 mm, articulate 1–5 mm from base, strongly flattened in fruit; sepals ovate, 9–10 × 6–7 mm in flower, (20–)25–30(–35) × (10–)13–20(–25) mm in fruit, coriaceous, covering the drupels; petals broadly obovate, 13–19 × 7–10 mm, unguiculate at base, rounded at apex; stamens: anthers 5–6 mm long; style 5–6 mm. Fruit: receptacle reaching 4–5 mm thick; drupels 2 or 3 well developed per receptacle, ellipsoid, 7–11 mm long; cotyledons more or less equal.

## Distribution

Southwestern Cameroon and northwestern Gabon (Cristal Mountains)

## Ecology

Primary and secondary coastal forest, along streams; in Gabon, at 430–500 m altitude, elsewhere at 50–1000 m. Taxonomic note: C. umbricola is a very distinctive species, easily distinguishable by its large coriaceous leaves and sepals that enlarge very greatly in fruit. b: Bissiengou (2014), Farron (1963, 1968, 1985), Walters et al.
