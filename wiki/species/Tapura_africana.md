## Description

HUTCHINSON & DALZIEL, FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (2): 325 (1928); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2): 439 (1958); KEAY, Onochie & Stanfield, Nigerian Trees 1: 327 (1960); HALLÉ & HEINE, Adansonia, sér. 2, 7 (1): 44 (1967); BRETELER, Act. Bot. Neerl. 19 (1): 14 (1970); MLW 82 (8): 1 (1982); AUWP 86(3): 46-51 (1986). TYPE: Equatorial Guinea, Bioko, Mann 1161 (holo-, K; iso-, LE, P).

Tree capable of exceeding 20 m in height and 100 cm in trunk diameter; dense branching and often branches near the base. Bole often irregularly or not fluted. Bark fairly smooth; cut surface white turning rapidly yellow. Wood whitish. Older twigs glabrous or glabrescent. Young twigs with short appressed pubescence, then glabrescent.

Stipules generally early caducous, appressed against the twig, ovate-triangular, (1) 2-5 (7) mm long; exterior with dense appressed silky hairs, interior subglabrous.

Leaf. Petiole sub- to hemicylindric, most often grooved above, (2) 5-10 (17) mm long; but also without groove and (6) 10-15 (30) mm long when fused to the peduncle; short appressed pubescence. Limb papyraceous to coriaceous, obovate-elliptic, 2-3 (3.5) times longer than wide, (6) 9-20 (22) × 3-8 (10) cm; base cuneate rarely rounded; acumen slightly to very marked, 0.5-2 (2.5) cm long, rounded at the apex; subappressed sparse pubescence on both faces of nerve I and the 3-5 (8) pairs of principal nerves II, as well as on the margin revolute at the base, subsequently caducous, very early above, likewise below, but with an arachnoid indumentum; major venation not prominent above and prominent below; glands often few, present on both faces or only below, fairly equally distributed, but more concentrated near the base on the lower face.

Inflorescences subumbellate, ≤ 50-flowered; ramifications indistinct, otherwise a single ± distinct one at the base; short subappressed pubescence. Peduncle generally entirely fused to the petiole or almost and approximately as long as it, rarely free. Bracts and bracteoles ovate-triangular, 1-2 mm long. Pedicel ≤ 3 mm long, of which ≤ 0.5 mm for the upper part.

Flowers 4.5-7 mm long. Sepals free, unequal ovate-elliptic to obovate, fairly thick, concave, 1.5-3 × 1-2 mm, the 2 outer ones distinctly smaller, the 3 inner ones slightly asymmetric, outer face glabrous to puberulent, margins ciliate, interior glabrous. Petals ≤ 5 mm long, fused to the stamens in a tube 2-4 mm high; apical parts free, the most deeply cleft near the fertile stamens; upper external part of the tube glabrous or with sparse puberulence extending slightly onto the free parts; internal pubescence denser in the upper part; free apical part of the 2 largest petals, cleft 0.5-0.7 mm, bicucullate, shorter and entire in the 3 smaller petals, pubescent on the inner side. Stamens, 3 fertile and 2 without anthers, the fertile ones equal to the large petals or slightly longer; the two lateral stamens bearing on one side only (that of the small petals) of their filament a small subacute lobe; anthers ≈ 0.5 mm long, connective very distinct often muricate; staminodia generally distinct, acute, pubescent on the inner side. Basal staminode(s), thick, ± fused, semi-annular slightly cupuliform, lacking in front of the large petals 0.6-0.8 mm and glabrous. Pistil 3-merous, 4-5.5 mm long; ovary with short dense velvety pubescence, depressed, subspheric; style glabrous in its lower part and pubescent in its upper part terminated by 3 lobes ≤ 0.5 mm long; style often inclined and attached by its hairs to the tube opposite the fertile stamens.

Fruits 1 (3 ?)-seeded, narrow and ellipsoid, rounded at both ends, 3-3.5 × 1-1.5 cm, very shortly velvety, resembling ultra-fine sandpaper to the touch; exocarp thin; mesocarp abundant, succulent, 2.2-3 mm thick; endocarp thin parchment-like, ± smooth, finely striated and shiny on the inner side. Seeds narrow and ellipsoid, glossy brown, acute at both ends, 20 × 5 mm; testa fairly strong.

Seedling from epigeal germination (Pl. 60, p. 199.); hypocotyl ≈ 10 cm long with appressed pubescence; cotyledons showing distinct petioles 5-8 mm long, still included in the endocarp and raised above the first pair of opposite leaves. These are elliptic, cuneate at the base and acuminate at the apex, with dense pubescence on the petiole, less dense on nerve I and the main nerves II and sparse on the rest of the limb, with arachnoid hairs on the lower face; the portion of epicotyl stem situated between the insertion point of the cotyledons and that of the first pair of opposite leaves is indiscernable.

The description given above of the seedling is based on 4 specimens from the Thomas 2470 collection from Cameroon. The label of this material provides no information about the parent plant, so it is difficult to assert a priori that these seedlings indeed come from T. africana. However, careful examination of these seedlings, in particular the form and texture of the endocarp as well as the leaf indumentum (sparse arachnoid hairs) leads to the conclusion that they belong almost certainly to T. africana.

It is to be noted that the seedlings of this species of Tapura possess a distinct hypocotyl whereas there is no distinct cauline part between the insertion of the cotyledons and that of the first leaves. In Dichapetalum, at least for what is known in about twenty species, there is no hypocotyl, hence germination is hypogeal and, when the first leaves are opposite, they are distinctly distant from the node bearing the cotyledons (BRETELER 1973: 29). On these Tapura seedlings, the terminal bud appears to be protected by the two subparallel cotyledonary petioles constituted in such a way that they form a "chamber" that exactly molds this first pair of opposite leaves. – Pl. 59, p. 197.
