# *Dicranolepis pulcherrima* Gilg

**Genus**: [[Dicranolepis]]
**Family**: [[Thymeleaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. 23 : 203 (1897)

## Description

— Pearson, F.T.A. 6 (1): 239 (1910). — Hutchinson and Dalziel, F.W.T.A. ed. 1 (1): 148 (1927). — Staner, Bull. Jard. Bot. État. Brux. 13: 333 (1935). — Keay, F.W.T.A., 2nd ed. 1 (1): 173 (1954).

Shrub or small tree 3-4 m tall, with erect trunk and branches spreading horizontally; the latter, fairly flexible and straight, few in number, are covered with grey or white pubescence when young, and become glabrous later.

Leaves quite variable, but rarely exceeding 8 cm in length and 3 to 5 cm wide; petiole less than 5 mm. Blade asymmetrical, oblong-rectangular in typical forms, with a straight or slightly curved acumen 8-12 mm long, dark green and glabrous on the upper surface, greyish and more or less covered with scattered pubescence on the lower surface. 12 to 25 secondary nerves nearly parallel to one another up to the blade margin, oblique at 45° to the primary nerve, and all very prominent on the lower surface. The leaves are sometimes nearly as wide as they are long, with a fairly short and triangular acumen. In young plants, the leaves appear to be small in size and more distinctly oblique-asymmetrical, arranged helicoidally on the erect part of the trunk, and distichous on the first horizontal branch.

Pentamerous flowers, most often solitary in the leaf axils and shorter than them, sometimes grouped in twos to fours; flowers or floral glomerules with very short pedicels, with at the base very small triangular or elongate-acicular bracts, approximately 1 mm long, densely velvet-silky like the pedicels, rapidly caducous. Large flowers frequently reaching 40 mm or even more at the time of opening. Perianth tube cylindrical, 20 to 35 mm long and 1 to 3 mm in diameter, very densely covered with velvety tomentum, grey, greyish-yellow or silvery. Sepals 10-15 mm long, 2 to 5 mm wide, oblong, lanceolate with obtuse distal portion, somewhat thickened or curved, equally tomentose-velvety on the outer surface, with much shorter and white pubescence on the inner surface. 5 petals very deeply bifid and appearing as 10 petaloid pieces spreading horizontally (in fresh state); lobes of petals very finely and deeply laciniate (often beyond the middle), with divisions often filiform exceeding the sepals by 5 to 10 mm; petals 20-30 mm long, mother-of-pearl white. 10 stamens 5 to 15 mm long, with anthers 1 to 2 mm, most often very exserted at anthesis. Ovary shortly pedicellate, 1 to 3 mm long in the unopened flower, surrounded by a hypogynous disc approximately 1 mm high; style short in the young flower, becoming longer then very exserted and reaching 30 to 50 mm, filiform; stigma more or less discoid with bombate upper portion, approximately 2 mm in diameter.

Drupaceous fruits red-orange, 20-30 mm long by 15-20 mm wide in the fresh state, borne by a short woody pedicel from which they detach at maturity, spherical but surmounted at their apical portion by a sort of sleeve formed by the remains of the perianth; pericarp fairly thin (0.5 mm), not very coriaceous, furnished with numerous silvery fibres visible on the fractures. Single seed approximately 10 mm.

Type described from Cameroon. (Staudt, 321).

Plant of fresh or waterlogged terrain, in poorly lit understory of forests or forest galleries.

The leaf types of individuals from populations of this species encountered in Gabon are fairly homogeneous; the leaf is generally distinctly longer than wide (2/1 to 3/1), which is the most frequent case in the equatorial region of the Congo Basin, whereas fairly marked variations can be noted with respect to plants from Cameroon.
