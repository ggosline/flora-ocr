# *Phaius occidentalis* Schltr.

**Genus**: [[Phaius]]
**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Protologue**: In Warb., Kunene-Sambesi Exped. : 211 (1903)

## Description

Leaves < 32 × 2.5–5 cm, elliptic-lanceolate, whitish or obovate, acute. Inflorescence, apical half of the axis finely pubescent. Flowers whitish, of medium size and distinctly smaller than those of P. mannii. Dorsal sepal with sparse pubescence on the outer face, oblong to oblong-lanceolate, shortly apiculate to obtuse; lateral sepals oblong, acute to subacute. Petals oblong-oblanceolate, obtuse, glabrous. Labellum very pubescent at the base, with 3 pilose prominent keels from the base almost to the apex, apical half vaguely trilobed; median lobe broadly obovate to elliptic, obtuse; lateral lobes rounded. Spur poorly marked, obtuse. – Pl. 53, p. 113.

## Distribution

Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Congo (Kinshasa), Angola, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, Malawi, Zimbabwe

## Ecology

alt. 1150–1500 m. Seasonally humid grassland, flooded or demo

II. ERIINAE Benth.

J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 18: 287 (1881).

Pseudobulbs with a few internodes, or stem. Leaves sometimes doubled or pleated. Inflorescence terminal or superior lateral. Labellum articulate, mobile. Column foot prominent. Rostellum generally ligulate to elongate. Viscidium simple, ellipsoid. Pollinia 8, flattened laterally, elliptic to obovate. Caudicles sticky, composed of apical pollen grains.

The majority of genera in this group are found in Australasia. The only member of the tribe PODOCHILEAE subtr. ERIINAE represented in Africa is the genus Stolzia.
