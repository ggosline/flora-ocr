Species widely distributed from Senegal to Angola, the former Belgian Congo, Uganda and the Republic of Sudan. In Gabon it is known only from a single specimen from the Lastoursville region; the following description is consequently based in part on specimens from neighboring territories. The multiple variations of this species make a narrow definition of F. leprieurii Engl. difficult and may raise some doubts about the synonymy mentioned above and adopted here.

Shrub, doubtless sometimes ± sarmentose, or small tree not exceeding 15 m in height and 40 cm in diameter at the base. On the trunk, spines of irregular size reaching 6-8 cm in length, conical, longitudinally wrinkled and often topped with a sharp prickle. Branches, ± furnished with small longitudinal ridges, spiny with straight prickles perpendicular to the branch or ± curved toward the base (form F. mezoneurospinosa Aké Assi) or toward the apex (form F. leprieurii Engl.); often 2 stipular prickles; prickles dark brown to black in color, ± laterally compressed, not exceeding 8-10 mm in length.

Leaves 15 to 55 cm, furnished with 4 to 8 (–10) pairs of opposite or subopposite leaflets (the lower ones) and a terminal leaflet distant 0 to 3 cm from the upper pair of leaflets; quite often paripinnate leaf through abortion with terminal leaflets ± dissimilar; petiole 3 to 8 cm, and rachis, up to 30 cm, ⊕ slightly flattened and channeled on the upper face, unarmed, more rarely sparsely prickly; leaflets with petiole 1 to 3 (–10) mm in length ± winged; blade oblong-elliptic to oblong-lanceolate 4 to 12 (–16) × 1.5 to 4.5 (–7.5) cm, very distinctly asymmetric and traversed by an oblique midvein, base acute ± cuneate or ± rounded, apex strongly caudate-acuminate; acumen narrow reaching up to 3 cm in length, ± emarginate at the tip, margin either entire, or often obscurely denticulate-crenulate and frequently distinctly crenate at least toward the apex; texture papyraceous to subcoriaceous, blade often distinctly discolorous; translucent punctations relatively few in number, almost all of the same size and dark yellow to reddish in color, visible in transparency and dark in color on the lower face of the blade, at least on dry specimen; often some large black glands, at least at the base of the midvein below the blade, and also apparent on dry specimen; 8 to 16 pairs of secondary lateral veins with fine tertiary veins and network of venules drawn between them, slightly prominent especially below the blade.

Terminal pyramidal panicles, multiflorous, 15 to 30 (–45) cm in length, generally unarmed, with axes puberulous to glabrous; main axis bearing ± horizontal racemes, 6 to 12 (–18) cm long at the base of the panicle; flowers arranged singly, more rarely in sparse fascicles, on these secondary axes. Tetramerous flowers, subsessile or briefly pedicellate (1 mm at most; 2.5 mm in form F. beniensis Engl.); calyx with rounded lobes ± puberulous, sometimes ciliolate; petals oval-elliptic (± 2 × 1 mm) pink-reddish in color. Staminate flowers with dome-shaped disk and gynoecium with 1 aborted carpel. Pistillate flowers with very short subcylindric disk, 4 nearly absent staminodes, gynoecium formed of a single globose carpel (± 1 mm in diameter) with short style ± curved terminated by a small capitate stigma.

Fruit formed of a single subglobose follicle (± 5 mm in diameter), subsessile, not or very weakly stipitate, glandular; 1 subglobose seed (± 3 mm in diameter) very dark blue in color, exceptionally 2 hemispherical seeds.

Property: Contains, like all Fagara, various alkaloids (cf. Palmer and Paris, Ann. pharm. franc. 13: 657, 1955).
