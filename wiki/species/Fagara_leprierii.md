## Description

( = F. attiensis (A. Chev.) Hutch. et Dalz. pro parte, F.W.T.A., 1st ed., 1,2 : 481 (1928).

Species widely distributed from Senegal to Angola, to ex-Belgian Congo, to Uganda and to the Republic of Sudan. In Gabon it is known only from a single specimen from the region of Lastoursville; the following description is consequently based in part on specimens from neighboring territories. The multiple variations of this species moreover make a narrow definition of F. leprieurii Engl. difficult and may leave some doubt about the synonymy mentioned above and adopted herein.

Shrub, doubtless sometimes ± sarmentose, or small tree not exceeding 15 m in height and 40 cm in diameter at the base. On the trunk, spines of irregular size reaching 6-8 cm in length, conical, longitudinally wrinkled and often topped with a sharp prickle. Branchlets, ± furnished with small longitudinal ridges, spiny with prickles straight and perpendicular to the branchlet or ± curved toward the base (form F. mezoneurospinosa Aké Assi) or toward the apex (form F. leprieurii Engl.); often 2 stipular prickles; prickles dark brown to black in color, ± laterally compressed, not exceeding 8-10 mm in length.

Leaves 15 to 55 cm, furnished with 4 to 8 (−10) pairs of opposite or subopposite leaflets (the lower ones) and a terminal leaflet distant 0 to 3 cm from the upper pair of leaflets; fairly often paripinnate leaf by abortion with terminal leaflets ± dissimilar; petiole 3 to 8 cm, and rachis, up to 30 cm, ⊕ slightly flattened and channeled on the upper face, unarmed, more rarely sparsely prickly; leaflets with petiole 1 to 3 (−10) mm in length ± winged; blade oblong-elliptic to oblong-lanceolate 4 to 12 (−16) × 1.5 to 4.5 (−7.5) cm, very distinctly asymmetric and traversed by an oblique median vein, base acute ± cuneate or ± rounded, apex strongly caudate-acuminate; acumen narrow reaching up to 3 cm in length, ± emarginate at the tip, margin either entire, or often obscurely denticulate-crenulate and frequently distinctly crenulate at least toward the apex; papyraceous to subcoriaceous texture, blade often distinctly discolorous; translucent punctations relatively not very numerous, almost all of the same size and dark yellow then reddish in color, visible by transparency and blackish in color on the lower face of the blade, at least on dry specimen; often some large black glands, at least at the base of the median vein below the blade, and also apparent on dry specimen; 8 to 16 pairs of secondary lateral veins with fine tertiary veins and network of veinlets drawn out between them, slightly prominent especially below the blade.

Terminal pyramidal panicles, multiflorous, 15 to 30 (−45) cm in length, generally unarmed, with axes pubérulent to glabrous; main axis bearing ± horizontal racemes, 6 to 12 (−18) cm long at the base of the panicle; flowers arranged singly, more rarely in pauciflorous fascicles, on these secondary axes. Flowers tetramerous, subsessile or briefly pediceled (1 mm at most; 2.5 mm in the form F. beniensis Engl.); calyx with rounded lobes ± pubérulent, sometimes ciliolate; petals ovate-elliptic (± 2 × 1 mm) pinkish-reddish in color. ♂ Flowers with domed disk and gynoecium with 1 abortive carpel. ♀ Flowers with subcylindric very short disk, 4 subnull staminodes, gynoecium formed of a single globular carpel (± 1 mm in diameter) with short style ± curved terminated by a small capitate stigma.

Fruit formed of a single subglobular follicle (± 5 mm in diameter), subsessile, not or very slightly stipitate, glandular; 1 subglobular seed (± 3 mm in diameter) very dark blue in color, exceptionally 2 hemispherical seeds.

Property: Contains, like all Fagara, various alkaloids (cf. Palmer and Paris, Ann. pharm. franc. 13 : 657, 1955).
