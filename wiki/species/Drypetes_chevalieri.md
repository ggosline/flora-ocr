# *Drypetes chevalieri* Beille

**Genus**: [[Drypetes]]
**Family**: [[Putranjivaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Bot. France 54 (Suppl. 3): 293 (1907)

## Description

Plate 52(3-5); Figure 8(C, D)

Lingelsheimia tessmanniana (Pax) Hutch., Fl. trop. Afr. 6 (1): 692 (1912).

Drypetes tessmanniana (Pax) Pax & K.Hoffm. in Engler, Pflanzenr. IV, 147, XV (Heft 81): 2 62 (1922).

Shrub or tree up to ±5 m tall; trunk up to ±8 cm in diameter, sometimes twisted, slightly furrowed; bark smooth, pale coloured, pale greyish-brown, with the odour of mustard oil; young branchlets furrowed, then cylindrical, sparsely puberulent, glabrescent; buds shortly hairy. Stipules caducous, reflexed, triangular-falciform, 3.5–3.6 mm long, puberulent at the apex. Leaf sometimes subsessile and clasping the stem at the base of young branches; petiole (1.7–2.8–3.3–4.6) mm, puberulent, glabrescent; blade obliquely ovate to obliquely elliptic-ovate, 9.7–13–(20.5) × (2.1)–4–5.5–(6.5) cm, subcoriaceous, asymmetric and truncate or subcordate, often slightly auriculate on one side at the base, secondary veins of the blade meeting the petiole separately, abruptly acuminate to slightly caudate at the apex, margin slightly serrulate, sometimes spinose, plane or slightly recurved towards the base, dark glossy green above, paler green below; venation reticulate, midvein glabrescent, secondary veins 4 to 6 (to 8), impressed above, ascending or slightly spreading, looping and anastomosing distinctly before the margin. Male inflorescence in the leaf axils, generally with 1 to 3 flowers, sometimes up to ±10 flowers; bracts ovate, 1.4–2.2 mm long. Male flower: pedicel slender, (0.5–1), 5–3.5(–4) mm, puberulent; sepals 4, imbricate, ovate to suborbicular, (2.2–2), 5–2.9(–3.1) × (2.2–2), 4–3.5(–3.8) mm, obtuse at the apex, yellowish-green, puberulent on both faces, often with minute dense marginal cilia; stamens (6 to 8) to 12, arranged around the edge of the disc and most penetrating it towards the centre, filament (0.9–1), 3–1.7(–2) mm, anther 0.5–1.1(–1.2) mm long; disc (1–2), 2–2.8(–3.1) mm in diameter, very wrinkled, the margin extending between the filaments, sparsely puberulent. Female inflorescence in the leaf axils or on leafless branches, generally with a single flower. Female flower: pedicel 1–2.3 mm, puberulent; sepals 4 (or 5), 4–4.5 × 3.9–4.7 mm, similar to those of the males; disc 3.2–4 mm in diameter, puberulent; ovary subglobose, 2-locular, generally slightly 2-lobed, puberulent; style 0.8–0.9 mm, with 2 branches that separate well during ovary growth, with reniform to gibbous stigma, 2.2–5.2 × 3.6–5.8 mm. Fruit globose to ellipsoid, sometimes weakly 2-lobed, (11.5)–17–22.5(–23.5) × (13–15–17.5(–20) mm, yellow to orange, with persistent stigmas and sepals, puberulent, glabrescent, 2-seeded; pedicel (1.3)–1.5–3.5(–6.5) mm, shortly hairy, glabrescent. Seed (7.2–10.5–12.5(–16) mm long.

## Distribution

Guinea, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, mainland Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon: Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

Habitat: primary and secondary evergreen forests; in Gabon at 250–670 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m.

## Source note

D. chevalieri was collected mainly in central Gabon, outside the coastal plain, throughout the Ogooué basin and its surroundings. The leaves of D. chevalieri can resemble those of D. cinnabarina (which has 5 sepals), sometimes of similar size and with an obtuse-truncate base. In D. cinnabarina, the young branches are thicker and more deeply furrowed than those of D. chevalieri.

D. inaequalis (5 sepals, young branchlets densely hairy) is another understory shrub with an obtuse-truncate leaf base, but its leaves are smaller and generally with a margin more densely crenate or dentate than that of D. chevalieri.
