# *Diospyros sanza-* Minika A. Chevalier

**Genus**: [[Diospyros]]
**Family**: [[Ebenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Vég. ut. Afr. trop fr. 5 : 155 (janv. 1909)

## Description

Veg. ut. Afr. trop fr. 5 : 155 (Jan. 1909). — F. WHITE, FWTA ed. 2, 2 : 12, tab. 202 B, 5 (1963).

— Diospyros nsambensis ("usambensis" sphalm.) Gürke, Bot. Jahrb. 43 : 202 (Mar. 1909). — PELLEGR., Fl. Mayombe 2 : 19 (1928).

— Diospyros glaucescens Gürke, Bot. Jahrb. 46 : 151 (1911), syn. nov.

PL. 23, p. 145, and Map 30, p. 170.

Tree capable of reaching up to 30–40 m in height and 0.60–1 m in diameter, with a straight bole, without buttresses at the base. Rhytidome black, very regularly and deeply fissured, exceedingly hard, more resistant to decay than the wood; internally tender bark and of pinkish colour. Wood whitish-grey when fresh, rosy at the heart and often with small black marbling. Crown globose. Young shoots glabrescenct or scattered with a few appressed hairs; older branchlets greyish-brown, fissured, glabrous. Very young leaves very pale green, reddish, slightly pubescent below, especially on the midvein (hairs scattered, appressed).

Leaves with slender, glabrous petiole, channelled above, 5–10 (–15) mm long; lamina lanceolate-oblong to lanceolate-elliptic, 12–27 × 3–10 cm, rounded or cuneate at the base and attenuate on the petiole, generally acute and imperceptibly acuminate at the apex, the acumen being able however to reach 20 × 5 mm, subcoriaceous to coriaceous, glabrous when mature, except for a few very small appressed hairs below (visible even on the specimen Büssen 463 in herb. B, considered as having glabrous leaves in the diagnosis of D. glaucescens Gürke); upper surface dark shining green, the lower one pale, glaucescent and, when dry, surfaces respectively shining dark red and greyish-pink; midvein impressed above; 7–12 pairs of lateral nerves, fine, slightly prominent above, as are the tertiary nerves, and branched well before the margin; small black glands, impressed, scattered over the entire lower face of the lamina, this having an epidermis covered with a fine whitish mineralized powder.

Male inflorescences in cymes of 3–5 flowers subfasciculate, axillary or on older branchlets, with pedicels very short or reaching up to 3–5 mm, covered with short dark brown pubescence, articulate at the apex, with bracts and bracteoles ± caducous; floral bud elongate, acute. Calyx cupuliform, 6–8 mm high, with margin entire then irregularly split, covered externally with loose pubescence of short dark brown hairs, internally with similar pubescence but appressed and dense; corolla, internally white or pinkish, ± 20 mm, densely felted externally on the floral bud with, when the corolla emerges from the calyx, appressed hairs refracted downward, dark brown above the calyx, silvery inside the latter; tube 10–12 mm, constricted at the throat and surmounted by 4–5 ovate lobes, approximately 10 × 5 mm, fleshy; 20–24 stamens inserted on the receptacle in 4 rows, the outer one also fused on the extreme base of the corolla tube, with filament ± 1 mm, with linear, acute anther, 4–5 mm, these stamens being glabrous, except the inner ones furnished with a few black curved hairs at the base of the anther; pistillode represented by a tuft of dark brown erect hairs.

Female inflorescences in fascicles of 3–5 subsessile flowers, inserted along the older branchlets and flowers solitary or rarely several on the leafy branchlets. Calyx similar to that of the male flower, somewhat accrescent; corolla likewise similar but with 3–5 shorter lobes, not exceeding 1/3 of the depth of the corolla; 5–6 staminodes, 6–7 mm long, slender, glabrous, inserted at the base of the corolla tube; ovary ovoid, 4 mm high, whitish, very pubescent, with styles very briefly fused, equally pubescent, surmounted by 3–4 erect stigmas, 2 mm, pubescent externally and in foliate blade glabrous internally, the ovary itself being hollowed out by 6–8 locules.

Fruits solitary or grouped (2–4) along already old branches, borne on very short pedicels (4–5 mm) and very thick (8–10 mm), ellipsoid-cylindric or slightly tetragons, 3–5 cm high and 2–4.5 cm wide, smooth, pale yellow, covered with rusty or brown hairs when young and until an advanced age; pericarp lignified, 2 mm thick; 4–8 seeds, in white mucilaginous pulp, oblong-cylindric somewhat flattened, rounded at both ends, 2–3 cm long, reaching 7 mm in diameter, slightly rusty finely papillose; fruiting calyx greenish, glabrescenct externally, pubescent internally, 1.5–2 cm in diameter, with 5 truncate lobes.

## Type

Chevalier 16284, Côte d'Ivoire (P)

## Discussion

This species is known in the dense humid evergreen forests from Sierra Leone to Gabon, but it has not yet been recorded from Nigeria; it would also seem to occur in Congo Brazzaville in the Sibiti region (Farron 4403).
