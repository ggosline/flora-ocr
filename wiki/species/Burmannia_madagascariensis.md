# *Burmannia madagascariensis* Baker Planche 2

**Genus**: [[Burmannia]]
**Family**: [[Burmanniaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 20 : 268 (1883)

## Description

Herb 7–30 cm tall; stem simple or rarely branched, pale green to purple. Basal leaves more or less in rosette, narrowly triangular to linear, 5–7 mm long, with 1 median nerve, cauline leaves 2–3 mm long. Inflorescence contracted, with (1–)2–9 flowers; bracts 1–2.5 mm long. Flower 7–14 mm long (ovary included); pedicel 0.5–2.5 mm long; wings extending from the apex of the floral tube to the base of the ovary, white to bluish or pale purple, semi-elliptic to semi-obcordate, 6–12 × 1.5–4 mm; perianth tube 2–3.5 mm long, white or yellowish to blue or violet-blue, the lobes white or yellow turning bluish, the outer ones triangular, 1–2 mm long, the inner ones narrowly triangular, 0.5–1.5 mm long; anthers 0.2–0.5 × 0.2–0.5 mm; ovary cylindric, 2–3 mm long; style 2–2.5 mm long. Fruit obovoid, 3–3.5 mm long. Seeds fusiform, 0.3–0.4 × 0.1 mm.

## Distribution

Species with wide distribution, known from all tropical regions of Africa and Madagascar; very common in Gabon, collected in almost all provinces and probably present also in the others

## Ecology

Common in humid or marshy savannas, open mangroves, on inselbergs, on sandy to clayey or even rocky soil; in Gabon up to 750 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m. Taxonomic notes: Morphologically, Burmannia madagascariensis closely resembles B. bicolor Mart., a species common in the savanna regions of tropical America, but according to Merckx (2008), there are differences at the molecular level. B. madagascariensis has almost always been attributed to Martius (1823), but who published the name without description. Baker (1883), being the first to give such a description and having not included a reference to Martius (1823), is therefore formally the author. Bibliography: Jonker (1938), Geerinck (1970), Merckx (2008), Perrier de la Bâthie (1946)
