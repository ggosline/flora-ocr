# *Sloetiopsis usambarensis* Engler

**Genus**: [[Sloetiopsis]]
**Family**: [[Moraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 39 : 573 (1907) ; BERG, BJBB 47 : 364 (1977)

## Synonyms

- Neosloetiopsis kamerunensis ENGL., l.c. 51: 426 (1914). TYPE: Mildbraed 4331, Cameroun (holo-, B).

## Description

Shrubs or trees dioecious or sometimes monoecious, reaching 10 m in height; latex white or yellowish. Leafy branchlets 1–2 mm thick, sparsely to densely puberulent, in part or solely with uncinate hairs, the latter often of markedly different lengths. Blade (elliptic to) oblong (to lanceolate), generally broader at midpoint, asymmetric especially towards the base, (1–) 3–16 (–23) × (0.5–) 1.5–6 (–10) cm, chartaceous to subcoriaceous, acuminate to caudate, sometimes subacute; base obtuse to acute; margin entire or sometimes weakly to strongly dentate towards the apex; upper and lower faces glabrous or subglabrous; nerves slightly prominent to plane above, prominent below; (4–) 6–9 (–13) pairs of secondary nerves, without parallel tertiary nerves. Petiole 2–7 mm long, mostly puberulent (with uncinate hairs). Stipules often subpersistent, 2–8 mm long, generally with ± distinct prominent parallel nerves, sparsely puberulent.

Male inflorescences, 0.5–5 cm long (ca. 4 mm in diameter at anthesis), subsessile to pedunculate. Peduncle reaching 1.5 mm in length. Flowers laxly arranged; perianth ca. 1.5 mm tall, 4- (6)-fid (to 4- (–6)-partite), membranous, subglabrous; stamens 4 with filaments 2–2.5 mm long and anthers 0.6–0.8 × 0.8–1.0 mm, with small connective; pistillode ca. 0.1 mm tall, conical. Bracts attached by the base or most often (sub)peltate and suborbicular, reaching 1 mm in diameter, puberulent and ciliolate.

Female inflorescences with puberulent peduncle 2–3 mm long. Perianth ca. 1.5 mm tall, with puberulent and ciliolate tepals; pistil ca. 1 mm tall; style ca. 2 mm long; stigmas 2–8 mm long, puberulent. Bracts ovate to reniform and attached by the base or (sub)orbicular and (sub)peltate.

Fruiting peduncle (including the rachis) reaching 4.5 mm in length; tepals enlarged green. Fruit ellipsoid, ca. 1 mm tall; stone subglobose, ca. 7 mm in diameter; endocarp black. Seed ca. 5 × 5–6 mm, with testa bearing a small hilum. – Pl. 2, p. 9.

## Type

Engler 3263, Tanzanie, Usambara (holo-, B)

## Discussion

S. usambarensis has a disjunct range extending from Guinea to Mozambique. One of its partial ranges extends from SE Nigeria to the southern part of the Central African Republic through Gabon. This species appears as small trees or shrubs in humid forests (or sometimes in forests bordering savanna), along streams (or streamlets), and as low-branched shrubs abundant on rocks in the riverbed or on riverbanks.
