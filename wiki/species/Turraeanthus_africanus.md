# *Turraeanthus africanus* (Welw. ex C.DC.) Pellegr. Planche 26

**Genus**: [[Turraeanthus]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Not. Syst. (Paris) 2 : 16 (1911)

## Synonyms

- Guarea africana Welw. ex C.DC. in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phaner . 1 : 576 (1878).
- Bingeria africana A.Chev., Vég. Ut. Afr. Trop. Franç. 5, Bois Côte Ivoire : 189 (1909).
- Turraeanthus zenkeri Harms, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 156 (1896).

## Description

Medium tree reaching 35(–40) m in height; trunk up to 100(–120) cm in diameter, often tortuous; bark grey or whitish; section yellowish, with a strong cedar odor, without exudate. Leaf: petiole 5–15(–33) cm, thickened at the base, flattened or even channelled and furnished with narrow wings on the upper face; rachis (10–)30–60(–80) cm; petiolules 3–10 mm; leaflets (9–)11–25(–31), alternate or sometimes subopposite, narrowly rectangular to obovate, (4.5–)8–16(–23) × (2–)2.5–5.5(–6) cm, subrounded or cuneiform and sometimes asymmetrical at the base, apex obtuse or attenuate, often terminating abruptly in a short spoon-like acumen; midvein usually depressed above, lateral veins (10–)15–25 pairs. Inflorescence axillary, 4–25 cm in length, shortly branched, with puberulent, ferruginous axes. Functionally male flower: pedicel 2.5–4 mm, with receptacle 1.5 mm in length; calyx with entire or vaguely 5-dentate margins, 2 × 3.5 mm, ferruginous tomentose; petals 5(–6), united into a tube 10–15 mm in length, tomentose, yellowish, spreading at the apex into 5(–6) spreading spatulate lobes, 6–8 × 2–3 mm; staminal tube 18–20 mm in length, pubescent inside, united with the corolla for 14–16 mm, with crenate margin, anthers 10(–11); pistillode filiform, 19–20 mm in length, entirely pubescent; stigma discoid. Functionally female flower similar; ovary clearly developed, 4–5-locular, pubescent. Fruit obovoid or subglobose, depressed at the apex, 2.5–3.5 cm in diameter, weakly (2–)3–4-lobed, glabrescent, yellow-orange. Seed ellipsoid, 15–22 × 9–12 mm, completely surrounded by a thin yellowish sarcotesta.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola and Uganda; in Gabon, relatively rare and scattered, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Old lowland evergreen forest; reported from Côte d'Ivoire and Equatorial Guinea as forming dense but localized stands; in Gabon, up to approximately 600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Uses: Yielding good white timber, frequently watered-silk figured. The bark is used in traditional medicine
