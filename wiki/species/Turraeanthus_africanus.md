# *Turraeanthus africanus* (Welw. ex C.DC.) Pellegr. Planche 26

**Genus**: [[Turraeanthus]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Not. Syst. (Paris) 2 : 16 (1911)

## Description

Not. Syst. (Paris) 2 : 16 (1911). Guarea africana Welw. ex C.DC. in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phaner . 1 : 576 (1878). Bingeria africana A.Chev., Vég. Ut. Afr. Trop. Franç. 5, Bois Côte Ivoire : 189 (1909). Turraeanthus zenkeri Harms, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 156 (1896). Medium-sized tree reaching 35(–40) m in height; trunk up to 100(–120) cm in diameter, often tortuous; bark grey or whitish; interior yellowish, with strong cedar odour, without exudate. Leaf: petiole 5–15(–33) cm, thickened at base, flattened or even canaliculate and bearing narrow wings on the upper surface; rachis (10–)30–60(–80) cm; petiolules 3–10 mm; leaflets (9–)11–25(–31), alternate or sometimes subopposite, narrowly rectangular to obovate, (4.5–)8–16(–23) × (2–)2.5–5.5(–6) cm, subrounded or cuneate and sometimes asymmetric at base, apex obtuse or attenuate, often terminating abruptly in a short spoon-shaped acumen; midvein usually impressed above, lateral veins (10–)15–25 pairs. Inflorescence axillary, 4–25 cm long, shortly branched, with axes puberulent, ferruginous. Functionally male flower: pedicel 2.5–4 mm, with receptacle 1.5 mm long; calyx with entire or vaguely 5-toothed margins, 2 × 3.5 mm, ferruginous tomentose; petals 5(–6), fused into a tube 10–15 mm long, tomentose, yellowish, expanding at apex into 5(–6) spreading spatulate lobes, 6–8 × 2–3 mm; staminal tube 18–20 mm long, pubescent inside, fused with corolla for 14–16 mm, with crenulate margin, anthers 10(–11); pistillode filiform, 19–20 mm long, entirely pubescent; stigma discoid. Functionally female flower similar; ovary clearly developed, 4–5-locular, pubescent. Fruit obovoid or subglobose, depressed at apex, 2.5–3.5 cm in diameter, weakly (2–)3–4-lobed, glabrescent, yellow-orange. Seed ellipsoid, 15–22 × 9–12 mm, completely surrounded by a thin yellowish sarcotesta.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola and Uganda; in Gabon, relatively rare and scattered, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

old lowland evergreen forest; reported from Côte d'Ivoire and Equatorial Guinea as forming dense but localized stands; in Gabon, up to about 600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Uses: Providing good white timber, frequently watered. The bark is used in traditional medicine

## Vernacular names

avodiré (fr.) ; avodire (angl.). B : Aubréville (1959), Owusu (2008), Staner (1941), Voorhoeve (1965). ibliographie
