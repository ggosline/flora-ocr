# *Pleiocarpa bicarpellata* Stapf Planche 88

**Genus**: [[Pleiocarpa]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1894 : 21 (1894)

## Description

Shrub or small tree reaching 8 m in height. Leaves opposite; petiole 4–14 mm; blade narrowly elliptic to oblong, 8–16 times as long as wide, 5–14 × 0.5–1.2 cm, acute to cuneate and decurrent on the petiole at the base, acuminate at the apex; secondary nerves 19 to 30 pairs, straight, forming an angle of 70–85° with the median nerve, diffuse from below, forming a distinct submarginale nerve, tertiary venation scarcely visible below and ± parallel to secondary nerves. Inflorescence axillary, ramified sometimes terminal at the same time, fasciculate, 0.5–2 × 0.5–1.2 cm, with flowers bracted, very small, squamiform. Flower fragrant; pedicel 1–2 mm; calyx with lobes 1.1–2.8 × 0.6–1.7 mm; corolla pure white, 15–18.5 mm long in mature bud and forming an oblong head representing ± 0.6 of the length of the bud, apex rounded, tube subcylindric, 9.2–10–15 mm long, 0.8–1.8 mm wide above the base, narrowed below insertion of stamens and 0.4–0.8 mm wide, widened again at level of anthers over 0.7–2 mm, with lobes spread and curved afterwards, narrowly oblong to broadly oval, 0.4–0.8 times as long as tube, 1.2–2.2 × 0.5–0.8–1.2 mm; stamens inserted distinctly above the middle of the tube, apex situated 2–5 mm below the throat, anther 0.8–1.2 times as long as wide, 1–1.4 × 0.2–0.35 mm; pistil 7.5–12.2 mm long, ovary with 2 distinct carpels, 2-ovulate; style 5.5–10.7 mm. Fruit orange, smooth, with 2 distinct mericarps, obovoid to ellipsoid, 11–18 × 7–12 × 8–9 mm, rounded at the apex, 2 seeds. Seed 9–11 mm long. Distribution: Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola and Kenya. In Gabon: Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: Primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 650 m altitude, elsewhere up to 900 m. Use: The wood is used for combs and planes. Notes: Omino mentions specimens from the eastern part of the distribution zone that resemble P. bicarpellata greatly but that bear carpels. Plants presenting this character have equally been collected in eastern Gabon (Walters 1375), and it is not evident that these specimens are indeed of the same species. B: Burkill (1985), Omino (1996, 2002). BIBLIOGRAPHY
