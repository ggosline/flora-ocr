# *Vigna pubigera* Baker var. pubigera

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Oliv., Fl. trop. Afr. 2 : 201 (1871). Climbing herb, pubescent. Stipules ovate-lanceolate, 2–5 × 1–2 mm, prolonged below the insertion by two unequal lobes, pubescent. Leaf: petiole 1.5–6.5 cm; rachis 4–15 mm; stipels 0.5–1 mm long; leaflets with petiolule of 1–2 mm, blade lanceolate to ovate-elliptic, 2.5–15 × 0.5–3.5 cm, rounded at the base, rounded-apiculate to acuminate at the apex, pubescent on both surfaces. Inflorescence: peduncle 1.5–15 cm; rachis 1–4 cm; bracts ovate-lanceolate, 2–4 mm long. Flower: bracteoles 1.5–5 × 1 mm; calyx pubescent, with tube 2–3 mm long, lobes ovate-rhombic to triangular, 0.5–2 mm long, rounded to acute at the apex; corolla 11–16 mm long, yellow, pubescent, standard 10–16 × 15–18 mm, with apex emarginate, tomentose externally, furnished internally with four parallel appendages, wings obovate, 11–15 mm long, keel curved, 11–15 mm long, with short and obtuse beak; ovary with 6 to 10 ovules; style extended beyond the stigma by a short beak. Pod pendulous, 3–6 cm × 4–5 mm, pubescent, terminated by a curved beak. Seed 4–5.5 × 2–4 mm; hilum and strophiole eccentric, 2–3 mm long.

## Distribution

from Senegal to Sudan and extending to Zambia and Angola; in Gabon, known only from the Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

savanna, fallow land; in Gabon at 500–700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Flowers in November; fruits in March

## Source note

Species with pubescent standard were grouped under V. ambacensis Baker by Wilczeck (1954), a concept adopted by Verdcourt (1970) and then Maréchal et al. (1978). However, V. pubigera (with yellow flowers) and V. heterophylla A. Rich. (with blue flowers, moreover earlier than V. ambacensis) constitute two well-differentiated taxa (Feleke et al., unpubl.), with clearly different ranges. V. pubigera is a low-altitude species, whereas V. heterophylla is found at higher altitude, de facto, from Cameroon to Arabia, Malawi and Angola. Var. benuensis (Pasquet & Maréchal) Pasquet & Maesen differs from it by the smaller and practically glabrous standard, a more trailing than twining habit, and flowers often subterranean and cleistogamous; it is distributed from Ghana to Chad.
