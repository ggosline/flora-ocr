# *Cyperus zollingeri* Steud. Planche 49

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Syn. pl. glum. 2 : 17 (1855), non C.B.Clarke

## Description

, not C.B.Clarke C. rubroviridis Cherm., Bull. Soc. Bot. France 66 : 350 (1920). Slender annual herb with a very small root system; stems 10–30 cm × 0.7–1 mm, trigonous, finely hairy to almost glabrous, with only 1–3 leaves at the base. Leaf: sheath 1 mm wide; blade up to 10 cm × 2 mm, scabrous on the margins and towards the apex. Inflorescence in a loose anthela formed of one sessile spike and 3–6 pedunculate spikes; involucral bracts 5–8, foliaceous, up to 20 cm × 3 mm, like the basal leaves but more scabrous along the central nerve; spikes approximately 1.5 × 1.5 cm, with 2–6 digitate and distant spikelets, sometimes with a short basal branch bearing 1–3 spikelets. Spikelet linear to linear–lanceolate, approximately 10 × 1.5 mm, subquadrangular in section, with 10–20 flowers; basal sterile bract smaller than the glumes, 1–2 mm long; glumes ovate, 3–3.8 mm long, rounded at apex, with wide pale margin, a green central nerve bordered by a brownish portion. Flower with 2–3 stamens; style usually with 3 long and fine stigmas. Achene obovate, 1.2–1.6 mm in length, trigonous, almost smooth.

## Distribution

widely dispersed but rare in the tropical regions of Africa, Asia and Australia; very rare in Gabon, found only in the Estuaire (Billagone plantation)

## Ecology

in thinned rainforest and other open habitats; in Gabon, at low altitude, up to 600 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: very close to C. sphacelatus, but differing in its smaller size, its different inflorescence (non-digitate spikes in C. sphacelatus) and its glumes lacking dark spots. Stem and leaf anatomy of chlorocyperoid type. Inflorescence in a loose or compact anthela, with a few to several distinct spikes, ovoid to cylindric, each with numerous spikelets and long foliaceous involucral bracts. Spikelets cylindric to slightly flattened, breaking at maturity into segments with 1 fruit, each comprising a rachis internode and an achene surrounded by the wings of the rachis; glumes densely imbricate, ovate, usually 2–3 mm long. Flower with 3 stamens; style with 3 branches. Achenes sessile, trigonous, slightly flattened dorso-ventrally, with finely papillose or fairly smooth surface. Small subgenus of 8 very closely related species, from marshes, lakeshores, marine shores and brackish marshes, growing only at low altitude in Africa, of which one species is pantropical and widely distributed, the others limited to the tropical and subtropical regions of America; in Gabon only one species.
