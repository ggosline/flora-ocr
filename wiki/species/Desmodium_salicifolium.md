# *Desmodium salicifolium* (Poir.) DC. var. salicifolium Planche 56

**Genus**: [[Desmodium]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. 2 : 337 (1825)

## Description

Subshrub lianiform or creeping, 0.9–2(–4) m tall; branches numerous, semiligneous, cylindrical, uncinate-pubescent. Stipules tardily caducous, triangular-acuminate, 7–18 mm long, glabrous, striate. Leaf: petiole 3–4 cm, finely hairy especially in a lateral row to almost glabrous; stipels subulate, 3–7 mm long; leaflets 3, with petiolule 2–4 mm, brown, hairy; with blade narrowly elliptic to narrowly ovate, (6–)9–17 × 2–6.5 cm, base rounded or cordate, oblique in the lateral ones, apex acuminate, the lateral ones slightly smaller; nerves prominent, in 12 to 20 pairs, these slightly hairy above, hairy below. Inflorescence in terminal pseudoracemes, lax, 10–20 cm long,

Flower: pedicel 2–4 mm, slender; calyx with tube 1–2 mm long, puberulent and pilose, with lanceolate lobes, at least 2 times longer than the tube; corolla yellowish-white to reddish, 2–3 times longer than the calyx, up to 8(–9) mm long, standard longer than the other petals. Pod sessile, straight to curved, 3–4 cm × 3–4 mm, with 3 to 6 rectangular articles, slightly uncinate-velutinous and reticulate, dorsal suture straight, the ventral one barely notched at the articulations. Seed ellipsoid, ± 3 × 1.5 × 1 mm, reddish-brown; hilum central.

## Distribution

throughout tropical Africa, southern Africa and the Mascarenes; in Gabon, known from Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

gallery forest, margins, humid places, banks; in Gabon at 2–550 m altitude, elsewhere, up to 1800 m. Flowers and fruits in May and August. Use: Used as green manure and cover crop. The leaves cooked with courgette seeds treat epilepsy in children and diarrhea in adults. Fang women use it as an anti-emmenagogue; used as a friction on rheumatic limbs, in maceration as an emetic. The Mindumu in Haut-Ogooué apply the plant against kidney pain or dazzling sensations
