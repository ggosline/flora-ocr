# *Lippia rugosa* A.Chev.

**Genus**: [[Lippia]]
**Family**: [[Verbenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Soc. Bot. France 8 : 191 (1912)

## Description

Shrub, 1.2–2.5 m tall, aromatic (lemon scent); stem striate with appressed non-glandular hairs distinctly soft. Leaves generally arranged in whorls of 3; petiole 5–10 mm, rugose; lamina elliptic to narrowly elliptic-obovate, (4.5–)10–15 × (1–)3.5–4.5 cm, attenuate at base, acute at apex, broadly to very narrowly serrulate, coriaceous, rugose to the touch above, densely hairy below; venation impressed above in herbarium material, distinctly hairy. Inflorescences terminal with a few axillary in the upper nodes, 1.5–6 cm long and 7–10 cm in fruit; spikes numerous, subcapitate to cylindric-oblong, sometimes lax, sessile or pedunculate; bracts oblong, acute to acuminate at apex. Flower weakly fragrant; calyx subglobose, ± 1.5 mm long, densely pubescent; corolla white, with pale yellow throat, tube funnel-shaped from a narrow base, 1.5–1.9 mm long, with scattered hairs on the outside, lower lip oblong, ± 1 × 1 mm. Seed semi-ovoid, 1–1.3 × 0.5 mm.

## Distribution

native to western and central tropical Africa including Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon and the Central African Republic; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ngounié and Nyanga

## Ecology

grasslands, savannas, roadsides; in Gabon at 80–170 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1650 m

Uses: The leaves are used in medicine throughout Africa to treat wounds, colds and internal infections. In Gabon, it is sold in markets and used to increase the production of breast milk.

## Vernacular names

wild grassland lantana (angl.) ; afing (fang) ; ungumba luunga (bapunu). B : Burkill (2000), Huber et al. (1963), Sosef et al . (2006). i B liographie
