# *Urera batesii* Rendle Planche 29(2)

**Genus**: [[Urera]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. Bot. 54 : 368 (1917)

## Description

Liane; stem glabrous and without or rarely with poorly marked protuberances, secondary growth unknown; cystoliths punctiform, very small and insignificant. Stipules caducous, poorly known, but probably more than 0.5 cm in length. Leaf: petiole 3–9 cm; lamina suborbicular to elliptic, ovate or rarely weakly obovate, 8–15 × 8–12 cm, base rounded to subcordate, apex rounded with a triangular acumen not exceeding 1 cm in length, margin entire or weakly crenulate in the upper half, membranous, upper surface with some stinging hairs and numerous elongate cystoliths, of variable size, lower surface glabrous, with elongate cystoliths along the nerves; the pair of basal lateral nerves reaching the upper two-thirds of the lamina and emitting ± 5 nerves towards the margin, upper lateral nerves in 2 or 3 pairs, tertiary venation scalariform. Inflorescence paniculate, the male ones lax, up to ± 5 × 5 cm, with a fleshy peduncle 1–1.5 cm, the female ones comparable to the male ones, but more compact and with more robust branches, with stinging hairs; peduncle 1–5 cm. Male flower subsessile or shortly pedicellate, ± 5 mm in diameter, 5-merous, sometimes with a few stinging hairs. Female flower subsessile; perianth ± 4-lobed, ciliate, divided from the middle to two-thirds and covering approximately half of the ovary; stigma penicillate. Fruit almost entirely enveloped in the accrescent and fleshy perianth.

## Distribution

from southeastern Nigeria to southern Cameroon where it is localized north of the border with Gabon, also in Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Rio Muni); in Gabon, a single collection in Nyanga (west of Tchibanga, between Malounga and Igotchi)

## Ecology

margins of large forests, elsewhere also on rocks; in Gabon at 145 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–200 m

## Source note

The presence of this species in Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of Congo, mentioned in the literature, has not been confirmed

It is known from the mountains of Cameroon, and the margin of the lamina appears almost entirely regular. In the absence of more complete material, both from Gabon and elsewhere, it was decided to maintain U. batesii as distinct and to refer to it from Gabon, the only material from Nyanga.
