# *Licania elaeosperma* (Mildbraed) Prance & F. White

**Genus**: [[Licania]]
**Family**: [[Chrysobalanaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Lecythidaceae]] subfam. Scytopetaloideae — *Scytopetalaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: BJBB 46:280 (1976)

## Synonyms

- Afrolicania eloeosperma MILDBR., Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Mus. Berlin 7 : 483 (1921); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 427 (1958); AUBR., FFCI, ed. 2, 1 : 174, tab. 52, fig. 1-5 (1959); KEAY, ONOCHIE & STANFIELD, Nig. Trees 1 : 322 (1960); IRVINE, Woody Pl. Ghana : 260 (1961).

## Description

— Homalium zenkeri GILG in sched. (Zenker 1629 et Zenker « Flora von Kamerun » 472) ex MILDBR., l.c. : 484.

— Parinari glabra auct. non Oliv. : FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (2) : 371 (1928), p.p.

— Pygeum (?) klainei PIERRE, ms. in sched. (P).

Small tree reaching 20–50 cm (sometimes 80 cm) in diameter, furnished at the base with buttresses extending up to 1–2 m in height along an irregular trunk, often furnished with depressions and elongated fluting. Bark dark brown, marbled with very large greyish or greenish patches, finely rugose; hard, thick, granular bark, in oblique section dark-red towards the outside and pink-orange towards the inside. Sapwood 1 cm, pale pink, then heartwood reddish-brown, darker towards the centre, very hard. Branches at low height (6–8 m) above the ground, obliquely erect, supporting a hemispherical very leafy crown; drooping branchlets dark violaceous glossy; very young branchlets reddish ± pendulous with intrapetiolar stipules, golden-brown, elongately triangular (4–6 mm), tardily deciduous ±. Leaves with canaliculate petiole, 5–10 mm, covered in the juvenile state like the young branchlets with an arachnoid pubescence of blonde-pink tint, then black and fissured; blade fairly coriaceous, elliptic, 3–8 × 7–16 cm, narrowly cuneate at the base, with an acute triangular acumen 1 cm; sometimes 2 elliptic brown then black glands at the extreme base of the lower face of the blade; approximately 8 pairs of arched ascending lateral nerves, anastomosing 2–3 mm from the blade margin; dense network of nervules apparent on the upper face; lower face of juvenile blades covered with the same arachnoid pubescence as above then gradually deciduous in patches.

Panicles reaching a width of 20 cm and a length of 25 cm, with few lateral axes and ± perpendicular to the main axis, these racemiform axes bearing pedunculate cymes (2–6 mm) of 3–5 grouped flowers; axes, small bracts and bracteoles collectively, lanceolate and golden-brown, as well as flowers, covered with a whitish-greyish puberulence. Fragrant flowers, with pedicel 1–2 mm; floral bud flattened, 2.5 mm in diameter; sepals triangular, with concave base, 1.5–2 mm in width and 1–1.5 mm in height; (e descr.) ovary 1 mm in diameter and style 1 mm.

Fruits with robust peduncle not exceeding 5 mm in length, furnished with desiccated sepals at the base, ovoid, 3.5 × 4.5 × 5 cm, brown but densely covered with very closely packed warts, silvery-golden; pericarp 3 mm and bitter almond 6 mm thick, in the fresh state. — PL. 20, p. 69.

## Type

Zenker 1629, Cameroun (« Homalium zenkeri Gilg »); fl. ♂ (lecto- (WHITE, 1976), B!; isolecto-, K! M! P!)

## Discussion

Small tree recorded along the western coast of Africa where it occurs in littoral scrubland, from Sierra Leone to Gabon; it is thus present behind the mangrove in the regions of Ndian-Mundemba and Douala in Cameroon, from Libreville in Gabon. In several territories it is found even further inland, along rivers; in Cameroon it is recorded along the banks of the Lokoundjé at Bipindi, but is especially abundant in the Nyong valley (regions of Akonolinga and Mbalmayo) as well as in the Dja valley (regions of Somalomo and Zoulabot II), always living in periodically flooded forests, mixed with Manilkara argentea, Uapaca heudelotii, Cathormion altissimum... or in company with Pericopsis elata, Lophira alata..., also on the sandy ridges of the banks of the Ngoko upstream of Ouesso, scattered then in the midst of riparian populations with Guibourtia demeusei. In Gabon it is also present in the region of Mitzic and, in Congo, has been found in the region of Ouesso.

## Uses

Fruit ("mahogany nut", "nico", or "nikko") formed 40% by the shell and 60% by the almond, the latter containing more than 50% of pale yellow oil, strongly desiccative, possible substitute for linseed oil (cf. Bull. Imp. Inst. : 38–40, 1918 and 271–274, 1935; BRUNNER, Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Mus. Berlin 8 : 188, 1922; IRVINE, l.c. : 260)
