# *Licania elaeosperma* (Mildbraed) Prance & F. White

**Genus**: [[Licania]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: BJBB 46:280 (1976)

## Description

— Afrolicania eloeosperma MILDBR., Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Mus. Berlin 7 : 483 (1921); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 427 (1958); AUBR., FFCI, ed. 2, 1 : 174, tab. 52, fig. 1-5 (1959); KEAY, ONOCHIE & STANFIELD, Nig. Trees 1 : 322 (1960); IRVINE, Woody Pl. Ghana : 260 (1961).

— Homalium zenkeri GILG in sched. (Zenker 1629 et Zenker « Flora von Kamerun » 472) ex MILDBR., l.c. : 484.

— Parinari glabra auct. non Oliv. : FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (2) : 371 (1928), p.p.

— Pygeum (?) klainei PIERRE, ms. in sched. (P).

Small tree, reaching 20–50 cm (sometimes 80 cm) in diameter, furnished at the base with buttresses extending up to 1–2 m in height along an irregular trunk, often provided with depressions and elongate furrows. Bark brown, marbled with very large greyish or greenish patches, finely rugose; hard, thick bark, granulous, in oblique section dark red toward the exterior and pink-orange toward the interior. Sapwood 1 cm, white-pink, then heartwood reddish-brown, darker toward the centre, very hard. Branches, at low height (6–8 m) above the ground, obliquely erect, supporting a hemispherical, very leafy crown; drooping branchlets brown-violet, shining; very young branchlets reddish ± pendulous with intrapetiolar stipules, brown-golden, elongately triangular (4–6 mm), deciduous ± tardily. Leaves with canaliculate petiole, 5–10 mm, covered in the juvenile state like the young branchlets with an aranose pubescence of blonde-pink tint, then black and fissured; limb rather coriaceous, elliptic, 3–8 × 7–16 cm, narrowly cuneate at the base, with an acute triangular acumen 1 cm long; sometimes 2 brown then black elliptic glands at the extreme base of the lower face of the limb; approximately 8 pairs of arcuate, ascending lateral veins, anastomosing 2–3 mm from the limb margin; dense network of veinlets apparent on the upper face; lower face of juvenile limbs covered with the same aranose pubescence as above then progressively deciduous in patches.

Panicles reaching 20 cm in width and 25 cm in length, with few lateral axes ± perpendicular to the main axis, these racemose axes bearing pedunculate cymes (2–6 mm) of 3–5 flowers grouped together; axes, small bracts and bracteoles gathered, lanceolate and brown-golden, as well as flowers, covered with a whitish-greyish puberulence. Fragrant flowers, with pedicel 1–2 mm; floral bud flattened, 2.5 mm in diameter; triangular sepals, with concave base, 1.5–2 mm in width and 1–1.5 mm in height; (e descr.) ovary 1 mm in diameter and style 1 mm.

Fruits with robust peduncle not exceeding 5 mm in length, furnished with desiccated sepals at the base, ovoid, 3.5 × 4.5 × 5 cm, brown but densely covered with very closely packed warts, silvery-golden; pericarp 3 mm and bitter kernel 6 mm in thickness, in the fresh state. — PL. 20, p. 69.

## Type

Zenker 1629, Cameroun (« Homalium zenkeri Gilg »); fl. ♂ (lecto- (WHITE, 1976), B!; isolecto-, K! M! P!)

## Discussion

Small tree recorded along the western coast of Africa where it occurs in littoral shrubby thickets, from Sierra Leone to Gabon; it is thus present behind the mangrove in the regions of Ndian-Mundemba and Douala in Cameroon, from Libreville in Gabon. In several territories it is found further inland, along rivers; in Cameroon it is recorded on the banks of the Lokoundjé at Bipindi, but is especially abundant in the Nyong valley (regions of Akonolinga and Mbalmayo) as well as in the Dja valley (regions of Somalomo and Zoulabot II), always living in periodically flooded forests, mixed with Manilkara argentea, Uapaca heudelotii, Cathormion altissimum... or in company with Pericopsis elata, Lophira alata..., also on the sandy berms of the banks of the Ngoko upstream of Ouesso, then dispersed in the midst of riverine stands of Guibourtia demeusei. In Gabon it is also present in the region of Mitzic and, in Congo, has been found in the region of Ouesso.

## Uses

Fruit ("mahogany nut", "nico", or "nikko") formed for 40% by the shell and 60% by the kernel, the latter containing more than 50% of pale yellow oil, strongly desiccative, possible substitute for linseed oil (cf. Bull. Imp. Inst. : 38–40, 1918 and 271–274, 1935; BRUNNER, Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Mus. Berlin 8 : 188, 1922; IRVINE, l.c. : 260)

ndonda, ndunda (douala); otop (éwondo)
