# *Uapaca guineensis* Müll.Arg. Planche 30B,C & 31

**Genus**: [[Uapaca]]
**Family**: [[Phyllanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Flora 47 : 517 (1864)

## Description

Tree at least up to 30 m in height; trunk at least up to 60 cm in diameter, with stilt roots up to 2 m in height; branchlets puberulent to tomentose around the base of the petiole. Stipules absent or very small, triangular, 1–2(–6) mm in length, tomentose. Leaf: petiole often somewhat canaliculate above, (1–)2–6.5 cm, glabrous; lamina obovate-elliptic, (9–)13–22 × (4–)7–12 cm, rounded to cuneate at the base, obtuse and retuse or rarely shortly acuminate at the apex, coriaceous to papyraceous, sparsely tomentellous beneath when young, quickly glabrescent; lateral veins (5–)8–9(–11) pairs. Inflorescence: involucral bracts 10, ± concave, up to 10 × 7 mm, glabrous or sometimes slightly ciliate; male peduncle up to 25 mm; female peduncle 5–9 mm. Male flower ± sessile; calyx irregularly lobed or sepals nearly free, 1–1.5 mm in length, sparsely pubescent; petals present, free, filiform, ± 1 mm in length, pilose at the apex; stamens 4–5, 2–2.5 mm in length, glabrous, anthers 0.5–0.7 mm in length; pistillode as long as the petals, dilated and pilose at the apex. Female flower with calyx ± 1 mm in length, glabrous or pilose on the exterior, generally with long hairs emerging from the interior, visible beneath the fruit; ovary ellipsoid, 3–8 mm in length, glabrous; stigmas often with a few hairs on the exterior. Fruit globose to ellipsoid or obovoid, slightly 3-lobed, 18–25 × 15–23 mm, glabrous, rugose, lenticellate.

## Distribution

from Sierra Leone to Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Central African Republic, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Angola and Democratic Republic of the Congo; well distributed in Gabon, but not yet collected in Moyen-Ogooué and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

gallery forest, in riparian and marshy habitats, but also on terra firme; in Gabon, at 0–500 m in altitude. Use: The roots are commonly used in traditional pharmacopoeia; sometimes also the bark of stems, leaves and fruits. Root preparations are commonly taken for their aphrodisiac properties and against male impotence. The bark is used as an enema, as an emetic, in lotions with salt, against skin affections, or as a dye for fishing nets. The wood is used to make charcoal for forges and ironing. The pulp of the fruit is edible.

## Vernacular names
