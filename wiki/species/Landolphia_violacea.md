# *Landolphia violacea* (K.Schum. ex Hallier f.) Pichon Planche 72

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 157 (1953)

## Description

Large liana; branchlets lenticellate, glabrous. Leaf glabrous; petiole 5–17 mm; blade narrowly ovate to ovate or obovate, 1–3 times as long as wide, 5.5–25 × 2.4–10 cm, acuminate at apex with acumen obtuse and generally abruptly acuminate at the apex; secondary veins 8 to 17 pairs, slightly curved, not prominent below, tertiary venation visible below, less visible above. Inflorescence axillary sessile or with peduncle reaching 0.5 mm; bracts 1 or 2, 0.6–1 mm long, pilose or pubescent on the outside. Flower: pedicel 0.1–2 mm; calyx with 4 or 5 unequal lobes, 0.7–1 times as long as wide, 1.5–2.5 mm long, ciliate, partly pilose or pubescent on the outside, glabrous on the inside; corolla light or dark red, bluish or violet, occasionally whitish or with a combination of these colours, tube often darker than the lobes, tube 12–23 mm long, 1.6–2.3 mm wide at the level of the anthers and 1.4–2 mm at the throat, glabrous or nearly so on the outside, hairy on the inside behind and below the anthers, lobes 0.4–1.3 times as long as the tube, 1–2 times as long as wide, 0.8–2 × 1.3–3.3 mm, hairy on the auricles to hairy or slightly pubescent or glabrous elsewhere; stamens inserted at 0.76–0.9 of the tube length, exserted to included up to 2 mm, anther 1.6–2.3 mm long; pistil 12.5–16.5 mm long, ovary densely hairy or pubescent; style 1–1.5 mm, pubescent or hairy, glabrous or nearly so towards the apex, sometimes widely glabrous. Fruit oblong-conical, ovoid or subglobose, 2.5–3 × 1.5–2 cm, dark blue or violet with longitudinal green, yellow or whitish patches, often with brownish-orange lenticels reaching 3 mm in diameter, with 10 to 25 seeds; wall 0.4–1.8 cm thick. Distribution: southeast of Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo, in Gabon, Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 700 m altitude, up to 800 m elsewhere.

## Source note

The difference between this species and L. ligustrifolia (see the key) is not evident and encompasses only a few foliar characters. Uniting the two seems not to be the solution. Supplementary research is necessary to see if more than two species are involved here. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
