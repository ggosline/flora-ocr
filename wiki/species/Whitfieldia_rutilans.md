# *Whitfieldia rutilans* Heine, sp. nov.

**Genus**: [[Whitfieldia]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]

## Description

W. coloratae C. B. Clarke ex Stapf most closely allied, differing in much shorter petioles, atrescing when dry, rugose, larger but narrower leaves, longer inflorescence bracts, ovate, corolla reddish-ochre glittering without striae of more intense colours, anthers only very slightly puberulous towards the base, style most glabrous.

Suffrutex (?) glabrous, branches and branchlets terete, bark dark brown, nodes 5–6.5 cm distant, petioles dark brown when dry, rugose, 1–1.5 cm long, leaf limb narrowly elliptic, cuspidate, attenuate towards the base (17)–20–28 × (5.5)–7.5–8.5 cm, most glabrous, chartaceous, dark olive-brown when dry, entire surface and midrib especially furnished with minute sessile orbicular brown and amber glands, lateral nerves on either side 8–10, inflorescences terminal, congested (nodes above first node 4–6 mm distant), up to 8 cm long, inflorescence peduncle 5–15 mm long, bract subulate or linear, up to 6 mm long, peduncle of individual flowers 4–7 mm long, bracteoles sessile, in living state tinged the colour of the corollas (according to collector's annotation on sheet), oval, acuminate, 10–11 × 5–5.5 mm, quintuplinerved, most glabrous, on the exterior furnished with the same minute glands as in the leaf limb—

tulate, pedicel short, but conspicuous, 1–1.5 mm long, calyx 16 mm long, sepals nearly equal, linear-lanceolate, 2–2.5 mm wide, tri- or quintuplinerved, apex rounded, in living state reddish-tinged (?), on both sides, but especially towards the margin, furnished with succinate stipitate glands, corolla reddish-ochre glittering or intense orange (according to collector's annotation), most glabrous, circa 2 cm long, tube 16–18 mm long, constricted 3 mm above the base, 15-nerved, lobes acuminate-ovate, nearly equal, 4 × 2.5 mm, constricted part of tube within forming a ring densely and briefly pilose, stamens inserted in this ring, filaments 18 mm long, most glabrous, anthers fusiform, 2.5 mm long, connective and thecae in lower parts slightly puberulous; ovary most glabrous, 1.5 mm long, disc thick, 2 mm in diameter, 0.6 mm high, style 22–25 mm long, most glabrous, stigma slightly thickened, subemarginate; fruit unknown.

Habitat in Gabon.

Robust plant (shrub?), glabrous, with terete stems, with dark brown bark; internodes 5–6.5 cm long. Petioles dark brown when dry, rugose, 1–1.5 cm long. Leaf limb narrowly elliptic, acute cuspidate at the base, (17) 20–28 × (5.5) 7.5–8.5 cm, glabrous, cartilaginous; entire surface is covered with small brown and transparent glands, especially along the midrib. Lateral nerves 8–10.

Inflorescences terminal, fairly condensed, up to 8 cm long; peduncle 5–15 mm long; bracts subulate-linear, up to 6 mm long; sub-floral peduncles 4–7 mm long; bracteoles sessile, lanceolate, same colour as the flowers, 10–11 × 5–5.5 mm, glabrous, exterior surface covered with very small sessile glands similar to those of the leaves; pedicel 1–1.5 mm long. Calyx 16 mm long, with nearly equal lobes, linear-lanceolate, rounded, 2–2.5 mm wide, furnished, especially towards the margins, with brownish-yellow transparent stipitate glands. Corolla bright reddish-ochre or deep orange-red (according to Le Testu), glabrous, approximately 2 cm long; corolla tube 16–18 × 3 mm constricted above the base, with 15 parallel nerves; ovate acuminate lobes nearly equal, approximately 3–4 × 2.5 mm; the constricted part of the tube forms an internal ring densely furnished with short hairs; the stamens are inserted at this point; filaments glabrous, 18 mm long; anthers fusiform, 3.5 mm

long; lower parts of connective and base of thecae very slightly puberulous. Ovary glabrous, 2 mm high. Disc 2 mm in diameter and 1 mm high. Style 22–25 mm long, glabrous, with stigma somewhat thickened, subemarginate. Fruit unknown.

## Source note

This new species is very closely related to Whitfieldia colorata C. B. Clarke ex Stapf. (in Johnston, Liberia, 2 : 640, 1906), from Sierra Leone, Liberia and Côte d'Ivoire (see Heine, F.W.T.A., ed. 2, 2 : 399, 1963). It differs nevertheless very clearly by the characters indicated above in the Latin diagnosis. Furthermore, this specific separation of two closely related taxa, distributed west and east of the border between Nigeria and Cameroon, corresponds to a well-known fact of the phytogeography of West Africa. It seems to be of considerable interest to mention here that, except for the very widespread species of Whitfieldia elongata, a species whose rather particular floral characters could characterize a separate subgenus, there apparently exists no species of the genus Whitfieldia whose distribution range overlaps the aforesaid phytogeographic boundary. The distribution of W. lateritia reported from the former Belgian Congo (cf. Th. et H. Dur., Bull. Jard. Bot. Etat Bruxelles 2 : 421, 1910) would moreover require verification.
