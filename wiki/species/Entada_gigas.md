A large liane forming tangled masses sometimes variously disposed over the vault, on several trees at once. Young stem ± sparsely subhirsute to densely shortly pubescent, pale green when fresh, light to dark brown when dry, longitudinally furrowed, shiny to dull when dry. Older branches glabrous, brown when fresh. Stipules glabrous to pubescent, linear, in a direction opposite to the leaves, 3–8 mm long, apex acute, tardily caducous. Petiole pubescent subhirsute to ± pubescent beneath, very pale green with rosebrown tints when fresh, 1.2–3.5 cm long, upper face deeply channelled bordered by 2 longitudinal ridges. Rachis entirely pubescent or ± densely above and glabrous beneath, (3–) 4–10.8 cm long, channelled above, often terminated by a mucro. Tendrils 2 or 0, opposite, twining, glabrous, at the apex of the rachis. Pinnae 2 pairs. Pinna axis with pubescence and morphology like the rachis, 4–8.5 cm long, sometimes terminated by a fleshy mucro, glabrous to ± sparsely appressed-pubescent, apex acute. Leaflets 3–4 pairs. Petiolule pubescent hirsute, 1–1.5 mm long, twisted. Blade weakly coriaceous, glabrous above and glabrous to subglabrous or sparsely pubescent at the base beneath, dark green to bright pale green and shining above and pale green beneath when fresh; black to shiny or dull blackish-brown above and yellow-brown to ochre-yellow beneath when dry; asymmetrically broadly elliptic falcate to broadly elliptic oblanceolate falcate, of decreasing size from apex to base, attaining 6 (–9) × 3 (–4.5) cm; base asymmetric: proximal margin rounded and distal margin distinctly shorter, very attenuate to attenuate-rounded; apex obtusely asymmetrically emarginate, mucronulate on the lower face. Primary vein sometimes almost white above when fresh, arched, ± close to the upper margin of the leaflet, very prominent and pubescent (especially lower half) above, prominent pubescent (especially towards the base) beneath. Secondary veins approximately 9–13 pairs, ascending, rectilinear, prominent on both faces, glabrous or very glabrescent beneath. Tertiary veins in a dense network with main meshes elongate parallel to the secondary veins, prominent on both faces.

Inflorescences in solitary or paired supra-axillary racemes: pale green at base becoming white at apex when fresh, 12–23 cm long. Rachis densely pubescent, furrowed. Bracteole pubescent, linear, 0.75–1 mm long. Floral bud oblongoid fusiform, green when fresh, with stamens with filaments folded upon themselves. Pedicel pubescent hirsute, 1–1.5 mm long. Flowers yellowish-green to white when fresh¹, feebly scented when fresh. Calyx pale green when fresh, obconic to cupular, pubescent hirsute externally, 1–1.25 mm long; lobes free narrowly to broadly triangular, 0.25–0.40 mm long, apex acute. Petals entirely glabrous, pale green when fresh, yellowish with median brown spot when dry, elliptic or weakly lanceolate, 2.5–5 mm long, adnate at base to the stamens, apex acute. Stamens 10, 5–6 mm long, long-exserted, adnate at base for 0.5 mm, filament white becoming yellow when fresh; anther ellipsoid oblong, 0.5 mm long, with a short globose subpedicellate gland. Disk in ± fleshy ridge adnate to the inner face of the stamineal filaments. Pistil with glabrous stipe, 0.50–0.75 mm long; ovary oblongoid glabrous, compressed, 1.25 mm long, with 9–12 ovules; style 3.75 mm long, stigma indistinct.

Lomentum dark dull brown when fresh, ± enrolled in a plane spiral, woody, flattened, 40–120 × 7.5–12 cm, irregular form with constrictions between articles, sutural margin to dorsal keel; venation of valves prominent under the epicarp; mesocarp very densely fibrous. Seeds up to 12, diameter 4–7 cm, hard, ellipsoid to orbicular, subcordate at the hilum, 1–1.6 cm thick. – Pl. 19, Fig. 1–8, p. 75; Pl. 20, Fig. 4, p. 77.

## Discussion

The range of this plant extends over several continents: Central America, Colombia, West Indies, West and Central Africa. It is a liane of forest edges or riverbanks.

This plant is morphologically very close to E. pursaetha. If the difference is easy for flowering or fruiting specimens, the same is not true when they are sterile. The presence of pubescence on young parts and at least on the upper face of the petiole is a very strong indication of belonging to E. gigas. Furthermore the tertiary venation of E. gigas is often more prominent.

This liane, when sterile, can easily be confused with E. gigas; only the glabrescence and less strong venation then allow determination.

The range of this species is very vast: from Senegal to Tanzania towards the East and to South Africa (Natal); from India to China, to the Philippines; to Guam; and to northern Australia. It is a plant of dense humid forest or forest edge. It should be looked for in Gabon.

## Uses

Coarse, long and resistant fibres are extracted, after retting of the inner bark, which are used to make ropes and fishing-line. The decoction of bark is used in hip baths or enemas for newly delivered women and anaemic persons. It would also be a remedy against gonorrhoea. This liane is maintained at the entrance of villages for its protective virtues against misfortunes, dangers and accidents.
