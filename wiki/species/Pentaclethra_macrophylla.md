Tree reaching a height of 25 m. Crown spreading in a dome. Trunk tortuous, short, with a diameter of 40 cm. Bark greyish, not very thick, with orange fracture. Young branches tomentellous to puberulent (stellate hairs). Stipules caducous, very narrowly linear-lanceolate, pubescent (stellate hairs). Petiole 2.5–9 cm long, stellate pubescent, canaliculate above. Rachis stellate pubescent, 10–33 cm long, flattened dorsiventrally, with prominent ridge above. Pinnae 9–16 pairs, oblong to oblong-elliptic. Pinnae axis 4.5–12 cm long, densely pubescent (simple hairs above, stellate hairs below), flattened dorsiventrally, upper surface carinate. Leaflets sessile, 13–20 pairs, glabrous above and ± sparsely stellate pubescent, sometimes glabrous below, dark brownish ± shining on upper surface and brown on lower surface when dry, asymmetric rhomboid, 8–20 × 4–8 mm, asymmetric base: proximal margin auriculate, distal margin attenuate, limb margin ± ciliate, apex obtuse. Primary vein diagonal, prominent on both surfaces and pubescent below. Secondary veins 8–11 pairs, ascending, slightly arched, anastomosing at ± 0.5 mm from the limb margin (basal secondary vein of proximal margin long parallel to limb margin), prominent on both surfaces. Tertiary veins in loose reticulation, prominent on both surfaces.

Inflorescence in a panicle of spikes, reaching 30 cm. Rachis reddish-brown pubescent. Flowers sessile, yellow and with strong odour when fresh. Calyx cupuliform, shortly pubescent externally (± appressed stellate hairs), 1.5–2 mm long, lobes ± ciliolate on margin, semi-orbicular, imbricate. Corolla glabrous externally, 3–3.5 mm long, fused into a tube 1 mm long¹, free lobes 2–2.5 mm long, elliptic, reflexed, with acute apex. Stamens² and staminodes fused to each other and to the corolla tube. Stamens 5, free part of filament 6.5 mm long; anther ellipsoid, medifixed, 0.6 mm long. Staminodes 5, with free filament 3.5 mm long, flattened towards the apex and ± undulate. Pistil with glabrous stipe 0.5 mm long; ovary strongly pubescent at apex, 0.8–1.2 mm long; style slender, reflexed, 3.2–3.5 mm long, with ± lateral stigma.

Legume pendulous, very woody, black, strongly ridged longitudinally, reaching a length of 65 cm and a width of 7–10 cm, with asymmetric oblong-elliptic valves rolling up on themselves at dehiscence, puberulent, with peduncle reaching 16 cm. Seeds ± 8, reddish-brown, shining, often wrinkled, 6–7 × 3.5 cm.

## Type

## Discussion

This plant is found in forest groupings, particularly in those that have been disturbed. Its range is very extensive and extends from Senegal (Casamance) to Angola and Zaïre (Kasai) as well as in the islands of the Gulf of Guinea.

## Uses

The hard wood, usable for heavy work, provides charcoal. The valves of the pods are incinerated and the ash is used in soap manufacture. The seeds, cooked and washed in running water, provide an "odika bread" comparable to that obtained from seeds of Irvingia gabonensis. Among the Fangs, women would reportedly consume a mixture of ground seeds and small brown ants as an abortifacient. Women of the Fernan-Vaz would consume a bark decoction as a galactogenic remedy. In southern Gabon, this decoction was formerly drunk by young warriors to stimulate them in combat. The dried leaves serve as filling for cushions
