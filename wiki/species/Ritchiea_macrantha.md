# *Ritchiea macrantha* Pax & Gilg

**Genus**: [[Ritchiea]]
**Family**: [[Brassicaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 33: 210 (1903); GILG & BENEDICT, Bot. Jahrb. 53: 180 (1915); HUTCH. & DALZ., FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (1): 89 (1927); PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17 b: 169 (1936); KEAY, FWTA, cd. 2, 1 (1): 91 (1954); KERS, FCAM 29: 108-110 (1987)

## Description

HUTCH. & DALZ., FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (1): 89 (1927); PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17 b: 169 (1936); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (1): 91 (1954); KERS, FCAM 29: 108-110 (1987).

Shrub or tree up to 5 m high, evergreen, abundantly branched, glabrous except on the margins of the sepals. Trunk reaching 10 cm in diameter at the base, wood whitish, pith narrow and white. Young branches slender cylindrical or somewhat striated, pale yellowish-green to olive. Older branches greyish-brown, cylindrical; lenticels numerous, grey, rounded or elongate. Axillary buds solitary or 2–3 buds in series, strictly axillary, apical bud dominant, the others ordinarily dormant except near the tip of the shoots.

Leaves 1–3-foliolate, long-petiolate, regularly distributed along the branches, persistent. Leaflets unequally stipitate, dark olive-green glossy above, somewhat paler below, slightly papyraceous and fragile when young, becoming somewhat coriaceous with age; median leaflet symmetric (8–12.5–20 × (3–4–7.5 cm, broadly elliptic or rarely obovate, long-acuminate, base attenuate and gradually narrowed into a petioluliform zone consisting principally of the midvein and approximately 1 cm long. Lateral leaflets asymmetric, somewhat smaller than the median leaflet, 6–13(–14) × 5–6.5 cm, ovate on the outer side, broadly elliptic on the inner side, acuminate; base asymmetric rounded on the outer side, cuneate or attenuate on the inner side; the lateral leaflets are rather abruptly narrowed into a petiolule. Midvein and lateral veins prominent below, depressed above, yellowish-green. Lateral veins 3–4(–5), those at the base dominant more densely distributed in the lower half of the blade, gently curved from the midvein and anastomosing in loops at their tip; venules in loose reticulum, very faintly marked on both faces. Median petiolule (petioluliform part included) 1–1.5(–2) cm long, the lateral ones 2–4(–5) mm. Unifoliolate leaves very rare, sometimes present near the base of shoots, and intermediate forms gradually passing to normal trifoliolate leaves. Petiole slender, (1–3.7–11(–14.8) cm long, cylindric towards the base, weakly canaliculate at the apex of its upper face, green

with a darker base, glossy brownish-green, not decurrent, nor articulate. Stipules very inconspicuous not exceeding 0.75 mm, subulate, yellowish-green, caducous, base not decurrent.

Inflorescence in a short corymbiform raceme, 5–20-flowered, most often terminating a short shoot of the year; this terminal raceme is sometimes accompanied by 1 or 2 subterminal axillary racemes of the leaves at the top of the branch of the previous year. Rachis rigid and erect, 1.5–3 cm long, glabrous or scarcely glandular, pale green, with limited growth, bearing flowers regularly spaced. Floral bracts generally simple, narrow and lanceolate, subsessile to sessile, reaching 1 cm × 1 mm, pale green, glabrous, early caducous; their stipules, at most 0.5 mm long, are subulate or narrow and triangular and become with age pale brownish and curved at the tip; they persist for a long time. Pedicels slender, 1.8–2.5 cm long at anthesis, pale olive-green, glabrous, with section somewhat angular especially towards the enlarged base, dark olive, distinctly articulate. Receptacle vaguely conical reaching 1–2 mm in length for 2–3 mm in diameter just after anthesis, becoming glabrous, papyraceous, dark olive-green glossy with age, apparently of short duration. Sepals 4, subequal, spreading horizontally at anthesis, papyraceous, broad and elliptic to obovate, 20–27 × 6–10 mm, acute to acuminate, glabrous, olive-green with white margin slightly tomentellous, finely pluri-veined from the base, caducous. Petals ca. 20, white, linear very fragile and narrow, 7–8.5 cm × 1.5 mm, glabrous, caducous, all divided (except sometimes a few) in their upper half into 3 or 4 narrow segments, long-acuminate to filiform, apical portion often twisted, with undulate margin. Androgynophore glabrous, exceeding the receptacle by 1–2(–3) mm after anthesis much wider than the gynophore. Stamens ca. 50, all fertile, long-exserted; filaments (3–) 4.5–5.5 cm long, white, very fragile; from the centre of the flower to the outside, the filaments become progressively longer, so that at full bloom, the anthers are situated on the same plane; anthers oblong 2 mm, mucronulate, dorsobasifix, labile. Gynophore slender, 3.5–4 cm long at anthesis, thicker than the filaments, cylindric at the base, vaguely 4-angular towards the apex, white turning bright purple with age. Ovary oblong or narrow and ellipsoid, 5–6 × 1.5 mm, base acute, apex obtuse, transverse section with 4 obtuse angles, glabrous, dark olive-green, finely marked with 4 veins near the ends, unilocular or sometimes partly bilocular at the ends. Ovules numerous on 2 parietal placentas. Stigma sessile, truncate, somewhat wider than the apex of the ovary, dark brown to black, rugose when fresh. Fruit unknown. – Pl. 6, p. 51 & Map E, p. 7.

## Discussion

This species, though well-defined and poorly known, is characterized by very long, narrow, and deeply divided petals, developed and very evident stamens, evenly distributed leaves, leaflets unequally stipitate and relatively few-veined. Sterile specimens of this species can quite easily be confused with those of Ritchiea albersi Gilg, which has never been collected in Gabon. The vegetative characters of R. macrantha resemble those of R. erecta. The median leaflet tapers to a very narrow basal position consisting principally of the midvein. This structure can be called a "pseudopetiolule". It is not easy to distinguish it from a true petiolule without careful observation. The true petiolule is recognized by its darker brownish colour and somewhat rugose surface, whereas the midvein is glossy and paler. The leaflets unequally stipitate also constitute a good diagnostic character for R. erecta and R. capparoides (Group A, subgroup 2).

Ritchiea macrantha is a rare species encountered in a few localities in Nigeria, Cameroon, and Gabon. It possesses the most developed and most spectacular flowers of the entire genus. It is found mainly in localities with stony or rocky soils in rugged forested regions. In Gabon, it was collected in the understory of a forest in the Cristal Mountains around 620 m altitude (De Wilde, Arends & al. 36). Flowering observed in January, July, and September. The fruit of this species is entirely unknown.
