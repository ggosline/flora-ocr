# *Acacia kamerunensis* Gandoger

**Genus**: [[Acacia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Bot. Fr. 60 : 459 (1913) ; BRENAN, Bol. Soc. Brot. 31 : 121 ; FTEA, Legum-Mimos. : 98 (1959) ; LIBERATO, Fl. Guiné Port., Mimosaceae : 25 (1972) ; Fl. S. ## Synonyms

## Description

- A. pennata auct. non (L.) Willd.: Hutch. & DALZ., FWTA, ed. 1: 362 (1928), pro parte majore saltem quoad specim. omnia citata; Exell, Cat. Vasc. Pl. S. Tomé: 170 (1944), pro parte quoad specim. Quintas; Gilbert & Boutique, FCB 3: 155 (1952), tantum quoad specim. dubium Devred 792; Keay, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2): 500 (1958), pro majore parte.

- A. pennata var. dolichosperma auct. non OLIV.: BAK. F., Legum. Trop. Afr. 3: 853 (1930), p.p. quoad syn. A. pentagona.

Woody liane exceeding 5 m. Young axis densely shortly pubescent or puberulent to ± glabrous. Old stem ochre-brown in the living state, light brown to grey-brown when dry; reaching a diameter of 3 cm; ± angular, very finely sparsely pubescent; numerous fine red punctations (glands or secretion products?). Stipules early caducous, glabrous, narrowly triangular, 4.5–5 mm long, margins ± ciliate, apex acute, venation ± prominently parallel. Petiole densely shortly pubescent; prominent gland ± conical, sometimes convex near the base, 1.5–3.5 cm long; channelled above; lateral longitudinal ridges. Rachis pubescent above fairly densely on the dorsal keel, weakly pubescent to subglabrous below with a few prickles, 3–14 cm long; lateral margins with fine wings; 2–12 prominent oval glands on the upper face, at the level of the insertion of the pinnae at the apex, more distinctly intermediate subsequently; apex prolonged below by a very narrowly triangular, pubescent mucro, 1.5–2 mm long, with acute apex. Pinnae 10–29 (–36) pairs. Pinnae axis densely pubescent under the first pair of leaflets, glabrous above except on the margin, 1.5–4.5 cm long, flattened dorsiventrally with lateral hyaline wings, flat above, apex terminated by a triangular mucro reflected laterally with acute apex. Leaflets (11–) 27–60 (–80) pairs, with short whitish petiole when dry; basal leaflets reduced, sometimes stipellate or even one absent. Blade glabrous on both faces; dark green above and paler below when dry; narrowly oblong, reaching 5 × 0.8 mm; base very unequal: proximal margin truncate to weakly cordate and distal margin linear; margins very sparsely ciliate or not; apex obtuse to subacute. Main vein diagonal, closer to the distal margin, plane and paler on both faces. Remainder of venation not visible.

Inflorescence terminal in panicles of capitate spikes. Spike peduncle pubescent. Bracteole ± pubescent externally, obovate to subspatulate, weakly reflexed toward the upper face, 0.5 mm long, apex acute. Floral bud oblong to very weakly obovoid, green with brownish apex in the living state, sessile. Calyx weakly obconic to subcylindric, finely shortly pubescent externally, 1.75–2 mm long; lobes free, lanceolate, 0.25–0.75 mm long, with acute apex. Corolla with petals fused, glabrous externally, 2.5–3 mm long, free lobes lanceolate 0.3–0.7 mm, apex acute. Stamens very numerous, 4–5.5 mm long, fused to each other at the base and to the disk for 0.5–0.75 mm; filament slender; anther broadly oblong, ± 0.15 mm long; connective prolonged by a globose pedicellate gland early caducous. Disk fleshy, fused to the stamen filaments, forming a prominent ridge on the inner face of the staminal tube. Pistil with stipe 1.25–1.5 mm long; ovary hairy ellipsoid-fusiform to weakly obovoid, 0.75–1 mm long, containing 12–16 ovules; style 1.5–3.75 mm long.

Pod papyraceous-chartaceous, dehiscent, finely puberulent, light brown when dry, elliptic to oblong, 8–14 × 1.7–1.8 cm, base attenuate and stipitate; apex attenuate apiculate; sutural margins slightly thickened; transverse venation ± perpendicular to the margin, little or not prominent. Seeds 19, black-brown, ellipsoid, 7–11 × 5–8 mm, central aril small 4–5 × 1.5–2.5 mm. - Pl. 33, Fig. 1–11, p. 125.

## Discussion

This plant has a very vast range extending from Sierra Leone to Uganda as well as in the islands of the Gulf of Guinea. It is a characteristic species of secondary scrub and the edges of paths or rivers in dense humid forest zones.

Species very closely related to the following one; the differences residing in the pubescence of the ovary and the petiole.
