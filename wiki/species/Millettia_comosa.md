# *Millettia comosa* (Micheli) Hauman

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Congo Belge 5 : 30 (1954)

## Description

Lonchocarpus comosus Micheli in T.Durand & De Wild., Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg. 36(2) : 69 (1897). M. gagnepainiana Dunn, J. Bot. 49 : 221 (1911). M. brunneosericea De Wild., Bol. Soc. Brot. 28 : 7 (1920). M. vermoeseni De Wild., Pl. Bequaert. 3 : 356 (1925). M. comosa (Micheli) Hauman var. vermoesenii (De Wild.) Hauman, Fl. Congo Belge 5 : 31 (1954). Liane 4–12 m in height; old stems becoming progressively trigonous then with a 3-winged cable-like structure; branches hirsute, with spreading reddish pubescence, generally composed of short and long hairs in mixture, the latter sometimes deciduous over time. Stipules generally persistent, linear or narrowly triangular, 5–11(–15) × 1–2 mm, hirsute. Leaf: petiole 2–12 cm and rachis 2–11 cm, both hirsute, becoming glabrescent; stipels absent; leaflets 5 to 9(–13), with petiolule 2–4 mm, with limb elliptic to obovate, 3–10 × 1.5–5 cm, rounded at base, acuminate at apex, slightly coriaceous, glabrous on upper face (except the veins shortly pubescent), covered on lower face with short appressed hairs (0.1–0.5 mm) with ± coppery lustre, dense when young, then ± scattered on older leaves, becoming brown or olive green when dry; veins plane or slightly sunken on upper face, the lateral ones in 5 to 10 pairs, very prominent and strongly ascending, joined in arches at ± 1 mm from the margin and scarcely visible, veinlets in a dense network but not prominent and often ± hidden by hairs. Inflorescence terminal or axillary, in simple pseudoracemes or rarely branched, 3–40 cm in length, hirsute; bracts linear, acute, 6–11(–18) × 0.5–1.5 mm, hirsute, exceeding the calyces. Flower: pedicel 2–3 mm; bracteoles inserted at base of calyx, linear, 1–4 × 0.2–0.5 mm, pubescent; calyx hirsute with appressed golden-brown hairs, tube 2–2.5 × 3–5 mm, lobes triangular 0.5–1.5 mm in length; bicoloured corolla, standard purple with pale green base, obovate, 10–15 × 9–10 mm, densely hairy outside, with cuneate or truncate base, with claw 1.5 mm, wings 9 × 1.8–2.5 mm, white, glabrous or with a few hairs towards the base, with claw 3–3.5 mm, keel ± 10 × 3.5–4 mm, white, hairy externally at apex, with claw 3–3.5 mm; stamens monadelphous, ± 12 mm in length, free part of filaments ± 3 mm, curved; ovary 8 mm in length, hairy, with 3 or 4 ovules; style curved, ± 3 mm, glabrous, stigma capitate. Pod narrowly oblanceolate, flattened, 8–14 × 1.4–2 cm, attenuate towards the base, the apex with a curved beak, with 1 to 3 seeds, with scarcely prominent sutures, with very dense velvety brown pubescence. Seed lenticular, 9–12 × 13–14 × 2 mm, brown; hilum central, ± 2.5 mm in length.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of Congo, Democratic Republic of Congo and Angola; common and widely distributed in Gabon, reported in all provinces except Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Secondary forests, forest edges and secondary growth, often abundant along trails; at 20–350 m altitude. Flowers August to November; fruits September to December. Uses: A decoction of the stem as a gargle is used against toothache. The vine cut into strips provides ties that are used for making baskets.

## Source note

The synonymy of M. gagnepainiana Dunn, already mentioned as probable by Hauman (1954a), is confirmed here. M. comosa is very close to M. soyauxii, of which it might be only a variant; their flowers and fruits are identical, and the only reliable criterion for distinguishing them appears to be the pubescence of the leaflets. M. comosa can be confused with certain species of sect. Efulgentes, as its leaves often show a slight metallic sheen on the lower face of the leaflets. It is distinguished from them, however, by the bicoloured corolla and pubescent outside, and by the linear stipules and bracts. b: Hauman (1954a), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). Bibliography
