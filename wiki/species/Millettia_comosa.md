# *Millettia comosa* (Micheli) Hauman

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Congo Belge 5 : 30 (1954)

## Synonyms

- Lonchocarpus comosus Micheli in T.Durand & De Wild., Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg. 36(2) : 69 (1897).

## Description

M. comosa (Micheli) Hauman var. vermoesenii (De Wild.) Hauman, Fl. Congo Belge 5 : 31 (1954). Liana 4–12 m high; older stems becoming progressively trigonous then with 3-winged cable-like structure; branchlets velutinous, with spreading russet pubescence, generally composed of short and long hairs in mixture, the latter sometimes caducous in time. Stipules generally persistent, linear or narrowly triangular, 5–11(–15) × 1–2 mm, velutinous. Leaf: petiole 2–12 cm and rhachis 2–11 cm, both velutinous, becoming glabrescent; stipels absent; leaflets 5 to 9(–13), with petiolule 2–4 mm, with limb elliptic to obovate, 3–10 × 1.5–5 cm, rounded at base, acuminate at apex, slightly coriaceous, glabrous on upper surface (except nervures shortly pubescent), covered on lower surface with short appressed hairs (0.1–0.5 mm) with ± coppery sheen, dense when young, then ± scattered on older leaves, becoming brown or olive-green when dry; nervures plane or slightly channelled on upper surface, the lateral ones in 5 to 10 pairs, very prominent and strongly ascending, joined in arcs at ± 1 mm from margin and scarcely visible, nervilles in dense network, but scarcely prominent and often ± hidden by hairs. Inflorescence terminal or axillary, in simple pseudoracemes or rarely branched, 3–40 cm long, velutinous; bracts linear, acute, 6–11(–18) × 0.5–1.5 mm, velutinous, exceeding the calyces. Flower: pedicel 2–3 mm; bracteoles inserted at base of calyx, linear, 1–4 × 0.2–0.5 mm, pubescent; calyx velutinous with appressed golden-brown hairs, tube 2–2.5 × 3–5 mm, lobes triangular 0.5–1.5 mm long; corolla bicolored, standard purple with pale green base, obovate, 10–15 × 9–10 mm, densely hairy externally, with cuneiform or truncate base, with claw 1.5 mm, wings 9 × 1.8–2.5 mm, white, glabrous or with a few hairs toward base, with claw 3–3.5 mm, keel ± 10 × 3.5–4 mm, white, externally hairy at apex, with claw 3–3.5 mm; stamens monadelphous, ± 12 mm long, free part of filaments ± 3 mm, curved; ovary 8 mm long, hairy, with 3 or 4 ovules; style curved, ± 3 mm, glabrous, stigma capitate. Pod narrowly oblanceolate, flattened, 8–14 × 1.4–2 cm, attenuate toward base, apex with a curved beak, with 1 to 3 seeds, with scarcely prominent sutures, with very dense velvety brown pubescence. Seed lenticular, 9–12 × 13–14 × 2 mm, brown; hilum central, ± 2.5 mm long.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; common and widely distributed in Gabon, recorded in all provinces except Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Secondary forests, forest margins and regrowth, often abundant along trails; at 20–350 m altitude. Flowers August to November; fruits September to December. Uses: A decoction of the stem as a gargle is used against toothache. The liana cut into strips provides ties that serve for making baskets

## Source note

The synonymy of M. gagnepainiana Dunn, already mentioned as probable by Hauman (1954a), is confirmed here. M. comosa is very close to M. soyauxii, of which it might be only a variant; their flowers and fruits are identical, and the only reliable criterion for distinguishing them seems to be the pubescence of the leaflets. M. comosa may be confused with certain species of sect. Efulgentes, as its leaves often show a slight metallic sheen on the lower surface of the leaflets. It is distinguished from them, however, by the bicolored corolla and pubescent externally, and by the linear stipules and bracts. b: Hauman (1954a), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). Bibliography
