# *Ceratophyllum demersum* L.

**Family**: [[Ceratophyllaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Ceratophyllum]]

*Species plantarum* **2**: 992 (1753).

## Description

Aquatic herb to about 3 m long. Main stem to 2 mm in diameter, robust and rigid, rarely delicate, finely longitudinally furrowed. Leaves 7–11 per whorl; blade once or twice, rarely three times, dichotomous, 0.8–4 cm long and 0.2–0.7 mm wide (one or two cells), olive-green to light green; lower segments sometimes to 1 mm thick; terminal and often lower segments with numerous, rarely few, marginal teeth (0.1–)0.2–0.5 mm long.

Male inflorescences with 1–3 flowers per node, often several per branch; female inflorescences with one flower per node and few flowers. Male flower variable, to 2.5(–3.5) mm in diameter; perianth broadly cup-shaped, lobes 0.5–1.3 × 0.2–0.4 mm with a glandular projection to 0.2 mm; stamens to about 30; anther subsessile, 1–2 × 0.4–1.5 mm at maturity; pistillode about 0.6 mm. Female perianth surrounding the ovary and persistent in fruit, its lobes like those of the male flower but with glandular projections to 0.7 mm; ovary to 1 × 0.6 mm, smooth; style generally over 2 mm.

Fruit ovoid or ellipsoid, rarely obovoid, not or only weakly laterally flattened; surface smooth with scattered to numerous dark spots; apical spine (1.5–)3.5–9 mm long; usually two prominent basal spines (1–)2–6(–10) mm long, sometimes reduced to projections about 0.5 mm long.

## Distribution

Cosmopolitan and known throughout Africa. In Gabon it is recorded from Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Nyanga and is very probably present elsewhere.

## Habitat and ecology

Fresh standing or fast-flowing water, near the surface or at depth, in lakes, rivers and marshes. It tolerates highly saline estuarine conditions but has not been found in seasonal alkaline pools. It is often associated with *Azolla*, *Pistia* and *Nymphaea* and may be locally abundant. Recorded at 60–500 m in Gabon and 3–1600 m elsewhere.

## Notes

Only var. *demersum* is recorded in Gabon. The treatment also keys var. *apiculatum* and var. *inerme*, which could occur because few Gabonese specimens are available and both are widespread elsewhere. The varieties are separated by basal fruit spines or projections, surface spotting and lateral flattening; unlike *C. submersum*, the fruit is never warty.
