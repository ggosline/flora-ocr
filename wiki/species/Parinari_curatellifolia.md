Shrub or savanna tree reaching 25(-40) cm in diameter and 15(-20) m in total height, with tortuous trunk and branches. Bark blackish, rough, divided into small square or rectangular scales, with hard, thick edge, granulate, dark red, lighter towards the interior; wood of variable reddish coloration, hard and heavy. Young twigs, petioles and base of the midvein above, tomentulose pubescent, rusty. Petiole 6-8(-10) mm bearing, most often, 2 small distant glands, circular, with plane surface, ± visible among the pubescence of the petiole, on its proximal half, sometimes towards its middle. Blade coriaceous, elliptic (4-5 × 7-10 cm) to elliptic-oblong (5 × 12 cm), sometimes broadly elliptic especially on shoots (8-9 × 10-15 cm); base broadly acute to obtuse, rarely rounded and subcordate (especially on shoots), margin frequently undulate and furnished below with small hydathodes at the level of the sinuses, apex rounded or somewhat emarginate, 15-20 pairs of lateral veins; upper surface often rough, lower surface at first grey or whitish woolly tomentulose then densely woolly tomentulose only in the crypts between the veins and veinlets tomentulose to sparsely pubescent.

Inflorescences in terminal pyramidal panicles, reaching 20 cm in height, covered with dense tomentulose pubescence, greyish silvery, to the tip of the sepals. Bracts and bracteoles brown and glabrous within, ovate lanceolate, caducous. Receptacle 3-4 mm high; sepals rather long triangular 2.5(-3.5) mm high; petals white tinged with pink, subspatulate, 2-3.5 mm high; 7-8 fertile stamens.

Drupes yellow then brownish with small golden suberified warts, irregularly subglobose or ellipsoid, 20 × 25 × 35 mm. — PL. 38, p. 129.

## Discussion

The taxon present in Cameroon would correspond to the subspecies curatellifolia (of the northern boreal dry savannas and forests) of certain authors, contrasted with subspecies mobola (Oliv.) Graham (of the southern boreal dry savannas and forests); this distinction, based on indumentum consistency and color as well as on the size of the flowers and sepals, cannot be accepted, given the variations observed throughout the range of this species. It approaches Parinari capensis Harv., a rhizomatous suffrutex of southern and eastern Africa which reaches, towards the north, only Congo, Zaïre and Tanzania.

Parinari curatellifolia Pl. ex Benth., by its leaf with rounded or slightly emarginate apex, differs from P. excelsa Sabine, a large tree of dense humid forest with acuminate leaf, and from P. congensis Didr., a small tree of riverbank with broadly acute leaf.

Species widespread in all the sudanozambezian dry savannas and forests of Africa south of the Sahara, from Senegal to Sudan and Transvaal, sometimes forming small stands; it is found here and there in the coastal savannas of western and eastern Africa, as well as in Madagascar (Parinari chapelieri Baill.).

In Cameroon, as elsewhere in western Africa, it is confined to the medio-sudanian sector (dry forests with Isoberlinia, Monotes, Uapaca...), north of the northern scarp of the Adamaoua, including to the west, along the northern border of the former western Cameroon, and to the east, in the Mbéré valley; it has been found, further south in the sudano-guinean sector (savannas with Lophira, Daniella...), only northeast of Bétaré Oya; towards the north it ventures into the sudano-sahelian sector of the Garoua region but still exists on the hills of Tinguelin, and on the Mandara Mountains, with medio-sudanian flora.

## Uses

In western Africa: roots with magical properties and (Zaïre) for toothache; barks and leaves for treatment of fractures, febrifuge barks, twigs to chew, leaves to chew (cf. cola) and for red dye

Species especially known for the pulp of its fruit, very tasty, fermentable, and for its edible seed, oleaginous, capable of providing a drying oil.
