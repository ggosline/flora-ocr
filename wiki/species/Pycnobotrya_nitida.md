# *Pycnobotrya nitida* Benth. Planche 96 ; Figure 9(I, J)

**Genus**: [[Pycnobotrya]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hooker’s. Icon. Pl. 12 : t. 1183 (1876)

## Description

Vine reaching 40 m in height; young branches with rusty pubescence, glabrescent. Leaf: petiole 3–7 mm, glabrous above, below with short recurved hairs; blade oblanceolate to elliptic, 9–15(–17) × 4–7(–8) cm, cuneate at base, acuminate with an obtuse apiculate point, or obtuse at apex, coriaceous, dark green, glossy, with midvein paler or whitish and glabrous above, below pale green, glabrous or pubescent especially on the midvein with short crisp hairs, and scattered with numerous black glands; secondary veins numerous, straight, parallel, scarcely visible. Inflorescence with first ramifications loose, the others condensed, 15–25(–30) cm, with numerous flowers with rusty tomentum; peduncle 1–5 cm; bracts with very small axillary glands. Flower fragrant; pedicel 1–3 mm; calyx with lobes 0.75–1.3 × 0.45–1 mm, exterior pubescent to tomentose, interior glabrous, ciliate; corolla pinkish, 5–7(–9) mm long, with campanulate tube, 1.4–2 mm long, with oblong lobes, 3.1–5.2(–7.2) × 1.5–2.5 mm, pubescent with glandular hairs on both faces; stamens inserted at the summit of the narrow basal part of the tube; filaments glabrous; anther narrowly triangular, 1–1.2 mm long; pistil 0.6–0.9 mm long. Fruit with follicles forming an angle of 180°, obliquely elliptic, compressed laterally, 4.5–7 × 3–4.5 × 0.5–1.5 cm, exterior green to dark brown at maturity. Seed 4–6 × 2.5–3.2 × 0.1 cm. Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Central African Republic; in Gabon noted in all provinces. Ecology: forests, often on riverbanks in Gabon, and elsewhere up to 700 m altitude. Uses: The leaves are consumed either as vegetables or as medicinal treatment. The fibre obtained from the bark is used in the manufacture of rope. B: Beentje (2021), van der Ploeg (1983), de Ruijter (2008). BIBLIOGRAPHY
