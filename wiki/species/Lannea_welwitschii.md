# *Lannea welwitschii* (Hiern) Engl.

**Genus**: [[Lannea]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot Jahrb. Syst. 24 : 498 (1898)

## Description

, syn. nov. L. zenkeri Engl. & Krause, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 46 : 330 (1911), syn. nov. Tree, reaching at least 35 m in height; trunk up to 1(–1.2) m in diameter; young branches with reddish-brown pubescence of stellate hairs, becoming glabrous rather rapidly. Leaf: petiole cylindrical, (6–)7–9(–11) cm, glabrous to glabrescent; rachis cylindrical, 5–8(–15) cm; leaflets 3 to 7(–9), opposite or subopposite, petiolule of lateral ones 2–8(–12) mm, of the terminal one 2–4.5 cm, with oval-elliptic blade, (8–)10–17 × (2.5–)4–8 cm, cuneate to rounded and often asymmetric at the base, acuminate for 1–2.5 cm at the apex, glabrous, with (7–)9 to 12(–13) pairs of lateral veins. Inflorescence terminal to subterminal, with reddish-brown pubescence of stellate hairs, becoming glabrous; male inflorescence as an ample panicle, up to 20 × 8 cm, the female as a narrow panicle, ± 13 cm in length. Male flowers in glomerules of 2 to 5; pedicel 2–7 mm, articulate at the apex; sepals ± free, oval, 0.7 mm in length, ± glabrous; petals spreading to reflexed, oblong-elliptic, 2–2.5 × 1.3 mm, glabrous; stamens 2.5–3 mm in length, glabrous, anthers ± 0.8 mm in length; pistillode distinct, 1 mm in length, glabrous. Female flowers grouped by 1 to 3; pedicel 1–2 mm; sepals slightly united at the base, oval, 0.6–1 mm in length, glabrous or with a few stellate hairs; petals obovate-elliptic, 1.5–2 × 0.8 mm, glabrous; staminodes 1–1.5 mm in length; ovary 1.5–2 mm in length, glabrous; stigmas 4, subsessile. Fruit obovoid-ellipsoidal, laterally compressed, sometimes ± reniform, (7–)9–10 × 5–7 mm, glabrous.

## Distribution

Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, São Tomé, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia, Uganda and Angola; in Gabon, known from all provinces

## Ecology

Primary and secondarized forest, often in swampy zones of the forest, sometimes more abundant in semi-deciduous forest; in Gabon at 0–620 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1100(–1250) m. Use: The wood is used for light carpentry, utensils, as well as for veneers and plywood, and it also serves as firewood and for charcoal production. The trunks are traditionally used to make canoes. The bark yields an orange-yellow to reddish-brown dye and is also used for making ropes and sandals. It is commonly used in traditional medicine.

## Source note

The variety ciliolata Engl. has been distinguished (synonym: Lannea amaniensis Engl. & K.Krause), whose taxonomic status is uncertain. The form is endemic to Kenya and Tanzania where it is present in lowland forest. It differs from L. welwitschii s.s. by its smaller leaflets with the underside slightly pubescent.
