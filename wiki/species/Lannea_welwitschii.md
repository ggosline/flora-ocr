# *Lannea welwitschii* (Hiern) Engl.

**Genus**: [[Lannea]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot Jahrb. Syst. 24 : 498 (1898)

## Synonyms

- Calesiam welwitschii Hiern, Cat. afr. pl. 1 : 179 (1896).

## Description

, syn. nov. L. zenkeri Engl. & Krause, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 46 : 330 (1911), syn. nov. Tree reaching at least 35 m in height; trunk up to 1(–1.2) m in diameter; young branchlets with reddish-brown pubescence of stellate hairs, becoming glabrous fairly rapidly. Leaf: petiole cylindrical, (6–)7–9(–11) cm, glabrous to glabrescent; rachis cylindrical, 5–8(–15) cm; leaflets 3 to 7(–9), opposite or subopposite, petiolule of lateral ones 2–8(–12) mm, of the terminal one 2–4.5 cm, with ovate-elliptic limb, (8–)10–17 × (2.5–)4–8 cm, cuneate to rounded and often asymmetric at the base, acuminate over 1–2.5 cm at the apex, glabrous, with (7–)9 to 12(–13) pairs of lateral veins. Inflorescence terminal to subterminal, with reddish-brown pubescence of stellate hairs, glabrescent; male inflorescence in ample panicle, up to 20 × 8 cm, the female one in narrow panicle, ± 13 cm in length. Male flowers in glomérules of 2 to 5; pedicel 2–7 mm, articulate at the apex; sepals ± free, ovate, 0.7 mm in length, ± glabrous; petals spreading to reflexed, oblong-elliptic, 2–2.5 × 1.3 mm, glabrous; stamens 2.5–3 mm in length, glabrous, anthers ± 0.8 mm in length; pistillode distinct, 1 mm in length, glabrous. Female flowers grouped by 1 to 3; pedicel 1–2 mm; sepals slightly united at the base, ovate, 0.6–1 mm in length, glabrous or with a few stellate hairs; petals obovate-elliptic, 1.5–2 × 0.8 mm, glabrous; staminodes 1–1.5 mm in length; ovary 1.5–2 mm in length, glabrous; stigmas 4, subsessile. Fruit obovoid-ellipsoidal, laterally compressed, sometimes ± reniform, (7–)9–10 × 5–7 mm, glabrous.

## Distribution

Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, São Tomé, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia, Uganda and Angola; in Gabon, known from all provinces

## Ecology

primary and secondarized forest, often in swampy zones of the forest, sometimes more abundant in semi-deciduous forest; in Gabon at 0–620 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1100(–1250) m. Uses: The wood is used for light carpentry, utensils, as well as for veneers and plywood, and also serves as firewood and for charcoal production. The logs traditionally serve to make pirogues. The bark yields an orange-yellow to reddish-brown dye and also serves to make ropes and sandals. It is commonly used in traditional medicine. The seeds serve as a purgative. The fruits are consumed fresh locally. The tree is sometimes planted as a shade tree in coffee and cacao plantations, in hedgerows, and as a street tree. The young leaves serve as fodder.

## Source note

The variety ciliolata Engl. (synonym: Lannea amaniensis Engl. & K.Krause) has been distinguished, whose taxonomic status is uncertain. The form is endemic to Kenya and Tanzania where it is present in low-altitude forest. It differs from L. welwitschii s.s. by its smaller leaflets whose underside is slightly pubescent.
