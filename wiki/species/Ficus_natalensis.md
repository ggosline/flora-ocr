## Discussion

- F. durbanii Warb., l.c.: 142 (1906); Hutch., l.c.: 208 (1917). LECTOTYPE: Rehmann 9009, South Africa, Natal, Durban (Z.; iso-, B).

- F. variabilis De Wild. fa. obtusifolia De Wild., Ann. Soc. Sci. Brux. 40: 282 (1921).

- F. scutata Lebrun, Mém. Inst. Roy. Colon. Belg., sect. Sci. Nat. et Méd., collect. in-8°, 3 (1): 68, tab. 4, fig. b (1934), nom et statut nouveaux pour F. variabilis fa. obtusifolia Lebrun & Boutique, l.c.: 152 (1948).

Shrubs or small epiphytic trees (stranglers and/or lianas), sometimes large trees. Leafy branchlets 2–5 mm thick, glabrous or sparsely puberulent, glabrescent, yellowish-brown, reddish-brown, or greyish-brown when dry. Leaves most often distichous, often nearly opposite, sometimes ± irregularly arranged. Blade elliptic to obovate to obtriangular (to nearly rhomboid), sometimes oblong or subobovate, coriaceous (to subcoriaceous), apex obtuse to acute (to obscurely acuminate) or truncate to emarginate, base acute to obtuse, margin entire; both faces glabrous, veins (nearly) plane above, prominent below, midvein usually not reaching the blade apex, 5–9 pairs of lateral veins, without distinct basal veins; glandular spot normally visible. Petiole 0.5–3 cm long, 2 mm thick, indumentum similar to that of the leafy branchlet. Stipules free, 0.2–0.8 cm long, glabrous or puberulent white to yellowish to pubescent, caducous.

Figs in pairs or solitary in the axils or just below the leaves, sometimes on already leafless branchlets; peduncle 0.2–1 cm long, 1 mm thick, glabrous to sparsely puberulent; basal bracts (probably) 2, caducous except for the basal parts fused, forming a rim approximately 0.1 mm wide, glabrous to puberulent; receptacle globose, 0.5–1.5 cm in diameter on fresh material, 0.5–1 cm when dry, glabrous, yellowish (with reddish spots) at maturity, pinkish, reddish, orange or reddish-brown to orange-brown, wrinkled, pale brown yellowish, often with darker spots when dry, ostiole prominent or plane, bilabiate.

Pistillate flowers with 3 tepals 1–1.6 mm long, united at the base; seed flowers sessile, style 1.5–2 mm long; gall flowers subsessile, pedicellate up to 1 mm, style 0.5–1 mm long. Fruits ellipsoid, approximately 1.3 mm long; gall fruits subglobose, approximately 1 mm long; fruits ± distinctly bicoloured. Staminate flowers few, subsessile or pedicellate up to 1 mm, perianth with 2 or 3 tepals, 1–1.5 mm long, united at the base, filament thick, approximately 0.8–1 mm long, anther approximately 0.5 mm long. Interfloralbracts 1–1.5 mm long. – Pl. 49, p. 175.

Pollinators: Alfonsiella longiscapa Joseph (Nigeria, Uganda) see also F. thonningii; Elisabethiella sp. (Cameroon, Gabon, Kenya).

This species ranges from Senegal across Cameroon and Gabon to Sudan and southward to South Africa; often along rivers in periodically flooded or swampy forests (gallery forests) and in areas of dense forest and savanna; up to an altitude of 1200 m.

F. natalensis can be subdivided into 3 subspecies: subsp. natalensis in the eastern part of its range, subsp. leprieurii (unpublished) in the western part to Central Africa, subsp. chrysocerasus (unpublished) from NW Zambia and N Angola to Cameroon. Subsp. leprieurii has small figs with caducous basal bracts and generally broadly obovate to obtriangular leaves, the midvein apex being far from the rounded to truncate apex; the stipules are (nearly) glabrous. Subsp. chrysocerasus has larger figs, leaves often narrower with a subacuminate to rounded apex, the midvein apex reaching almost the blade apex; the stipules are often densely hairy.
