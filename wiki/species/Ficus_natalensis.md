## Discussion

- F. durbanii WARB., l.c.: 142 (1906); HUTCH., l.c.: 208 (1917). LECTOTYPE: Rehmann 9009, Afrique du Sud, Natal, Durban (Z.; iso-, B).

- F. variabilis DE WILD. fa. obtusifolia DE WILD., Ann. Soc. Sci. Brux. 40 : 282 (1921).

- F. scutata LEBRUN, Mém. Inst. Roy. Colon. Belg., sect. Sci. Nat. et Méd., collect. in-8°, 3 (1) : 68, tab. 4, fig. b (1934), nom et statut nouveaux pour F. variabilis fa. obtusifolia LEBRUN & BOUTIQUE, l.c. : 152 (1948).

Shrubs or small trees, epiphytic (stranglers or) (and) scandent, sometimes large trees. Leafy branches 2-5 mm thick, glabrous or sparsely puberulent, glabrescing, yellowish-brown, reddish-brown, or greyish-brown when dry. Leaves distichous most often, often nearly opposite, sometimes ± specially distributed. Blade elliptic to obovate to obtriangular (to nearly rhomboid), sometimes oblong or subbovate, coriaceous (to subcoriaceous), apex obtuse to acute (to obscurely acuminate) or truncate to emarginate, base acute to obtuse, margin entire; both surfaces glabrous, veins (nearly) plane above, prominent below, midrib not usually reaching the apex of the blade, 5-9 pairs of lateral veins, without distinct basal veins; glandular spot normally visible. Petiole 0.5-3 cm long, 2 mm thick, indumentum similar to that of the leafy branch. Stipules free, 0.2-0.8 cm long, glabrous or puberulent white to yellowish to pubescent, caducous.

Figs in pairs or solitary in the axils or just below the leaves, sometimes on already defoliated branches; peduncle 0.2-1 cm long, 1 mm thick, glabrous to sparsely puberulent; basal bracts (probably) 2, caducous except for the fused basal parts, forming a ridge approximately 0.1 mm broad, glabrous to puberulent; receptacle globose, 0.5-1.5 cm in diameter fresh, 0.5-1 cm dry, glabrous, yellowish (with reddish spots) at maturity, pinkish, reddish, orange or reddish-brown to orange-brown, wrinkled, light brown yellowish, often with darker spots when dry, ostiole prominent or plane, bilabiate.

Pistillate flowers with 3 tepals 1-1.6 mm long, basally connate; seed flowers sessile, style 1.5-2 mm long; gall flowers subsessile, pedicellate to 1 mm, style 0.5-1 mm long. Fruits ellipsoid, approximately 1.3 mm long; gall fruits subglobose, approximately 1 mm long; fruits ± distinctly bicolored. Staminate flowers few, subsessile or pedicellate to 1 mm, perianth with 2 or 3 tepals, 1-1.5 mm long, basally connate, filament thick, approximately 0.8-1 mm long, anther approximately 0.5 mm long. Interflorals bracts 1-1.5 mm long. - Pl. 49, p. 175.

Pollinators: Alfonsiella longiscapa Joseph (Nigeria, Uganda) see also F. thonningii; Elisabethiella sp. (Cameroun, Gabon, Kenya).

This species extends from Sénégal across Cameroun and Gabon to Sudan and southward to Afrique du Sud; often along rivers in periodically flooded or swampy gallery forests and in areas of dense forest and savanna; up to an altitude of 1200 m.

F. natalensis can be subdivided into 3 subspecies: subsp. natalensis in the eastern part of its distribution range, subsp. leprieurii (unpublished) in the western part to Centrafrique, subsp. chrysocerasus (unpublished) from NW Zambia and N Angola to Cameroun. Subsp. leprieurii has small figs with caducous basal bracts and generally broadly obovate to obtriangular leaves, the midrib apex far from the rounded to truncate apex; stipules (nearly) glabrous. Subsp. chrysocerasus has larger figs, leaves often narrower with a subacuminate to rounded apex, the midrib apex reaching almost to the apex of the blade; stipules often densely hairy.
