# *Murdannia simplex* (Vahl) Brenan Planche 9, Figure 3(E)

**Genus**: [[Murdannia]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 7(2) : 186 (1952)

## Synonyms

- Commelina simplex Vahl, Enum. pl. 2 : 177 (1805).

## Description

Herb erect or ascending, up to 120 cm tall; stems in tufts, unbranched or little-branched, 2–5 mm in diameter, base sometimes bulbous; roots hard, not tuberous. Leaf: sheath 4 cm long, pubescent to glabrescent, ciliate at apex; basal blades narrowly elliptic-ovate to linear, 7–40 × 0.4–1.7 cm, the cauline ones shorter, often conduplicate, strongly narrowing toward the apex of the plant, the apical ones bracteolar, faces glabrous to densely pubescent. Inflorescence composed of a lax terminal thyrse and one or several subterminal thyrses in the axil of the upper leaves; terminal inflorescence with (1–)2 to 6 subequal branches, 3–10 cm long, each bearing 1 or more cincinni, reaching 3.5 cm long, each with 5 to 35 unilateral bisexual flowers

Flower up to 16 mm in diameter; pedicel 4.5–8 mm, erect during fruiting; sepals narrowly elliptic-ovate to elliptic-ovate, 4.5–7 × 2–3.5 mm, green, glabrous, translucent; petals spreading to reflexed, broadly ovate-orbicular to ovate-elliptic, 5.5–11 × 4.5–8 mm, blue, purple, lilac to pink or mauve; fertile stamens 2, filament curved to one side (in bisexual flowers) or divergent (in male flowers), 5–7 mm, with long spreading flexuous hairs, violet or purple, anther 1–1.3 mm long, dark blue, with white pollen; staminodes 4, 3 opposite the petals and one opposite the lower sepal, antheroде with 3 subglobose lobes, pale yellow, or sometimes absent; ovary 1.5–2.5 mm long; style filiform, curved to one side, 4–6 mm, stigma punctiform. Fruit oblong, 4–5(–7.5) × 2.5–4 mm, with apiculate apex, glabrous, opening into 3 valves; locules with 2(–3) uniseriate seeds. Seed transversely ovate-elliptic to rectangular, often flattened on one side, (1.1–)1.4–1.8 × 1.2–1.7 mm; testa covered with a dull, brown layer, scrobiculate to alveolate, generally provided with small white knobs arranged radially between the depressions.

## Distribution

from Senegal to Ethiopia, into southern Africa, Madagascar and also in Asia; in Gabon in Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

coastal savannas and other wet sandy places; in Gabon up to 500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1700 m. Flowering from mid-afternoon until 6 o'clock in the evening. B: Brenan (1952, 1968b), Brunel et al. (1984), van der Burg (2006), Cabezas et al. (2014), Bibliography Faden (2012), Vandeweghe et al. (2016)
