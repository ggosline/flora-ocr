# *Liparis nervosa* (Thunb.) Lindl.

**Genus**: [[Liparis]]
**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gen Sp. Orchid. Pl.: 26 (1830); FAC 1: 274 (1984); FCAM 2: 356 (2001). - BASIONYME : Ophrys nervosa Thunb., Fl. Jap.: 27 (1784). - TYPE: Thunberg s.n., Japon, holo- G

## Description

PL. 59. – Liparis epiphytica Schltr.: A, general view; B, pseudobulb with leaves; C, flower; D, E, labellum; F, dorsal sepal; G, petal; H, lateral sepal (A, ex SCHLECHTER 1905, modified; B-D, Le Testu 5766, P; E-H, Schlechter 12694, P).

– Cymbidium bituberculatum Hook., Exot. Fl. 2: 116 (1824).

– Liparis bituberculata (Hook.) Lindl., Bot. Reg. 11: sub. t. 882 (1825).

– Liparis elata Lindl., Bot. Reg. 14: t. 1175 (1828).

– Liparis guineensis Lindl., Bot. Reg. 20: tab 1671 (1834).

– Liparis rufina Rchb.f. ex Rolfe, FTA 7: 19 (1897).

– Liparis nyassana Schltr., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 53: 560 (1915).

Pseudobulbs 2-3.2 × 1.3 cm, conical-cylindric to fusiform. Leaves petiolate, < 35 × 7.5 cm, lanceolate to oblong-elliptic, acute or subobtuse. Inflorescence < 70 cm, 10-40-flowered. Flowers small, resupinate, green, greenish-yellow or yellow, tinged or not with brown or purple. Dorsal sepal obtuse with revolute margins, oblong-lanceolate to oblong; lateral sepals obliquely elliptic-ovate to elliptic-oblong. Petals linear to linear-oblanceolate, obtuse, margins revolute. Labellum broadly obovate to obcordate, fleshy, fairly thick, apical margin crenate to almost entire, basal auricles short to prominent. – Pl. 61, p. 127.

Pantropical species of very wide ecogeo-graphic range:

## Distribution

all countries of the Atlantic coast from Senegal to Angola, São Tomé, Annobón, Central African Republic, Congo (Kinshasa), Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia, Malawi, Zimbabwe (and also in South and Southeast Asia to the Philippines and Japan as well as the Caribbean and South America)

## Ecology

alt. 80-1800 m. Herbaceous terrestrial plant in swampy prairie, periodically flooded and swept by fires, marsh, forest with Chrysobalanus icaco and Syzygium guineense dominant, mangrove border, lowland rainforest, secondary forest, savanna grazed with Daniellia and Lophira, overgrazed savanna, sometimes on pure sand, lithophyte on rocks of riverbed banks, epiphyte at 2.5 m from the ground on oil palm trunk, in full sun and in shade
