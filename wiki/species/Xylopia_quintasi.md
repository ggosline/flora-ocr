## Description

Engler, Monogr. Afr. Pflanzenfam. 6: 62 (1901). — Exell, Journ. of Bot. 70, Suppl. Polypet: 212 (1932). — Pellegrin, Bull. Soc. Bot. Fr., Mém. 31: 70 (1949), pro parte. — Exell et Mendonga, Consp. Fl. Angol. 1: 18 (1937). — Boutique, Fl. Congo Belge 2: 337 (1951). — Keay, F.W.T.A., ed. 2, 1: 42 (1954). — Tisserant et Sillans, Not. Syst. 15: 344 (1958). — Aubréville, F.F.C.I., ed. 2, 1: 136 (1960). — Walker et Sillans, Enc. Biol. 56: 73 (1961). — Keay, Onochie et Stanfield, Nigerian trees, 1: 37 (1960). — Paiva, Mem. Soc. Brot. 19: 68 (1966).

Tree 10–35 m in height, reaching 40–50 cm in diameter, with straight and slender bole; rhytidome reddish-brown, fissured longitudinally, exfoliating in rectangular plates; bark thin, fibrous, rose-orange, fragrant; wood very hard, heavy, orange. Branches reddish-brown, glabrous, zigzag, forming a compact crown at the apex; branchlets sparsely pubescent to glabrous. Young leaves rose-coloured. Leaves with petiole 5–6 mm long, puberulent to glabrous; blade subcoriaceous, obovate to obovate-oblong, 5.5–10 cm long, 2.5–5 cm wide, obtuse, slightly emarginate or shortly and obliquely acuminate at the apex; upper surface lustrous and glabrous, lower surface puberulent to glabrous. Lateral veins 7–8 pairs, prominent on both faces; reticulation ± prominent, visible on both faces.

Flowers yellowish-white, very fragrant, axillary, solitary, geminate, fasciculate or in small 3–11-flowered cymes; pedicel 3–8 mm long, sparsely reddish pubescent to puberulent. Bracteoles 2–3, broadly ovate, semi-amplexicaul, rounded at the apex, pubescent on the outside, 1–1.5 mm long. Flower buds linear-oblong, obtuse at the apex. Sepals broadly triangular-ovate, 2.5–3 mm in length and width, pubescent on the outside, glabrous on the inside. External petals linear-oblong, obtuse at the apex, slightly widened at the base, 10–20 mm long, 2.5–3 mm wide, tomentellous on the outside, puberulent on the inside; internal petals somewhat shorter and narrower, linear-cylindric, obtuse at the apex, widened at the base, rectangular, concave, equal to about one-quarter of the petal, bearing two blackish longitudinal membranes on the internal sides, puberulent on both faces. One row of external staminodes oblanceolate. Stamens linear, about 2 mm long, inserted on the receptacle all ± at the same level as the carpels, filaments short, connectives triangular, dilated above the anthers. Carpels 2–4, 2.5 mm long, scarcely exceeding the stamens, ovaries ovoid, silky-hairy, styles shortly cylindric, blackish, 0.5–0.7 mm long, puberulent at the apex. 4 uniseriate ovules.

Fruits with pedicel about 1 cm long; mericarps cylindric, moniliform, 3–5 cm long, 0.6–0.8 cm wide, constricted between the seeds, longitudinally striate, glabrous; stipes 0.5–1 cm long. Seeds with longitudinal axis ± oblique, ellipsoid, 1–1.5 cm long, 0.7–0.8 cm wide; aril membranous, laciniate, covering almost the entire seed.

Pl. 27. — Xylopia Quintasii Engl. et Diels: 1, floriferous branch × 2/3; 2, flower, petals removed × 6; 3, 4, external and internal petals × 4; 5, staminode × 8; 6, stamen × 8; 7, carpel and section × 8 (Le Testu 9438); 8, fruits × 2/3 (SRFK 1913); 9, seed nat. size (Heitz 3). — Xylopia aurantiiodora De Wild. et Th. Dur.: 10, floriferous branch × 2/3; 11, flower, three petals and one sepal removed × 3; 12, internal petal × 4; 13, carpel × 8 (Letouzey 1374).

The reduced number of carpels, the styles scarcely exceeding the stamens, the internal petals bearing two membranes at the base, and the seed surrounded by a laciniate aril are characteristics found only in Xylopia aurantiiodora. These important features place these two African species clearly apart within the genus Xylopia, but do not justify for us the creation of a distinct genus.

Species widespread from Sierra Leone to the Central African Republic, São Thomé, Fernando Po, Cabinda and Congo-Kinshasa.

This rainforest tree resembles Xylopia Staudtii closely in its habit and leaves but is very distant from it in its flowers.

In Gabon the wood is used to make crossbows and hunting bows.
