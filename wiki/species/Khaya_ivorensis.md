# *Khaya ivorensis* A.Chev. Planche 10F

**Genus**: [[Khaya]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Vég. Ut. Afr. Trop. Franç. 5, Bois Côte Ivoire : 207 (1909)

## Description

K. klainei Pierre ex Pellegrin, Notul. Syst. (Paris) 2 : 77 (1911). Very large tree reaching 45 m in height; trunk 210 cm in diameter, with base furnished with buttresses, bole usually cylindrical and straight; bark scaly, brownish; inner surface bright red or pinkish, fairly fragrant, no appreciable exudate, sometimes a little blonde gum. Leaf: petiole 3–10 cm, plane-convex at the base; rachis 9–13 cm, glabrous; petiolule 3–6 mm, narrowly canaliculate above; leaflets 8–12(–14), rectangular-elliptic to ovate, (3–)5–10(–11.5) × (1.5–)2.5–5(–8) cm, rounded or cuneate at the base, acuminate or sometimes with abrupt acumen at the apex, with 5–9 pairs of slightly prominent veins, subcoriaceous, glabrous. Inflorescence pyramidal, erect, loosely branched, 15–30(–35) cm in length, glabrous. Functionally male flower: pedicel 1–1.5 mm; calyx deeply 5-lobed, the lobes spreading, imbricate, 1–1.5 × 1.5 mm; petals 5, narrowly elliptic to ovate, 5–6 × 2.5–3 mm, cucullate, glabrous; staminal tube urceolate, 5–6 mm in length, with margin bearing 10 short lobes alternating with the 10 anthers included and inserted between the lobes inside the tube, sessile, elliptic, 1 × 0.5 mm; gynoecium (pistillode) 3–4 mm in length, glabrous, supported by a cupuliform disc 0.5–1 mm in height, ovary narrowly ovoid; stigma discoid. Functionally female flower similar, anthers reduced; ovary cylindric, weakly 5-lobed, 2–3.5 mm in diameter, glabrous, 5-locular, with 10–14 ovules per locule; style 0.5–1 mm. Fruit subspherical, 6–10 cm in diameter, opening by 5 lignified valves; central columella pentagonal. Seeds flattened and with winged margins, 4–5 × 2.5–3 cm.

## Distribution

Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

old forest and moist evergreen secondary forest; in Gabon, up to approximately 500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m. Use: Excellent wood used in cabinetmaking and locally for monoxylous canoes. The roots and bark of bitter taste, the leaves and seeds are commonly used in traditional pharmacopoeia for the treatment of very diverse diseases

## Vernacular names

Acajou du Gabon, Acajou d’Afrique, Acajou rouge (fr. ; noms pi lotes) ; red mahogany (angl.) ; amambèga (Ivéa), amanguila (Gabonais), bokaso (Bakota),

## Discussion

mombèga (Ivéa), mumbèga (Éshira, Bovarama, Bavungu, Bavili, Balumbu, Masangu, Ngowé), mumbègè (Banzabi), ndjambi-wè-ngilo (Béséki), ndjamèngila (Benga, Mpongwè), nzam`ngila (Fang, Bakèlè), ombèga (Galoa, Mpongwè, Nkomi, Orungu, Mitsogo, Apindji, Simba, Ivéa, Bavové), umbèga (Benga, Béséki), zaminguila (Gabonese). Taxonomic notes: It is noteworthy that the original diagnosis of K. anthotheca (Welw.) C.DC. (1878) mentions leaves "5–10-jugate" whereas 3 to 6 pairs of leaflets are described by A. Chevalier (1909) to characterize K. ivorensis. The additional discriminating characters are weak and it is not impossible that the two species in fact represent a single specific entity. A deeper taxonomic study is highly desirable. Bibliography: De Saint Aubin (1963), Lemmens (2008a), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Styles & White (1991), Wilks & Issembé (2000).
