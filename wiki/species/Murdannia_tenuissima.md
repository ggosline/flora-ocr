# *Murdannia tenuissima* (A.Chev.) Brenan Planche 10

**Genus**: [[Murdannia]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 7(2) : 189 (1952)

## Description

Kew Bull. 7(2) : 189 (1952). Baoulia tenuissima A.Chev., Bull. Soc. Bot. Fr. 58, Mém. 8d : 217 (1912). Aneilema tenuissimum (A.Chev.) Hutch., Fl. W. trop. Afr. ed. 1, 2 : 314 (1912). Perennial herb, erect, very slender, 20–50(–90) cm tall; stems in tufts, unbranched or little-branched, 1 mm in diameter, with decumbent base rooting at nodes; roots fine, fibrous. Leaves 6 to 9, distichous; sheath 0.5–1.5 cm long, with fused margins, but often partially separating, distinctly ribbed, puberulent, apex ciliolate; blade very narrowly elliptic-ovate, 2–6 × 0.2–0.5 cm, base cuneate to subrounded, apex acuminate, margins generally ciliate, surfaces glabrous or with white pubescence on the upper side. Inflorescences in terminal and axillary fascicles arising from the almost open sheaths of the 1 to 3 upper leaves; fascicles with 1 to 4(–7) subequal peduncles, (0.3–)0.7–1.7(–2) cm, bearing 1(–2) bisexual flowers. Flower: 6–9 mm in diameter; pedicel 0.4–1 cm, at fruiting the peduncle and pedicel forming a straight axis of ± 2 cm; sepals elliptic-ovate, 2.5–5 × 1–2 mm; petals broadly ovate-elliptic to broadly elliptic, 3–5.5 × 2.8–5 mm, pale blue or mauve; fertile stamens 3, with filament 1.5–2.3 mm, with long spreading flexuous white hairs, anther 0.5–1.3 mm long, blue; staminodes absent; ovary 0.7–2 mm long; style tapered toward the apex, 0.5–1 mm, stigma punctiform. Fruit oblong to oblong-ellipsoid, (3.5–)4.5–6 × 1.5–2 mm, apiculate by the persistent base of the style, opening in 3 apiculate valves, with (1–)2 or 3 uniseriate seeds per locule. Seed cubic to rectangular, slightly trapezoidal, broadly ovoid or ellipsoidal, 1–1.3 × 0.8–1.1 mm; testa light brown, provided with numerous prominent lines, often interrupted, radiating from the embryostega.

## Distribution

Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Río Muni), Gabon, Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Angola and Zambia; in Gabon in the Ogooué-Ivindo. A rare or neglected species. It is rarely collected, perhaps owing to its inconspicuousness, growing among grasses.

## Ecology

herbaceous swamps, flooded savannas, rice fields; in Gabon at 100 m altitude, elsewhere from 1650 to 1900 m. Flowering (in West Africa) from ± 10:30 until early afternoon.

## Source note

The absence of staminodes led Chevalier to describe this species in a separate genus, Baoulia A.Chev. However, this genus was placed in synonymy with Murdannia by Brenan (1952) and the current name is widely accepted. The habit of the plant resembles that of a Poaceae and in the sterile state it is sometimes confused with a species of this family. Bibliography: Brenan (1952, 1968b), Brunel et al. (1984), van der Burg (2006), Faden (2012), Vanden Berghen (1988).
