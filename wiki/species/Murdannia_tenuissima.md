# *Murdannia tenuissima* (A.Chev.) Brenan Planche 10

**Genus**: [[Murdannia]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 7(2) : 189 (1952)

## Synonyms

- Baoulia tenuissima A.Chev., Bull. Soc. Bot. Fr. 58, Mém. 8d : 217 (1912).
- Aneilema tenuissimum (A.Chev.) Hutch., Fl. W. trop. Afr. éd. 1, 2 : 314 (1912) .

## Description

Perennial herb, erect, very slender, 20–50(–90) cm in height; stems tufted, not or little branched, 1 mm in diameter, with decumbent base and rooting at the nodes; roots fine, fibrous. Leaves 6 to 9, distichous; sheath 0.5–1.5 cm in length, with margins connate but often partially separating, distinctly ribbed, puberulent, apex ciliolate; blade very narrowly elliptic-ovate, 2–6 × 0.2–0.5 cm, base cuneate to subrounded, apex acuminate, margins generally ciliate, faces glabrous or with white pubescence on the upper surface. Inflorescences in terminal and axillary fascicles emerging from the almost open sheaths of the 1 to 3 upper leaves; fascicles with 1 to 4(–7) subequal peduncles, (0.3–)0.7–1.7(–2) cm, bearing 1(–2) bisexual flowers. Flower: 6–9 mm in diameter; pedicel 0.4–1 cm, at fruiting the peduncle and pedicel together form a straight axis of ± 2 cm; sepals elliptic-ovate, 2.5–5 × 1–2 mm; petals broadly ovate-elliptic to broadly elliptic, 3–5.5 × 2.8–5 mm, pale blue or mauve; fertile stamens 3, with filament 1.5–2.3 mm, bearing long spreading flexuous white hairs, anther 0.5–1.3 mm in length, blue; staminodes absent; ovary 0.7–2 mm in length; style tapering toward the apex, 0.5–1 mm, stigma punctiform. Fruit oblong to oblong-ellipsoid, (3.5–)4.5–6 × 1.5–2 mm, apiculate by the persistent base of the style, dehiscing in 3 apiculate valves, with (1–)2 or 3 uniseriate seeds per locule. Seed cubic to rectangular, slightly trapezoidal, broadly ovoid or ellipsoid, 1–1.3 × 0.8–1.1 mm; testa light brown, provided with numerous prominent lines, often interrupted, radiating from the embryostega.

## Distribution

Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Río Muni), Gabon, Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Angola and Zambia; in Gabon in Ogooué-Ivindo. A rare or overlooked species. It is seldom collected, perhaps because of its inconspicuousness, growing among grasses.

## Ecology

herbaceous marshes, flooded savannas, rice fields; in Gabon at 100 m altitude, elsewhere from 1650 to 1900 m. Flowering (in West Africa) from ± 10:30 until early afternoon.

## Source note

The absence of staminodes led Chevalier to describe this species in a separate genus, Baoulia A.Chev. However, this genus was placed in synonymy with Murdannia by Brenan (1952) and the current name is widely accepted. The habit of the plant resembles that of a Poaceae and in the sterile state it is sometimes confused with a species of this family. Bibliography: Brenan (1952, 1968b), Brunel et al. (1984), van der Burg (2006), Faden (2012), Vanden Berghen (1988)
