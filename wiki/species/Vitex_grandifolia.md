# *Vitex grandifolia* Gürke Planche 27(7-14) ; Figure 6(D, E)

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 18 : 169 (1893)

## Description

V. bipindensis Gürke, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33(2) : 295 (1903). V. grandifolia Gürke var. bipindensis (Gürke) W.Piep., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 62, Beibl. 141 (« 142 ») : 73 (1928). Tree 6–10(–30) m tall, evergreen; branches almost glabrous, angular, often dark red, with lenticels, with internodes generally hollow and inhabited by ants. Leaf: petiole 10–30 cm, flattened above, often with prominent angles, glabrous; leaflets 5(to 7), with petiolules 5–15 mm, channelled, poorly separated from the leaflets, with narrowly obovate-elliptic blade, 10–40(–50) × 4–12(–15) cm, very narrowly attenuate at the base, distinctly acuminate at the apex, entire, entirely glabrous, coriaceous, becoming brownish or livid in herbarium, upper surface dark green, lower surface distinctly paler, without glands, main vein generally keeled below, domatia absent; secondary veins 10 to 15 on each side of the main vein, reticulation invisible above, lax and faintly marked below. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia, typically localized at the two upper leafy nodes of the branches, 4–8(–9) cm long, of which 1–5 cm for the peduncle, remarkably shorter than the axillant leaf; axes angular-flattened, thick, puberulent. Flower: pedicel almost absent; calyx 3–4(–5) mm long, with teeth ± 0.3–0.5 mm long, with short grayish appressed hairs; corolla 10–15 mm long, brownish yellow on the outside, yellowish on the inside with brownish veins, reddish at the throat; stamens slightly exsert; ovary with conical apex and bristly with stiff hairs. Fruit ovoid, ± 15–20 × 9–12 mm, often with a few hairs at the apex, subtended by the spreading calyx.

## Distribution

Tropical West and centre-West Africa, from Guinea to the Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: known from all provinces. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

Dense humid forests, forest galleries; at 0–750 m altitude. Use: The wood is used locally for small carpentry. The species is medicinal and its fruits are edible

## Vernacular names

bikó-na, evul, évus, nnom-angóna (fang) ; obandja-gétété (mitsogo)

## Source note

V. grandifolia is close to V. doniana and some specimens of V. doniana with short inflorescence and large leaves may be misleading; V. doniana almost always has some hairs on the veins of the lower surface of the leaflets and is never associated with ants. At flowering time, the two species are easy to distinguish: V. grandifolia has the corolla brownish yellow on the outside, yellowish on the inside with brownish veins, reddish at the throat, V. doniana has the corolla pale blue or pale purple, whitish at the throat. Quite numerous records of V. grandifolia in Gabon are erroneous and actually refer to V. gabunensis (see note under this latter species).
