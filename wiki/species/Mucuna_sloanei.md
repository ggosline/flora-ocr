# *Mucuna sloanei* Fawc. & Rendle Planche 97.10–12

**Genus**: [[Mucuna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: J. Bot. 55 : 36 (1917)

## Synonyms

- Mucuna urens auct., non (L.) Medik.
- Stizolobium urens auct., non (L.) Pers.

## Description

Slender liane, with stem reaching 6 m; branchlets finely grey pubescent. Stipules caducous, lanceolate, 5–8 mm in length, hairy. Leaf: petiole 10–12 cm, ± glabrous; rachis ± 3 cm; stipels filiform, 2–3 mm in length; leaflet: petiolule 0.5 cm, the terminal one with ovate-oblong blade, 8–12 × 5–5.5 cm, cuspidate, the lateral ones somewhat larger and clearly asymmetric, with rare appressed and caducous hairs above, denser and persistent below. Inflorescence in an umbel, with 2 to 10(–15) flowers; peduncle 5–10 cm. Flower: pedicel 6–8 mm; bracteoles caducous; calyx campanulate, shortly silvery pubescent, bristly with rare, brown and caducous setae, tube 6–8 mm in length, upper lobes connate, rounded or truncate, lower lobes 6–8 mm in length; corolla ± 3 cm in length, sulphur yellow, glabrous, standard ± 1.5 cm in length. Pod oblong, 10–15 × 4–5 × 1.5–2 cm, marked with numerous irregular transverse crests or lamellae, with stiff, urticating, reddish-brown setae, somewhat constricted between the 2 or 3 seeds if present, but often with a single seed. Seed suborbicular, ± 2.5 cm in diameter, ± 1.5 cm in thickness; hilum on 4/5 of the seed circumference.

## Distribution

Tropical cosmopolitan; in Gabon, known from Ngounié, Nyanga and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Wooded savannas, abandoned cultivations, terra firme; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m. Fruits in June. Use: Seeds boiled give a decoction that alleviates pain caused by hemorrhoids. It is also a dye plant of local importance

## Vernacular names

grand pois pouilleux, ouil de bourrique (fr.) ; hamburger bean, horse eye bean (angl.) ; asisi-nkyanghè (bakèlè) ; mankundi (Loango) ; modjangi-a-mikuku (mitsogo) ; osangé-w’abambo (mpongwè) ; yareghe-mitsang, yarege-ntsangha (fang)
