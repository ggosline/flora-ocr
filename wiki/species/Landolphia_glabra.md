# *Landolphia glabra* (Pierre ex Stapf) Pichon Planche 51(7-20)

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 158 (1953)

## Description

Large liana with scattered, sometimes lenticellate, brown lenticels, glabrous or slightly pubescent. Leaf: petiole 2–17 mm, clearly separated from the blade; blade narrowly ovate to ovate or obovale, ± two times as long as wide, 0.5–2 cm wide, ± rounded to attenuate at the base, acuminate at the apex with acumen often spathulate, dark yellowish-brown beneath when dry, glabrous on both faces, with numerous small dark dots beneath; secondary veins 8 to 17 pairs clearly visible, prominent above, nearly straight. Inflorescence axillary. Flower perfumed; pedicel up to 6 mm, bracteate; the distal sepals sepaloid calyx with equal or unequal lobes ± two times as long as wide, 1.1–2.2 mm long, glabrous or weakly pubescent on the exterior; corolla often bright yellow, tube 6–11 mm long, 0.8–1.2 mm wide at the base, 1–1.8 mm at the level of the anthers and 0.9–1.7 mm at the throat, glabrous on the exterior, slightly pubescent on the interior, with narrowly ovate to narrowly elliptic lobes ± as long as the tube ± as long as wide, 0.5–1 mm, ciliate; stamens inserted at 0.5–0.7 of the tube length, at the level of the throat or included 0.7 mm, anther 1.1–1.5 mm long; pistil 7.8–10.4 mm long, ovary hairy or tomentose; style 4.6–7.2 mm, gradually widening toward the base, hairy, less densely toward the apex. Fruit globose to globose-depressed, less often ellipsoid, 0.5–2 cm, yellow to orange, blue, violet or black, with 1–4 seeds; wall thick 0.1–1.1 cm; pulp white to yellow. Distribution: Southeast Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon indicated in all provinces.

Ecology: Primary and secondary forests, forest patches in savanna; in Gabon, up to 1 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2 m.

Use: The latex is sometimes used for rubber. The fruits are edible.

B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953).
