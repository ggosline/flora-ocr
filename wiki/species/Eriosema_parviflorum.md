# *Eriosema parviflorum* E.Mey. subsp. parviflorum

**Genus**: [[Eriosema]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Comm. pl. Afr. austr. : 130 (1836)

## Description

Perennial herb, densely pilose with long spreading or reflexed hairs, reddish-brown or reddish-yellow; stem up to 2.5 m long, erect or rather sarmentose or decumbent. Stipules lanceolate, 6–10 × 1.5–2 mm, scarious, silky-tomentose on the outside, glabrous on the inside. Leaf: petiole 1.5–4 cm; rachis 2–4 mm; leaflets 3, with obovate to elliptic lamina, 2.2–6.2 × 1.5–4.0 cm, base obtuse, apex obtuse to acute; lateral veins in 5 to 7 pairs, ascending. Inflorescence axillary or terminal; racemes dense, 2–7 × 0.6–1.2 cm; peduncle 5–17(–20) cm, with spreading hairs, yellowish-brown; bracts filiform, 2–4 mm. Flower pendulous: pedicel 6–8 mm; calyx campanulate, 2–3 mm long, with white hairs and scattered glands, teeth triangular, short; petals red in bud, then yellow to greenish-white or pale pink, with purplish-brown apex, sometimes striped with red, standard oblong-obovate, 5–7 × 2.5–4.2 mm, auriculate and biappendiculate, appendage situated clearly above the claw, apex pilose and glandulous, wings 5–7 mm long, glabrous, keel oblong, 4–6 mm long, glabrous, but with glands on the apex. Pod oblongovo­id, 10–11 × 5–7 mm, with 1 or 2 seeds, with dense yellowish-brown hairs. Seed oblonge-trapezoidal, 3–4 × 2.5–3 × 2 mm, green to brown with black spots; hilum surrounding ⅓ of the seed, white to yellowish.

## Distribution

fairly widely distributed: from Nigeria to the Central African Republic, to Natal and to Zanzibar; in Gabon, known from the Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

rather coastal, from Sierra Leone to Gabon; in Gabon, known from Ngounié

## Ecology

herbaceous savanna, savanna in flooded forest, scrubland, forest edge, roadside, clearings, on brown sand or rocks; in Gabon up to 750 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Flowers and fruits from December to February, May and July

humid sands, marshes, roadside, marshy soil; at low altitude
