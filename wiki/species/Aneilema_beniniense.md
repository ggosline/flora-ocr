# *Aneilema beniniense* (P.Beauv.) Kunth Planche 3, Figure 1C

**Genus**: [[Aneilema]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. Pl. 4 : 73 (1843)

## Synonyms

- Commelina beniniensis P.Beauv., Fl. Oware 2 : 49, t. 87 (1816).

## Description

A. lujae De Wild. & T.Durand, Ann. Mus. Congo belge, Bot., sér. 2, 1(1): 63 (1899). Perennial herb, decumbent, with fibrous roots; stem erect to ascending, (15–)40–130 cm, rooting at the nodes, branched at the base, then ± simple, glabrous. Leaves spiral, a few (2 to 4) concentrated at the apex of the stem; pseudopetiole 0.5–2 mm; sheath 1–4 cm long, glabrous or sparsely puberulent on the suture, sparsely ciliolate or not at the apex; blade narrowly ovate to elliptic, (1.5–)6–15(–20) × (1–)2–5(–6.7) cm, base cuneate to ± rounded, apex acuminate, with scabrous margin toward the apex, glabrous or sparsely puberulent below. Inflorescence terminal, sometimes also arising from the apical leaf, in a thyrse, very dense, ovoid to cylindric, 2–6 × 1.5–6 cm, composed of (10–)18 to 55 simple cincinni, spreading to ascending, inserted on a single central erect and relatively short rachis, (1.5–)3.5–6 cm, glabrous or slightly puberulent; peduncle 2–4 cm, with a bract at mid-length; cincinni elongating to 4 cm, with ± 9 bisexual and male flowers. Flower 7–10(–13) mm in diameter; bracteoles cupuliform, perfoliate, clearly glandular below the apex, glabrous; pedicel 4–7 mm, erect to strongly curved during fruiting, glabrous; sepals ovate to elliptic, 2–3 × 2–2.5 mm, the median one slightly wider, concave, with cupuliform apex, glandular over most of their surface, but glabrous at the apex; upper petals spatulate, 5–6.5 × 2.5–4 mm with a claw of 1–1.5 mm, with ovate to circular apical part, white or more rarely pale lilac, the lower one ovate to broadly ovate, 3–4 × 2–3 mm, white or whitish green; lateral stamens with filament of 3.5–5 mm, sigmoid, slightly bearded, anther 0.8–1 mm long, median stamen with filament of 3–3.5 mm, anther 0.8–0.9 mm long; staminodes 2 or 3, antheroid equal, bilobed, yellow; ovary 1.5–2 × 1 mm, glabrous; style curved-descending, ± 4 mm, stigma capitate. Fruit 2- or 3-locular, oblongellipsoid, (4.5–)5–7 × 2.5–3.5 mm, bivalved, with dorsal locule bearing 0 or 1 seed, ventral locules bearing (1–)2 or 3 seeds. Seed from the ventral locule broadly ovoid to reniform, 1.3–2.4 × 1.2–1.8 mm; testa pinkish brown, orange-brown, or gray.

## Distribution

Senegal, Guinea, Guinea Bissau, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Río Muni), Democratic Republic of Sao Toméet-Príncipe, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Chad, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Angola and Zambia; very common in Gabon, present in Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Evergreen forests, forest edges, forest clearings, riverbanks, marshes; in Gabon from 0 to 950 m elevation, elsewhere from 700 to 1700 m. Flowering over most of the year; flowers open in the morning

## Source note

Morton (1966) separated the subspecies sessilifolium (Benth.) J.K.Morton by its smaller white flowers and glabrous or nearly glabrous vegetative parts (among other characters). We follow Brenan (1968b) and Faden (2012) who do not consider a satisfactory distinction to separate this subspecies. A. beniniense may be confused with the rarer A. silvaticum Brenan (present in Cameroon, Bioko and the Democratic Republic of the Congo), which bears leaves with ciliate margins (vs. scabrous) and slightly pulverulent fruits with rather pointed apex (vs. smooth and with rounded to truncate apex) and which has not yet been found in Gabon. Sometimes the name of this species is written as "A. beninense," which is an orthographic variant. Bibliography: Brenan (1968b), van der Burg (2006), Cabezas et al. (2014), Faden (2012), Morton (1966), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Sosef et al. (2006), Vande weghe et al. (2016).
