# *Centrosema pubescens* Benth. Planche 24.7–9

**Genus**: [[Centrosema]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Comm. legum. gen. : 55 (1837)

## Description

Suffrutescent herb, twining, pubescent; stem reaching several meters in length. Stipules oval-lanceolate, 2–4 mm long. Leaf: petiole 1–5.5 cm; rachis 0.4–1.5 cm; leaflets 3, with petiolule 1–3 mm, with blade oval to elliptic, 3.5–7 × 1.5–4 cm, rounded at base, apex acuminate to obtuse, mucronate, papyraceous, pubescent on both faces; lateral veins in 5 or 6 pairs. Inflorescence with flowers 2 or 3 at the apex of the peduncle 6–13 cm; bracts caducous, oval-lanceolate, 6–9 × 4 mm, with parallel and dense veins. Flower: pedicel slender, 4–12 mm; calyx with unequal teeth 4–8 mm long; corolla pink to mauve or sometimes yellow and/or striped with purple, standard 3–4 × 2–3 cm, ± pubescent on the back, wings narrowly obovate, ± 2.5 × 0.7 cm, keel semicircular, ± 2.5 × 1.6 cm. Pod linear, 10–17 × 0.6–0.7 cm, straight or nearly so, terminated by a beak of ± 1 cm, dark green to purple, with lax and short pubescence, glabrescent, with 18 to 22 seeds, sutures thickened and subwinged, 1.5 mm wide. Seed oblongoid, 5 × 4 × 2 mm, brown spotted with black; hilum ± central, ± 2.5 mm long.

## Distribution

native to tropical America, introduced around the tropics and one of the most widespread legumes dispersed by humans in the world; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

ruderal stations and cultivated fields, palm plantations etc., garden hedges; in Gabon up to 575 m altitude, elsewhere up to 900 m. Use: Good green manure, pasture and cover, fixing atmospheric nitrogen, popular since the 1950s. It is also one of the most palatable plants for livestock. The flowers are ornamental.

## Vernacular names

fleur-languette, pois bâtard (fr.) ; centro (angl. austral.), butterfly pea, spurred but terfly pea (angl.)

## Source note

Fantz (1996) considers that the name C. molle would be correct for this species if cultivated, widespread and naturalized under the tropics. However, the diagnostic characters overlap and we agree with Adema (in prep.) in considering that C. pubescens and C. molle represent a single species.
