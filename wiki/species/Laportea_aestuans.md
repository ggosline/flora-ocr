# *Laportea aestuans* (L.) Chew

**Genus**: [[Laportea]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gard. Bull. Singapore 21 : 200 (1965)

## Synonyms

- Fleurya aestuans (L.) Miq., in Martius, Fl. bras. 4(1) : 196 (1853).
- Fleurya perrieri Léandri, Ann. Mus. Colon. Marseille , sér. 6, 7-8 : 16 (1950).
- Laportea bathiei Léandri, in Humbert, Fl. Madagascar 56 : 10 (1965).

## Description

Annual herb, reaching approximately 1 m in height or more; stem somewhat aqueous and fleshy, becoming weakly woody at the base, weakly branched, glabrescent or covered with dense stinging hairs, up to ± 1 mm in length and soft and glandular hairs, 1–2(–3) mm in length. Stipules linear-lanceolate, fused for approximately half their length, glabrous, except for a few hairs on the midvein. Leaves restricted to the apex of the erect stems; petiole 5–15 cm, generally covered with soft and dense glandular hairs, mixed with a few stinging hairs; blade ovate to broadly ovate, 7–20 × 5.5–14 cm, base broadly cuneate to cordate, apex acute to acuminate, margin serrulate, with on each side 30 to 40 teeth reaching 0.5 mm in length, chartaceous, upper surface with rigid stinging hairs and punctiform cystoliths, lower surface with stinging hairs on the veins; venation symmetrically triplinerved, the basal pair reaching the 10 to 12 apical teeth, upper lateral veins in 4 to 8 pairs. Inflorescence bisexual, rarely unisexual, in a well-branched panicle, 15–20 cm long with a peduncle of 4–10 cm, at the axils of the upper leaves and exceeding them, axis covered with glandular hairs; flowers in glomules of ± 5 mm in diameter, the male flowers generally dispersed throughout the inflorescence and mixed with the female ones or only at the base, rarely in a distinct inflorescence (see notes), rapidly caducous, the female ones in dense glomules. Male flower: pedicel ± 1 mm; perianth ± 1.5 mm in diameter; tepals 4 or 5, generally with glandular hairs. Female flower: pedicel ± 0.5 mm, generally weakly winged dorso-ventrally; lateral tepals ± 0.5 mm in length, the dorsal about half their length, weakly geniculate, generally with glandular hairs, the ventral shorter; ovary ovoid; stigma filiform, ± 0.5 mm, ± recurved. Fruit stipitate, ovoid, 1(–1.5) mm in length, each side with a crest surrounding a verrucose zone.

## Distribution

practically pantropical – but apparently absent from Oceania (invasive in Hawaii) where the species is replaced by L. ruderalis (Forst.f.) Chew which differs from it by details of the inflorescence and the flowers – known from tropical America, from Mexico to Peru and in the West Indies;

Laportea 59 in tropical Asia, widely distributed from Yemen to Indonesia; in tropical Africa, from Senegal to Cameroon, the Central African Republic, and towards Ethiopia and Eritrea, to the south, to Angola, Zimbabwe and Mozambique, also in Madagascar; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

ruderal of humid perennial cultivation, in disturbed forest stations, always a plant of semi-shade, sometimes in rock crevices, in more open vegetation; in Gabon at 50–800 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1300 m. Uses: The stem contains textile fibres. The plant is also used as a fetish plant of secret societies.

## Source note

The distribution of male and female flowers is variable: in African material a few specimens have been observed with female flowers only in the upper inflorescences and male flowers in the lower ones and one can exceptionally see specimens that are entirely male.
