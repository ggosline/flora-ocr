# *Sansevieria aubrytiana* Carrière

**Genus**: [[Sansevieria]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Asparagaceae]] subfam. Nolinoideae — *Dracaenaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Rev. Hort. (Paris) 1861 : 449 (1861)

## Synonyms

- Dracaena aubrytiana (Carrière) Byng & Christenh., Global Fl. 4 : 64 (2018).

## Description

Herb; rhizome ± 3 cm thick. Leaves 2 to 4 per shoot, in rosette, erect to spreading, the base surrounded by 1 or 2 reduced leaves, early caducous, triangular, up to 10 × 3 cm, with apex long-apiculate; blade ligulate to narrowly obovate, ± 70 × 12 cm, with concave base, apex gradually narrowed to a soft and early mucro, 0.5–1 cm, margins ± 0.5–1 mm wide, dark red-brown with whitish hyaline margin, more or less undulate, dark green concolorous, obscurely transversely marked in zigzag with grey-green above, very striking pattern below, plane with 2 to 4 impressed and striking longitudinal grooves above, prominent but discreet below. Inflorescence erect, spiciform, dense, up to ± 70 cm long, with green axes, flowers on the ± 25 cm upper portion, spaced 0.5–2 cm apart, denser towards the apex; peduncular bracts 4 or 5, persistent, narrowly ovate, ± 6 cm × 12 mm, scarious; fascicles with 3 to 5 flowers, each subtended by a triangular bract, 8–12 mm long, scarious. Flower fragrant; pedicel 6–8 mm; perianth 8–9.5 cm long, pale green, tube 5–6 cm long, with spreading lobes; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 1 mm shorter than the lobes, filament filiform, anther ± 5 mm long; ovary ovoid, ± 4 × 2.5 mm; style exsert up to 8 mm, stigma 3-lobed, ± 0.5 mm diameter. Fruit and seed not seen.

## Distribution

Native to Equatorial Guinea and Gabon, but cultivated throughout the world; in Gabon known from Ogooué-Maritime and Estuaire

Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

sandy soil, on the edge of coastal forests, in open areas; in Gabon at 0–25 m altitude. Flowering recorded in November and December. Usage: The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental plant under the cultivar names 'African Dawn', 'Ayo' and 'Jiboja'

open stations, rocky outcrops and flat slabs, inselbergs, shrubby mantles; in Gabon at 300–650 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Flowering recorded in April, July and November. Usage: The species is planted as a fetish to protect farms against thunder and female infertility and to treat blisters. Throughout the world, as in Gabon, it is cultivated as an ornamental plant and there exists an enormous variety of cultivars. S. trifasciata 'Laurentii', with its golden-yellow margin, being the most famous. It is sold in the spontaneous flower markets of Libreville

## Source note

We treat here S. aubrytiana in the strict sense. Other authors have accepted this species with broader variation, which, in our view, is incorrect. The taxonomic situation remains doubtful and further study will be necessary to establish the relationship between S. aubrytiana and other Central African species, such as S. bracteata Baker, S. longiflora Sims and S. fasciata Cornu ex Gérôme & Labroy. Leaves 2 to 6 per shoot, in rosette, strictly erect, the base surrounded by 1 to 4 reduced bracteate leaves early caducous, triangular, up to 9 × 3 cm, with apex long-apiculate; blade linear to narrowly ovate, up to ± 80 × 7 cm, with concave base, apex gradually narrowed to a soft and persistent mucro 0.5–1 cm, margins ± 2–3 mm wide, dark green, more or less undulate, above and below pale to very dark green uniform, obscurely to very striking transversely marked in zigzag with pale to grey-green, plane with 2 to 4 obscure sunken longitudinal grooves above. Inflorescence erect, spiciform, up to ± 60 cm long, with green axes with white spots, flowers well spaced 1–4 cm apart in the upper half of the inflorescence; peduncular bracts 3 to 5, persistent, narrowly ovate to triangular, reaching 3 × 1 cm, with apex cuspidate, decreasing in size distally, scarious; fascicles with 2 to 5 flowers, each subtended by a persistent bract, narrowly ovate, 6–10 × ± 3 mm, scarious. Flower with strong hyacinth fragrance; pedicel 3–6 mm; perianth 1.8–2.5 cm long, pale green in bud, white at anthesis, with tube 0.5–0.8 cm long, with reflexed lobes; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 1 mm shorter than the lobes, filament filiform, white, anther ± 2 mm long, pale yellow; ovary ovoid, ± 1.5 × 0.8 mm; style white, exsert up to 1 mm, stigma 3-lobed, ± 0.3 mm diameter. Fruit orange, tuberculate, spherical when with 1 seed and ± 8–11 mm diameter, deeply 2- or 3-lobed when with 2 or 3 seeds; receptacle up to 5–6 mm long. Seed reniform, ± 8 × 5 mm.
