# *Scaphopetalum blackii* Masters.

**Genus**: [[Scaphopetalum]]
**Family**: [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mast., J. Linn. Soc. X: 29, t. 3 f. 8-14 (1869), réf. orig

## Synonyms

- Sc. letestui Pellegr. nov. syn. pro var.

## Description

, orig. ref. — K. Schum. Sterc. Afr.: 91, t. 6 f. A (1900). — Pellegr. Sterc. Gab., Mém. S. B. Fr. . 47-48 (1950-1951).

Shrub 1.50 to 6 m tall, reaching 20 cm in diameter, with a contorted trunk. Branches glabrous; young extremities puberulent, sometimes glabrescent. Stipules subpersistent, puberulent, sometimes glabrescent, triangular to ensiform, (2) 3-10 (12) mm long, 1-3 mm wide towards the base. Petioles thick, moderately canaliculate above, 5-13 mm long, glabrescent or more or less clothed with a short pubescence sometimes very dense. Blade with fugacious juvenile hairs, glabrous, subcoriaceous, elliptic, 13-30 (38) cm long by 4-12 cm wide; base variable from subacute to rounded; acumen variable. Midvein in slight depression on the upper surface of the blade; (8) 9-13 (15) pairs of pinnate veins, the lowest pair basal.

Inflorescence in axillary floral fascicles, sometimes cauline, pauciflorous or pluriflorous, surrounded basally by a variable number of small acute bracts. Pedicel glabrous or not, slender, 7-19 mm long, articulate at the base of the conical floral receptacle; 3 small acute bracteoles alternate along the pedicel. Bud spherical more or less pubescent, or glabrous with at least some apical hairs; the 5 sepals are fused together to the apex where one can distinguish the beginning of the 5 divisions.

Flowers yellow, flesh-coloured and violet, 10-15 mm in diameter. Calyx flesh-coloured red, tearing irregularly at anthesis into 3 convex divisions: one comprising a single lobe, the other two lobes fused together. 5 petals more or less yellow, free from each other but adnate basally to the staminal tube. Petal cochleate 7-10 mm long, with ca. 7 longitudinal veins, apex weakly 3-angled, moderately pubescent. Androecial collar membranous, violet, pentagonal funnel-shaped, ca. 10 mm in diameter and 4-7 mm in height; at each angle is held a tight group of (4-5) 6 sessile pollen sacs each less than 1 mm long; each group is capped at anthesis by a petal; margin of the tube adorned between the angles with short pairs of staminodes and thin membranous folds. Ovary subcylindric, 5-furrowed, 5-locular, pubescent, 2-3 mm long, surmounted by a central narrow and glabrous style ca. 1.5-2.5 mm. Ovules biseriate ± superposed, ca. 6-7 per locule.

Fruit dry 2.5-4.5 cm long by 1-1.6 cm wide, elliptic to subconical, apex more or less acute, finely clothed with stellate hairs, locicidal dehiscence; the fruit opens by 5 valves adorned with undulate marginal projections; they spread out showing each on the axis of the internal surface the intercarpellary septum. The calyx is persistent. Approximately 2-4 seeds per carpel, pubescent, 5-12 mm long, oval or with truncate extremities, provided with an irregularly dentate aril.

Type of the species: Mann, number not cited by Masters, Mont John, Kongui riv. in Spanish Guinea.

It was not possible for us to maintain Sc. letestui Pellegr. as a species, insufficiently different from Sc. blackii Mast., because of the variability of the distinctive characters: thus the base of the blade is sometimes broadly rounded in very glabrous forms; the pubescence of the calyx is very variable whereas the other floral characters are very constant; the length of the stipules is variable. It would not be impossible either to question the validity of Sc. amoenum A. Chev. from West Africa. This species differs appreciably from Sc. blackii only in the fairly abundant pubescence of its calyx and the brevity of its fruit.
