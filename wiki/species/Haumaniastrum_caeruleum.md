# *Haumaniastrum caeruleum* (Oliv.) P.A.Duvign. & Plancke Planche 13

**Genus**: [[Haumaniastrum]]
**Protologue**: Biol. Jaarb. 27 : 225 (1959)

## Description

Perennial herb, 0.2–1 m tall; stem ascending or decumbent at the base, quadrangular to rounded-quadrangular, sometimes with small buds bearing orbicular leaves at the base, nearly glabrous to densely pubescent with spreading or upward-directed hairs. Leaf: petiole 0–2 mm; blade linear to narrowly elliptic, sometimes folded along the midvein, 2–9(–10) × 0.2–1.5 cm, attenuate to cuneate at the base, sometimes clasping the stem, acute at the apex, dentate, nearly glabrous to densely pubescent; blade of the upper leaves subtending the heads 0.5–2.5 cm long, caudate to apiculate at the apex with a basal portion ovate to orbicular or obcordate, sinuate to dentate or crenate or deeply crenate, white or bluish at the base, apex green, margin often involute in the apical part, flat toward the base. Inflorescence with globose to cylindric capitules 0.5–2(–4) × 0.5–1.5 cm, arranged in a dense or lax corymb. Flower: calyx 1.5–2 mm long at anthesis and 4–6 mm in fruit, villous with appressed hairs, upper lip with triangular to tridentate apex, slightly cucullate, often bluish, lower lip equal to or slightly shorter than the upper, apex with 2 deltoid teeth; corolla 4–6 mm long, white, pink, blue or purple, with tube 3–4.5 mm long. Fruit: achenes 1–1.5 mm long, brown.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, East Africa, Angola, Zambia, Malawi and Zimbabwe; in Gabon: Haut-Ogooué

Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC).

## Ecology

wet zones in grasslands or open forests, also recorded as an adventive in crops; in Gabon at 480–630 m altitude, elsewhere at 220–2100 m. B: Paton (1997a), Paton et al. (2009, 2013), Walters et al. (2011). Bibliography
