# *Hunteria umbellata* (K.Schum.) Hallier f. Planche 43 ; Figure 6(F)

**Genus**: [[Hunteria]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Jahrb. Hamb. Wiss. Anst. 17, 3 Beih . : 190 (1900)

## Description

Tree or shrub reaching 22 m in height, with dense crown; trunk sinuous or straight, fluted, reaching 40 cm in diameter; inner bark white with orange stripes or patches; wood very hard; branchlets smooth. Leaf: petiole 8–25 mm long, oblong to 4 times as long as wide, 0.5–1 cm wide, acuminate, obtuse at base or decurrent on petiole, obtuse to acuminate at apex; midvein deeply channelled above, secondary nerves 13 to 25 pairs, prominent below, ± straight, forming a submarginal nerve and an angle of 45–60° with the midvein. Inflorescence terminal, rarely axillary, 5–25 cm long, dense to lax with 4–30 flowers, peduncle 2–20 mm; primary axes 3–10 mm. Flower very fragrant; pedicel 3–7 mm; calyx with lobes 1.5–3 mm, arranged of collateral sepals on the interior up to three-quarters of their length; corolla white to pale yellow, 8.5–17.5 mm long in the mature bud and at anthesis, relatively narrow, as long as the bud at apex, acute, with a pubescent band on the interior just below the insertion of the stamens, tube 4–8.2 mm long, enlarged at the level of the anthers over 0.7–2 mm, with lobes twisted in the bud, (0.8–)1.2–2.7 times the length of the tube, 1–2 times as long as wide, 3–6 × 2–4 mm, cream slightly pink; stamens with apex 0.3–1 mm below the throat, inserted at 0.54–0.86 of the length of the tube, anther 0.8–1.4 mm long; pistil 2.5–6.4 mm long, with 12 to 30 ovules in each carpel. Fruit: mericarps subglobose, 30–60 mm in diameter, yellow, smooth, with 8 to 26 seeds; wall 0.5–1.5 mm thick, slightly fibrous. Seed oblong to ellipsoid, 4–8 × 2–4 × 1.5–2.5 mm. Distribution: from Senegal to Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola, in Gabon: Moyen Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime. Ecology: primary and secondary forest in Gabon and elsewhere up to 1000 m altitude. B: Beentje (2021), Omino (1996). BIBLIOGRAPHY
