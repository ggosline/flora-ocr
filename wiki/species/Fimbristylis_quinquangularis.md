# *Fimbristylis quinquangularis* (Vahl) Kunth Planche 97

**Genus**: [[Fimbristylis]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. pl. 2 : 229 (1837)

## Synonyms

## Description

Fimbristylis miliacea sensu auct., non (L.) Vahl (1805), nom. rej. Scirpus miliaceus L., Syst. Veg. éd. 10: 868 (1759), nom. rej.

Original drawing by Richard W. Haines ©. Haines ©. Annual herb or perhaps sometimes perennial, in tufts; stems usually 20–60(–100+) cm × 1–1.5 mm, with 4–5 acute angles. Leaf as long as or shorter than the stems; ligule absent; blade plane with the midvein prominent, 2–3 mm wide, gradually reduced to an acute or obtuse point, their axils subtending new leafy shoots (as in F. littoralis). Inflorescence a loose open compound anthela, 4–10 cm long; involucral bracts shorter than the inflorescence, setaceous, up to 3 cm long. Spikelet ovate-lanceolate, 2–5(–10 with the naked rachis) × 1–1.5 mm; glumes 1–2 mm long, with a short mucro, with a brown stripe on each side of the midvein, with hyaline margins. Flower with 1(–2) stamens, anther about 0.3 mm long; style triangular, glabrous or slightly fringed in the upper half, with 3 branches ± as long as the style. Achene obovoid, 0.4–0.7 × 0.3–0.4 mm, triangular with rounded angles, light brown, irregularly verrucose, finely transversely striate with transversely oblong-linear epidermal cells in 4–6 vertical rows on each face.

## Distribution

Asia and Australia; perhaps introduced to Africa and America with rice cultivation; infrequent in Gabon, found only in Estuaire, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (3 collections)

## Ecology

in marshy grasslands, cultivated soils particularly rice fields; in Gabon, at 0–500 m elevation, up to about 1100 m in East Africa. B: Goetghebeur & Coudijzer (1984a), Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1997)
