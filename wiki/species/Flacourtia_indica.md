# *Flacourtia indica* (Burm. f.) Merr.

## Description

Comm. Lour., in Trans Amer. Phil. Soc., n. s., 24 (2) : 274 (1935) ; GILG, in ENGL. & PRANTL, NPF 21 : 440 (1925) ; WILD, Fl. Zamb. 1 : 277, t. 47B (1960) ; BAMPS, FCBR, Spermat., Flacourt. 1 : 48 (1968) ; SLEUMER, FTEA, Flacourt. : 57 (1975).

– Gmelina indica BURM. f., Fl. indica : 132, t. 39, fig. 5 (1768). TYPE : Burman (in Herb. Delessert), Java.

– Flacourtia ramontchi L'HÉR., Stirp. Nov. 3 : 59, t. 30 et 30B (1786) ; OLIV., FTA 1 : 120 (1868) ; WARB., in ENGL. & PRANTL, NPF 3 (6a) : 43 (1893) ; GILG, in ENGL., PWA 3 (2) : 586 (1921). TYPE : Herb. Commerson, Madagascar (holo-, G! ; iso-, P!).

– F. flavescens WILLD., Sp. Pl. 4 (2) : 830 (1805). TYPE : Herb. Willdenow, Guinea (holo-, B!).

– F. gambecola CLOS, Ann. Sc. Nat. 4(8) : 219 (1857). TYPE : Heudelot 349, Gambia, Ile Mac Carthy (holo-, iso-, P!).

– F. hirtiuscula OLIV., FTA 1 : 121 (1868). TYPE : KIRK s.n., Mozambique (holo-, K!).

– Xylosma ellipticum TUL., Ann. Sc. Nat. 5 (9) : 343 (1868). TYPE : Boivin 3300, Tanzania (holo-, P!).

– F. elliptica (TUL.) WARB., in ENGL. & PRANTL, NPF 3 (6a) : 43 (1893).

Tree (15–20 m tall) or shrub, generally spiny; branchlets brownish to greyish, pubescent to glabrous; spines sometimes branched on the trunk.

Leaves with petiole 0.5–2 cm long, pubescent to glabrescent; blade ovate, elliptic, obovate, or suborbicular, cuneate to rounded at base, rounded, obtuse, acute or shortly acuminate at apex, 4–16 × 2–8 cm, crenate-dentate to subentire, subcoriaceous, pubescent or glabrescent on both faces; secondary veins 4–7 pairs; tertiary veins arranged in a network.

Male flowers in axillary racemes, 1–2 cm long; sepals suborbicular, 1.5–2 × 1.5 mm, pubescent to glabrescent externally, ± tomentose within, ciliate; stamens with filament glabrous or ± hairy in lower half, 2–2.5 mm long; anthers orbicular. Female flowers in axillary or solitary racemes; sepals suborbicular, 1.5–2.5 × 1.5–2 mm, pubescent to glabrescent, ciliate; ovary glabrous, with 4–8 placentas; styles 4–8, ± connate at base, then divergent and ± drooping, 0.5–1.5 mm long.

Fruits: globose or ellipsoid drupes, 1.5–2 cm in diameter, often ribbed when dry; seeds numerous (8–16), ellipsoid. - Pl. 1, 1–7, p. 9.

## Type

Burman (in Herb. Delessert), Java (holo-, G!)

## Distribution

This species is widely distributed (naturalized or cultivated) in tropical and subtropical countries of Africa, Asia and in certain islands of Oceania

## Ecology

Open forests, forest galleries, wooded savannas
