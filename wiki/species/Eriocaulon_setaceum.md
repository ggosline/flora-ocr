# *Eriocaulon setaceum* L. Planche 1

**Genus**: [[Eriocaulon]]
**Family**: [[Eriocaulaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 1 : 87 (1753)

## Description

Aquatic herb; stem floating beneath the water surface, slender, branched, up to 50 cm or more in length terminating in a rigid umbel emerging from the water. Leaves scattered on the stem and densely inserted, filiform, 3–10 cm × 0.1–0.3 mm, semi-translucent, yellow-green, soft, 1-nerved. Inflorescence: scapes up to about twenty, up to 30 cm in height and 0.6–0.8 mm in diameter, 5–7-sulcate; sheaths shorter than the leaves; capitulum subglobose, (2–)3–5(–6) mm in diameter, glabrous and blackish or with white pubescence; involucral bracts biseriate, obovate-oblong to suborbicular, 0.9–1.5 mm in length, blackish to yellowish-grey, firm, apex broadly rounded and scarious, weakly reflexed at maturity; floral bracts loosely arranged, cuneate-oblong, concave, blackish, external face of internal bracts sometimes with white papillae, acute; receptacle glabrous or finely pilose. Flowers shortly pedicellate, 3-merous, 0.9–1.4 mm in length, black. Male flowers: sepals unequally fused into a spathe or almost free, oblong, concave, glabrous or with white papillae near the rounded apex; petals small, glandular; stamens 6, anthers black. Female flowers: sepals 3, variable, subequal or the median smaller, obovate-oblong, deeply concave, dorsally gibbous with the broadly rounded apex, often spongy along the midline, rarely weakly keeled and winged with some white hairs in the upper part of the keel and the acute apex, apical margin entire or denticulate; petals subequal, linear-spatulate, glabrous or with white papillae at the apex, glands absent or small; ovary black, very tardily dehiscent. Seeds ellipsoid, 0.4–0.5 mm long, medium brown, but often appearing blackish due to the adherent ovary wall, transversely striated with white papillae.

## Distribution

Widely distributed throughout tropical regions, including Australia; in Africa: from Senegal to Sudan, southward through the centre and east, to Botswana; in Gabon, in the Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Rooted in the mud of stagnant or slow-flowing water up to 1 m in depth and with only the capitula emerged; in Gabon, up to 100 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Taxonomic notes: E. setaceum is immediately recognized by its distinctive long-floating stems and numerous filiform leaves. Unlike the usual situation in other species where mature seeds are easily expelled from the capitulum by means of capsule dehiscence, in E. setaceum the entire fruit falls into the water and sometimes opens into 3 separate mericarps. The seed then appears blackish due to the adherent ovary wall and surrounded by a layer of mucilage and opens only in water, expelled by a slit usually loculicidal. E. setaceum is one of the species of the genus with the widest distribution. The variability in the structure and pubescence of the bracts and flowers led to the description of several species within its range. In particular, specimens with blackish subglabrous capitula are often separated as E. melanocephalum, contrasting with forms having denser white pubescence. Nevertheless, the variation is continuous and not geographically correlated. The shared vegetative and seminal characters, distinctive and immediately recognizable within these minor variants, are therefore best retained as included within the single species E. setaceum. Bibliography: Hess (1955, 1957), Kimpouni, Lejoly & Lisowski (1992), Meikle (1968), Phillips (1997a, 2010, 2011)
