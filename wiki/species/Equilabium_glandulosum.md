# *Equilabium glandulosum* (Hook.f.) Mwany. & A.J.Paton Figure 4(B, C)

**Genus**: [[Equilabium]]
**Protologue**: Phytokeys 129 : 114 (2019)

## Description

Perennial aromatic herb, 1–2.5 m tall; stem ascending or erect, often swollen at the nodes, branched in the upper part, pubescent with spreading hairs, non-glandular, long and short, and also with short glandular hairs and yellowish or pale sessile glands, becoming more glandular in the inflorescence axis. Leaf: petiole 1.5–10 cm; blade cordate, the young sometimes ovate, 3–18 × 2–15 cm, acute to acuminate at the apex, dentate, with deltoid or sometimes irregular teeth, slightly pubescent, with pale sessile glands on the lower surface. Inflorescence branched, rather lax, with verticillasters of 4 to 6 flowers, spaced up to 20 mm apart; cymes of (1 to)3 flowers, shortly pedunculate; lower bracts sometimes resembling a leaf, but most entire or slightly dentate and much smaller than the leaves, ovate, 3 mm long, persistent, forming a discrete tuft above; peduncle 5–12 mm, rarely absent in some cymes. Flower: pedicel 3–12 mm; calyx shortly tubular-campanulate, 3 mm long and 8–11 mm in fruit, pubescent, with pale sessile glands, upper lip obovate, acute to apiculate at the apex, lateral and median lobes of the lower lip narrowly triangular, with the median ones slightly longer; corolla 10–15 mm long, pale blue, with scattered hairs and pale sessile glands on the lobes and tube, tube 5–7 mm long, upper lip erect, equal to the lower, lower lip 5–10 mm long, spreading or ascending, slightly hairy inside. Fruit: achenes broadly ovoid, 1.5–2 mm long, pale brown, with darker and reddish zones ± visible.

## Distribution

West and Central Africa and western parts of East Africa, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko), Gabon, Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Estuaire. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC).

## Ecology

Forest margins; in Gabon 25–450 m, at 700–1800 m in neighbouring countries. Uses: Cultivated in eastern Cameroon as a spice. B: Burkill (1995), Morales (2011), Morton (1963), Paton et al. (2019). Bibliography
