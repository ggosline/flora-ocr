# *Ritchiea aprevaliana* (De Wildeman & T. Durand) Wilczek

## Description

in HAUM. & WILCZ., Fl. Congo 2: 477 (1951); DE WOLF, Kirkia 1: 9 (1961); ELFFERS & AL., FTEA: 24 (1964); KERS, FCAM: 96 (1986).

Maerua aprevaliana DE WILD. & T. DUR., Ann. Mus. Congo, sér. Bot. 2 (1): 5 (1899) & 3 (1): 9 (1901); DE WILD., Ann. Mus. Congo, sér. Bot. 5 (1): 37 (1903) & 5 (2): 252 (1908) & 5 (3): 395 (1912); GILG, Bot. Jahrb. 33: 225 (1903); T. & H. DURAND, Syll. Fl. Congo: 30 (1909); GILG & BENEDICT, Bot. Jahrb. 53: 261 (1915); GILG, in ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1): 255 (1915); ROBYNS, Fl. Sperm. Parc nat. Albert 1: 208 (1948); TISSERANT & SILLANS, Not. Syst. 15: 200 (1956).

Low shrub reaching 2 m in height, sparsely branched. Trunk most often simple: tortuous sympode, at most 1.5 cm thick at the base, dark mat brown, numerous grey or brown lenticels, pulverulent, wood whitish and hard, pith narrow. Young branches rather slender, pale green, glabrous, lacking epidermal asperities, vaguely striated. Axillary buds solitary or rarely 2 serial buds, dormant most often except near the branch apices. Simple leaves (16-20-26(-36) × (5-)6-9(-14) cm, shortly petioled to subsessile, oblanceolate or elliptic, acute or acuminate sometimes shortly cuspidate, base narrowly cuneate or decurrent on the petiole. Leaves unequally distributed: ordinarily 5-7 subverti-

cillate at the branch apices, the lower half of which is leafless or bears a few scattered leaves. Blade becoming coriaceous with age, dark olive green lustrous above, paler and often greyish-green mat below; the upper surface finely wrinkled when dry (with high magnification!). Midvein clearly prominent and longitudinally ridged below, depressed above, straw-yellow. Lateral veins (8-)10-14, equidistant, prominent below, sunken above, gently curved from their base and terminating in loops forming a marginal vein; veinlets loosely reticulate, faintly marked on both surfaces. Petiole (2-)6-8-10 × 2-4 mm, yellowish-green, shortly decurrent, vaguely channelled or flat above, weakly articulate or not at the base. Stipules very little visible, broadly triangular, scarious, marcescent.

Inflorescence a short raceme often corymbiform, 5-15(-20)-flowered, approximately 4 cm wide at anthesis, erect. Racemes sometimes terminal but chiefly pseudo-terminal in the axil of leaves grouped at the branch apices of the previous year, or on old wood. In the axil of the same leaf 1-2 or 3 racemes arise. Rachis rigid and erect 0.9-5(-8) mm long, finely glandular and puberulent or glabrous, pale yellowish-green, with limited growth; base without leaves but sometimes bearing numerous very reduced prophylls, subulate, rigid soldered to the stipules. Floral bracts at most 4 mm long, linear or narrow and lanceolate falling ordinarily before anthesis; stipules very little visible, coriaceous, soldered to these bracts by their base, triangular, very slightly glandular and puberulent, persistently long. Pedicel 5-10 × 1 mm at anthesis, 10-15 × 1.5 mm for young fruits; young pedicel finely striated, densely and finely glandular and puberulent with slightly angular section and clearly articulate enlarged base. Receptacle cylindric or narrow and campanulate at anthesis, (3-)4(-5) × 1.5-2(-3) mm, very finely striated, dense puberulence; the lower half particularly inflated with age, coriaceous at fruiting, long persistent and marcescent. Sepals 4, equal 9-10(-13) × 2-3.5(-4) mm, pale green, oblong or lanceolate, acuminate, papyraceous, dorsal surface finely puberulent, margin whitish tomentulose; the sepals, vaguely 3-5-nerved, divaricate at anthesis, rather tardily caducous, sometimes fall together as if they were coherent. Petals 4, thin, 14-18 × 1.5-2.5 mm, entire fragile, narrow lanceolate or elliptic, long acuminate to cuspidate, apical part ± twisted, margin not undulate, blade gradually narrowed to a narrow base, white when fresh, early caducous. Androgynophore (5-)8-9 mm, exceeding the receptacle by 1-2 mm just after anthesis, 2-4 mm at fruiting, cylindric, slightly wider than the gynophore. Stamens ca. 35-50, all fertile and equal;

filaments 10-13 mm long, slightly exceeding the sepals, fine, glabrous, deciduous; anthers oblong 1.5-2 mm, apex very slightly mucronate, dorsobasifix, versatile; Gynophore somewhat shorter than the filaments at anthesis, 12-21 mm long at fruiting, glabrous. Ovary oblong to narrowly ellipsoid, unilocular, 25-35 × 1 mm, base acuminate, apex obtuse, glabrous, green and smooth; stigma sessile, inflated-capitate, much wider than the ovary apex at anthesis, blackish and enlarged on the developing fruit and forming a flat circular grey patch at the apex of the mature fruit.

Fruit oblong, ovoid (if containing few seeds) 1.5-3.2 × 1.2 cm, rounded or beaked at the apex, straight or somewhat curved (when oblong), dirty green or yellowish-brown at maturity, pericarp slightly coriaceous swollen at the level of the seeds, weakly 4-nerved when young, surface becoming verrucose with age. Seeds 1-5 per fruit, subspherical to ellipsoidal, diameter 6-9 mm; testa firmly adhering to the thin dissepiment separating the seeds, embryo somewhat curved, cotyledons semi-circular in transverse section, not or slightly folded in the longitudinal direction. Pl. 5, p. 47 & Map D, p. 7.

## Type

Dewèvre 787, Zaïre (holo-BR)

## Discussion

This species is clearly characterized by its foliage made up solely of simple leaves, rather short pedicels, flowers of little importance and an elongated receptacle; it resembles by its vegetative apparatus R. simplicifolia var. simplicifolia, from which it is difficult to distinguish in the vegetative state. Each raceme regularly produces several fruits. Young fruits can be encountered in flowers that have not yet lost their petals and stamens. The inflorescences are often visited by large red ants (Breteler & de Wilde 414); it is therefore possible that the flowers are adapted for pollination by ants.

This species is widely distributed in the Congolian phytogeographical domain; it is found in South Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, Congo, Zaire and Uganda. In Gabon it occurs in the eastern regions. It is encountered in the understory of deciduous forest often in clearings or other open biotopes. Flowering has been observed there in April, May and September, young fruits in November. No local name has been cited; apparently, this plant is little used.
