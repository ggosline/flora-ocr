# *Irvingia gabonensis* (Aubry-Lecomte ex O'Rorke) Baill.

**Family**: [[Irvingiaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Irvingia]]

## Description

Medium tree to 1 m diameter. Young branchlets glabrous, lenticellate and geniculate. Stipules very caducous, to 1.5 cm; petiole 6–10 mm. Blade obovate-elliptic or elliptic, shortly broadly acuminate, base cuneate to rounded, 3.5–16 × 2–8 cm, glossy on both faces; secondary veins 6–8 pairs; fine raised double network of transverse veinlets.

Axillary racemes short and slender; flowers grouped 3–5, greenish-yellow, pedicels 5–10 mm. Sepals 5, reflexed; petals 5, oblong, 4–5 mm, caducous; stamens 10 beneath a thick bright-yellow ten-grooved disc. Ovary flattened-ellipsoid, glabrous, 2-locular; stigma capitate. Fruit yellow-green, mango-like, broadly ellipsoid and compressed, about 5–6 × 5 × 4 cm; fibrous flesh surrounds a fibrillose stone and one flattened oily edible seed.

## Distribution and ecology

Widespread from Casamance to Angola and Uganda. In Gabon reported as uncommon in old forest and especially associated with formerly inhabited places. Seeds are roasted and ground to make dika or odika bread.

## Specimens examined

Gabon collections include Griffon du Bellay 217; Klaine 2353, 2571, 3363; Fleury 26533, 33552, 33552 bis, 33556; and Le Testu 1159, 8065, 8787, 9151, 9156.

## Vernacular names

*oba* (Mpongwe); *andok*, *hanedock*, *essou* (Fang); *mwiba* (Mayombe); *muvayu* (Punu); *mouiba* (Loango).
