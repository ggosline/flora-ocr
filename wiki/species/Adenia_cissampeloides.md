# *Adenia cissampeloides* (Planch. ex Hook.) Harms Planche 25.3

**Genus**: [[Adenia]]
**Family**: [[Passifloraceae]]
**Protologue**: Modecca cissampeloides Planch. ex Hook., Niger Fl. : 365 (1849)

## Description

in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. 3, 6a, Nachtr. 1: 255 (1897). Modecca cissampeloides Planch. ex Hook., Niger Fl.: 365 (1849). A. gracilis Harms in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. 3, 6a, Nachtr. 1: 255 (1897). A. reticulata (De Wild. & T.Durand) Engl. in Engl. & Drude, Veg. Erde 9, Pflanzenw. Afr. 3(2): 602 (1921). A. reticulata (De Wild. & T.Durand) Engl. var. cinerea W.J.de Wilde, Meded. Landbouwhogeschool Wageningen 71(18): 270, fig. 43 (1971). A. guineensis W.J.de Wilde, Meded. Landbouwhogeschool Wageningen 71(18): 259 (1971). Herbaceous to woody vine, reaching 30 m in length; stem at base reaching 10 cm in diameter. Stipules transversely ovate, ± deeply incised. Leaves with petiole 0.75–12 cm; blade highly variable, broadly ovate, (1–)3–14 cm in length, the upper ones entire, the largest ones slightly 3(–5)-lobed, base truncate, apex obtuse, upper surface often with a paler central zone, discolorous, the underside distinctly lighter, whitish; gland at base of blade single, spatulate, laminar glands minute, very numerous and distributed in a homogeneous manner, sometimes absent; sterile tendrils simple, up to 20 cm. Male inflorescence with up to 20 flowers, the female one with 1–6 flowers; peduncle 0.5–20 cm; tendrils 0–3, 0.5–2(–3.5) cm; bracts and bracteoles triangular, 0.5–1 mm in length, acute to ± denticulate at apex, with entire margins, sometimes undulate. Male flower with pedicel 1–15 mm and stipe 1.5–8 mm, ± campanulate, 6–12 mm in length; hypanthium cupuliform, shallow, 0.5–2 mm in height, septa not prominent; sepals free, oblong-elliptic, 5–10 mm in length, obtuse to acute at apex, punctate or not; petals narrowly ovate, ± as long as the sepals, + obtuse, with finely fimbriate-laciniate margins; corona absent or poorly developed and annular, hypanthium glands absent; stamens inserted at base of hypanthium, filaments 1–3.5 mm, free to connate, anthers 3–6 mm; pistillode approximately 0.5 mm in length. Female flower similar to male flower, but with pedicel 1–6 mm and stipe 0.2–6 mm, 3–8 mm in length; hypanthium 0.5–1 mm in height, septa 0–0.2 mm in height; sepals narrowly ovate to ovate, 2.5–7.5 mm in length; petals narrowly ovate to linear, 1–6.5 mm in length, acute at apex, with (sub-)entire margin; corona poorly to well developed, annular, fleshy, 0.5 mm in height; staminodes 0.2–1 mm; ovary broadly ovoid to ellipsoid, 3(–6)-ribbed, 2–6 × 1.5–3.5 mm, smooth or finely verrucose, punctate or not, gynophore 0.3–1 mm; styles entirely or partially connate, 0.5–1 mm, stigmas 3(–4) sessile, reniform or globose, 1–1.5 mm in diameter, papillose. Fruit globose to ellipsoid or ± fusiform, 3–6-angular, 1–4 × 0.75–3 cm, with coriaceous to woody pericarp, ± smooth to strongly verrucose, sometimes foveolate, with gynophore 0.5–2 cm. Seeds 30–60 per capsule, ovoid, 3–5.5 × 2.5–4 mm, with 5–11 fossettes on a meridian line.

## Distribution

Tropical Africa, from Guinea to Sudan and from southern Ethiopia to South Africa, also in the Seychelles; common in Gabon, present throughout the country.

## Ecology

undemanding, in shaded humid places and in savanna, forest margins, fallows, cultivations; in Gabon up to 900 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1550 m. Flowers and fruits from August to March. Uses: Used in medicine; used for making ties; crushed stems are thrown into water to asphyxiate fish.
