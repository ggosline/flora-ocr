# *Oncinotis glabrata* (Baill.) Stapf ex Hiern Planche 78(1), 80

**Genus**: [[Oncinotis]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Cat. afr. pl. 1 : 674 (1898)

## Description

Liane reaching at least 50 m in length; branches glabrous. Leaf: petiole 7–24 mm, glabrous or rarely puberulent, with small colleters above, often in 1 to 3 groups of elliptic to ovate or exceptionally obovate leaves ± as long as wide, 3.9–15.4 × 1.5–7.4 cm, cuneate at base, acuminate or exceptionally rounded or emarginate at apex, glabrous or rarely puberulent on midvein and secondary veins; secondary veins 5 to 12 pairs, tertiary venation clearly visible, reticulate to weakly scalariform; domatia sometimes absent, generally consisting of small pits sometimes with ciliate margin. Inflorescence 2.5–12.5 × 1.5–5 cm; peduncle and axes with rust-brown pubescence, glabrescent; bracts 1.2–1.7 × 0.3–1.2 mm. Flower: pedicel 2–5 mm; calyx with lobes 1.5–3.6 × 1.1–2.2 mm, with rust-brown external pubescence; corolla 7–11 mm long in bud, yellowish, glabrous or puberulent on exterior, generally only at base, with turbinate tube 2.5–4.7 × 2–2.5 mm, with tufts of hairs within, with triangular lobes, ± as long as the tube 2–4 × 1–2 mm with long crown of ± mm; anther 2.4–2.8 mm long; pistil 2.9–3.5 mm long. Fruit: follicles fusiform or almost often recurved or incurved forming an angle of ± of ± × 1.2–6 cm; exocarp very hard, with undulate wings, 0.1–3 mm wide, sometimes with numerous small protuberances, puberulent and glabrescent. Distribution from Guinea to Uganda and Tanzania and as far as Angola, Gabon. Estuary Ngou, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Uses In Cameroon and equatorial Guinea, the latex of the bark is sprayed on lesions caused by yaws. The plant has particular ornamental interest. In Nigeria, the stems are used to make bowstrings. B: Boone (2008b), de Kruif (1985), Omino (2002). BIBLIOGRAPHY
