# *Samanea leptophylla* (Harms) Brenan & Brummitt

Tree reaching 25 m in height with a diameter of 1 m. Crown in plateau form. Bole with flared and bosselated base, axis bosselated and minutely wrinkled. Bark with dark gray to grayish-violet rhytidome, exfoliating; reddish-brown externally on thick section of approximately 1 cm, fibrous, orange-pink, with sticky exudation. Outer sapwood orange-yellow, fibrous. Young twig densely pubescent tomentose reddish-brown. Older twig pubescent reddish-brown. Stipules early caducous, densely tomentose reddish-brown yellowish, elliptic-oblong to asymmetrically lanceolate, 8–8.5 × 2–2.5 mm, apex obtuse. Petiole densely pubescent to tomentose reddish-brown, 0.5–2.2 (3) cm long, canaliculate above bearing a very salient cupular discoid gland most often at its apex. Rachis pubescent tomentose to pubescent reddish-brown, 8.5–19 cm long, ± transversely flattened, slightly widened at base; upper surface flattened to canaliculate bearing glands between the terminal pinnae. Pinnae 12–34 pairs, oblong or broadly elliptic-oblong. Pinna axis pubescent as previously described, reaching 4 cm in length, with dorsal keel, bearing 1–3 glands at apex; short mucro pubescent ± obtuse at apex on lower surface. Leaflets 15–37 (45) pairs, sessile. Basal pair of leaflets very modified; abaxial leaflet ± reduced and adaxial leaflet absent or reduced to a stipel. Blade glabrous on both surfaces except margin ± ciliate; dark green to silvery green above and ± brownish green below when dry; asymmetrically oblong; slightly falciform; reaching 3–4.5 × 0.50–0.75 mm; base asymmetric; proximal edge rounded-truncate and distal edge attenuate-rounded, apex obtuse subacute. Primary vein weakly salient above and salient below. Secondary veins not visible to weakly salient above and ± salient below. Veinlets not visible.

Inflorescences in capitate-form spikes, axillary, fascicled by 2–4. Peduncle pubescent to pubescent tomentose reddish-brown, apex ± claviform. Bracteoles pubescent externally, spatulate, incurved, 0.5 mm long; apex acute. Floral bud obovoid with stamens having filament initially straight then reflexed and ± spiraled; floral bud of apical flower oblongoid larger. Flowers white on living plant, fragrant. Basal flowers with pubescent pedicel 0.75–2.5 mm long. Calyx narrowly obconic, pubescent puberulent externally, 2–2.25 mm long, free lobes unequal triangular to semicircular 0.25 mm long, incurved toward interior, apex obtuse. Petals pubescent externally, ± united on 1/3–1/2 of length, 4–4.5 mm long, free lobes ± elliptic, (0.5) 1.5–2 mm, apex thickened weakly hirsute, acute. Stamens 11–14 mm long, united in non-exsert tube 1.5–2.5 mm long, anther oblong 0.20–0.30 mm long. Pistil with stipe 0.25–0.75 (1.25) mm long; ovary obovoid sparsely pubescent, 1–1.5 mm long, with 25 ovules; style 1.2–1.6 mm long terminated by a darker capitate stigma. Apical flower sessile¹, conical pubescent calyx, 2.5 mm long, free lobes triangular, 0.75 mm long, apex acute; petals pubescent externally in their free parts, 6–6.5 mm long, united in tube at base, free lobes elliptic, 4.5 mm long, apex thickened acute; stamens approximately 1.2 cm long, united in irregular tube at base for approximately 4 mm, continuing in exsert phalanges

## Type

Mildbraed 3520, Zaire : Kimuenza, 17 km S Kinshasa (holo-, HBG ; iso-, HBG, P !)

## Discussion

This species appears very close to Samanea dinklagei (HARMS) KEAY in its vegetative apparatus. However the presence of glands on the pinnae of S. leptophylla and the clearly pedicelled flowers (at least for the peripheral ones) allow very reliable separation.

We have found in certain flowers pistils with 3 ovaries.

The distinction between S. guineensis and S. leptophylla rests solely on a difference in the number of pinnae and leaflets per pinna. Counts show numerous intermediate cases. Under these circumstances, we do not maintain the distinction between these two taxa. S. leptophylla remains valid on account of its priority.

This plant is Guinean. It is encountered from Cameroon to Zaïre where it is never abundant; then in Zambia. It is localized at forest edge or along watercourses. To be sought in Gabon.
