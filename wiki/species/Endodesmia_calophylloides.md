# *Endodesmia calophylloides* Benth. Planche 3

**Genus**: [[Endodesmia]]
**Family**: [[Clusiaceae]]

## Description

in Benth. & Hook.f., Gen. pl. 1 : 166 (1862). Tree up to 40 m tall; trunk up to 80 cm in diameter, with buttresses up to 80 cm tall; branchlets smooth, glabrous. Leaf: petiole 3–6 mm, plane-convex, 2-winged above; blade elliptic-oblong to elliptic (on seedlings and young trees sometimes narrower), (4–)6–11(–14) × 1.5–3 cm, cuneate to obtuse at base, apex often caudate but sometimes acuminate to attenuate, papyraceous to subcoriaceous, discolorous; midvein prominent below, depressed above, lateral veins 12–15 per cm, prominent below and often also above. Inflorescence in subumbelliform or fascicled cymes 3–4 cm long, dense to open; peduncle 0.5–3.5 cm. Flower: pedicel 2–4 mm, accrescent, articulate at base; sepals narrowly elliptic to ovate, 2.5–10 × 1–3 mm, thick, green; petals obovate, 8–10 × 5–8 mm, yellow but brown at base; staminal tube 5–6 mm tall, yellow, anthers ovate, small, long-apiculate; ovary ovoid; style 5 mm long. Fruit: pedicel 7–17 mm long and 4–10 mm thick, ± tetragonous, red; drupe globose to ovoid, 13–20 × 10–20 mm, pointed, greenish-brown or brown at maturity, with rugose surface. Seed ellipsoid, 11–17 × 6–16 mm, with smooth testa.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda); uncommon in Gabon, not yet known from Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, especially in riparian and swampy forest, sometimes in mangroves, on sandy or clayey soil; in Gabon, up to 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 950 m

## Vernacular names

nom ossol, noum-ossol, noung-ossol (fang), mongoli-nyondje (eschira). Usage : Le bois est dur, mais son utilisation n’a jamais été rapportée. En République du Congo, le jus des feuilles a été appliqué dans les yeux contre les filaires

## Source note

Regularly, the flowers show a monstrosity, probably caused by galls, and produce numerous leafy structures at the center. The species shows remarkable morphological variation. The inflorescence can be open to compact and the sepals can be relatively small to quite broad. It seems to us that compact inflorescences often bear flowers with comparatively broad sepals. We hesitated to delimit these two forms as different taxa (subspecies?). This variation could also be due to the fact that the flowers are not bisexual, as indicated in the literature, but rather functionally male and female. This situation should be studied further. Bi: Bamps (1970a), Hutchinson et al. (1954), Pellegrin (1959), Spirlet (1966). Bibliography
