# *Angylocalyx pynaertii* De Wild. Planche 12

**Genus**: [[Angylocalyx]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Mus. Congo Belge, Bot. sér, 5, 3(2) : 196 (1910)

## Description

Ann. Mus. Congo Belge, Bot. sér, 5, 3(2) : 196 (1910). A. zenkeri Harms, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 305 (1910). A. zenkeri Harms var. gossweileri (Harms) Pellegrin, Legum. Gabon : 262 (1949). A. gossweileri Baker f., J. Bot. 66 ( Suppl. 1) : 133 (1928).

Leaf. – 11. Inflorescence. – 12. Pod. Drawing by Maria Povysysh (©), reproduced with permission from Povydysh et al. (2010).

Tree reaching 35 m in height; trunk fairly straight; bark grey, base of trunk with a few small buttresses. Leaf: petiole 1–3 cm; rachis 6–12 cm; leaflets 5 to 9, with elliptic blade, 6–17 × 3–6 cm, base rounded-obtuse, apex long-acuminate, thick and ± coriaceous; lateral nerves in 3 to 5 pairs, alternate to subopposite, reticulations not prominent. Inflorescence cauline or axillary, 5–18 cm in length, 2 or 3 per node, glabrous or somewhat pubescent, with 10 to 20 flowers. Flower: pedicel 7–10 mm; receptacle concave or conical, 5–7 mm in length and reaching 8 mm in width at the apex; calyx cupular 4–6 × 3 mm, extension of receptacle straight; corolla cream to pink, standard with narrow claw 8 mm, blade oval to suborbicular, 8–10 × 5–10 mm, other petals similar but narrower and 5 mm in length; stamens ± 15 mm in length; ovary ± 15 mm in length, glabrous. Pod reaching 18 × 2 cm, glabrous, yellow-orange, with fleshy pericarp, brown pulp. Seed with yellowish cotyledons.

## Distribution

Southern Nigeria to the Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

terra firme forests; in Gabon at 60–480 m altitude, elsewhere at 350–800 m. Uses: The wood is yellow and homogeneous; in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the wood is used in the construction of chairs. Bibliography: Povydysh et al. (2010), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Toussaint (1953), Yakovlev (1968)
