# *Strophanthus sarmentosus* DC. var. sarmentosus Planche 108 ; Figure 12(C)

**Genus**: [[Strophanthus]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]

## Description

Bull. Sci. Soc. Philom. Paris Shrub sarmentose reaching 4 m in height or liana reaching 40 m in length, deciduous, flowers appearing with or before the leaves, exudate clear or white, stem reaching cm in diameter; bark corky; branchlets glabrous or rarely puberulent. Leaves sometimes whorled in 3 or 4, petiole 0.5–5 mm, limb elliptic to ovate 4–18 × 2–12 cm, rounded to cuneate at base, acuminate at apex, glabrous; secondary venation 3 to 6 pairs, with an angle of 45–65° to the midvein. Inflorescence condensed, with 1 to 5 (to 11) flowers, axes puberulent, bracts of 2–6 × 1–3 mm. Flower: pedicel 2–12 mm; calyx with subequal lobes, 6–20 × 2–10 mm, tinged with purple, puberulent on the outside; corolla with tube 17–40 mm long, white becoming yellow in the lower part, pink becoming purple higher up, widening over 20–36% of its length into an upper cupuliform part, 15–30 mm wide at the throat, crown with narrowly triangular and often undulate lobes, 5–22 mm long, white to yellow with pink or violet streaks, corolla lobes ovate, 7–20 × 6–18 mm, white becoming yellow on both faces, pink or violet near the base on the outside, narrowed into pendulous tails 3.5–11.5 cm × 1–2.5 mm, pale yellow; stamens included, anther 6–11 mm long; style 9–18 mm. Fruit: follicles divergent at of 13–28 × 1.4–4.4 cm, attenuate into a broad obtuse apex, exocarp thick and hard, sulcate, glabrous, lenticellate. Seed 8–20 × 2–4 mm, pubescent; beak glabrous over 10–50 mm, bearing a coma over 11–40 mm of its length; coma 33–105 mm long.

## Distribution

From Senegal to Uganda and Angola; in Gabon: Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest in Gabon at 0–700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1,100 m. Uses: The seed is used to manufacture arrow poison. The plant is part of the pharmacopoeia and traditional medicine. The root is taken to treat gonorrhea, leprosy and joint pain and hernia. The latex is applied to abrasions and wounds as a cicatrizant. In the 1950s, a seed extract was used in the United States and Europe to combat rheumatoid arthritis. The stem would serve to make bows and the bark to make cords, hats and mats. The plant is ornamental for its flowers.

## Source note

The second variety, var. globriflorus Monachino, which differs notably in the axes and calyx glabrous and the leaf limb generally narrower, is known from Guinea. B: Beentje (1982, 2008d, 2021), Omino (2002), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). BIBLIOGRAPHY
