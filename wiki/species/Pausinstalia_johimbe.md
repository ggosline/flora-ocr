# *Pausinstalia johimbe* (K. Schum.)

## Description

Pierre ex Dup. et B. (PL. 10)

Yohimbé

— Raymond-Hamet et H. Vignes, La Presse Médicale 83 : 3-15 (16 Oct. 1926). — Walker et Sillans, Pl. Ut. Gabon : 368 (1961). — F.W.T.A. ed. 2, 2 : 112 (1963).

Medium to large tree 30-35 m tall with a trunk 30-50(60) cm in diameter. Bark ± longitudinally fissured. Branchlets glabrous. Internodes trigonous with rounded ridges. Stipules glabrous, attenuate, very acute, 15-20 × 5 mm. Leaves whorled in threes. Petiole 0-8 mm long; lamina glabrous, subcoriaceous, reddish-brown or black when dry, elongately obovate, (11) 15-38 (43) × (5) 6-13 (19) cm; base elongately attenuate and ± abruptly obtuse or cordate, apex very briefly and often abruptly acuminate. 12-20 pairs of secondary veins. Veinlet network prominent on the upper surface when dry.

Terminal paniculate inflorescences with whorled ramifications, reaching 8 to 30 cm long; peduncle with 3 ridges, 0.5-5 cm long. Main axes glabrous, the ultimate ones ± puberulent. Buds green, yellow, or pinkish. Flowers fragrant, white (or dark purple: Le T. 8225), 4-6-merous, grouped subsessile. Ovary 1 mm, puberulent, sometimes trilocular. Calyx lobes acute, c. 0.5 mm. Corolla with tube narrow for 0.5 mm then widened for 1.5 mm, and pubescent inside. 4-6 lobes very briefly emarginate, erect; bearing a filiform appendage 15-20 mm long. Anthers 1.5 mm, with apex attenuate acute, exceeding the emargination of the corolla lobes. Style 1 mm including the stigma.

Fruit 10-16 × 5-7 mm; 2-(3) valves ± retained between them at the base slightly emarginate at the apex; pedicel 1-2 mm. Seeds 8-9 × 1.5 mm.

## Discussion

The regularity of the foliage and the size of the leaves have already caused young specimens of this species to be confused with Schumanniophyton.

In southern Cameroon, Kribi region, and at Souanké, congolese region near northeast Gabon, specimens of P. johimbe are found which are distinguished by their slightly pubescent corolla. The species is distributed from southern Nigeria to the congolese Mayombe.

The bark of P. johimbe is well known for its powerful stimulating, antihypnotic, and aphrodisiac properties. The active element or yohimbine, is an alkaloid commonly used in therapy in the form of hydrochloride. Its physiological action is complex: modification of the rhythms of respiration and blood circulation; it also exhibits a local anesthetic action. Yohimbine is relatively non-toxic, the lethal dose being 20 milligrams per kg body weight. The medical and veterinary value of yohimbine justifies the production of P. johimbe bark. Commercial exploitation of this product is already being carried out in the Kribi region of Cameroon. R. Paris and R. Letouzey (journ. Agr. et Bot. Appl. 1957 and 1960) demonstrated that it is the barks of the roots and the trunk, principally at the level of the first branches, which present the highest yohimbine contents.

Adulteration of the product with barks, often highly toxic, of false yohimbé (Pausinystalia or Corynanthe sp.) yield after extraction inferior or very minimal percentages of usable product.

Other uses: the bark when unrolled is used in the construction of huts; it appears sometimes to be used as a fish poison, but the specific determination of the

product used should be verified. The wood is neglected because of its commonly mediocre diameters, it is nevertheless of fine quality.
