# *Gnetum africanum* Welw. Planche 7

**Genus**: [[Gnetum]]
**Family**: [[Gnetaceae]]
**Protologue**: Trans. Linn. Soc. London 27 : 73 (1869)

## Description

Thoa africana (Welw.) Doweld, Turczaninowia 3(4) : 32 (2000). Sarmentose vine, dioecious, up to 10 m in length, sometimes more; branches slightly thickened at the nodes, glabrous. Leaves decussate or sometimes verticillate by 3; petiole up to 1 cm in length, channelled above; blade ovate-oblong to elliptic-oblong or rarely lanceolate, 5–14 × 2–5 cm, base attenuate, apex abruptly acuminate to finely apiculate or obtuse, papyraceous, glabrous, pale green above, paler beneath; lateral veins 3–6 pairs, strongly arcuate and forming a loop near the margin. Strobili unbranched, axillary or terminal, up to 8 cm in length, articulate; peduncle 1–1.5 cm. Male strobili solitary, but often grouped by 3 at the apex of branches; internodes slender and cylindric; staminal column long-exsert from the involucre, bearing 2 sessile anthers. Female strobili solitary, with internodes slightly turbinate; involucre saucer-shaped; flowers 2–4 per node, 2–2.5 mm in length, with moniliform hairs at the base; ovule sessile. Seed ellipsoid, 10–15 × 4–8 mm, apiculate, enclosed within the fleshy orange or red involucre at maturity, with copious endosperm.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon uncommon throughout the country

## Ecology

humid forest, forest edges, disturbed forest, gallery forests and forest fallows; in Gabon up to 550 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Uses: The supple and strong stems are used as snares to make traps. The seeds are eaten after cooking in certain provinces of the country. The leaves are widely used as a vegetable and form part of the diet of all social classes. Beyond this food use, they are also used in traditional medicine: treatment of bad mood or misfortune; as a purgative against nausea or as an antidote for certain poisonings; reduces labour pains (herbal tea); against haemorrhoids and hypertension, sore throat and limiting the effects of intoxication. They are also used to treat boils and fungal infections of the fingers
