# *Mapania purpurascens* (Cherm.) Lye Planche 112

**Genus**: [[Mapania]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lidia 7 : 90 (2011)

## Synonyms

- Hypolytrum purpurascens Cherm., Bull. Soc. Bot. France 80 : 508 (1933).

## Description

Robust herb, with stolons covered in scales and terminating in a stem; central stem, solitary, 40–100 cm × 1.5–4 mm, triangular, green to slightly brownish, smooth below, distinctly scabrous in the upper part. Leaves numerous, mostly basal, with 1–3 blades arranged along the stem; sheath green or pale reddish-brown to purplish; blade flat or slightly folded, the largest 30–100 × 1–3 cm, green, often densely spotted with purple, with scabrous margin at least near the apex, gradually narrowed to an acute apex. Inflorescence terminal, in a loose or fairly dense panicle, 3–15 × 3–15 cm, light to dark brown, with 3–10 primary branches 0.5–4 cm; involucral bracts foliaceous, often spreading, linear, the largest 10–25 × 0.7–2.5 cm, mainly green, apex acute; spikelets linear–lanceolate to obovoid, 5–8 × 2–5 mm, obtuse, brown; spicoid bracts 2, oblong to obovate, 3–5 × 1.5–2 mm, glabrous, with narrow central vein terminating in the rounded apex or slightly exceeding it. Flower: floral bracts 2, narrow, lanceolate, 2–2.5 mm long, keel ciliate; style with 2 branches. Achene ellipsoid, 2–2.5 × 1–1.5 mm, lower part reddish-brown, irregularly wrinkled, upper part almost smooth, somewhat spongy, pale brown, apex acute.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; widespread in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (30 collections)

## Ecology

humid forests, in areas sometimes flooded along watercourses; in Gabon, up to 600 m altitude, in Cameroon, up to 650 m. Taxonomic note: recognized at maturity by its achenes, which are pale brown with purple dots and smooth or slightly ribbed. Other parts of the plant also sometimes have a purplish tint. It is very close to M. afro-orientalis which differs from it by its constricted achene. It is also close to M. chevalieri, which has larger spikelets (9–12 mm long versus 5–8 mm) and different achenes (entirely wrinkled in M. chevalieri). M. heterophylla differs by its smaller spikelets and achenes. b: Nelmes (1955a), Hooper & Napper (1972). Bibliography
