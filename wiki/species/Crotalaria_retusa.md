# *Crotalaria retusa* L. var. retusa

**Genus**: [[Crotalaria]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 715 (1753)

## Description

Annual or short-lived perennial herb, 1–1.3 m in height; stem erect; branchlets striate, puberulent to pubescent. Stipules subulate, 1–5 mm long. Leaf simple; petiole 2–4 mm; blade oblanceolate-oblong, 4–7.5 × 1.3–2.8 cm, cuneate at base, apex obtuse to emarginate, glabrous above, appressed-pubescent below. Inflorescence axillary or terminal, in pseudoracemes 10–30 cm long, lax, with 10–20 flowers; bracts small, subulate-lanceolate, 2–6 mm long. Flower: bracteoles filiform, 1–2 mm, on the pedicel 3–7 mm; calyx 11–15 mm long, with acute lobes, somewhat longer than the tube, glabrous or slightly appressed-puberulent; standard obovate-rounded, 15–20 mm long, yellow or purple, striate, wings oblong-obovate, longer than the keel, yellow, the back often with some hairs on the midvein, keel geniculate-erect, 13–22 mm long, with short and twisted rostrum. Pod briefly stipitate, cylindric to oblong, 3.5–4 × 1–1.8 cm, glabrous, black when mature, with 12 to 20 seeds. Seed oblique-cordiform, ± 5 × 3.5 × 2 mm, brown or yellowish, finely papillose.

## Distribution

Widely distributed throughout all tropics, perhaps of Asian origin; in Gabon, reported from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Coastal stations, fields and fallows, roadsides, herbaceous savannas and ruderal stations; in Gabon at 0–200 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1800 m. Uses: The species is used as a green manure and as a cover crop. The flowers are edible as a vegetable. The plant has medicinal properties.

## Vernacular names

pistache marron, pois franc marron (fr.) ; devil bean, large yellow rattlebox, wedgeleaved crotalaria (angl.)

## Source note

The var. tunguensis (Lima) Polhill has short and dense racemes and subglabrous calyces. It is distributed in East Africa.
