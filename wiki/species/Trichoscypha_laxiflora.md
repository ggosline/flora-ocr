# *Trichoscypha laxiflora* Engl. Planche 21(F)

**Genus**: [[Trichoscypha]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 15 : 110 (1892)

## Description

T. dusenii Engl., Veg. Erde 9, Pflanzenw. Afrikas III(2) : 191 (1921). Shrub reaching 2.5 m in height. Leaf: petiole ± cylindrical, slightly wrinkled, 7–15(–20) cm; rachis 4–6 cm; leaflets (1–)3 to 5(–13), petiolule 3–7 mm, with very variable blade in form and size, broadly elliptic to obovate-oblong, (5–)12–30(–40) × (2–)5–10(–16) cm, (1.5–)2–3(–5) times longer than wide, cuneate to rounded at the base, acuminate over 10–20 mm at the apex, ± glabrous except sometimes pubescent on the impressed midvein, with small brown dots below, with 3 to 12(–17) pairs of lateral veins impressed above. Male inflorescence in loose panicle, multiflorous, the female less loose, ± pauciflorous, glabrous to sparsely puberulent; bracts shorter than the axillary floral cluster. Male flower dark red to purple; pedicel slender, (2–)3–5(–7) mm, glabrous to sparsely puberulent; sepals ovate, 0.8 × 0.6 mm, ± glabrous; petals valvate, spreading, ± 2 × 1 mm, with papillose margin, with visible veinlets; stamens distinctly shorter than the petals, with anther 0.2 mm in length; disk square, glabrous. Female flower with pedicel more rigid than in the male flower, 2–2.5 mm, sparsely puberulent; sepals thick, ovate, ± 1 × 0.8 mm, ± glabrous; petals thick, valvate, ± erect, ovate, 2–2.5 × 2 mm, glabrous to sparsely puberulent; staminodes slightly longer than the disk; disk glabrous, with small brown dots; pistil 3-merous, white, glabrous, ovary 1 mm in diameter; stigmas ± sessile. Fruit ellipsoid, 1.5–4.5 × 1–2.5 cm, orange, glabrous, smooth; pulp sweet. Seed: cotyledons purple.

## Distribution

Southeast Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon and Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Understory of primary and secondarized forest; in Gabon at 190–850 m elevation, elsewhere at 50–920 m. B: Breteler (2004), Van der Veken (1960a). Bibliography
