# *Campylospermum engama* (De Wild.) Farron Planche 10

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 35 : 396 (1965)

## Description

Tree reaching 11 m in height; branchlets greenish. Stipules caducous, triangular, approximately 2 mm in length. Leaf: petiole (5–)10–18(–25) mm; lamina narrowly elliptic to narrowly elliptic-oblong, (9–)16–25(–39) × (2.5–)4–7(–9) cm, ratio 3–4(–5.5), typically attenuate at base and decurrent on petiole, acute to obtuse at apex, sometimes mucronate, papyraceous, serrate throughout the margin; primary vein plane on upper surface, prominent on lower surface, lateral veins generally 12 to 19 pairs, prominent on both surfaces, tertiary venation scalariform, more or less perpendicular to primary vein, distinct on both surfaces, connected by veinlets. Inflorescence terminal or sometimes axillary, branched, lax, main axis more or less angular to flattened, (20–)25–33(–35) cm; gemmate scales absent; racemes (3–)4 to 8, (3–)8–23(–40) cm in length, spreading, sometimes with secondary racemes; cymules distant 1–2(–3) cm, 1- to 8(–11)-flowered; bracts caducous. Flower: pedicel 5–10(–15) mm, articulate 1–5(–8) mm from base; sepals oval, 5–7 × 2–3 mm in flower, 8–10 × 3–5 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 7–10 × 3–6 mm, clawed, apex rounded; stamens: anthers 4–7 mm in length; style 5–7 mm. Fruit: receptacle approximately 6 × 5 mm; drupels 3 or 4 well developed per receptacle, broadly ellipsoid to globose, 6–9 × 5–7 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Cameroon, southern Central African Republic (Sangha-Mbaéré), Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, rather rare and found in Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Primary forest, secondary forest, gallery forests, sometimes in dry forests, on sandy soil; in Gabon, at 100–550 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 620 m. Use: The species is used as timber for construction (Bamps & Farron, 1967). Taxonomic note: Because both have a long petiole, C. engama could be confused with C. laeve, particularly if material of the latter has inflorescences with long racemes. However, C. laeve is easily distinguished by its thickened and often revolute margin and notably longer sepals (see also note under C. laeve). Bibliography: Bamps & Farron (1967), Bissiengou (2014), Farron (1963, 1985).
