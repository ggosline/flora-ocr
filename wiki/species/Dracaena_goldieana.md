# *Dracaena goldieana* Bullen ex Mast. & T.Moore Planche 11

**Genus**: [[Dracaena]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gard. Chron. 1872 : 1232 (1872)

## Description

Draco goldieana (Bullen ex Mast. & T.Moore) Kuntze, Revis. Gen. Pl. 2: 710 (1891). Pleomele goldieana (Bullen ex Mast. & T.Moore) N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1914: 278 (1914). Shrub up to 4 m tall; trunk simple. Leaves spiral, concentrated apically over 1–1.5 m where they are uniformly distributed, leaving the stem visible between the sheaths or cataphylls; sheath fairly open, embracing the stem over clearly more than the circumference; pseudipetiole shorter than the blade, cylindric, with involute margins, green to greenish-purple, base passing abruptly into the sheath; blade oval to elliptic, up to 38 × 13 cm, base rounded, apex acuminate to mucronate reaching 8 mm, firm, coriaceous, upper surface dark green, transversally and irregularly striped with pale green to grey striae, with 4 to 6 pairs of evident veins, lower surface pale green to greyish-green, matte. Inflorescence capitate, sessile, dense, with ± 30 flowers, ± 6 cm in diameter, subtended by broadly elliptic bracts, ± 2 × 1.8 cm, with cuspidate apex, green to red, scarious; floral bracts persistent, elliptic, up to 18 mm long, with cuspidate apex, scarious, dark brown to black. Flower with sweet fragrance; pedicel 0–1 mm; perianth 20–25 mm long, whitish-red after anthesis, with lobes shorter than the tube, spreading to reflexed; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 1 mm shorter than the lobes, filament filiform, white, anthers 1.5 mm long, pale yellow; ovary oblong, up to 3 × 2 mm; style exsert up to 1 mm, white, stigma ± 0.8 mm in diameter. Fruit subsessile, oblong, ± 15 × 18 mm, orange, smooth, shiny, surrounded by dried floral bracts. Seed not seen.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Understory of deeply shaded primary forests, on well-drained sandy soil, sometimes on rocky soil; in Gabon at 30–600 m altitude, elsewhere at 30–1600 m. Flowering recorded in June and October. Use: Ornamental plant, cultivated outdoors and indoors throughout the world.

## Source note

In various botanical gardens, a plant is cultivated under the name of D. goldieana which in sterile state resembles this species closely. However, the difference is that these plants have a spiciform inflorescence with glomerules and not a capitate inflorescence, as is usual in D. goldieana. The provenance data are not clear; it could be a hybrid between D. goldieana and D. fragrans. B: Baker (1898), Bos (1984), Damen et al. (2018), Hepper (1968), Mwachala (2005), Velayos & Cabezas (2018)
