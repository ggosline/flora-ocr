# *Landolphia stenogyna* Pichon Planche 68

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 151 (1953)

## Description

Large liane; branches with pale lenticels, glabrous. Leaf: petiole 6–20 mm, glabrous or subpubescent; blade ovate to obovate, 1–4 times as long as wide, 1.5–9 × 0.8–3.5 cm, rounded to acute at base, acuminate at apex, glabrous, with small black glandular dots beneath; secondary nerves 5 to 12 pairs, curved, often with 1 or 2 less prominent between two secondary ones, tertiary venation reticulate, visible beneath. Inflorescence axillary, with 1 to 3 flowers. Flower: pedicel 2–6 mm; bracteoles 2 to 10, generally in pairs, the distal ones epicalyx-like of 0.7–1.2 mm, as long as wide or 1.5–2 mm long as long as wide; sepals of calyx globose to subglobose, 1–2.5 times as long as wide, 0.5–1.2 × 0.4–1 mm, membranous, ciliate, otherwise glabrous or with some hairs at the apex exterior; corolla white to yellowish-green or reddish, with tube 15–21 mm long, 1.1–3.1 mm wide at base, 1.5–2.1 mm in the middle, 2.4–3 mm at anther level and 2.2–2.9 mm at throat, glabrous outside, inside hairy from base up to 0.5–2 mm below the anthers; lobes narrowly ovate to elliptic, 1–2.5 times as long as the tube, 0.6–2 times as long as wide, 0.8–2.2 × 0.4–1 mm, inside hairy on the auricles otherwise glabrous or nearly so. Stamens inserted at 0.3–0.65 of tube length, exserted over 1.5 mm to included up to 1 mm, anther 1.3–2.2 mm long; pistil 12–18 mm long, ovary glabrous in the lower part over 1.2–4.5 mm, otherwise hairy or pubescent, more densely towards the apex; style 8.5–11 mm, gradually widening towards the base, hairy or pubescent, more sparsely or glabrous towards the apex. Fruit subglobose to ovoid, 0.8–1.5 × 0.7–1.2 cm, rounded at apex, yellow; wall 1–3 mm thick. Distribution: southwest of Nigeria, Cameroon and Gabon to Gabon, Estuary, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest, gallery forest in Gabon, up to 1600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 800 m. B: Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
