# *Malouetia mildbraedii* (Gilg & Stapf) J.Ploeg Planche 74

**Genus**: [[Malouetia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. Nat. Belg. 54 : 288 (1984)

## Description

Malouetiella parviflora Pichon, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 22 : 133 (1952). Malouetiella mildbraedii (Gilg & Stapf) Pichon, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 24(3) : 219 (1954). Shrub or tree reaching 17 m in height; trunk reaching 40 cm in diameter; bark smooth, white, milky or latex-producing, arising from the petioles and from the cambial zone; branches glabrous. Leaf: petiole 3–7 mm, glabrous, with a dense group of small colleters; blade oblong to lanceolate, 12–35 × 4.5–11 cm, base cuneate to rounded, apex acuminate, adaxial surface glabrous, abaxial surface with domatia in pitted fossae with ciliate margin; secondary veins 6 to 10 pairs. Inflorescence: terminal, cymose, compact, ovate, with small colleters. Flower: pedicel 3–8 mm; calyx with lobes 0.6–1 × 0.5–0.6 mm, puberulent on the outside, ciliolate, alternating with colleters 0.3–0.4 × 0.25–0.3 mm; corolla white, 10–12 mm long in the mature bud, with tube 4.5–5.5 mm long, composed of a basal cylindrical part and an apical conical part, glabrous on the outside, interior velvety in the basal part but glabrous at the base, in the upper part with 5 pilose zones terminating in lines at the throat; lobes narrowly oblong 4.5–6.5 × 0.9–1.5 mm, ciliolate at the apex; stamens included, inserted at the base of the apical conical part; filament velvety and extending into the pistil; anther triangular, 0.8–1 mm long; disc absent; pistil 1.1–1.5 mm long; ovary glabrous at the base, pilose at the apex; style 0.8–2.3 mm, glabrous. Fruit: follicles 8–11 × 0.7–0.8 cm. Seed (immature) 36 × 6 mm. Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, and Democratic Republic of the Congo, in the provinces Gabon, Ngounié, Ogooué, Ivindo and Ogooué Maritime.

## Ecology

mountain forest; in Gabon at 575–850 m altitude, elsewhere at 900–1400 m. B: Beentje (2021), van der Ploeg (1985). BIBLIOGRAPHY
