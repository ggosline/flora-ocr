# *Cyperus richardii* Steud. Planche 60

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Syn. pl. glum . 2 : 8 (1855)

## Synonyms

- Kyllinga bulbosa P.Beauv., Fl. Oware 1 : 11 (1805), non C. bulbosus Vahl (1805).

## Description

Perennial herb, with solitary stems at the end of long, slender stolons, 0.5–1 mm thick (easily broken if the plant is not carefully unearthed), pale, covered with scales; stems 5–30 cm × 0.7–1.5 mm, trigonous, glabrous, with longitudinal ridges, base usually swollen and covered with sheaths lacking a blade. Leaf: blade 5–25 cm × 2–5 mm, flat or incurved, very scabrous on the margin and central nerve. Inflorescence an irregular dense head, formed of several whitish spikes, often with triangular outline, 7–13 mm wide; involucral bracts usually 3–6, foliaceous, the principal ones 2–13 cm long, reflexed or spreading. Spikelet 2.5–3.5 mm long, with 3–5 flowers, but usually producing only 2–3 achenes, white at flowering and becoming progressively brownish at maturity; principal glumes 2.5–3 mm long, acuminate, whitish with the central nerve greenish and 3–4 lateral nerves, keel not winged, smooth. Flower with 2–3 stamens; style with 2 stigmas.

Achene ellipsoid to obovoid, 1–1.6 × 0.5–0.7 mm, flattened, light to dark brown, finely papillose.

## Distribution

widespread in tropical Africa, with the exception of the driest regions; rare or poorly known in Gabon, found only in Estuaire and Ogooué-Maritime (2 collections)

## Ecology

open grasslands, roadsides, lawns and other disturbed ground; in Gabon, at 0–100 m elevation, up to 2000 m elsewhere. Taxonomic note: recognized by its spikelets with 3–5 flowers and its fine stolons. It resembles no other species in Gabon and West Africa, but is close to a species from East Africa, C. purpureo-glandulosus Mattf. & Kük. (Kyllinga sphaerocephala Boeckeler), which was wrongly included in Kyllinga bulbosa in Flora of Tropical East Africa (Hoenselaar et al., 2010). Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1997)
