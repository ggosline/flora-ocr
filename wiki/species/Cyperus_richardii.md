# *Cyperus richardii* Steud. Planche 60

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Syn. pl. glum . 2 : 8 (1855)

## Synonyms

- Kyllinga macrocephala A.Rich., Tent. fl. abyss . 2 : 491 (1850), non C. macrocephalus
- Kyllinga bulbosa P.Beauv., Fl. Oware 1 : 11 (1805), non C. bulbosus Vahl (1805).

## Description

Perennial herb, with solitary stems at the apex of long, slender stolons, 0.5–1 mm thick (easily broken if the plant is not carefully uprooted), pale, covered with scales; stems 5–30 cm × 0.7–1.5 mm, trigonous, glabrous, with longitudinal ribs, base generally swollen and covered with sheathing leaves without a blade. Leaf: blade 5–25 cm × 2–5 mm, plane or incurved, very scabrous on the margin and central nerve. Inflorescence in a dense irregular head, formed of several whitish spikes, often triangular in outline, 7–13 mm wide; involucral bracts usually 3–6, foliaceous, the main ones 2–13 cm long, reflexed or spreading. Spikelet 2.5–3.5 mm long, with 3–5 flowers, but usually producing only 2–3 achenes, white at flowering and becoming progressively brownish at maturity; primary glumes 2.5–3 mm long, acuminate, whitish with the central nerve greenish and 3–4 lateral nerves, keel not winged, smooth. Flower with 2–3 stamens; style with 2 stigmas.

Achene ellipsoid to obovoid, 1–1.6 × 0.5–0.7 mm, flattened, light to dark brown, finely papillose.

## Distribution

widespread in tropical Africa, with the exception of the driest regions; rare or poorly known in Gabon, found only in Estuaire and Ogooué-Maritime (2 collections)

## Ecology

open grasslands, roadsides, lawns and other disturbed areas; in Gabon, 0–100 m altitude, up to 2000 m elsewhere. Taxonomic note: recognized by its spikelets with 3–5 flowers and its fine stolons. It does not resemble any other species in Gabon and West Africa, but it is close to a species from East Africa, C. purpureo-glandulosus Mattf. & Kük. (Kyllinga sphaerocephala Boeckeler), which was incorrectly included in Kyllinga bulbosa in Flora of Tropical East Africa (Hoenselaar et al., 2010). Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1997)
