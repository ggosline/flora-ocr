# *Rotheca violacea* (Gürke) Verdc. subsp. violacea Figure 4(I)

**Genus**: [[Rotheca]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull . 55 : 154 (2000)

## Description

, syn. nov. Vine up to 5 m tall or climbing shrub reaching 2 m tall; stem generally solid or rarely hollow; young branches ± rounded, quadrangular and/or finely striated, with short pale ferruginous pubescence or glabrescent, older ones generally quadrangular; lateral leafy shoots often with ± shortened internodes. Leaves opposite or rarely ternate; petiole 0.2–2.5(–3) cm; blade ovate to elliptic or oblong-elliptic, 3–13.5(–16) × 1.5–7(–10) cm, rounded or subcordate to narrowly cuneate or weakly attenuate at the base, shortly or longly acuminate for 0.5–2 cm at the apex, entire to ± slightly undulate or slightly to coarsely serrate with apiculate teeth, membranous to coriaceous, releasing an unpleasant odour when rubbed, ± glabrous to slightly pubescent above and on the venation below or rarely more densely pubescent/tomentose. Inflorescence terminal, lax, paniculate, 8–20 cm long; cymes 4(–8) × 2(–4.5) cm, distant by ± 1–4.5(–7) cm, with 2 to 6 flowers; peduncle reaching 2.5(–4) cm, glabrous to pubescent; bracts sessile, foliaceous, generally cuneate at the base, the lower ones ovate, 3–5 × 2–3 cm, acuminate, the upper ones ± narrowly elliptic-ovate, 5–10 × 1–4 mm. Flower: pedicel 4–9(–12) mm; calyx pink, reddish or purple, with cupuliform tube, 3–6 mm long, with broadly rounded to ovate lobes, sometimes reflexed, 3–5 × 2–5 mm; corolla zygomorphic, pale blue, violet, streaked with violet and white, or greenish, with tube 4–10 mm long, with lobes 8–16 × 5–5.5 mm, central lobe often darker than the others; stamens exserted for 1–3.5 cm. Fruit depressed-globose, 8 × 11 mm, red, with 3 or 4 seeds.

## Distribution

Tropical West Africa, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda), Burundi, tropical southern Africa to South Africa (Natal); in Gabon: Haut-Ogooué and Woleu-Ntem. Conservation status: Preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC) by Pollard in Cheek et al. (2004), under the name C. violaceum

## Ecology

primary and secondary forests, forest margins; in Gabon at 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1700 m

## Source note

Several authors have recognized Clerodendrum carnosulum as a distinct species. The key of Huber et al. (1963) separated C. carnosulum and C. violaceum on the basis of phyllotaxy (ternate vs. opposite; the type of C. carnosulum, Welwitsch 5702 (K) being ternate), pubescence, leaf texture and the depth of the calyx lobes. All these characters are variable within Clerodendrum. Some species exhibit both opposite and ternate leaves even on the same plant. Pubescence and leaf texture can vary considerably in the genus, notably depending on altitude. Calyces can also show considerable variation in the size of the lobes, depending on the degree of accrescence during fruit maturation. These characters do not seem to us sufficiently robust to recognize two distinct species and C. carnosulum is therefore considered here as a synonym of C. violaceum. Leaves opposite or verticillate by 3s, petiolate, palmatifid; leaflets (1–)3 to 7, with entire, dentate or more rarely lobed blade, often provided with sessile glands. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia or of thyrses in the axils of leaves or grouped into lax panicles; bracts small, generally not exceeding the calyx. Flowers: calyx campanulate, with 5 lobes, sometimes slightly bilabiate or truncate; corolla zygomorphic, bilabiate, of varied colour, often whitish to bluish, with straight or bent tube, with upper lip bilobed, the lower trilobed; stamens 4, didynamous, inserted in the tube, exserted or included; ovary before anthesis with 2 imperfect locules then with 4 uniovulate locules; style terminal. Genus of about 250 species principally in tropical regions of all continents. In Gabon: eleven species and one to be sought.

The genus Vitex has long been placed in the family Verbenaceae, subfamily Viticoideae. The placement of Viticoideae within Lamiaceae has been well established since Harley et al. (2004). Very recently, the subfamily was reduced to three genera following the phylogenetic analyses of Li et al. (2016). Vitex is the only genus of this subfamily represented in Africa. The genus Vitex is awaiting revision for Africa (Cabral 2013). The most useful synthesis is that of Pieper (1928). Moldenke devoted to the genus very many notes (notably between 1955 and 1958) which have not always contributed to clarifying the taxonomy; reference will be made to it, if necessary, only in the bibliography of certain species. Apart from the species treated below, Vitex trifolia L., a species from southeastern Asia and islands of the Pacific Ocean, sometimes cultivated in Africa and locally naturalized, has been collected in Gabon. This species is easily recognized by its strongly discolorous leaflets, tomentose-greyish on the lower face and subglabrous on the upper face, and by its paniculate inflorescence at the extremity of the branches; the number of leaflets varies from 1 to 5; the unique specimen from Gabon has unifoliolate leaves.
