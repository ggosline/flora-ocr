# *Landolphia buchananii* (Hallier f.) Stapf Planche 49

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 4(1) : 35 (1902)

## Description

Fl. trop. Afr. 4(1) : 35 (1902). Large liana; branches glabrous or hairy. Leaf: petiole 1.5–8 mm; lamina narrowly RYDOHRYDOHRUREREDWH±WLPHVDVORQJDVLWLVZLGH±±FPRXQGDWWKHEDVH sometimes slightly decurrent on the petiole, obtuse to acuminate at the apex, glabrous or sparsely hairy below near the base; secondary veins 7 to 19 pairs, almost straight to slightly curved; tertiary venation reticulate; labyrintine, all veins impressed above. Inflorescence terminal, sometimes also axillary, ± compound, 1.6–5.5 cm long, with ± flowers; peduncle ± mm, sometimes in whorls, glabrous to velvety; bracts long of 1–2.5 mm. Fragrant flower; pedicel 1–6.5 mm; calyx with subequal lobes, 1.2–3.1 × 1.2–2.7 mm, membranous at margin, glabrous to hairy on the outside; corolla white to yellow, often with greenish tube, tube 4.5–12 mm long, 1.9–3.2 mm wide at the level of the anthers, outside glabrous or sometimes hairy, inside sparsely to or densely hairy below the stamens, lobes 0.7–2 times the length of the tube, 5.3–15.4 × 1.9–4 mm; stamens inserted at 0.16–0.5 of the tube length, anther 1–2 mm long; pistil 2.5–4 mm long, ovary glabrous or sometimes with a few hairs, with conical apex; style 0.3–0.8 mm, glabrous. Fruit globose or pyriform, 2.5–6 cm long, yellowish to orange, with grey or light brown warty spots ± mm in diameter, occupying sometimes the entire surface, each spot near the edge with a narrow non-warty border; with ± seeds, ± mm thick; pulp white becoming yellow when exposed. Distribution from southeastern Nigeria and Cameroon to the east as far as Ethiopia and to the south as far as Angola and Mozambique; in Gabon known from a single collection in the W or Ntem.

## Ecology

dense forests, gallery forests, woody or herbaceous savannas, river margins; in Gabon at 500 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–2500 m. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
