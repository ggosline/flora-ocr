## Discussion

— M. applanatum BAK. F., in Cat. Talb. : 37 (1913). TYPE: Talbot 1606, southern Nigeria.

Shrub to small tree 8–10 m tall; branchlets rounded; sometimes a few aphyllous nodes. Leaves coriaceous, opaque, subsessile; lamina oblong-elliptic, ± 6 × 18 cm, or elliptic, ± 4 × 10 cm, caudate-acuminate; cuneate at base, acumen 1.5 cm, often curved, acute; midvein finely impressed above, moderately prominent below; 20 pennate veins, not or scarcely visible, perpendicular, straight; convergent veins not or very scarcely visible, submarginal; margins often revolute when dry.

Cymes axillary and sometimes also terminal, solitary or in pairs, up to 3 cm long, 2–3 times branched; stipe short, flattened, branchlets developed; flowers by 3, or each pedicellate below the pedicel; bracts triangular, persistent or caducous. Flower with pedicel 2–4 mm; floral bud at first entirely closed, globose-ellipsoid or obovoid, 4 × 5 mm; hypantho-calyx campanulate; calyx lobes valvate, thick, triangular, up to 3 × 3 mm, or shorter when the calyx opens earlier before anthesis. Corolla scarcely developed; petals fleshy, semi-ovate, 3 × 2 mm, to triangular, 3 × 3 mm, imbricate or amplexicaul and, in the latter case, substantially unequal. Stamens with anther almost oblong, thecae 1.5–2 mm; connective curved-glandular on the back, almost parallel to the thecae, except the base divergent and conical; filament 3–5 mm; epigynous chamber deep, cupuliform, 8 prominent septa, touching almost the style; ovary with thick wall; locules with nearly complete septa; 4–8-ovulate; style 6 mm.

Fruit globose to obovoid, with thick wall, 13 × 17 mm, crowned by the 4 calyx lobes. — Pl. 42, p. 117.

This species is fairly well known from the forests of West Africa. In Cameroon we have been led to distinguish a related species, which appears to be localized in a few riverine galleries. On the other hand, the collection by LE TESTU is identifiable with M. lateriflorum, although the petals are imbricate or amplexicaul. Indeed, the position of the petals in Memecylon appears to be fairly variable.
