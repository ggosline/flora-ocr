# *Cyperus surinamensis* Rottb. Planche 70, Figure 8a

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]

## Description

Robust perennial herb, in tufts with a few to many stems arising from a short woody rhizome; stems 15–60 cm × 1–5 mm, trigonous with rounded angles, green, scabrous on the longitudinal ridges, leafy only towards the base. Leaf: upper sheaths green, the lower ones often reddish-brown near the base; blade linear, flat or involute when dry, the largest 15–40 cm × 2–5 mm, scabrous on the margins and the central nerve at least near the apex. ## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical America, but infrequent in West Africa; rare in Gabon, found only in Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (2 collections)

Leaves usually with well-developed blade. Anatomy of stems and leaves of chlorocyperoid type (that is, with C4-type anatomy). Inflorescence in a loose or dense anthela, with one to several groups of digitate, sessile or pedicellate spikelets, rarely reduced to a solitary spikelet or to a spiciform inflorescence with few spikelets (C. cataractarum). Spikelets cylindric to distinctly flattened, indehiscent; glumes ovate, usually 1.4–4 mm long, densely imbricate. Flower with 1–3 stamens; style 2-branched. Achenes sessile, usually biconvex and laterally flattened, more rarely cylindric or rounded, with finely papillose surface, transversely wrinkled or rather smooth. Subgenus fairly large, of about 120 species from marshes, lake shores and wet meadows, widely distributed in tropical and subtropical regions, especially in Africa; in Gabon, 10 species.
