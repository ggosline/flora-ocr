# *Acioa letestui* R. Letouzey

**Genus**: [[Acioa]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Adansonia, ser. 2, 16 (2): 240, tab. 5 (1976)

## Description

— ? A. tessmannii ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 311 (1915), nom. nud. See taxonomic note below.

— A. scabrifolia auct. non Hua : De Wild., BJBB, 7 : 206 (1920), p.p., quoad specimen Tessmann 221 only.

Tree attaining 60 cm in diameter and 25 m in total height but with short bole (6 m), furnished with a few buttresses at the base, slightly fluted and supporting a very leafy globose crown, dark green, supported by a fascicle of sinuous branches. Rhytidome greyish-red exfoliating in fine longitudinal scales; bark hard, granular, 0.5 cm thick, red. Sapwood yellow-orange passing rapidly to very hard heartwood, orange-brown and increasingly reddish towards the centre. Twigs dark brown, lenticellate. Young twigs glabrous. Stipules linear acute, 3–6 mm, apparently intrapetiolary and fixed at the base of the petiole, with prominent midvein and sometimes subdenticulate on the margin. Petiole 3–5 mm, transversely rugose when dry, glabrous as is the leaf. Blade elliptic, 3.5–6 × 6–11.5 cm, sometimes somewhat oblong, frequently somewhat ovate, broadly acute to obtuse at the base and apex, the latter acuminate (4 × 6 mm) with obtuse apex; lateral veins 3–4–5 pairs, curved ascending and ± anastomosing between themselves far from the margin: 2(–3) large circular glands beneath the base generally triangularly narrowed at the blade base; sometimes a few smaller glands above these and distant from the midvein, likewise some small glands beneath the acumen and beneath the apex of the blade towards the upper part, sometimes also small peripheral glands here and there below, at the level of the anastomosis of the lateral veins; veinlets in fairly dense reticulation.

Racemes solitary terminal and axillary towards the end of the twigs, glabrous, 3–8 cm long with peduncle not exceeding 0.5–2 cm and sometimes furnished with a few tricuspid bracts, the median tooth lanceolate (even subfoliaceus) which may be ornamented towards the base with circular glands. Bracts ovate to lanceolate-triangular, approximately 2–3 mm, with margin often subglandular, even glandular towards the base, at the tip of the veins, occasionally also bearing (1–2(–3) large lateral crater-like glands towards mid-height. Flowers numerous, sometimes forming a corymb towards the top of the raceme. Floral bud globose, somewhat pointed, glabrous; portion of the pedicels below the articulation 5–10(–15) mm, provided, between their midpoint and the articulation, with 2 alternate bracteoles, rarely subopposite, lanceolate, 1–1.5 mm, 5–7-dentate with subtipitate to stipitate glands towards the base, the terminal articulated portion of the pedicel slightly exceeding the apex of the upper bracteole in general. Pedicel of the disarticulated flower 6–10 mm long. Receptacle 5–6 mm, tubular or narrowly obconic and ± gibbous at the base; sepals green, 4–5 mm, glabrous for the parts exposed externally in the floral bud (with external margins subglandular at the tip of the veins sometimes); petals white, obovate with obtuse base, 4–5 mm in height; androecium 20–25 mm, approximately 15 filaments.

Infructescences sparingly furnished with fruits borne on strong peduncles, not exceeding 10–20 mm, provided at the apex with reflexed sepals. Drupe subglobose attaining 17 × 20–25 × 20–30 mm (in vivo), with bossed then mamillate wrinkled surface, pale green then greenish-brown in colour, tomentellous with scattered hispidulous hairs. — PL. 13, p. 45.

## Type

Le Testu 9352, Gabon (holo-, P!)

## Discussion

The name Acioa tessmannii Engl., nom. nud. (l.c. : 311), would very likely have been able to be adopted to designate this species since the specimen Tessmann 221 (in K!) from Equatorial Guinea appears to be situated in the position of this taxon in the key established by Engler, despite the imperfections of the latter, on the one hand, and on the other hand, no known specimen of Tessmann, among the Acioa and Chrysobalanaceae, appears to correspond to this summary classification. The possible holotype for Acioa tessmannii Engl., nom. nud., appears to have disappeared from the Berlin herbarium, no isotype appears to be present at Hamburg and the possible isotype at Kew bears only the inscriptions "Griffonia barteri Hook. f. ex Oliv." presumably in the latter's hand, then "Acioa scabrifolia Hua" in De Wildeman's hand (cf. BJBB 7 : 206, 1920). For nomenclatural caution, the new designation Acioa letestui R. Let. was therefore chosen, at least for the present.

CHRYSOBALANACEAE

This species is very close to Acioa barteri Hook. f. ex Oliv., but is readily distinguished from it by its glabrous receptacle and its globose fruit, the latter character also differentiating it from A. scabrifolia Hua with similarly glabrous receptacle, all reservations being made however as to the incomplete and imprecise knowledge of the fruits of these two other species. (See "fruit", Acioa barteri Hook. f. ex Oliv.).

Comparisons are also possible with Acioa lehmbachii Engl. but here the leaf has a pubescent midvein and 5–7(–9) pairs of lateral veins, the blade is not furnished below with well-visible glands, the inflorescence is ± branched, the bracts are devoid of glands, the bracteoles more deeply divided, finally the drupe would be pointed.

Acioa letestui R. Let. is known from Cameroon, but only to the E of the longitude of Yaoundé, in Equatorial Guinea (Tessmann 221, K!) and in Gabon, towards the Cameroonian and Guinean borders on the one hand and in upper Ngounié on the other. In Cameroon, this species is recorded in periodically flooded forests along rivers and in swampy raphiales on sandy-clay soil.

As a result of the taxonomic notes above, it should be emphasized that Acioa scabrifolia Hua is known only from Guinea to Ghana. A. barteri Hook. f. ex Oliv. extends conversely from Sierra Leone to Gabon, but in the latter territory as well as in Cameroon it appears to be confined to the vicinity of the Ocean (Libreville, Campo, Bipindi, Kribi). With regard to A. lehmbachii Engl., localized in SE Nigeria and in regions neighbouring Cameroon, it is to be noted that it was found again (Bates 855) in the vicinity of the Dja loop where it then cohabits with A. letestui R. Let. (Bates 1740).
