# *Millettia viridiflora* O.Lachenaud, sp. nov. Planche 89

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

Climbing habit, with 3- or 4-jugate leaves without stipels, leaflets glabrescently, inflorescences very shortly bracteate, calyx shortly dentate, standard and keel pubescent outside, resembles M. barteri, but differs by the green corolla and conspicuously shorter (standard 7–8.5 mm long) and leaflets with lateral veins less conspicuous and slightly ascending.

## Discussion

slightly ascending and scarcely more marked than the nervilles, united fairly far from the margin. Inflorescence in pseudoracemose spikes 10–27 cm long, puberulent; flowers grouped in glomerules of 7 to 15; bracts caducous, lanceolate, ± 2 × 0.5 mm. Flower: pedicel 1–2.5 mm; bracteoles caducous, situated at the base of the calyx, < 1 × 0.3 mm; calyx greenish-brown, sparsely and very shortly pubescent, with tube 2–2.5 mm long and triangular teeth 0.3–0.5 mm long, the upper ones entirely connate; corolla pale green with a darker patch at the base of the standard, standard 7–8.5 × 5–7.5 mm, shortly pubescent over the entire outer face, with suborbicular limb folded lengthwise, with claw 1.5–2 mm, wings elliptic, 6–7.5 mm, entirely glabrous, with claw 2–2.5 mm, keel elliptic, 6 mm long, pubescent externally in the upper half, with claw 2 mm, with truncate or very slightly auriculate base; stamens monadelphous, tube 5 mm long, divided into 2 in its upper part (the 3 or 4 dorsal stamens separated from the rest), anthers medifixed; disk ring-shaped, ± 0.3 mm; ovary 5 × 0.5 mm, with dense appressed pubescence; style 2 mm, erect at right angle, glabrous, with slightly thickened stigma. Pod and seed unknown.

## Distribution

species endemic to Gabon, known only from two collections in Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

secondary forest and forest margins, on terra firme; at ± 450 m altitude

## Source note

Other specimen studied: Breteler 6730 (BR, WAG), Gabon, Haut-Ogooué, km 30 Moanda Bakoumba, 1°45'S 13°05'E, 2 October 1970 (fl). This species resembles M. barteri at first sight by its climbing habit, its leaf without stipels with 7 to 9 glabrescently leaflets, its pseudoracemose inflorescence with very short bracts, its calyx with equally very short teeth, and its pubescent corolla on the outside; however, it is easily distinguished by the green flowers and clearly smaller, and by the leaflets with lateral veins slightly ascending and scarcely more marked than the nervilles.
