Lianas or climbing shrubs with long, flexible branches capable of reaching a length of 8–15 m, evergreen or deciduous, glabrous except on the sepals and petals. Young shoots cinnamon-brown, shiny or dull, smooth or scabrous (very fine epidermis asperities), generally lenticellate. Older branches dull or shiny dark brown. Numerous small rounded or slightly elongated lenticels, pale brown, farinosе, rarely confluent; epidermal asperities absent or dense and distributed among the lenticels, persistent on old wood. Trunk, diameter at base 1–3 cm but occasionally reaching 10 cm, wood whitish, pith narrow and white. Axillary buds solitary or 2–3 buds in series, the apical one dominant and developing into a shoot that is either floriferous or vegetative, the other buds in series remaining dormant. Leaves evenly distributed along the shoots, (1–) 3 (–4–5) -foliolate, rarely simple near the racemes, long-petioled. Leaflets evenly or sometimes unevenly and longly stipitate; 1-foliolate leaves long-petioled, leaflet symmetric, ovate or oblong, acute, base rounded. Lamina of 3- and 5-foliolate leaves of variable shape, elliptic-lanceolate or ovate, rarely narrow and elliptic or obovate, acuminate, base rounded or cuneate, (4–) 6.5–17 (–20) × (2.5–) 3.5–7 (–12) cm; median leaflet symmetric, lateral ones ± asymmetric at least at the base, somewhat smaller than the median leaflet. Midvein prominent beneath, depressed or deeply impressed above. Lateral veins (2–) 3–5 (–7) evenly spaced along the midvein or denser in the lower half, prominent beneath and depressed or deeply impressed above, gently curved from the midvein, anastomosing in loops at their termination; veinlets forming a loose, very inconspicuous network. Leaflets slightly to strongly coriaceous, sometimes papyraceous, unicolorous on both faces or paler below, upper surface dark olive-green, lower surface sometimes grayish-green; the surface of mature leaflets is polished and shiny or finely wrinkled-rugose when dry (high magnification!). Petiolules 4–1 mm long, equal or sometimes longer for the median leaflet, channeled on the upper face, smooth or rough (epidermal asperities), dark brown-greenish, articulate at the base. Petiole slender, 3–9 (–13.5) cm long for 3–5-foliolate leaves, 1–2 cm for 1-foliolate ones, pale green or tinged with brownish, vaguely channeled in its apical part, almost cylindric toward the base, weakly or scarcely fused at the base, not decurrent. Stipules very inconspicuous, at most 0.5 mm long, subulate, membranous, scarious, free, early withered.

Inflorescence a corymbiform raceme with 1–15 flowers or more rarely solitary flowers at the leaf axils. The racemes are generally at the axils of the most distant leaves, rarely terminal on the long leafy shoots of the year. Axillary racemes most often solitary, sometimes 2 or 3 at the same axil; these racemes may appear at the axils of persistent leaves as well as at those of already fallen leaves. Flowers made evident by their long-exserted stamens. Rachis often curved at the base, 0.5–10 (–31) cm long, smooth or finely scabrous (epidermal asperities); flowers loosely spaced or densely grouped at the apex of the raceme, without leaves but sometimes with very small scaly and scarious prophylls near the base; axis very rarely continuing its growth into a vegetative shoot. Floral bracts simple, usually small, narrow and elliptic or subulate, sessile or subsessile, glabrous, often falling before anthesis. Stipules of floral bracts very inconspicuous, at most 1 mm long, narrowed from a broad base into subulate to filiform, glabrous, scarious, free, longly persistent, base generally slightly decurrent. Pedicel slender, (2–) 3–7 (–8) cm long at anthesis, 10 cm at fruiting, yellowish-green, glabrous or slightly scabrous, striated at the apex, cylindric toward the base, enlarged above the receptacle, base distinctly articulate. Receptacle vaguely cupuliform, 1–2 (–3) mm high and 3–4 mm wide just before anthesis, slightly striated, coriaceous, margin often 4-angulate at the edges; marcescent and very shallow, it persists fragmented under the fruit. Crown practically invisible, margin entire or slightly prominent on either side of the base of the petals. Sepals 4, equal or nearly so, broad and lanceolate or obovate, 16–32 × (6–) 8–10 (–12) mm, acuminate, dull olive-green, thin, papyraceous, 3–5-veined at the base (decurrent veins), outer face glabrous, inner face finely puberulent near the apex, margins finely tomentulose; they are divaricate at anthesis and caducous. Petals 4–8, entire or divided (when there are 4) in the apical region, rarely cut almost to the base; lamina papyraceous (4–) 5–6 (–7.2) × 0.5–0.7 (–1) cm (entire petals are wider than those that are divided), narrow and spatulate or blanceolete, attenuate at the apex and apiculate, base narrowed into a linear unguis shorter than the sepals; the petals pale green initially turn pale sulfur-yellow at anthesis and often remain yellow when dry; glabrous or rarely ciliate on the margin, the lamina shows 5–7 distinct and parallel veins from the unguis; the petals are caducous.

Androgynophore 1–2 mm long, robust, somewhat wider than the gynophore, exceeding the receptacle by 0.5–1 mm after anthesis, smooth or weakly striated, glabrous. Stamens ca. 35–40, all fertile, falling rather late; filaments 21–35 mm, somewhat finer than the unguis of the petals, narrowed at the apex, white or pale greenish, browning when dry, remaining ± coiled at anthesis; anthers narrow and oblong, 2.75–3.5 mm, shortly mucronate at the apex, dorsobasifix, labile. Gynophore slender, glabrous, approximately as long as the stamens at anthesis, subsequently 3–4.5 cm long, cylindric, smooth or slightly striated near the apex and base, pale green, browning when dry; at fruiting, 3–4 mm wide, lignified, aligned with the pedicel or slightly bent. Ovary cylindric to narrow and ellipsoidal, base acute or cuneate, apex generally narrowed and obtuse, (3–) 4.5–5 (–7) × 1–1.5 mm, green, glabrous, smooth or with 3–4 or 6 inconspicuous longitudinal veins; the young ovary in transverse section shows 4 obtuse angles. Ovary unilocular, 2–3 or 4 parietal placentas; ovules numerous in 2 rows on each placenta. Stigma sessile or subsessile, approximately 1 mm wide, truncate, green, blackening when dry, margin slightly recurved, entire or slightly lobed: it enlarges after anthesis and generally ends by incorporating into the apex of the fruit where it forms a flat circular gray patch at maturity.

Fruit oblong or ellipsoidal, straight, rarely terminating in a beak at the apex, dark dingy green, variously ornamented: distinctly ridged in ellipsoidal fruits reaching 6 × 3 cm, more weakly in oblong fruits which are up to 7.5 × 2 cm. Fruit walls showing no swelling at the level of the seeds, pericarp approximately 4 mm thick, crustaceous, finely but distinctly verrucose. Seeds, 0–20 per fruit, suborbicular or irregularly rounded, approximately 1 cm in diameter, separated by fine and fleshy dissepiments, testa coriaceous smooth, pale brown. - Pl. 8, p. 63 & Map, F, p. 7.

## Discussion

, the rhizome of "R. fragariodora" would be edible; it would also be used as a medicine, which would merit confirmation.

Ritchiea capparoides is accepted here sensu lato; thus, this entity comprises all African species with lianescent habit. Only taxa described from West Africa have been listed as synonyms. R. capparoides (sens. lat.) is, of all the species, the most widely distributed, since its geographical range coincides with that of the genus.

The regional variation of its morphological characters, particularly those of the vegetative apparatus, is considerable. This variation within West Africa is elucidated by the following key.

KEY TO VARIATION in R. capparoides:

1. Epidermal asperities abundant on the branches, petioles, petiolules, pedicels and rachis. Lateral veins of lateral leaflets 3–4, rarely up to 7. Surface of dry leaflets shiny and polished or sometimes dull and finely wrinkled-rugose. Ovary usually ellipsoidal, 3–4 (–5) mm long, with 3–4 or 6 longitudinal veins, generally well marked. Placenta 3 or 4. Fruit ellipsoidal, at most 3 cm wide, most often with 6 deep longitudinal ridges. Peduncle often long and slender, prophylls absent or few.

2. Leaflets equally stipitate at the base ..... Subgroup 1.

2'. Median leaflet distinctly more longly stipitate than the lateral ones (unknown from Gabon)..... Subgroup 2.

1. Epidermal asperities absent or poorly formed on the branches close to the base of the petioles. Secondary veins of lateral leaflets 4–6 (–7). Surface of leaflets dull and finely wrinkled-rugose. Ovary narrow and cylindric, 5–6 (–7) mm long, smooth or with 2–3 or 4 inconspicuous veins near the base. Placenta 2–3 or 4. Fruit oblong, at most 2 cm wide, with 3–4 or 6 slight longitudinal ridges. Rachis short with prophylls densely grouped at the base.....Group B.

Specimens from Gabon can be referred to 2 of these entities: Subgroup 1 of Group A and Group B. Both are widely distributed in the interior regions and adjacent territories. Their respective distribution areas are not sharply separated. Subgroup 2 of Group A, identical to Ritchiea fragariodora Gilg, is known from the coastal regions of Cameroon but apparently has not penetrated into Gabon.

GROUP A, SUBGROUP 1

It comprises the types of the following species: R. capparoides, R. longipedicellata, R. immersa, R. apiculata and R. thorethiae.

Group A is easily distinguished by dense epidermal asperities among the lenticels. They cannot be confused with lenticell primordia. They are responsible for the dull brown color of the branches, which contrasts with the shiny brown color in Group B. The adult leaves are more or less pale below, in contrast to Group B where they are unicolorous. The leaflets of Group A are generally shorter and more rounded than those of Group B. The lateral leaflets are often broadly ovate, distinctly asymmetric, their base is rounded. A collection from Bélinga (Breteler & de Wilde 533) has more slender leaves resembling those of Group B; likewise, the elongated ovary is similar to that typical for Group B. All flowers studied have 4 entire petals. In Nigeria and Cameroon have been collected specimens with 4 deeply divided petals to 8 petals. Most Gabonese collections correspond to R. immersa, particularly characterized by vigorous, rounded leaflets, whose upper surface is polished with distinctly impressed veins. Subgroup 1 was encountered in several interior localities, very distant from one another. Their flowering was noted from August to September. Ripe fruits unknown from Gabon.

## Specimens examined

Breteler & de Wilde 533, Bélinga (fl., sept.), WAG. Le Testu 2098, Malemba (fl., août), BM, P; 9244, Bilo (fl., -), BM, P, S. Thollon 1336, Loanga (fl. & j. fr., sept.), P.

GROUPE B

Cette entité inclut entre autres le type de R. leucantha et le type conspécifique de R. boukokoensis. Ses caractères les plus marquants sont le manque d'aspérités épidermiques et le fruit oblong légèrement cannelé. Les folioles adultes ont une texture plus fine que dans le Groupe A et leur face supérieure est le plus souvent finement ruguleuse. Les folioles ont presque la même couleur sur les deux faces ; elles sont plus élancées et plus minces que celles du Groupe A ; leur base est atténuée et cunée tandis que, dans le Groupe A, elle est arrondie. La marge du stigmate est entière ou très vaguement bilobée. Le fruit, à bords parallèles (oblong), possède de vagues cannelures longitudinales.

Le Groupe B est largement réparti au Sud Cameroun et de là en Centrafrique et au Gabon où il a été récolté près de Lastoursville ainsi qu'à Oyem dans le Nord. Floraisons constatées en avril, juillet et août. Fruits inconnus au Gabon.
