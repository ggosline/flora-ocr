Tree 80 cm in diameter and 25–30 m in total height, with trunk often fairly regular and relatively short (10–20 m under branches), without buttresses or only with a few large basal projections; may nevertheless attain 120(–150) cm in diameter and 40 m in total height and then furnished with buttresses of concave profile, 2 m high and 1.5 m long on the ground. (Flowering specimens Klaine 2801 and 2883, syntypes of Parinari robusta var. klainei Aubr. & Pellegrin, would according to the collector have come from shrubs 2–4 m tall; see taxonomic notes below.)

Rhytidome mossy, orange-grey to greyish-red, rugose and lenticellate, exfoliating in ± irregular flakes; bark thick (10–15 mm); blood-red exudate at the cambium level; sapwood yellowish, not very thick (3 cm), passing into reddish heartwood, heavy and very hard. Large sinuous branches ± erect, relatively little branched, sometimes supporting in very large trees a tabular crown reaching 50 m in diameter with fairly dense foliage. Branchlets dark brown ± lenticellate; young branchlets slender and smooth; very young branchlets and petioles bearing scattered ascending hispid pubescence; intrapetiolar stipules fused into an elongated bifid piece, early caducous. Petiole relatively slender, 5–10 mm long, often transversely cracked and frequently bearing at the apex, at the decurrent base of the blade, 2 small circular glands, marginate or crater-form, these glands sometimes only incipient or sometimes absent. Blade coriaceous elliptic, 25–50 × 60–120 (including acumen 10, rarely 15) mm, with acute and slightly decurrent base, rarely obtuse, with apex equally acute furnished with an acumen rounded at the apex; sometimes blade somewhat obovate, sometimes more broadly elliptic and then reaching up to 60–75 × 100–140 mm (acumen 10 mm included), with base and apex rounded; 5–6(–8) pairs of arcuate lateral veins, slightly prominent; surfaces glabrous, bright green in color, dark above, pale below, becoming shining brown-reddish in tint on dried specimens, fallen leaves frequently rolled longitudinally.

Inflorescences corymbiform, sometimes very numerous and dense under the crown at the tip of stout branchlets (5 mm), forming then very abundantly flowering plateaus (several hundreds of flowers) reaching 25 × 50 cm in width and 5–8 cm in height, ± leafy at the base; axes short (from 20 to 2 mm) and very branched with short scattered ascending pubescence, or glabrescent. Bracts and bracteoles lanceolate, minute, early caducous. Pedicel 2–6 mm and receptacle green obconic 3–5 mm, glabrous; sepals green obovate to suborbicular 3–4 mm long, glabrous for the parts exposed externally in the bud, elsewhere ashey tomentellous; sometimes pedicel 6–8 mm long and, as well as receptacle and sepals for their external parts, covered with short scattered ascending pubescence (see taxonomic notes below); petals cream-white, pinkish toward the base, obovate to suborbicular concave, 4–6 mm long; 20 cream-colored staminal filaments 10–15 mm long and orange-colored anthers, a few dentate staminodes; 1 posterior hispid carpel fertile (sometimes 2 lateral abortive ones).

Drupe obovoid flattened, 20–30 × 30–35 × 50–60 mm, with acute base and obtuse apex, glabrous and smooth or lenticellate, yellowish-green then brownish then blackish, with persistent calyx and androecium. — PL. 32, p. 109.

## Discussion

CHRYSOBALANACEAE

Maranthes glabra (Oliv.) Prance is fairly uniform throughout its range; in Gabon however small trees occur (shrubs, cf. Klaine 2801, 2883) and forms characterized by more broadly elliptic leaves, longer pedicels and flower buds externally shortly pubescent; these forms were considered, particularly in the Southwest of Zaïre where they are found, as belonging to a distinct species or variety (gilletii), whereas in Gabon itself they became Parinari robusta var. klainei Aubr. & Pellegr.; they differ however from this latter species of ± coastal swamp forests of West Africa by the trichome of the flower bud (receptacle pubescent and not copper-felt, sepals pubescent and not subglabrous), by their acuminate leaf (and not subacuminate), glabrous below (and not with whitish araneous felting).

This species is found throughout the Congo-Guinean forest, becoming rarer in the relatively most humid zones as well as in the relatively most arid zones; it is thus recorded from Sierra Leone to the Central African Republic and Angola, most often in scattered state, sometimes locally somewhat gregarious. In Cameroon this tree frequents however the fairly humid zones of Kumba (Rumpi Mountains) and Mamfe (Nta Ali Massif) but then appears to localize at altitude, between 800 and 1000 m; elsewhere in forest it is more frequent to the East of the longitude of Yaoundé than to the West; toward the exterior of the Cameroonian forest massif it reaches Tibati, Deng Deng, Batouri and thence Moloundou. In Gabon it is known as far as Makokou, Lastoursville, Mbigou and Tchibanga. Normally a terra firme tree, it exists in Zaïre in swamp forests or periodically inundated forests; just as in Cameroon it also rises in the East of Zaïre to 850 m altitude.

## Uses

In Congo it is involved (root powder) in the treatment of chronic mange and alopecia in domestic animals. In Zaïre the cambial exudate would be used, applied to the eye, as an ordeal poison; the fruit constitutes bait for river hogs and antelopes. Throughout the range of this species the oleaginous seed is consumed and the wood is considered only for carpentry, sometimes (Sierra Leone) for the making of canoes.
