# *Pollia condensata* C.B.Clarke Planche 25, Figure 6(A, B)

**Genus**: [[Pollia]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: P. cyanocarpa K.Schum., Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 33(2) : 376 (1903)

## Description

in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phan. 3 : 125 (1881). P. cyanocarpa K.Schum., Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 33(2) : 376 (1903). Aclisia condensata (C.B.Clarke) G.Brückn. in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. , ed. 2, 15a : 176 (1930). Stoloniferous herb, creeping-ascending, forming colonies, 30–130(–200) cm in height, sometimes supported by aerial roots arising from basal nodes. Leaves primarily concentrated at the stem apex; sheath 2–6 cm long, puberulent, apex glabrous; blade narrowly obovate-oblong to narrowly ovate, 11–31 × 2.5–8(–10) cm, base narrowly cuneate, apex acuminate, upper surface glabrous, lower surface sparsely puberulent to glabrous. Inflorescence solitary, very dense, capitate, ovoid, 1–5 × 1.5–3 cm, composed of numerous subsessile cincinni reaching up to 1(–1.5) cm in length, with bisexual flowers; peduncle 0.5–3.5 cm, arising from a reduced leaf and bearing usually 1 or 2 foliaceous bracts; cincinni bracts ovate, covering the inflorescence when young, then caducous, puberulent; bracteoles caducous. Flower: pedicel 2.5–3 mm at fruiting; sepals ovate, 4–5.5 mm long, apex enlarged and glandular, puberulent, green or greenish white; petals obovate, ± 6 × 4 mm, white, without a claw; stamens: the 3 upper ones short, ± 1.5 mm long, hidden in the flower, anther underdeveloped or very reduced, the lower ones long-exserted, ± 7 mm long; style exsert, ± 7 mm. Fruit ± spherical, (3–)4.5–5.5 × (3–)4–5 mm, blue or metallic blue-black, sometimes tinged with violet or purple; seeds biseriate, (2–)4 to 6 per locule. Seed dorsiventrally compressed, with polygonal or trapezoidal outline, 1.5–2.5 mm in diameter; testa smooth, grey to dark grey-brown.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, São Tomé and Príncipe, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Río Muni), Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Uganda, Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Angola and Mozambique; in Gabon in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Secondary forest, periodically inundated forest, open stations in high forest; in Gabon up to 900 m elevation, elsewhere 40 to 1550 m. Use: Fetish plant of elephant hunters. The infusion of pounded stems is drunk against gonorrhea
