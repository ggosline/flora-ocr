# *Landolphia letestui* (Pellegr.) Pichon Planche 58 ; Figure 7(D, E)

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 82 (1953)

## Description

Large liana with lenticels, smooth, narrowly fissured, glabrous. Glabrous leaf: petiole of 3–7 mm, limb elliptic to obovate or narrowly elliptic to narrowly obovate, 2–4 times as long as wide, 2–5.5 × 0.8–1.5 cm, acute or sometimes rounded at the base and decurrent on the petiole or not, obtuse to acuminate at the apex; secondary nerves 7 to 13 pairs, straight to slightly incurved, tertiary venation reticulate, often labyrinthine and dark beneath. Inflorescence axillary, sometimes also terminal, 0.3–1.4 cm long, with 4–8 flowers, pedicels of 1–2 mm, bracteate, bracts 0.5–1 mm long, the distal ones often sepaloid. Flower: pedicel 1–3 mm; calyx with unequal lobes, oval to very broadly oval, 1.5–2 times as long as wide, 1.5–2.5 × 1–1.5 mm, weakly ciliate, glabrous or sometimes pilose on the exterior; corolla whitish to yellowish, tube 1.8–2.8 mm long, ± barrel-shaped, 1.3–1.9 mm wide at the level of the anthers, glabrous or pubescent inside with some stiff hairs just below the anthers, lobes oval to elliptic or narrowly oval to narrowly elliptic, 0.8–1.5 times as long as the tube, 1.4–3.5 times as long as wide, 1.5–3.5 × 1.1–1.7 mm, glabrous; stamens inserted at 0.3–0.7(–0.9) of the tube length, exserted 0.2 mm to included 1.4 mm, anther 0.6–1 mm long; pistil 1.6–2.5 mm long, ovary glabrous or sometimes slightly puberulent; style 0.2–0.5 mm. Fruit globose to ellipsoid or obovoid, 0.8–1.1 × 0.7–1 cm, pale yellow sometimes with an orange-red tinge, often with scattered red-orange lenticels (0.1–1.5 mm in diameter), with 5 to 17 seeds; wall 1–2.5 mm thick.

Distribution Cameroon, Gabon, western Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Angola. Cabinda to Gabon: Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo. Ecology Primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 800 m altitude. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
