Liane reaching up to 50 m in length and 12 cm in diameter, lianescent shrub or shrub (sometimes tree?). Woody cylinder of lianescent stems deeply divided by phloem intrusions; bark fairly smooth and pale, sometimes whitish, generally distinctly lenticellate; lenticels very often arranged in rows, either large and scattered or dense and generally smaller. Older branches very often glabrous and lenticellate. Young branches with short and appressed to tomentellous and hispidulous pubescence, early glabrescences, generally early lenticellate with pale brown to dark red hairs; the same indumentum is present on the stipules, petioles, and inflorescences.

Stipules fairly early caducous, narrow and oblong-triangular, (1) 2–5 (10) × ≤ 1 mm.

Leaves. Petiole sub- to hemicylindric, grooved above or not, (1) 2–5 (13) mm long. Blade papyraceous to coriaceous, obovate-elliptic, (1.5) 2–3 (4) times longer than wide, (3.5) 9–12 (25) × (1.5) 3–5 (9) cm, tapering to a narrow rounded to cuneate base; acumen moderate to abrupt, rounded to obtuse, sometimes emarginate, rarely mucronate, (0.5) 1–1.5 (2) cm long; upper surface ordinarily glabrous or with a few hairs becoming denser on the impressed primary vein; lower surface with sparse and subappressed pubescence especially on the primary vein and the main secondary veins; sometimes both faces are equally pubescent or, in young state, the blade is entirely covered with short and appressed hairs, then glabrescences; main secondary veins II, (4) 5–7 (9) pairs, often impressed on papyraceous leaves and prominent below; tertiary veins III usually plane and indistinct rarely prominent above, generally distinct plane or slightly prominent below; glands generally present only on the lower face, most often few and small, sometimes slightly larger near the base, especially distributed in a narrow zone along the lower half of the primary vein.

Inflorescences ≤ 50-flowered, either sessile in nodosities (sometimes split in two at the base), or very shortly pedunculate, rarely otherwise and clearly branched; pubescence subappressed short to tomentellous, hispidulous. Peduncle 0–1 (3) mm. Bracts and bracteoles ovate-triangular, ≤ 1 mm long. Pedicel ≤ 4 (7) mm, upper portion very short or absent.

Flowers. Sepals erect to reflexed, free, oblong-obovate, 2–4 × 0.7–1 (1.5) mm; pubescence appressed to tomentellous on the external face; interior glabrous or with a few hairs on the apical portion, rarely puberulent throughout. Petals erect or nearly so, glabrous, narrow and obovate-oblong 4–5 (6) mm, cleft for 0.5–1 mm; base free or very shortly fused to the filaments; lobes generally concave. Stamens erect or nearly so, most often slightly longer than the petals, (4) 4.5–5.5 (6.5) mm, glabrous; anthers ≈ 0.2–0.5 mm long, connective often distinct. Staminodes subsquare to obovate-oblong, 0.2–0.7 × 0.2–0.5 mm, glabrous. Pistil 3 (4)-merous, 4.5–7.5 mm long; ovary 3 (4)-lobed, pubescence dense, white to brown velvety; style glabrous, shortly 3 (4)-lobed at the apex.

Fruits sub- to dispherical to obliquely ellipsoidal, generally 1–2-seeded, diameter 1–2 cm, yellow to orange at maturity, sparse to dense puberulence, tomentellous to velvety, most often also with long irritant rigid hairs, acicular, easily caducous; mesocarp succulent, 1–2 (4) mm thick; endocarp parchment-like glabrous to furnished with dense short and scabrous hairs inside. Seed spheric to ellipsoidal, 5–12 × 5–8 mm; testa thin to rather robust, brown, glossy. - Pl. 50, p. 169.

## Ecology

evergreen and semi-deciduous rain forests, alt. ≤ 1700 m

This species is related to D. heudelotii (Planch. ex Oliver) Baill.; it is easily distinguished from it by its vegetative characters (see p. 58) and by its fruits usually much smaller. Like D. heudelotii, this is a variable species, especially with regard to leaf size.
