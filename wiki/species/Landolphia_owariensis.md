# *Landolphia owariensis* P.Beauv. Planche 63

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Oware 1(6) : 55 (1806)

## Description

Fl. Oware 1(6) : 55 (1806). L. pierrei Hua, Compt. Rend. Hebd. Séances Acad. Sci. 135 : 868 (1902). Large liana; branches pubescent to glabrous. Leaf: petiole 1–12 mm; lamina ovate to obovate, as long or slightly longer than wide, 0.5–12 × 0.5–8 cm, cuneate to rounded at base, acuminate to obtuse at apex, pubescent to glabrous beneath; secondary nerves 6 to 23 pairs. Inflorescence terminal or on tendrils, lax to compact, with numerous flowers, sessile or with peduncle reaching 15 cm. Flower: pedicel 0.5–3 mm; calyx with erect lobes, 0.8–1.9 times as long as wide, 1.2–4 × 1.5–3.2 mm, pubescent externally; corolla white, yellowish, becoming rose, brown or red after anthesis, with ceolate tube, almost cylindrical, narrowed at throat, 3.7–10 mm long, variously puberulent externally, pubescent internally from the stamens or below them to the throat or base of lobes, with oblong lobes, 0.8–2 times as long as the tube, 0.3–1.5 times as long as wide, 1.7–9 × 1–3.2 mm; stamens included 0.3–2 mm, anther 0.9–1.6 × 0.3–0.6 mm; pistil 3.4–6.3 mm long, ovary with one apical pubescent to woolly band; style 1–3.2 mm, glabrous or pubescent at basal part. Fruit globuleux to broadly ellipsoid or broadly ovoid, 0.7–12 × 0.6–8 cm, yellow, orange, red, reddish or blue, maturing; with numerous seeds, pericarp sclerified, 1–7 mm thick, pulp white to orange. Distribution from Senegal to Sudan and southward to Angola, Zambia and Malawi; in Gabon recorded in all provinces. Ecology: primary and secondary forest, forest galleries, savanna in Gabon, up to 600 m altitude; flowers up to 1200 m. Use: The latex is a known source of rubber. The fruit pulp is edible. The plant would also be used as a remedy against intestinal worms.

## Source note

L. owariensis clearly comprises on the whole in Gabon and Cameroon several species with flowers and fruits of different forms. A large part of the herbarium material available comprises only flower buds or immature fruits or even simply sterile specimens. While there are clear differences between open flowers and ripe fruits, the form and pubescence of dried leaves vary widely. Field study is necessary, in particular to have a better idea of the form, color and taste of the fruits. As the fruits are (mostly?) edible and tasty, it is probable that the different forms of fruits bear different local names. The fruits are sometimes confused with those, axillary, of Clitandra cymulosa. B : Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961).
