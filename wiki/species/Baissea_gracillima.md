# *Baissea gracillima* (K.Schum.) Hua Planche 19 ; Figure 2(G, H)

**Genus**: [[Baissea]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Linn. Paris , sér. 2, 1 : 12 (1898)

## Description

Liana reaching 40 m in height; branches densely pubescent to puberulent. Leaf: petiole 1–20 mm, pubescent to puberulent; limb oval to obovate or oblong, 1–3.7 times as long as wide, ± 0.8–12 cm, rounded to cuneate at base, obtuse to acuminate at apex, pubescent to glabrous on both surfaces; secondary nerves (5)8 to pairs, straight at base and somewhat arched towards the apex, tertiary venation scalariform and forming right angles with the secondary nerves and the midvein; domatia pilose. Inflorescence axillary and also terminal, sometimes only terminal, lax, with dense multiflorous cymes; peduncle absent or reaching 0.3 cm, pubescent to puberulent. Flower: pedicel 0.6–2.8 mm; calyx with lobes 0.5–1 mm long, puberulent externally, glabrous internally and with 4 to 7 colleters at the base; corolla white to yellowish, greyish at the base, with cylindrical tube, 1.3–2.2 mm long, ± puberulent externally, with tufts of hairs internally, otherwise glabrous, with erect lobes, oval to oblong, 0.3–1 times as long as the tube, 1–3 times as long as wide, ± 0.3–1 mm, obtuse to subacute at apex, with variable pubescence and fine externally, glabrous internally; stamens inserted 1–2 mm from the base of the tube, exserted 0.2–0.7 mm, anther 1.8–2.1 mm long; ovary pubescent at the apex. Fruit: follicles 17–50 × 0.4–0.5 cm. Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Angola; in Gabon Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest in Gabon up to 800 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m.

## Source note

In the revision of the genus (van Dilst 1995), Blaxiflora was placed in synonymy with B multiflora and the distribution map of this species showed a very unusual pattern. Here the plants from the two disjunct zones on the map each represent again two different species, B multiflora s.s. to the west and B laxiflora to the east. There are clear characters to separate the two, as Huber had done previously. It is quite possible that there are still more cryptic species under these two names. The pilosiness of the ovary may be an index to the west of the range of B laxiflora and in western Gabon, the ovary is distinctly hairy at the apex (for example Arends et al. 583 & Le Testu 1738) and further east, the ovary is glabrous (for example Thollon 852 – holotype of Baissea thollonii Hua – and Le Testu 7141 & 7152).
