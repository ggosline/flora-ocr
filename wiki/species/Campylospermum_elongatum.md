# *Campylospermum elongatum* (Oliv.) Tiegh. Planche 9

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]

## Description

J. Bot. (Morot) 16 : 43 (Feb. 1902). Ouratea elongata (Oliv.) Engl. ex Gilg in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 1, III, 6 : 142 (1893). Spongopyrena cyanescens Tiegh., J. Bot. (Morot) 16 : 201 (June 1902). Tree reaching 7 m in height, monocaulous or rarely with a few branches towards the apex; bark brown. Stipules persistent, triangular, 8–10 mm long. Leaves in a tuft at the apex of the trunk; petiole 5–8 mm, stout; blade narrowly elliptic to narrowly obovate, 18–61 × 4–15 cm, ratio 4–5(–7), slightly cordate or sometimes rounded at the base, apex acute to acuminate, papyraceous to coriaceous, plane, with margin serrulate to spaced teeth; midvein plane and situated in a groove on the upper surface, angular on the lower surface, lateral nerves in 20 to 35 pairs, slightly canaliculate above, prominent below, tertiary venation scalariform, perpendicular to the lateral nerve, distinct on both surfaces. Inflorescence axillary, simple, rather lax, pendulous, the main axis distinctly flattened and then ribbon-like, 23–60(–83) cm × 4–5 mm, flowering part (5–)8–25 cm long; gemmal scales persistent, triangular, 6–11 mm long; cymules distant by 0.3–2.2 cm, 1- to 3(–6)-flowered; bracts very short. Flower: pedicel 5–10 mm, articulate at approximately 1 mm from the base; sepals ovate to triangular, 7–9 × 2–4 mm in flower, 8–10 × 3–5 mm in fruit; petals elliptic, 7–9 × 3 mm, cuneate at the base, apex acute; stamens: anthers 5–7 mm long; style approximately 6 mm. Fruit: receptacle approximately 10(–12) mm in diameter; 3 to 5 well-developed druplets per receptacle, broadly reniform to almost globose, 8–10 × 6–10 mm; cotyledons accumbent, equal.

## Distribution

from Nigeria to Cameroon, southern Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda) and the Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, common throughout the country

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, riparian or periodically inundated forest, on sandy humic soil; in Gabon, up to 800 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1150 m. Taxonomic note: Species easily recognizable by its remarkably long and flattened axillary peduncles. b: Bamps & Farron (1967), Bissiengou (2014), Exell & Mendonça (1951), Bibliography Farron (1965, 1968, 1985), Hutchinson, Dalziel & Keay (1954)
