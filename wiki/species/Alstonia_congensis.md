# *Alstonia congensis* Engl. Planche 10

**Genus**: [[Alstonia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 8 : 64 (1887)

## Description

Tree reaching 25(–30) m in height; trunk reaching 1 m in diameter, generally fluted at the base, with or without buttresses reaching up to 6 m in height; bark smooth or lenticellate, white in the bark, branches, leaves, flowers and fruits. Leaves verticillate in whorls of 4 to 8, with numerous large axillary colleters; petiole absent or up to 0.5(–1) cm; limb obovate, or sometimes narrowly obovate, 1.4–2.9 times as long as wide, 8–24 × 4–11 cm, with acuminate or sometimes obtuse or retuse apex; secondary veins 36 to 72 pairs, almost perpendicular to the midvein, connected to each other by a submarginal vein. Inflorescences composed of quasi-umbels or up to an ensemble of — × 5.5–19.5 cm; ultimate ramifications sometimes slightly pubescent, the 2 or 3 first ramifications umbellate, peduncle and the upper ramifications relatively long, bracteate up to mm in length. Flower: pedicel (0.2–)0.5–0.9 cm; calyx with lobes 1.3–3 × 1.3–2.5 mm, with a few marginal hairs; corolla yellow or pale pink, pubescent on the outside and on the lobes on the inside, on the inside with a ring of hirsute hairs at the throat and stiff curved hairs lower down, tube 0.6–1.1 times as long as the lobes, 4–7.5 mm long, narrowed above the ovary and 1–2 mm wide, lobes deformed in bud, curving to the left, obliquely obovate or sometimes obliquely ovate, — × — mm; stamens inserted in the upper part of the tube, anther 1.3–1.7 mm long; pistil 3–5.5 mm long, with style 1–3 mm, glabrous. Fruit: linear follicles, connate at the base, 17–40 × 0.2–0.4 cm, glabrous or with a few small hairs, with numerous biseriate seeds. Seed flattened, 6–9.2 mm long, with margin thickened all around, with long stiff hairs at the extremities and much shorter ones on the sides. Distribution: Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola. Cabinda in Gabon. Estuary, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué, Maritime.

Illustration by Juliet Williamson, Naturalis Biodiversity Center (©), reproduced from de Jong (1979).

Ecology: confined to swampy zones or to shallow soils on rocks in Gabon up to 440 m altitude, elsewhere at 10–470 m. Uses: The bark is an efficacious remedy against blenorrhagia. It is also known for its galactogenic properties. The wood is used to make spoons, combs, masks, harps, chairs, drums and bells for hunting dogs.
