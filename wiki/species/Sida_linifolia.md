# *Sida linifolia* Juss. ex Cav. Planche 24

**Genus**: [[Sida]]
**Family**: [[Malvaceae]]
**Protologue**: Diss. 1 : 14, t. 2., fig. 1 (1785)

## Description

, nom. illeg. , non St. Hilaire (1827). Herb or suffrutex erect, 0.3–1 m in height, with simple or very branched stem. Stipules filiform, very small, 1–2(–5) mm, persistent even after leaf loss, with some long hairs. Leaf: petiole 3–5 mm, hirsute; lamina linear, up to 12 × 1.2(–1.5) cm, briefly attenuate toward the rounded base, very long attenuate toward the acute apex, with long appressed hairs, margin entire. Flowers in terminal corymbs, 4–20-flowered; pedicel 1 cm, articulate at 2 mm from the apex; calyx campanulate, 5 mm in length, with triangular lobes approximately 2.5 mm in length, acuminate, foliaceous, pilose; petals approximately 10 mm in length, white or pale yellow, with yellow, orange or purple base, faded in the afternoon; staminal tube 3 mm in length, filaments free on the half of their length, anthers numerous; ovary ± spherical, with 5–7(–9) carpels; style divided into 5–9 branches, 5 mm in length, far exceeding the anthers. Fruit with trigonous mericarps 2–2.5 mm in length, not or briefly apiculate, opening by the thin lateral walls. Seeds trigonous, brown.

## Distribution

very common species present throughout tropical Africa and tropical America; in Gabon common, known from Estuaire, Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga, Ngounié and Moyen-Ogooué

## Ecology

sandy savannas, fallows, fields, palm groves, open forests, damp and shaded or sunny places; in Gabon, observed up to 130 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m. Flowers and fruits from August to May. Uses: The stems are used for the manufacture of brooms

## Vernacular names

balai guad, sida à feuille de lin (fr.) ; balai grand (angl.) ; « noms vernaculaires locales les mêmes que ceux de Abutilon mauritianum Sw. » (RapondaWalker & Sillans 1961). B : Akoègninou et al. (2006), Berhaut (1979), Hauman (1963), Rapondai B liographie Walker & Sillans (1961), Verdcourt (2009)
