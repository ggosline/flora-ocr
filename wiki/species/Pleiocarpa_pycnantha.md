# *Pleiocarpa pycnantha* (K.Schum.) Stapf Planche 91 ; Figure 9(E, F)

**Genus**: [[Pleiocarpa]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Trop. Afr. 4(1) : 99 (1902)

## Description

Fl. Trop. Afr. 4(1) : 99 (1902). Shrub or tree 1.5–20(–30) m tall, rarely scandent; branches often with exfoliating bark. Leaves opposite and in whorls of 3 to 5, always whorled at least at one node of each branch of the same plant; petiole 5–20 mm; blade narrowly elliptic to oblong, rarely obovate—sometimes also with the width of—to—cm, rounded to acute or decurrent on the petiole at the base, obtuse to acuminate at the apex; secondary venation 15 to 25 pairs forming an angle of—with the midvein and united in a submarginal nerve, tertiary venation discrete. Inflorescence axillary, rarely also terminal, sometimes ramified, fasciculate of—to—cm with sessile flowers or with pedicel of—mm, bracteate with very small squamiform bracts. Flower fragrant; pedicel 1–3 mm; calyx with lobes 1–3 mm long; corolla white to orange-yellow, 8–13(–15) mm long in the bud and forming a comparatively narrow ovoid head of—of the length of the bud, rounded at the apex to subcylindric tube 2–4 mm long, 0.8–1.2 mm wide above the base, narrowed to 0.6–1.5 mm width under the insertion of the stamens, again enlarged to 0.7–2 mm at the level of the anthers, with spreading and subsequently recurved lobes with an incurved apex, ovate or elliptic to suborbicular—sometimes also as long as the tube—sometimes as long as the wide of—to—mm, stamens with apex arriving just below the throat, inserted above the midpoint of the tube, anther 0.6–1.3 × 0.15–0.5 mm; pistil 4.6–8.6(–9.7) mm long, ovary with 2 distinct carpels, 2-ovulate; style 3.8–8 mm long. Fruit yellow to orange, with 2 distinct mericarps, subglobose to pyriform, sometimes stipitate, 13–23(–30) × 6.5–24 × 5–15 mm, acuminate to rounded at the apex, smooth or wrinkled, with 2 seeds. Seed 6.5–13.5 mm long. Distribution from Senegal to Kenya, Mozambique and Angola to Gabon, Ogooué, Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology submontane forest in Gabon at—to—m altitude elsewhere up to—m. Use: The wood is used for local construction, for the making of combs, plane bodies, tool handles and pestles, and also for carved objects. Pounded roots mixed with seeds of Aframomum melegueta K.Schum. and palm wine are taken as a laxative. In Benin, leaves macerated in lemon juice are administered to patients suffering from jaundice, edema, decreased urine secretion or ascariasis. B : Lemmens (2008), Omino (1996, 2002). BIBLIOGRAPHY
