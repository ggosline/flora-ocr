# *Vitex doniana* Sweet Planche 26 ; Figure 5(D-F)

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]
**Protologue**: Hort. Brit . : 323 (1826)

## Description

? V. milnei W.Piep., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 62, Beibl. 141 ('142') : 71 (1928). V. pachyphylla Baker, in Thiselton-Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 5(2) : 328 (1900). ? V. puberula Baker, in Thiselton-Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 5(2) : 330 (1900). Tree reaching 15–30 m in height; branches pubescent, greyish to tawny. Leaves sometimes whorled by three; petiole 5–15 cm, with short appressed hairs; leaflets 5 to 7, with petiolules 5–15 mm, well distinct from the leaflets, with elliptic to obovate-elliptic lamina, 5–18 × 4–8(–10) cm, cuneate at the base, rounded or, more rarely, triangular to obtusely acuminate at the apex, coriaceous, glabrous or, rarely, shortly pubescent on the lower surface of the midvein, upper surface glossy, lower surface without prominent glands; lateral veins 8 to 12(to 16) on each side, reticulation almost invisible on both surfaces. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia, 4–12(–18) cm long, of which 2–6(–12) cm for the peduncle, generally much shorter than the axillary leaf; axes angular, with short appressed hairs, greyish to pale tawny. Flower: calyx 2–)3–4 mm long, entirely covered with short appressed hairs, tawny to greyish, with lobes ± 1 mm long; corolla 8–12 mm long, pale blue to pale purple or rarely yellow, whitish at the throat; stamens slightly exserted; ovary hairy at the apex. Fruit ± 15–30 × 10–20 mm, blackish, subtended by the spreading calyx.

## Distribution

widely distributed throughout tropical Africa, including the Comoros, the Seychelles and Madagascar; in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

savannas, shrubby and wooded savannas, termite mounds, semi-deciduous forests, more rarely dense forests, edges of estuaries, lakes and lagoons; in Gabon at 0–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1400 m. Uses: The wood is extensively used in joinery and cabinet-making, also for making pirogues and paddles. The fruits are highly sought after and consumed raw or as jam. Cooked leaves are consumed as a vegetable. The plant has numerous medicinal uses. This species is the subject of research programs aimed at its domestication and multiplication in cultivation (Achigan Dako et al. 2014)

## Source note

There exist in Gabon forms with larger, more diffuse inflorescence, and with short calyx (2 mm in length), which were described under the name V. milnei W.Piep. Other forms, with the lower surface of leaflets greyish, shortly pubescent on the veins, were described under the name V. puberula Baker. The taxonomic value of these variations needs to be re-examined. For relationships with V. grandifolia, see note under that species.
