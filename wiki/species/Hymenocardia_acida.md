# *Hymenocardia acida* Tul. Planche 9(F-H)

**Genus**: [[Hymenocardia]]
**Family**: [[Phyllanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann . Sci . Nat ., Bot ., sér. 3, 15 : 256 (1851)

## Description

Shrub to small tree up to 5 m tall; branches with rhytidome exfoliating in thin lamellae covering a pulverulent and red tissue; branchlets pubescent to glabrescent. Stipules linear, 4–6 mm long. Leaf: petiole 4–14 mm, densely pubescent when young; blade narrowly elliptic to oblong or ± obovate, 3–11 × 1.5–4(–7) cm, acute at base, ± obtuse, rounded or sometimes acute at apex, densely tomentose to glabrous above, densely tomentose to glabrous and with numerous glandular discs below; lateral veins 5–9(–11) pairs. Inflorescence: the male ones solitary or fascicled, 2.5–9 cm long, with velutinous-pubescent rachis and glandular discs. Male flower very caducous; calyx dentate or scarcely lobed, glabrous or ± pubescent; anthers pilose at apex; pistillode 1–2 mm long, bilobed with pilose lobes. Female flower axillary, 1 or 2 per node, with pedicel 2 mm, reaching 1–1.5 cm in fruit; sepals 5–9, 3 × 0.5 mm; ovary winged at upper angles, 2 × 1 mm, scattered with glandular discs; styles 3–4 mm, accrescent to 1.5 cm. Fruit V-shaped (see Plate 9F), 1.5–3.5 × 2.5 cm, generally separating into two winged valves in the upper half. Seeds ± ovate, 0.9–1.3 × 0.5 cm.

Domatia. (A ‒ D: Le Testu 9404; E: Breteler & de Wilde 597; F ‒ H: Sosef et al. 2186). Drawing by H. de Vries, NCB Naturalis (section NHN) ©.

## Distribution

Tropical Africa, from Senegal to Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Republic of Congo towards Ethiopia in the east and to Mozambique, Angola and South Africa; in Gabon known only from Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié and Nyanga

## Ecology

shrubby savanna; in Gabon at 100–600 m altitude

## Uses

multiple uses in tropical Africa, but very few reported from Gabon. The acidic leaves, stem bark and roots are commonly used remedies. The wood is used to make charcoal for forges and house stakes. Some peoples use an infusion of the bark to dye raphia cloth a reddish-brown colour

## Vernacular names

cœurs-volants (fr.) ; heart-fruit (angl.) ; ogaraga (ambèdè). b : Keay (1958), Leonard & Mosango (1985), Pax & Hoffman (1922), ibliographie Schmelzer (2008b)
