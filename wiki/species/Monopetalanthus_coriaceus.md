# *Monopetalanthus coriaceus* Morel ex Aubréville, sp. nov. $ ^{1} $

**Genus**: [[Monopetalanthus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

Trees. Buds scaly, globose, attaining great development at the time of leaf emergence. The upper elliptic bracts then reach up to 3 cm in length by 1.5 cm in width. Very caducous, they leave on the branchlet pronounced annular stipular scars, and at the base of the branchlet very closely-spaced scars. Young branchlets and rachis rusty-tomentose. Rachis up to 17 cm in length. Leaves with 25-30 pairs of opposite leaflets. Leaflets narrowly oblong, attenuate-acute at the apex, sessile, with truncate base, ± auricate, glabrous, finely nerved, coriaceous, measuring 2-3 cm × 4-5 mm.

Flowers unknown. Legumes woody, flat, oblong, with a poorly pronounced median longitudinal nerve, approximately 14 × 6 cm.

## Type

Morel 134, Gabon (P)

## Discussion

This species is described in "La Forêt du Gabon" by de Saint-Aubin, with photographs of the leaves and tree base (p. 54) under the name "Andoung de Morel, Monopetalanthus sp. nov. (herb. C.T.F.T., Gabon 1935)". In the herbarium the specimens are labelled "Monopetalanthus coriaceus". This is the name we have adopted.

De Saint-Aubin describes it as a large tree, widespread throughout the entire Noya basin and in the Crystal Mountains where it forms significant stands in low-lying zones. The base of the trunk is sometimes fairly strongly buttressed. Trunk cylindrical. Bark smooth, approximately 17 mm thick, with a hard, fibrous fracture, ocre-red in colour. Wood pale pinkish-brown, rapidly becoming fairly dark pink, soft, light.
