# *Odyendyea gabonensis* (Pierre) Engl.

**Family**: [[Simaroubaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Odyendyea]]

## Synonymy

- *Quassia gabonensis* Pierre (1896)
- *Quassia Klaineana* Pierre (1896)

## Description

Tree with thick, cylindrical, blackish branchlets. Leaves glabrous, imparipinnate, with 4–6 pairs of leaflets; rachis 30–50 cm, swollen and broadly channelled at the base. Leaflets oblong, apex truncate or slightly emarginate, base cuneate or rounded, to 12 × 4–5 cm; blade coriaceous, very dark glossy green above and pale matt green beneath; midrib prominent beneath, about ten almost perpendicular secondary veins nearly invisible; petiolules about 3 mm, thick and channelled.

Terminal panicles shorter than the leaves, with glabrous branches. Pedicels 3 mm. Flowers bisexual, usually 4-merous, sometimes 5-merous. Calyx shortly cup-shaped with very short obtuse lobes. Petals imbricate in bud, 5 mm, glabrous outside and pubescent inside. Stamens 8(–10), about 8 mm, longer than the petals, folded in bud and bearing a woolly ligule at the base. Disc prominent and cushion-shaped. Carpels 4, free but united in appearance; styles fused.

Fruit a large red obovoid drupe to 7 cm, with a median groove above. Stone very thick and hard, reinforced by a longitudinal crest, enclosing one oily seed.

## Distribution

Known from Cameroon and Gabon. In Gabon it is scattered and never abundant in primary forest; cited localities include Libreville, the lower Ogooué, Lambaréné, Tchibanga, Bilagone, and the Libreville–Kango road.

## Habitat and ecology

Subcanopy tree with a deeply fluted trunk, enlarged base, broad spherical crown and dense dark foliage. Bark ash-grey and finely longitudinally fissured; slash thick, fibrous and pale yellow. Flowers in August–September and attracts bees; fruit recorded in May. The white, soft wood was used by hunters to carve bells for hunting dogs.

## Specimens examined

**Gabon** — Klaine 49 (holotype, Libreville), 125, 223-172, 410; Autran 249; Dybowski III (Achouka, lower Ogooué); 26570 (Lambaréné); 33548 (Libreville); Le Testu 1782 (Tchibanga); Sargos 95 (Kouilou); Aubréville 116 (Bilagone); S.R.S.F. 121 (km 34, Libreville–Kango road).

## Vernacular names

*onzan*, *nzan* (Fang); *ozènegé*, *odyendyè* (Nkomi); *moucighiri* (Bayaka); *limbi limbi*.
