# *Clerodendrum umbellatum* Poir. Planche 9 ; Figure 3(G)

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Encycl. 5 : 166 (1804)

## Description

C. umbellatum Poir. f. scandens (P.Beauv.) Moldenke, Phytologia 2 : 330 (1983). Herb erect, monocaulous, or shrub or twining liana, climbing from a woody rootstock, reaching 5 m in height; stem subcylindrical to (sub)quadrangular, narrowly hollow, sometimes weakly striated, often twisted, pale brown, brown or reddish-brown, densely pubescent, eventually ± glabrescent, often with pseudospines of 2–6 mm. Leaves opposite; petiole 1–4.5(–5.5) cm; lamina ovate to oblong-ovate or suborbicular, 3.3–7.8(–18) × 1.5–11 cm, truncate or slightly to distinctly cordate at base, long-acuminate at apex, ± discolorous, ± glabrous except on the veins beneath, often with clear or black punctiform glands. Inflorescence terminal, lax, paniculate, reaching c. 30 × 10(–20) cm, with length/width ratio of 1.5–2; axes shortly pubescent; cymes distant by 0.5–6.5 cm, with 6 to 20 flowers; peduncle 2.8–6(–8.5) cm; bracts foliaceous, 1.5–7 × 0.5–2.8(–5) cm, sometimes also articulate to leave retrorsely directed pseudospines. Flower: pedicel 4–7(–12) mm; calyx often whitish-green, entirely or marginally pink, dark red, plum or brown at maturity, puberulent, with tube 5 mm long, with lobes narrowly elliptic-ovate to triangular, 3–7 × 3–4 mm, acute at apex, pinkish; corolla fragrant, white, cream or greenish, with red throat, one lobe often with crimson or red marks or all mauve, with slender tube, 10–15(–17) × 1–2 mm, with elliptic lobes, 6–10 × 4 mm, rounded at apex; stamens exserted by 3–4(–5) cm. Fruit 10–12 × 7 mm, shiny, dark green, greyish-black at maturity.

## Distribution

Tropical West Africa, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gulf of Guinea islands, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, North-East Africa and tropical East Africa; introduced and cultivated in Meso-America, tropical South America, Florida and India; in Gabon: Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem. Conservation status: preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC) by Pollard in Cheek et al. (2004)

## Ecology

Secondary forests, swamp forests, forest margins, roadsides, gravel pits; in Gabon at 250–600 m elevation, elsewhere up to 1800 m. Use: The leaves yield a black dye material used in the Republic of the Congo. This use is not known from Gabon
