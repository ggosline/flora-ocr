# *Millettia griffoniana* Baill. Planche 90

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Adansonia 6 : 222 (1866)

## Description

Lonchocarpus griffonianus (Baill.) Dunn, J. Bot. 49 : 15 (1911). Tree, often 5–10 m tall, but can reach 45 m; branches tortuous. Stipules caducous, not peltate, ovate-lanceolate, ± 5 × 1–3 mm, finely striate. Leaf: petiole 2–5 cm; rachis 3–10 cm, glabrous, channelled above; stipels caducous, setaceous, (1–)2–4.5 mm; leaflets (5–)7 to 11, sometimes alternate, with glabrous petiolule 3–4 mm, lamina ovate to oblong, 5–10 × 2–5 cm, base obtuse to cuneate and furnished with a pocket on each side, apex long-acuminate, shiny above, glaucous below, glabrous; lateral veins in 6 to 9 pairs. Inflorescence axillary, in pendulous and simple pseudoracemes, 10–30 cm long, slender; bracts fairly persistent, lanceolate, ± 1 mm long, curved, on a small cushion. Flower: pedicel slender, 4–8 mm, somewhat hairy; bracteoles very caducous, at the base of the calyx; calyx with tube 4–5 mm long, lobes 1–2 mm long, the upper ones joined, dark brown to purple, margins white-hairy; corolla white or pink to purple; standard elliptic-obcordate, ± 18 × 12 mm, with claw 3.5 mm, with 2 callosities, wings elliptic, ± 15 × 3 mm, keel obtuse, ± 15 × 5 mm, with auriculate base; stamens diadelphous (9 + 1); ovary linear, pubescent, with 3 to 7 ovules. Pod pendulous, very tardily dehiscent, oblong, flattened, 3.5–14 × 0.9–3 cm, with base and apex attenuate, with 1 to 4 seeds, pale brown, with very short pubescence. Seed very flattened, reniform to ovate or ± rectangular, 13–14 × 10–12 × 2–3 mm, dark brown; hilum 2.5–4 mm.

## Distribution

from Côte d'Ivoire to Cameroon, to Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

riparian and coastal forest, swampy, also in savannas; in Gabon at 60–470 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–500 m. Flowers and fruits throughout the year

Use: The flowers clean linen. The leaves mixed with other essences are used in fumigation. There are several uses in traditional medicine in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cameroon and West Africa. The species is mentioned as very ornamental.

## Vernacular names

awonghe, awong, mewonghe-nkwara (fang) ; gitsiga (éshira?) ; kota-gota (mit sogo) ; mbola (béséki) ; nkwètyè (bakèlè) ; nyimu (nkomi du Bas-Ougoué) ; ogota (ivéa)

## Source note

In the literature, this species has been confused with M. thonningii (Schumach. & Thonn.) Baker, of the section Sericanthae due to confused nomenclature (Adomou et al., in prep.). The two taxa are clearly distinct. b: Banzouzi et al. (2008), Pellegrin (1949), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). Bibliography
