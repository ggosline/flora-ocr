# *Millettia griffoniana* Baill. Planche 90

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Adansonia 6 : 222 (1866)

## Synonyms

- Lonchocarpus griffonianus (Baill.) Dunn, J. Bot. 49 : 15 (1911).

## Description

Tree, often 5–10 m in height, but reaching up to 45 m; branches tortuous. Stipules caducous, not peltate, ovate-lanceolate, ± 5 × 1–3 mm, finely striate. Leaf: petiole 2–5 cm; rachis 3–10 cm, glabrous, channelled above; stipels caducous, setaceous, (1–)2–4.5 mm; leaflets (5–)7 to 11, sometimes alternate, with glabrous petiolule 3–4 mm, blade ovate to oblong, 5–10 × 2–5 cm, base obtuse to cuneate and bearing a pocket on each side, apex long acuminate, shiny above, glaucous below, glabrous; lateral nerves in 6 to 9 pairs. Inflorescence axillary, in simple pendant pseudoracemes, 10–30 cm long, slender; bracts fairly persistent, lanceolate, ± 1 mm long, curved, on a small cushion. Flower: pedicel slender, 4–8 mm, slightly pubescent; bracteoles very caducous, at the base of the calyx; calyx with tube 4–5 mm long, lobes 1–2 mm long, the upper ones joined, dark brown to purple, white-hairy margins; corolla white or pink to purple; standard elliptic-obcordate, ± 18 × 12 mm, with claw 3.5 mm, with 2 callosities, wings elliptic, ± 15 × 3 mm, keel obtuse, ± 15 × 5 mm, with auriculate base; stamens diadelphous (9 + 1); ovary linear, pubescent, with 3 to 7 ovules. Pod pendant, very tardily dehiscent, oblong, flattened, 3.5–14 × 0.9–3 cm, with base and apex attenuate, with 1 to 4 seeds, pale brown, with very short pubescence. Seed very flattened, reniform to ovate or ± rectangular, 13–14 × 10–12 × 2–3 mm, dark brown; hilum 2.5–4 mm.

## Distribution

from Côte d'Ivoire to Cameroon, in Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

riverine and coastal forest, swampy, also in savannas; in Gabon at 60–470 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–500 m. Flowers and fruits year-round

Uses: The flowers remove grease from laundry. The leaves mixed with other essences are used in fumigation. There are several uses in traditional medicine in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cameroon and West Africa. The species is noted as very ornamental.

## Source note

In the literature, this species has been confused with M. thonningii (Schumach. & Thonn.) Baker, of the section Sericanthae due to confused nomenclature (Adomou et al., in prep.).
