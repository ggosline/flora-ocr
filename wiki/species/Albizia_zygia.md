Tree reaching a height of 24 m and a circumference of 2.4 m. Bole base with small, barely distinct buttresses (sometimes well developed). Main branches tortuous, widely spreading. Bark with normally greyish rhytidome, smooth, sometimes weakly fissured; thin section, yellowish or orange-brown, granular, slowly exuding dark brown gum. Petiole glabrous or pubescent, 4-10 cm long; weakly flattened or weakly canaliculate above towards the apex, bearing a prominent gland at the base. Rachis pubescent, 2-13 cm long, prominent ridge on the upper face, apex sometimes bearing a gland. Pinnae 2-3 pairs, broadly obtriangular. Pinna axis pubescent, 2.5-9.5 cm long; canaliculate at the base then prominent ridge above on the upper face with prominent gland at the distal extremity. Leaflets 2-5 pairs opposite, of decreasing size from apex towards base, subpetiolulate. Blade glabrous on both surfaces (except the margins and base at the level of the subpetiolule); brown-green to black-brown; bright appearance above and light brown-green to pale matt green below when dry; rhombic to broadly asymmetrically elliptic ± falciform, reaching 11.4 × 6 cm; asymmetric base: proximal edge attenuate to rounded and distal edge attenuate to cuneiform, apex ± obtuse to acute, sometimes mucronate. Main vein diagonal, straight to arched (concave towards the distal edge), prominent on both surfaces; pubescent to glabrescent on both surfaces. Secondary veins 4-5 pairs, not prominent above and prominent below. Tertiary venation in dense reticulum above, scarcely visible below.

Inflorescences in capitate spikes, axillary to young leaves, solitary or fascicled, or in terminal panicles of capitate spikes. Spike peduncle pubescent, with hairs reflexed towards the base, 2.5-4 cm long, slightly claviform at the apex. Bracteole pubescent externally, narrowly linear or narrowly lanciolate with slender base, approximately 1.25 mm long. Floral bud ellipsoid; apex of the stamen tube spiral. Basal flower sessile, white in life. Calyx cylindric, pubescent externally, approximately 3 mm long; 5-7 free lobes, short with acute apex. Corolla with 5 petals pubescent externally, approximately 8 mm long, united into a tube for approximately 5 mm, free lobes fairly narrowly lanceolate, with acute apex. Stamens numerous, united into a red tube in life, 2-2.5 cm long, long-exsert, anther early caducous, ellipsoid, 0.5 mm long. Pistil shortly stipitate; ovary glabrous, narrowly ellipsoid, approximately 2.5 mm long, with 9-12 ovules; style slender, 1.75-2.25 cm long with capitate stigma. Central flower sessile. Calyx cylindric to ovoid, pubescent, approximately 7 mm long, apical teeth acute (more than 5). Corolla pubescent, approximately 10.5 mm long. Stamens approximately 16 mm long, united into a non-exsert tube, free parts reflexed; anthers as previously. Pistil not observed.

Pod glabrous, bright shining brown, 10-20 × approximately 2 cm; cuneiformed stipitate base; faces with fairly dense venation, in elongate meshes ± perpendicular to the sutural edges; apex rounded sometimes apiculate. Seeds 9-12 ± orbicular, reaching 1.25 cm, oblong central areole.

Epigeal germination. Main root developing for a long time (6-7 cm) before the emergence of cotyledons. Cotyledons orbicular or broadly elliptic. Hypocotyl axis pubescent, 4-4.5 cm long. Epicotyl axis pubescent, 1-3 cm long. Stipules of the first leaves ± persistent, glabrous to pubescent, very narrowly deltoid, apex acute. Primordial leaves opposite with a single pair of pinnae. Petiole pubescent, approximately 0.6-1 cm long, bearing a prominent gland at the apex of the upper surface. Pinnae with 3-4 pairs of leaflets; pinna axis pubescent, 1.15-1.7 cm long, bearing or not bearing glands at the level of the 1-2 pairs of apical leaflets; axis terminated by a slender mucro; leaflet like the adult ones, entirely glabrous, 0.6-1.7 cm long. Following young leaves alternate, similar to the preceding ones but pubescent on the veins of both surfaces. - Pl. 35, p. 139.

## Discussion

The essential distinction between A. zygia and A. letestui rests on the greater pubescence of the vegetative and floriferous apparatus of the latter type (Le Testu 8060, Gabon, Levaca). In fact A. zygia is very variable in its pubescence and that observed on A. letestui is only an extreme case. It is therefore appropriate to accept the synonymy between the two taxa. A. zygia remains alone valid because of the priority of its basionym.

Specimen IRCAM 15895 HNC (Cameroon) bears on the rachises and axes of the pinnae, on the base of the leaflets, short ± conical excrescences giving the impression of stipellary spines or glands. These morphological modifications are the result of predatory animals.

This plant is widely represented throughout tropical Africa. It is a common species, characteristic of secondary forests. It can also persist in savannas near forest regions. Flowering appears abundant during the two rainy seasons.
