# *Jasminum pauciflorum* Benth. Planche 5

**Genus**: [[Jasminum]]
**Family**: [[Oleaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. preussii Engl. & Knobl. forma minutiflorum Roberty, Bull. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 15 : 1423 (1953),

## Description

in Hook., Niger Fl. : 443 (1849). J. preussii Engl. & Knobl. forma minutiflorum Roberty, Bull. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 15 : 1423 (1953), syn. nov. Liane or lianescent shrub; branchlets, leaves (at least on the main veins) and inflorescences puberulent or pubescent to hirsute. Leaves simple (unifoliolate), opposite or subopposite; petiole straight or geniculate, subcylindric, 3–7(–9) mm; blade ovate-lanceolate to elliptic, (4–)5–8 × (2–)2.5–4 cm, cordate to obtuse or rarely cuneate at the base, acute to slightly acuminate over 0.5–1.5 cm at the apex, with plane margin; lateral veins in (2–)3 to 5 pairs, the basal pair often very prominent and ascending to the midpoint of the blade or beyond; domatia present, sometimes somewhat obscure. Inflorescence axillary and terminal, cymose, with up to 3 flowers; peduncle 8–13 mm; bracts filiform, 2–8 mm. Flower: pedicel (8–)11–15 mm; calyx with tube 1.5–2(–3) mm long, with 5(–6) lobes (4–)6–7 mm long, puberulent, slightly accrescent in fruit; corolla white, glabrous, with tube 14–18(–20) mm long, with 7 narrowly lanceolate lobes 10–15 mm long; stamens inserted in the upper half of the corolla tube, anthers 3.5–4.5 mm long, apiculate, slightly exsert; pistil ± as long as the corolla tube or ± less than half as long (heterostyled flowers); ovary subglobose, ± 1 mm in diameter; stigma ± 2 mm long. Fruit ± spherical, 8–10 mm in diameter, glabrous. Seed ± ellipsoid, 7–8 × 5–6 mm.

## Distribution

throughout tropical Africa, from Guinea-Bissau to Kenya, Mozambique and Angola; in Gabon, known from a single collection from the Estuaire and perhaps also from Ogooué-Ivindo (see Notes)

## Ecology

primary and secondarized forest, forest galleries, fallow land; in Gabon, at low altitude up to 700 m, elsewhere up to 2000 m

## Source note

The single Gabon collection of this species, dating from the early twentieth century, perhaps indicates that in this case it is an old introduction, at least near Libreville. On the other hand, photograph no. 2081 in Vande weghe et al. (2016) suggests that its presence in Minkébé National Park is indeed natural. Investigation of the type of J. preussii f. minutiflorum Roberty (1953) led to the conclusion that it is a very hairy specimen of J. pauciflorum. Bibliography: Breteler (2002), Green (1963), Kupicha (1983), Liben (1973), Turrill (1952), Vande weghe et al. (2016)
