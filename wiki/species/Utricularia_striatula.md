# *Utricularia striatula* Sm. Planche 44, Figure 16

**Genus**: [[Utricularia]]
**Family**: [[Lentibulariaceae]]

## Description

in Rees, Cycl. 37 : No. 17 (1819). Epiphytic herb (not in the Flora) or lithophyte; rhizoids and capillary stolons, numerous at the base of the flowering scape. Leaves numerous, visible at anthesis, in a rosette at the base of the flowering scape and scattered on the stolons, petiolate, obovate, orbicular or reniform, 3–10 × 1–6 mm. Utricles numerous on the stolons, globose, long-stipitate, 0.6–0.8 mm in diameter; orifice lateral, with upper lip shortly prominent, furnished with 2 divergent appendices covered with hairs terminating in a gland. Inflorescence erect, straight, 1–15 cm long, with 1 to 10 spaced flowers; peduncle filiform, glabrous; bracts medifixed, 1.5–2 mm long, narrowly ovate, slightly narrowed at the base, acute or obtuse above the point of insertion, ovate or oblong and truncate below. Flower: bracteoles similar to the bracts, but narrower; pedicel spreading, sometimes reflexed in fruit, capillary, 2–6 mm; calyx with unequal, accrescent lobes, upper lobe orbicular-obcordate, 1.5–2.5 mm long at anthesis, emarginate at the apex, lower lobes broadly ovate or oblong, about half the length of the upper lobe, retuse or truncate; corolla 3–6 mm long in chasmogamous flowers, strongly reduced in cleistogamous flowers, white or mauve with a yellow patch on the palate, upper lip much smaller than the upper calyx lobe, reduced to 2 minute teeth, lower lip orbicular, 3–10 mm wide, ± deeply 5-lobed, palate slightly prominent, obscurely 2- to 4-ridged, spur subulate, curved or ± straight and equalling ± the lower lip. Fruit globose, obliquely and dorsiventrally compressed, with abaxial surface keeled and dehiscent by a single longitudinal slit, with few to numerous seeds. Seed ovoid, ± 0.25 mm long, glochidiate and with prominent terminal hilum.

## Distribution

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Bioko, Príncipe, Chad, Central African Republic, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, Ethiopia, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia and Angola; also from India to Southeast Asia and to Papua New Guinea; in Gabon, observed in the Estuaire and Ngounié

## Ecology

among mosses on trees and rocks in forest, particularly in sites with high humidity such as, for example, near waterfalls; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 3300 m. B: Taylor (1964, 1972, 1989). Bibliography
