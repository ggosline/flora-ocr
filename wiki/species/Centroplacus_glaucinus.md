# *Centroplacus glaucinus* Pierre

**Genus**: [[Centroplacus]]
**Family**: [[Centroplacaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Mens. Soc. Linn. Paris n.s. 14: 115 (1899)

## Description

Plate 10

Microdesmis paniculata Pax, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 28(1): 25 (1899).

Tree reaching up to 20 m in height; trunk up to 23(−30) cm in diameter, with smooth or finely fissured bark, dark brown, with red inner bark and reddish wood; branchlets slender, cylindrical. Stipules narrowly triangular, ±1 mm long. Leaf: petiole 3–6 mm;

lamina narrowly elliptic to elliptic-oblong or sometimes elliptic-obovate, (6–7).5–19 × (2–2).5–7 cm, cuneate at base, gradually to narrowly acuminate and mucronate at apex, chartaceous to subcoriaceous, reddish violet when young, often glaucous above when dried, with margin entire to slightly serrate in upper half; secondary nerves 5 to 7, curved. Inflorescence: male one 4–8 cm long, female one (2.5–)4–10 cm long, with puberulent axes; bracts subulate, ±1 mm long, obtuse at apex, puberulent. Male flower: pedicel ±1 mm, puberulent; sepals broadly ovate, ±1 mm long, obtuse to rounded at apex, coriaceous, whitish green to yellowish green, puberulent on outside; petals broadly elliptic to broadly obovate, ±2 mm long, whitish, puberulent on both faces; stamens with short filament, anther ±0.4 mm long, brown; disk fleshy, slightly lobed; pistillode 1.5 mm long. Female flower: sepals obovate, ±1.5 mm long, whitish green; staminodes very small; ovary ovoid to broadly ellipsoid, puberulent to tomentellous; styles ±0.2 mm long, dark violet. Fruit ovoid to broadly ellipsoid or broadly obovoid, 5–8 × 5–7 mm, tomentellous, dark pink to red pink, exocarp cartilaginous, with 1 to 3 seeds. Seed 3–4 mm long, with red, orange aril.

## Distribution

known from southern Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and south-western Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, widely distributed and known from all provinces

## Ecology

primary and secondary forests, on hills, ridges, but also on rock slabs and along rivers, on terra firme to swampy ground; in Gabon at 15–800 m elevation, elsewhere up to 900 m

Usage: In Gabon, a decoction of leaves is taken to treat schizophrenia.

## Vernacular names

poumbeghani (massango)
