# *Lonchocarpus sericeus* (Poir.) Kunth Planche 81

**Genus**: [[Lonchocarpus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Humboldt, Bonpland & Kunth, Nov. gen. sp. 6: 383 (1824). Tree or shrub 4–15 m tall; trunk up to 60 cm in diameter; bark dark grey; branchlets brown-pubescent becoming glabrous, with thick, white lenticels. Leaf: petiole 3–4 cm; rachis 4–8 cm; stipels absent; leaflets (5–)7 or 9, with petiolule 5–7 mm, blade ovate-oblong, 6–15 × 4–8 cm, apex rounded and slightly acuminate, with rusty pubescence on all the veins of the lower surface. Inflorescence 8–15 × ± 4 cm, with pubescent axis generally bearing 2 flowers per brachyblast. Flower: pedicel 5–6 mm; bracteoles 1.5 × 2 mm; calyx 4–6 mm long, with 5 teeth up to 0.5 mm long; corolla pink-purple to mauve, standard ± orbicular, briefly auriculate at the base, without gibbosities, but furnished with crests on the auricles, wings and keel 14–17 mm long, of which 4–5 mm for the claws; stamens monadelphous (but vexillary stamen free at the base), anthers rather large; ovary 10 mm long, pubescent; style glabrous, 2 mm, stigma capitate. Pod asymmetric, often elliptic or sometimes oblong, 4–10(–13) × 1.5–2 cm, acuminate at the apex, flattened, with 1 to 5 seeds, semi-woody, the sutures reinforced/thickened, the ventral one strongly and irregularly emarginate between the seeds at maturity. Seed ± 1.5 × 0.8 cm, reddish, shiny; hilum white.

## Distribution

Tropical America, West Africa, from Senegal to Angola, India and Southeast Asia, to New Guinea; planted in Mozambique, Zimbabwe, and probably elsewhere; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

along beaches, on the edge of lagoons or rivers and in coastal forest; at low altitude. Uses: The wood is used for the framing of boats, house construction and as firewood. The bark is laxative, especially for young children. Elsewhere, colouring matters from the leaves and seeds are used to colour mats etc. The fruit would be toxic. Traditional medicine in Africa uses an infusion of the leaves as treatment against gastric, intestinal and hepatic disorders, and to alleviate malaria. Also cultivated as an ornamental plant and for magico-religious uses

## Vernacular names
