## Description

Liana with glabrous and black branchlets, ramuli glabrescent. Internodes 1.5-4 cm long. Leaves with petiole ±5 mm long; blade coriaceous, elliptic-obovate, 4.5-17 cm long, 2-6 cm wide, acute or subacute at the base, apex shortly acuminate; glabrous on both faces, glaucous on the lower face. Midvein prominent on both faces, very prominent on the lower face; lateral nerves 8-14 pairs, obliquely ascending; venation prominent on both faces.

Flowers axillary or subaxillary, solitary, 2-3-fasciculate, or in ± lax racemes of 5-6 flowers, ± fasciculate. Pedicel 1.4-5 cm long, bearing towards the upper half a very small bract. Floral buds conic, ±8 mm long, ±5 mm wide. Sepals broadly triangular-ovate, 2-3 mm in diameter, with ciliate margins, slightly connate at the base. External petals cordate-ovate, 7-8 mm in diameter, glabrous but ciliate on the margins; internal petals ovate-oblong, ±6 mm long, 2.5-3 mm wide, connivant at the apex. Stamens numerous, claviform, ±1.2 mm long, thecae extrorse, connectives developed into a triangle above the anthers. Carpels 30-40, about 2 mm long, ovaries cylindric curved, glabrous, styles linear, stigmas scarcely thickened. Ovules 2, lateral.

Fruits with pedicel 2.5-4 cm long; mericarps stipitate, with 1-2 oblong articles, 2.5-3 cm long, 0.8-1.3 cm wide, glabrous, with ± longitudinally plicate pericarp; stipes about 1 cm long. 1-2 oblong seeds, 2-2.5 cm long, 0.7-1 cm wide.

## Discussion

Sprague and Hutchinson transformed the variety longipedicellata Bak. f. of Cleistopholis albida Engl. et Diels into a species Oxymitra longipedicellata distinguished from O. albida by the length of the pedicels, the external petals more acute, the number of stamens and carpels greater. All the material we examined of these two species is perfectly homogeneous; only the type of F. albida (Unona albida: Zenker 1715, Bipindi, B!) possesses flowers with pedicel and petals shorter, but it must be noted that the flowers of this specimen did not reach maturity.

We refer on the other hand these two species to F. gracilipes (Oxymitra gracilipes) known from a single type specimen from Fernando Po, whose flowers are likewise immature but perfectly similar.
