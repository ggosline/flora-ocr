# *Cyperus kyllingia* Endl. Planche 56

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Cat. horti. Vindob . 1 : 94 (1842)

## Synonyms

- Kyllinga nemoralis (J.R.Forst. & G.Forst.) Hutch., Fl. West trop. Afr . 2 : 487 (1936).
- Thryocephalon nemorale J.R.Forst & G.Forst., Char. gen. pl . : 130, t. 65 (1776), non
- Cyperus nemoralis Cherm. (1936).
- Kyllinga monocepahala Rottb., Descr. icon. rar. pl . 2 : 13, t. 4, f. 4 (1773), non Cyperus

## Description

monocephalus Roxb. (1832).

Vahl (1805). Perennial herb, very leafy, with a branched, thin, flexible rhizome, sheathed by an alternation of tubular prophylls and bracts, both pierced by roots; stems usually scattered, 10‒25 cm × 0.8‒1.5 mm, trigonous, striated, glabrous, without swelling at the base. Leaf: sheath brownish, the basal ones without blade; blade 10‒35 cm × 2‒5 mm, generally exceeding the stems, dark green, flaccid, plane, scabrous on the margin and central nerve, often with hairs on the secondary nerves on the upper face. Inflorescence in a globose or slightly ovoid head, formed of a single spike (or rarely in a more irregular head with 1‒2 additional smaller spikes) 3‒8 × 3‒8 mm; involucral bracts 3‒5, foliaceous, the principal one 8‒20 cm long. Spikelet 2.5‒3.5 × 0.8‒1 mm, with 1(‒2) flowers; glumes 2‒3.5 mm long, of different widths (one with a broader and more dentate keel than the other), whitish, but becoming pale reddish-brown, acuminate, with 2‒4 lateral nerves, median nerve with a winged spiny keel, but sometimes only on certain parts (on certain plants, the wing itself is poorly developed). Flower with 3 stamens; style with 2 branches. Achene oblong, 1‒1.3 × 0.7‒0.9 mm, blackish, finely papillose.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical Africa and Asia and in the Pacific; scattered in Gabon, found in Ogooué-Maritime, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem (4 collections)

## Ecology

on partially shaded soil in forests, often along forest paths, also as a weed of disturbed ground in very rainy regions; in Gabon, at 0‒800 m altitude, up to 1200 m elsewhere

## Vernacular names

white kyllinga, whitehead spikesedge (angl.)
