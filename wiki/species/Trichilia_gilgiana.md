# *Trichilia gilgiana* Harms Planche 17

**Genus**: [[Trichilia]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 161 (1896)

## Description

Medium tree 5–30 m tall; trunk cylindrical, up to 95 cm in diameter; cross-section pale pink, with strong cedar-like odor, exuding some creamy-colored latex. Leaf: petiole (4.5–)6–11 cm; rachis (5–)10–20(–26) cm; petiolules (2–)4–8(–9) mm, those of terminal leaflets (6–)10–20(–25) mm; leaflets (5–)11–17, opposite or subopposite, narrowly obovate to rectangular or elliptic, (5.5–)9–21(–28) × (1.5–)2.5–5.5(–7.5) cm, cuneate or obtuse at base, acuminate at apex, riddled with translucent dots and dashes. Inflorescence axillary or supra-axillary, (5–)10–20(–30) cm long, with very fragrant flowers; bracts often persistent. Functionally male flower: pedicel (3–)4–5(–8) mm; receptacle 1–3 mm long; calyx 2–3 × 4.5–6 mm, puberulent outside; petals narrowly elliptic to obovate, (7–)8–10(–11.5) × 2–4.5(–5) mm, cream and puberulent outside, pink and glabrous inside; staminal crown 4.5–6.5 mm long, including anthers, pink, with filaments densely bearded inside in their free part, the rest glabrous, anthers narrowly rectangular, 1.2–2 × 0.5–0.8 mm; pistillode weakly developed, 5–6 mm long, puberulent to glabrous. Functionally female flower similar, but with antherodia; ovary well developed, 3-locular, ovoid, depressed, 2 × 2.2–3 mm, pubescent; style 2.5–3.5 mm, somewhat hairy, stigma cylindric. Fruit pyriform or obovoid, approximately 3 cm in diameter, stipitate. Seed approximately 20 × 12 mm, with shiny brown testa, the ⅔ modified into an orange-red sarcotesta.

## Distribution

Southern Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda) and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, observed in all provinces

## Ecology

Dense humid or semi-deciduous forest, primary or secondary; in Gabon, up to 750 m altitude, elsewhere up to 950 m

## Uses

The wood is suitable for light construction, interior woodwork, cooperage, furniture, etc. Bibliography: Lemmens (2008b), Pellegrin (1940), Staner & Gilbert (1958), de Wilde (1968)
