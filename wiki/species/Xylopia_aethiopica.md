Tree 15-30 (45) m in height and 60-75 cm in diameter, with a straight bole with a few buttresses at the base, 50 cm-1 m high. Rhytidome whitish grey, marbled with brown; bark orange-brown; wood soft, yellowish white. Primary branches straight, spreading and radiating from the base of the bole, then curved upward; foliage drooping, forming a conical crown slightly rounded at the apex. Branchlets brown, with lenticels, glabrous; twigs puberulent to glabrous. Leaves with petiole 2-6 mm long, puberulent to glabrous; blade coriaceous, elliptic-oblong to elliptic, 7-20 cm long, 3-9 cm wide, obtuse or ± cuneate at the base, ± longly acuminate at the apex; upper surface glabrous, lower surface appressed-pubescent to glabrescent. Midvein prominent below, enlarged toward the base; lateral veins 7-8 pairs, very oblique ascending, scarcely visible; fine reticulation prominent on both surfaces.

Flowers whitish-green or cream-white, solitary, geminate or fasciculate 2-6, axillary or subaxillary; pedicel thick, 0.5-1 cm long, appressed-pubescent; two reduced bracteoles. Floral bud linear. Sepals greenish, broadly triangular-ovate, acute, 2.5-3 mm long, 5-6 mm wide, fused at the base, pubescent outside, glabrous inside. External petals thick, narrowly linear, 4-5 cm long, 3-5 mm wide, ± enlarged and concave at base, attenuate at apex, tomentose outside, tomentellous inside; internal petals subequal or somewhat shorter, narrower, enlarged and concave at base, garnet-red, tomentellous on both surfaces except at the base. Stamens inserted on a convex extension of the receptacle, linear, 1.5 mm long, filaments short, thecae linear, connectives dilated above the anthers and puberulent. An outer cycle of staminodes wider than the stamens, ciliate on the margins, an inner cycle of staminodes narrower, cuneiform. Carpels 30-32, approximately 5 mm long, ovaries cylindric, pubescent, enclosed in the receptacle, styles linear, ± 3 mm long, glabrous. Ovules 6-8 uniseriate.

Fruits with thick pedicel, 1-1.5 cm long; mericarps numerous (approximately 30), green, smooth, bright red inside, subsessile, narrowly cylindric, 5-6 cm long, 0.4-0.8 cm wide, slightly constricted between the seeds, dehiscent on the external surface, glabrous. Seeds vertical, 4-9, ellipsoid-oblong, 5-6 mm long, 2-3 mm wide, with shiny brown integument; aril whitish-orange, bilobed, 2-3 mm long.

Types from Sierra Leone (holotype in herb. Dunal, MPU?; isotype in herb. DC., G!).

A tree or shrub well known to Africans who use the strongly peppery seeds as spices or condiments, hence its most common name 'Guinea pepper'. It is still used as firewood, in carpentry, for the construction of huts, and in indigenous pharmacopoeia as a stimulant, against rheumatism or as an emetic.

Very widely distributed in Africa, from Senegal to Sudan, and southward to Angola, Northern Rhodesia, Mozambique.

It is found in dense humid forest, especially near the coast, on the margins of streams, in forest galleries. Particularly abundant in savanna woodlots where it appears as one of the pioneer forest species in the reconquest of the savanna.

Vernacular names: Ogana (Gallois), Onkalla, Akola, Bikué (Pahouin), Nkana (Bavili), Moughana (Bayaka), Okala (Fang).
