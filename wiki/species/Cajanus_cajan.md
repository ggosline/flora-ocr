# *Cajanus cajan* (L.) Millsp. Planche 20

**Genus**: [[Cajanus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Publ. Field Columb. Mus., Bot. Ser. 2(1) : 53 (1900)

## Description

Publ. Field Columb. Mus., Bot. Ser. 2(1) : 53 (1900). C. bicolor DC., Cat. pl. horti monsp. : 85 (1813). C. flavus DC., Cat. pl. horti monsp . : 86 (1813). Herb with woody base or erect shrub 0.5–4 m tall; indumentum variable in density. Stipules triangular, 2–6 mm long. Leaf: petiole 1–8 cm; rachis 0.5–3 cm; stipels setaceous, 1–4 mm long; leaflets with petiolule 1–4 mm, blade elliptic to lanceolate, 4–12 × 1.3–4.5 cm, base cuneate, apex acute to acuminate, mucronate, slightly coriaceous, green above, greenish-grey to ± silvery below, the lateral ones somewhat smaller and oblique. Inflorescence many-flowered; peduncle 1–8 cm; bracts triangular, 1–4 mm long. Flower: pedicel 7–15 mm; calyx campanulate, pubescent, tube 4–5 mm long, teeth 3–5(–7) mm long, acuminate; corolla yellow or sometimes red, orange, purple, or striped red on the back, standard obovate-orbicular, 14–22 × 14–20 mm, with 2 symmetric and callose auricles, wings obovate, 15–20 × 6–7 mm, keel oblique, 14–17 × 5–7 mm, ventral sutures adnate; stamens 15–18 mm, anthers dorsifixed; ovary 5–8 mm long, pubescent. Pod oblong, straight or sigmoid, laterally compressed, 2–13 × 0.4–1.7 cm, pale brown at maturity, often spotted with purple, pubescent, with 2 to 9 seeds. Seed globose, 4–9 mm long, white to brown or black; hilum central, occupying ± half the width of the seed, strophiole vestigial.

## Distribution

Native of India, arrived in Africa probably around 2000 B.C., distributed since their discovery in 1492, to the West Indies and other countries of warm regions; in Gabon, little cultivated (Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Maritime) and rarely naturalized

## Ecology

Agricultural situations in humid seasons followed by the dry season, in (sub)tropical climate, on soils preferably friable and well-drained; in Gabon, at low altitude, elsewhere cultivated up to 2400 m. Use: A seed vegetable of great importance in Asia and West Africa. It is eaten cooked in the fresh state or after drying. The plant is used in traditional medicine. The leaves are consumed by livestock. The stems are used as firewood. Cultivated mainly as an annual (Asia); it is the fifth seed vegetable used in the world

## Vernacular names
