# *Treculia obovoidea* N.E. Brown

**Genus**: [[Treculia]]
**Family**: [[Moraceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull.: 361 (1894); HAUMAN, FCB 1: 92 (1948); BERG, BJBB 47: 399 (1977)

## Description

- T. staudtii ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., Nachtr. 1:120 (1897), nomen ; Mon. Afr. Pfl. 1, Moraceae : 33 (1898), descr. TYPE : Staudt 633, Cameroon (holo-, B ; iso-, COI, C).

- T. staudtii ENGL. var. angustifolia ENGL., l.c. : 34 (1898). TYPE : Staudt 270, Cameroon (holo-, B ; iso-, COI, E, G, K, P, S).

– Acanthotreculia winkleri ENGL., Bot. Jahrb. 40: 548 (1908). TYPE: Zenker 2295, Cameroon (lecto-, B; iso-, BR, COI, K, L, P).

- Treculia brieyi DE WILD., Rep. Sp. Nov. 13: 374 (1914), published by error as Trichilia brieyi. TYPE : De Briey 133, Zaire (holo-, BR).

Dioecious trees reaching 20 m in height ; latex white becoming brown or reddish. Small leafy twigs thick 1.5–5 mm, (rather) sparsely puberulent with appressed hairs. Limb oblong to lanceolate, broader in the middle or near it, ± slightly asymmetrical, (3–) 6–25 × (1–) 2–9 cm, chartaceous to subcoriaceous, acuminate to caudate ; base acute to obtuse ; margin (sub)entire ; upper surface glabrous ; lower surface sparsely puberulent with appressed hairs on the midvein and secondary nerves, becoming glabrous ; nerves plane to slightly prominent above, ± prominent below ; 7–16 pairs of secondary nerves, without parallel tertiary nerves. Petiole 3–9 mm long. Stipules caducous, 4–10 mm long, sparsely puberulent with appressed hairs and shortly ciliate.

Male inflorescences generally solitary, sometimes grouped by 2, rarely more, in the axil of a leaf or behind the leaves, obovoid often almost oblong or ellipsoid, sometimes almost globose, 1–2.5 cm long and 0.8–1.5 cm in diameter, subsessile to pedunculate. Peduncle reaching 7 mm in length, bearing (10–12) oval bracts, 2–3 mm long, puberulent. Flowers numerous, sessile or subsessile ; perianth 2.5–3 mm high, 3 (–4)-lobed to 3 (–4)-fid, membranous, glabrous except on the margin ; stamens 3–4, generally unequal with filament most often 3–4 mm long and anthers ca. 0.3–0.4 × 0.3–0.4 mm, latrose (to introse), with narrow connective ; pistillode subulate, 0.1–0.5 mm long, or almost lacking. Interflorals bracts numerous reaching 0.3 mm in length ; primary bracts ± cushion-like and ± thickened puberulent, with or without acute apex, secondary bracts surrounding the cavities openings, spiny and puberulent only at the base.

Female inflorescences solitary, sometimes grouped by 2 behind the leaves, often on old branches or on the stem, (sub)globose, ca. 10 mm in diameter. Peduncle reaching 5 mm in length, bearing about 12 oval bracts, 2–3 mm long, puberulent ; openings letting pass the styles surrounded by hairs. Flowers numerous ; ovary ca. 1.5 mm long ; style ca. 2 mm long ; stigmas filiform, 6–9 mm long. Primary interflorals bracts cushion-like or subclaviform, at first with or without acute apex, reaching 2 mm in length, puberulent, passing gradually to spiny secondary bracts, almost glabrous, reaching 6 mm in length, surrounding the openings allowing passage of the styles.

Infructescences pale yellow, globose to ellipsoid, 5–6.5 cm in diameter, covered with hardened spiny bracts reaching about 2 cm in length ; primary bracts generally shorter than secondary bracts and sometimes (sub)claviform. Fruit ellipsoid to oblong, 15–20 cm long. - Pl. 5, p. 21.

## Type

Mann 2303, Nigeria, riv. (old) Calabar (lecto.-, K)

## Discussion

T. obovoidea extends from SE Nigeria to Angola-Cabinda and neighboring regions of Congo and Zaire.

It is locally common in the lower strata of humid primary forests and near watercourses.
