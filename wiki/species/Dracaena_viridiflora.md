# *Dracaena viridiflora* Engl. & K.Krause Planche 16(5–9)

**Genus**: [[Dracaena]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 153 (1910)

## Description

Pleomele viridiflora (Engl. & K.Krause) N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1914 : 279 (1914). D. letestui Pellegr., Bull. Mus. Natl. Hist. Nat., sér. 2, 2 : 571 (1930). Shrub or small tree reaching 8 m in height; trunk up to 15 cm in diameter, sparsely branched; bark grey, granular on older specimens, milky white when cut, without resin. Leaves spirally arranged, uniformly distributed and subdistichous along horizontal stems, leaving the stem visible between the sheaths or cataphylls; sheath clasping the stem on clearly more than the circumference, over a length of 1–1.8 cm, pale brown; pseudopetiole absent; blade extremely variable, linear to elliptic-obovate, 15–28 × 1–3 cm, base narrowed into a sheath, apex long-acuminate with a mucro reaching 6 mm, thin, coriaceous, upper surface dark green uniform, lower surface paler. Inflorescence erect or pendulous on horizontal branches, in a thyrsoid or subcapitate form, up to 12 cm in length, smooth, basal bracts resembling reduced leaves; fascicles well spaced or dense, each generally composed of far more than 6 flowers; bracts subtending the glomérules caducous, concave, up to 18 mm in length, decreasing in size toward the apex, scarious; floral bracts enveloping the pedicel, up to 9 mm in length, scarious, purplish brown. Flowers very fragrant; pedicel up to 8 mm in length, articulated up to 7 mm from the base; perianth 22–25 mm in length, greenish white to purplish white, with lobes somewhat shorter than the tube, acuminate; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 3 mm shorter than the lobes, filament filiform, white, anther ± 2 mm in length, pale yellow; ovary oblong, up to 3.5 × 2 mm; style exserted up to 4 mm, white, stigma slightly 3-lobed, ± 0.5 mm in diameter. Fruit subsessile, obnapiform to obloid, slightly 3-lobed, ± 20 × 25 mm, orange to yellow. Seed spherical, up to ± 9 mm in diameter.

## Distribution

Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Rather open and dry forest, also in dwarf forest and along river margins; in Gabon at 0–1000 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–2000 m. Flowering recorded in February, May, August and September. Bibliography: Bos (1984), Damen et al. (2018), Figueiredo & Smith (2008), Hepper (1968), Mwachala (2005), Velayos & Cabezas (2018)
