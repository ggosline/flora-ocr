# *Cleome spinosa* Jacquin

**Genus**: [[Cleome]]
**Family**: [[Capparidaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Cleomaceae]] — *Capparidaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Capparidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. Syst. Pl. Carib.: 26 (1760); L., Sp. Pl., ed. 2: 939 (1763); DC., Prodr. 1: 239 (1824); ILTIS, Amer. J. Bot. 54: 953 (1967); KERS, Bot. Not. 122: 249 (1969); ADAMS, Fl. pl. Jamaica: 304 (1972); KERS, FCAM 29: 42 (1987)

## Description

– C. sandwicensis A. GRAY, Bot. Univ. St. Exped. 1838-42, 1: 65 (1854).

Annual herb reaching 1.5 m in height, of vigorous appearance, armed with stipular spines, glandular-pubescent to puberulent, branched from the base; main stem dominant, sometimes lignified at the base. Stipular spines acute, swollen on each side, at most 4 mm long, straight or somewhat curved, pale shining yellow, distinctly developed in the lower half of the main stem, increasingly reduced toward the apex, weak or fully developed at the level of upper cauline leaves, absent on the racemes. Prickles and acicular hairs (all intermediates exist up to ordinary hairs) present except on the petioles and leaflets.

Leaves 3–5 (–7)-foliolate, long-petiolate, passing fairly rapidly into floral bracts. Leaflets elliptic to lanceolate, long-acuminate or acute, base attenuate or narrowly cuneate;

blade thin, uniformly olive-green or somewhat paler beneath, symmetric or slightly asymmetric in the lateral leaflets. Terminal leaflet, 6–11.5 × 1.5–3 cm for the lower leaves; lateral leaflets, somewhat smaller. Lateral veins 8–14 (terminal leaflet). Midvein and lateral veins prominent beneath, hairy above, impressed and glabrous beneath. Both surfaces of the blade also show very small hairs between the veins. Petiolules 1–5 mm long, glandular-puberulent to pubescent on both surfaces. Petioles 4–10 mm long, channelled on the upper surface, glandular-puberulent, furnished with hairs, some long, others short, on both surfaces.

Inflorescence dense at the apex, width reaching 4–9 cm at flowering; its length reaches 50 cm at fruiting; primary raceme taller than the lateral racemes. Floral bracts simple, ordinarily about as long as wide, suborbicular, cordate or ovate, sometimes broadly lanceolate at the level of the upper flowers, short-petiolate to subsessile, about 1 cm long at the level of the basal flowers, progressively shorter toward the apex, internal, persistent. Pedicels 2.5–3.5 mm long at fruiting, about half that of the gynophore, vaguely puberulent on both surfaces. Sepals 4, narrowly lanceolate to subulate, (4.5–) 6–7 × 0.5–1 mm just before anthesis, yellowish-green, slightly unequal, united at the base, dorsally furnished with very small glandular-puberulent hairs, strongly reflexed from the base at anthesis, caducous. Petals 4, white or pale pink, subequal, (1.1–) 1.4–2 × 0.4–0.5 cm, ascending, oblong to obovate in their upper half, rounded and mucronulate at the apex, narrowed from the middle into a basal claw much longer than the sepals at anthesis; their blade glabrous sometimes bears a few sessile glands on the dorsal surface; the petals are caducous. Androgynophore broadly conical with a fleshy base at the time of anthesis, 2–3 mm long at fruiting, subcylindric, longitudinally channelled by the decurrence of the stamen bases; the scars of the 2 dorsal stamens are situated slightly higher than the others. Stamens 6, all fertile, inclined, long-exserted, very fragile; filaments 4–5 cm, purple or pink in the fresh state; anthers linear, very slender, 7–10 mm long before dehiscence, basidorsifixed, tilting rapidly. Gynophore slender, becoming at fruiting, glabrous, 4.5–6.5 cm long much exceeding the pedicel and oriented in the same direction as the fruit. Ovary linear, glabrous: stigma subsessile, swollen and capitate.

Capsule linear, slender, cylindric, ordinarily attenuate at both extremities, 75–95 (–120) × (1.5–) 2–3 (–4) mm, green, deployed horizontally and nodding at maturity; replum glabrous. Valves papyraceous, glabrous, with approximately 30–40 fine veins richly anastomosed; stigma sessile or nearly so, rather prominent, reaching 1 mm in

width, truncate. Seeds suborbicular-reniform viewed from the side, swollen-spherical, 1.5–1.75 (–2) mm wide, hilar slit short, narrow and open, without elaiosome; testa dull, yellowish to greyish-brown, ornamented with numerous slightly marked longitudinal striae and a few fine hyaline and brownish ridges. Pl. 3, p. 31.

## Discussion

Cleome spinosa is distinguished from C. afrospina by its fewer lateral veins, a more slender capsule, various seed characters and rounded floral bracts. This species is much more weakly armed than C. afrospina; its flowers are relatively small, notably in comparison with those of the closely related species C. hassleriana. Unlike this species, C. spinosa is not cultivated as an ornamental plant. The name Cleome spinosa had previously been applied to various spiny species: the "Cleome spinosa" of the early American Floras is actually C. afrospina, whereas the correct name for the ornamental plants cultivated as "C. spinosa" and spread from gardens is C. hassleriana Chod.

C. spinosa is found in or near encampments, in ruderal stations, along tracks or on the banks of watercourses. In Africa it is mainly present in coastal regions; it appears to be rare in Gabon. This is a species native to Central America, northern South America and the Antilles, which has naturalized in tropical West Africa. Its presence is well established in Cameroon and Gabon. Unlike that of C. afrospina, its introduction is certainly recent; it probably took place from ports and mooring installations after transoceanic transport by ships. The oldest known collection from Africa dates from 1961 at Edéa in Cameroon. Specimens from Gabon were collected in 1966 and 1968.

Uses: its cultivation has been noted in a few African gardens, both in Gabon (Hallé 3985) and in Cameroon. Its use is unknown. It may be supposed that the young fruits and leaves are consumed, as is the case with another species in tropical West Africa, C. rutidosperma.
