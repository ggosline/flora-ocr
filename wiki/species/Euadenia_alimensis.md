Small shrub reaching 2 m, sparingly branched, deciduous or evergreen (in Gabon). Young branches pale green or brownish-red, vaguely longitudinally channelled. Older branches cylindrical, dull cinnamon-brown, longitudinally fissuring with age, wood whitish, pith narrow; lenticels sparse, elongate, grey; axillary buds solitary often situated 2-4 mm above the axil, usually remaining dormant. Leaves 3-foliolate and long-petiolate, sometimes simple and shortly petiolate, distributed almost regularly along each branch, persistent on some fairly aged branches. Leaflets dull olive, paler beneath, sometimes tinged reddish, papyraceous, generally larger and more slender than those of E. trifoliolata; the median and lateral leaflets are roughly equally stipitate. Midrib prominent beneath, depressed above, pale yellowish-green; lateral veins 5-6 (-9), slightly incurved and ending in an anastomotic loop near the margin; venules loosely reticulate, visible beneath, scarcely evident above. Median leaflet symmetric, reaching 21 × 10 cm on lower leaves, obovate to lanceolate, acute to shortly cuspidate, base attenuate and narrowed into a petiolule; lateral leaflets somewhat smaller, asymmetric, outer half lanceolate or ovate, inner half lanceolate or ovate; lamina at base acute rather abruptly narrowed into a petiolule, apex often acuminate and shortly caudate. Petiolules equal or nearly so, 2-7 mm long, not articulate at the base. Petioles slender reaching a length of 24 cm on lower leaves, progressively shorter toward the branch apex, 1.5-4 cm long near the inflorescence, generally equal to the median leaflet, rigid, slightly angular in section, vaguely channelled above, shiny yellowish-green and often reddish toward the base which is broad and usually compressed laterally, weakly articulate, not decurrent, leaving a slightly depressed grey scar; simple leaves shortly petiolate or subsessile. Stipules scarcely visible, at most 0.5 mm long, subulate membranous, early withering.

Inflorescence in a lax raceme, 10-40-flowered, 10-20 cm long at full anthesis, width reaching 15 cm at the base, in terminal position on the main branches or subterminally placed shoots, or short floriferous shoots arising from subterminally dormant buds on older branches. Inflorescence axis slightly angular, rigidly erect, glabrous; apex sometimes sterile, bearing rudimentary leaves, but growth limited. Floral bracts simple; the lower ones narrowly lanceolate, maximum length 1.5 cm; the upper ones narrowly lanceolate, maximum length 1.3 cm, very shortly stipitate, falling mostly well before anthesis; stipules free, persistent, greatly reduced. Pedicels slender about 4 cm long at anthesis, slightly angular, becoming woody during and 3-5 mm wide at fruiting. Sepals 4, pale green, membranous, tardily caducous, midvein visible, lateral veins often 2 and faintly marked; upper sepal 1.2-1.5 × 0.2 (-0.4) cm; lower sepal 0.8-1.0 × 0.15 (-0.3) cm; lateral sepals equal of intermediate size. Petals 4, pale yellowish-green to sulphur-yellow, often yellow when dry; upper petal lanceolate, reaching 6.0-6.8 × 0.9-1.5 cm, acute, without mucro, penninerved; veins distinct often reddish; lamina narrowed from the lower 1/4 into a linear claw; lower petal reduced and narrow, 1.2-3.3 × 0.1-0.25 cm narrowly lanceolate or elliptic, acuminate, narrowed into a short linear claw. Fertile stamens 4, subequal, inclined; filaments 1.7-1.9 cm long, somewhat wider than the androgynophore, whitish, base enlarged; anthers oblong 4 mm, basifixed, early caducous; staminodial appendage linear, formed of (3-) 4-6 elements, entire or bifurcate near the apex, flat or channelled, 0.9-1.2 cm × 0.5 mm, caducous; anther rudiments (3-) 4-6, ovate-oblong 0.5 mm subsessile, early caducous. Androgynophore slender, 1.0-1.5 mm long at anthesis, becoming woody and 2 mm wide at fruiting. Ovary narrowly cylindric, 4-5 × 0.5-0.75 mm, glabrous, acute or attenuate at base, obtuse at apex, unilocular sometimes bilocular with age; stigma sessile, vaguely bilobed, truncate, very small. Ovules numerous on 2 parietal placentas.

Fruit long and slender, linear, pendulous, reaching 40 × 1 cm, attenuate at both ends; fruit walls densely marked with grey striae, 1-2 mm thick, the outer part greyish, the inner part white and soft, forming a false pulp around the very numerous uniseriate seeds. Seeds subspherical to elongate-rounded in side view, somewhat compressed laterally, often dilated in section, 9-11 mm long, 6-9 mm high; hilum fissure short and narrow without elaiosome; testa dark cinnamon-brown, thick and coriaceous, smooth or pelliculate; cotyledons very developed and fleshy, apex rounded and thick, that of the radicle short and pointed. ## Discussion

The size of the upper petals is variable in this species. When fully developed, they exceed in length those of E. trifoliolata and are distinctly wider than those of related species. The length of the staminodial appendage is also variable. This apparent variation seems to result from the geographic isolation of relatively small populations.

There are only very few collections from Gabon from the vicinity of Libreville and Upper Ngounié. The material of KLAINE dates from 1898-1916 while that of WALKER dates from 1930: there are no modern collections. It is unlikely that this species could have escaped the attention of botanists given its singular and rather attractive flowers. It must therefore be quite rare in Gabon. Euadenia alimensis is a representative of the understorey of rainforest; it is found in clearings and on the banks of watercourses. It has sometimes been found in plantations (Cameroon). It is not known if this type of habitat is common in Gabon. Flowering was recorded from December to April; fruits were collected in July. This species is distributed somewhat further south compared to E. trifoliolata. It has been noted in Zaire, Congo, Uganda as well as in Gabon and Cameroon.
