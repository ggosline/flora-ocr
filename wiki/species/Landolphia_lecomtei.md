# *Landolphia lecomtei* Dewèvre Planche 56

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Soc. Sci. Brux. , sér. B, 19(2) : 117 (1895)

## Description

Ann. Soc. Sci. Brux., sér. B, 19(2): 117 (1895). Large liana; branches with pale lenticels, bearing often brown hairs. Leaf: petiole 0.5–1.2 cm, glabrous or pubescent, obovate to elliptic obovate, 0.5–0.7 times as long as wide, 0.5–12 × 0.3–7 cm, generally rounded or obtuse at base, generally acuminate at apex, pilose or glabrous; secondary veins 6 to 15 pairs, tertiary venation reticulate, visible beneath. Inflorescence terminal, 2.8–4.3 cm long, sometimes in scorpioid cyme and longer, with 1–8 flowers, pedicel reaching 4–8 mm. Flower with sweet fragrance; pedicel reaching 4–8 mm, bracteoles glabrescent, usually more distally; sepals of calyx globose, 0.8–1 times as long as wide, 1.5–2.5 mm long, tomentose to pubescent on the exterior; corolla white, tube often yellowish, pale orange or green, with tube 11–21 mm long, 1.3–3 mm wide at the level of the anthers and 1.1–2.7 mm at the throat, exterior glabrous at base and puberulent higher up, interior pilose around the anthers, with lobes ovate to narrowly ovate-elliptic, 0.6–1.4 times the length of the tube, 0.9–1.2 times as long as wide, 9–27 × 2.8–8 mm, sometimes partially pilose, often with long hairs at apex; stamens inserted at 0.57–0.74 of the tube length, included, anther 1.6–2.2 mm long; pistil 8–14 mm long, ovary villous, hairiness more sparse and shorter towards base; style 5.2–11 mm, with hairiness forming a cylinder around the lower half, then sparsely hairy higher up and finally glabrous. Fruit subglobose to ellipsoid, 2–6 × 1.5–4 cm, yellow, often with large darker stains and or numerous small paler stains, often with small lenticels, with numerous seeds; wall 2–9 mm thick; layer of sclerified cells 0.3–0.8 mm thick. Distribution Gabon southeast of the Republic of the Congo, west of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola. Cabinda in Gabon. Moyen-Ogooué or Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology Primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 600 m altitude. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
