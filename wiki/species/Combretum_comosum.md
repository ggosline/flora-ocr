# *Combretum comosum* G. Don

**Genus**: [[Combretum]]
**Family**: [[Combretaceae]]
**Protologue**: Edinburgh Philosoph. Journ. 11 : 344 (1824) ; ENGLER & DIELS, Mon. Afr., Pfl., 3 : 91 (1899) ; KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1(1) : 273 (1954). TYPE : Sierra Leone (fl., fr.) Don 24 (holo-BM ; iso-BR, K, MO)

## Description

KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1(1): 273 (1954). TYPE: Sierra Leone (fl., fr.) Don 24 (holo-BM; iso-BR, K, MO).

– C. hispidum Lawson, FTA 2: 421 (1871); Engler & Diels, Mon. Afr., Pfl., 3: 91 (1899); Keay, FWTA, ed. 2, 1(1): 274 (1954); Liben, FZ: 33 (1968); Exell & Garcia, CFA: 78 (1970); Liben, Fl. Cam. 25: 33 (1983). Type: Gabon, Rio Muni (fl.) Mann 1760 (holo-K; iso-P) – syn. nov.

– C. rhodanthum Engler & Diels, Mon. Afr., Pfl., 3: 92 (1899); Keay, FWTA, ed. 2, 1(1): 274 (1954); Liben, FZ: 32 (1968); Wickens, FTEA: 59 (1973); Liben, Fl. Cameroun 25: 32 (1983). Type: Sierra Leone, near Mafari (fl., fr.) Scott Elliot 4438 (non 4638!) (holo-B delet.; Lectotype designated here: BM) - syn. nov.

Shrub with lianescent habit or large liana.

Leaves 2 or 3-whorled; petiole ≤ 12 mm, its lower portion often transformed into a recurved and lignified spine, ≤ 2 cm long; limb elliptic ≤ 20 × ≤ 8 cm, base rounded to cordate, apex acuminate and mucronate; large lateral veins 7–10 pairs, depressed above as well as the midvein; domatia pubescent.

Scales absent.

Inflorescences either in axillary or terminal racemes, or in terminal panicles ≤ 16 cm; bracts pale green to pink, elliptic, ≤ 17 × ≤ 4 mm, most often inserted on the pedicel.

Flowers 5-merous, stipitate, fragrant. Receptacle, from green to reddish: the lower part 5 mm tall, the upper part campanulate-infundibuliform, 5–10 mm tall. Calyx: lobes triangular. Petals red, glabrous, 7–10 × 2 mm. Stamens exserted 10–14 mm; filaments red; anthers yellow, 0.6 mm long. Style red, exserted 15 mm.

Fruit, subcircular in profile, diameter ≤ 2.5 cm, with membranous, puberulent wings, pale reddish; stipe ≤ 10 mm long.

Study of dozens of collections from West Africa, both of C. comosum s.s. and of C. rhodanthum and C. hispidum, shows that the distinction between these 3 species is artificial because the differences in colour and indumentum have no taxonomic significance here.

## Distribution

from Guinea to Uganda and Angola

## Ecology

dense humid forests or gallery forests, altitude 0–1300 m
