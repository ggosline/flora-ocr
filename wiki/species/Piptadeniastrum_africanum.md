A large tree reaching a height of 45 m and a girth of 3.60 m. Trunk slender, tall, furnished at its base with aliform buttresses extending up to 3 m or more, often branched. Bark brownish, smooth, finely fissured on old trees; inner surface cream-coloured becoming reddish towards the interior. Crown flat and broad with fine foliage. Young branchlets pubescent tomentellous to glabrous. Older branchlets glabrous dark brown. Stipule caducous, densely pubescent, linear, 5.5–9 mm long; apex acute. Petiole pubescent tomentellous, 0.7–3 cm long, flat at the base, canaliculate towards the apex. Rachis pubescent tomentellous tawny, 2.5–11 cm long (reaching up to 22 cm in young leaves), canaliculate above; prominent keel below. Pinnae 10–12 pairs alternate to rarely opposite. Pinnule axis pubescent with longer hairs on the margins of the upper face at the level of insertion of the leaflets, 1–4.5 cm long; semicircular in section, upper face plane. Leaflets 26–60 pairs, sessile. Blade glabrous on both faces; black above and brown below when dry; rhombic asymmetric linear ± falciform, 1.5–7.5 mm long; base asymmetric: proximal margin auriculate and distal margin linear to weakly rounded; margin ciliate; apex obtuse. Primary nerve in the distal half of the blade, straight, invisible above and very weakly prominent to concave when dry below. Secondary nerves invisible above and almost indistinct below. Venules not visible.

Inflorescence in a dense terminal panicle. Axis densely pubescent tomentellous. Bracteole pubescent tomentellous externally, lanceolate, 0.50–1 mm, apex acute, early caducous. Pedicel pubescent to puberulent tawny, ± 0.75 mm long, with apical abscission zone. Floral bud glabrous, ellipsoid fusiform, narrowed at the apex. Flowers ♢, pentamerous, yellow, yellowish or white in the living state. Calyx cupuliform, glabrous on both faces, 0.75 mm long; lobes free broadly triangular, 0.25 mm long; apex obtuse sometimes very shortly ciliate. Petals white to yellow when fresh; glabrous; fused to the stamens; 2.5–2.75 mm long, apex acute. Stamens 10, red when fresh; fused to each other by the filament and to the disk, for 0.75 mm; free filament slender 3–4 mm long; anther ellipsoid oblong, 0.25 mm long, connective terminated by a globulose sessile gland early caducous. Intrastaminal fleshy disk present; fused in a pad to the stamen filaments. Pistil glabrous, red in the living state; with a stipe 2.75–3.50 mm long; ovary 1.50–2 mm long with 9 ovules; style slender 2.75–3 mm long, terminated by a weakly enlarged and hollow stigma.

Pod dehiscent by a single slit, chartaceous subwoody, flattened, straight or incurved, linear, 12.5–36 × 1.6–3.2 cm; base attenuate to a stipe 0.5–0.8 cm long; apex ± obtuse apiculate; transverse venation prominent slightly to moderately abundant. Seed winged with membranous wing, shiny brown when dry, oblong, 3–9.5 × 1.3–2.5 cm; elongate along the axis of the fruit; funicule linear, inserted on a long face.

Seedling with hypocotyl reaching 3 cm, ribbed and pubescent to tomentose. Cotyledons epigeal, weakly fleshy, transversely elliptic, with an emarginate base. Epicotyl pubescent reddish-brown grooved. First leaf alternate pinnately compound with a petiole 3–4 mm long, pubescent rachis 3–5 cm long, with 30–50 pairs subsequently in pseudoverticil of pinnately compound leaves as before. Roots with moderately abundant nodosities. – Pl. 14, p. 59.

## Discussion

The range of this species extends from Senegal (Casamance) to Sudan and Uganda to the east and Angola to the south. It occurs in forest groupings. Flowering and fruiting take place during the rainy seasons.

1. BRENAN, in FTEA, l.c., reports flowers with shorter styles.

## Uses

The wood, yellowish brown, quite soft, is used in joinery. A decoction of the bark is reported to have abortive properties; it is also used as a gargle against toothache.
