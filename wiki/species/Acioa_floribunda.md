# *Acioa floribunda* (Welwitsch) Exell

**Genus**: [[Acioa]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. of Bot. 66, Suppl. Polypet. : 161 (1928); HAUM., FCB 3 : 51 (1952); MENDES, Consp. Fl. Angol. 4 : 20 (1970)

## Synonyms

- Dactyladenia floribunda WELW., Apont. Fl. Prov. Angola, Ann. Conselho Ultram. : 572 (1859).
- Griffonia bellayana OLIV., FTA 2 : 371 (1871), p.p., quoad specim. angol.

## Description

— Acioa ícondere var. welwitschii De Wild., BJBB 7 : 195 (1920).

— A. bellayana auct. non BAILL. : HIERN, Cat. Pl. Welw. 1 : 321 (1896); CAVACO, Bull. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat. Paris 26 : 638 (1954).

— A. bellayana var. pilosa ENGL. ms. in sched., BR, fide DE WILD., l.c. : 196 (1920).

Shrub attaining 20 m in height and 20 cm in diameter, with small buttresses at the base and smooth, pale greyish-brown, lenticellate bark; sometimes sarmentose-lianescent and reaching 10 m in height. Young branches covered with dense velvety golden-yellow tomentum, persistent. Stipules linear (1 × 5 mm) to lanceolate (1.5 × 6–7 mm, fide HAUMAN, l.c.: 51), densely covered with appressed hairs. Petiole 5–8 mm, likewise velvety tomentose. Lamina elliptic, (4.5)–6–9 × 11–15(–18) cm, sometimes oblong (5 × 13 cm), sometimes slightly obovate; base broadly cuneate to rounded; apex obtuse to rounded, with small acumen 3–5 mm; midrib tomentellose above, tomentose below; lateral veins 7–10 pairs covered with sparse yellow pubescence as well as veinlets and lamina underside; sometimes 2 circular glands difficult to see towards the base of the lamina below.

Racemes solitary terminal and axillary, 2–6 cm long, bearing numerous very crowded flowers; peduncle very short (0–1 cm) covered with trilaciniate bracts, bearing appressed hairs; axis sparsely pubescent but scarcely visible as it is hidden by the bracts; these lanceolate, 4–5 mm, also bearing appressed hairs, especially towards the apex; portion of the pedicels below the articulation 6–9(–12) mm, bearing towards the apex 2 subopposite bracteoles, the terminal articulated portion of the pedicel ending at the level of the apex of the upper bracteole; bracteoles palmatilobate-palmatisect, approximately 2 mm, 5 elongate triangular lobes with orange terminal gland. Pedicel of the disarticulated flower 3 mm. Receptacle 12–18 mm, tubulous and distinctly gibbous at the base, sometimes bearing a few scattered hairs; floral bud apiculate; sepals 6–7(–8) mm, likewise very sparsely pubescent on the parts exposed to the exterior in the floral bud; petals broadly obovate with obtuse to subcordate base, 15 × 6 mm, white; androecium (30–35–45(–60) mm, (15–20–25(–30) filaments.

According to the specimen Gossweiler 719 from Angola: Infructescences bearing persistent bracts, pedicels and bracteoles; fertile peduncle properly so-called 25–30 mm. Drupe elongately ellipsoid, 20–25 × 40 mm (in sicco), terminated in sicco by a beak 10 mm, with yellowish dark tomentellate surface, bearing clear yellow hispid hairs. — PL. 8, p. 31.

## Discussion

Acioa floribunda (Welw.) Exell appears very close to A. icondere Baill. known from Cameroon, Gabon and Zaïre (Mayumbe). All the Cameroonian, Gabonese and Angolan specimens studied and referred to A. floribunda (Welw.) Exell have leaves broadly cuneate to rounded at the base (and not very distinctly cordate), 7–10 pairs of lateral veins (and not 10 to 16), very contracted inflorescences (and not lax ones), flowers generally smaller. As mentioned concerning A. icondere Baill., there could be reason to reexamine in the future, after collection of new material, the relationship between these two taxa.

Acioa floribunda (Welw.) Exell is known from somewhat hilly regions of Cameroon, Gabon, Cabinda, Zaïre (Mayumbe), and even mountainous and misty regions of Angola (Cuanza); it would thus also be ecologically differentiated from Acioa icondere Baill. which lives essentially in scrubland near coastal beaches.
