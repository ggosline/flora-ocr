# *Chytranthus atroviolaceus* Baker fil. ex Hutchinson & Dalziel

**Genus**: [[Chytranthus]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Protologue**: FWTA, ed. 1, 1 : 504 (1928); Kew Bull. 1929 : 26; RADLK., in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 786 (1932); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 : 717 (1958); AKÉ ASSI, Et. Fl. Côté d'Ivoire, tab. 6 (1963)

## Synonyms

## Description

PL. 23. — Chytranthus angustifolius Exell : 1, leaf × 2/3; 2, petiolar base; 3, section of a bud; 4, opened flower, diameter 4 mm; 5, 6, petals; 7, petaloid appendage of a specimen from Côte-d'Ivoire (approximately 1 mm); 8, disc of 2 mm; 9, stamen of 5.2 mm; 10, anther front and back, 1.7 mm long; 11, upper and median section of the anther, width 0.8 mm; 12, aborted ovary of 0.5 mm; 13, young fruit, diameter 12 mm; 14, ripe fruit and its section, 9 × 9 cm; 15, seed 2.8 cm long (1, 2, 14, 15; N. Hallé 1177; 3, 4, 5, 6, 8; Letouzey 1054; 7, N. Hallé IA. 3275; 9, 10, 11, 12; Chevalier 26900; 13, Baudon s.n., Souanké.)

Monocaulous shrub 0.80–4 m tall, with trunk reaching 15 cm in diameter. Apex very finely puberulent. Small axillary buds hirsute. Leaves 0.5–1.2 m long with 4–6 pairs of leaflets; petiole 20 to 50 cm; rachis longitudinally striate, with extremely short pubescence or glabrescent; petiolule slightly swollen 8–13 mm, with very short pubescence extending more or less to the veins; leaflets, when dry, often subconcolorous brownish, elliptic or obovate 15–40 (–46) × 7–13 (–17) cm; acumen short, acute and often mucronate; base obtuse or acute; upper leaflets larger than the lower ones, sometimes twice the length; midrib with a narrow line of erect hairs on the upper surface; 15–25 pairs of secondary veins spaced 10–15 mm apart; tertiary veins apparent.

Cauline inflorescences on the lower 50 cm of the stem, rather slender, the two types of ♂ or ♀ flowers appearing to originate from different plants, 6–25 cm long, with striate rachis, clothed in fine brown pubescence; pluriflorous glomerules spaced, sessile; the lower ones sometimes briefly pedunculate or even replaced by small branches. Flowers velvety reddish-black or violaceous, approximately 6 mm in diameter, with robust pedicel 2–3 mm long; 5 sepals notched on half the calyx, with broad overlapping and lighter margins; 4 (–5) unguiculate petals, white, exceeding the calyx by 2–4 mm, blade and apex pubescent; ligule variable, often split or emarginate at the apex, with or more frequently without an appendage; convex disc in pentagonal crescent shape exceeding 3 mm. ♂ flower with 7–8 (–10) stamens; filament pubescent; anther glabrous, 1.6 × 0.9 mm, with convex connective without groove or wart, slightly emarginate at the apex; aborted ovary hirsute, with 5 locules. ♀ flower with stigma slightly exsert; ovary hirsute, with 5 locules and 5 grooves.

Fruit approximately 7 × 7 cm, with 5 ridges; surface very densely bristling with stiff reddish-black or brownish hairs when dry. Locules lined with soft hairs; 5 brown seeds, 23–32 × 14–18 × 6 mm. PL. 24, p. 105.

## Type

Talbot 1853 et 1596, Oban, Nigeria (K)

## Discussion

In humid or inundated forest. Sierra Leone, Côte-d'Ivoire (N. Hallé 515 bis), Ghana, Nigeria, Centrafrique (Tisserant 2013), and as far as the Congolese Mayombe.
