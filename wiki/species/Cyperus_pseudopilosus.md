# *Cyperus pseudopilosus* (C.B.Clarke) Govaerts & Simpson Planche 42

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: World Checklist Cyp. : 352 (2007)

## Synonyms

- Mariscus socialis (C.B.Clarke) Hooper, Kew Bull . 26 : 578 (1972).

## Description

Robust herb, perennial, with solitary stems arising from a thick creeping rhizome, resembling a stolon, covered with scales; stems 90–150 cm × 4–8 mm, trigonous (triquetrous beneath the anthela), glabrous. Leaf: sheath reddish-brown, rather wide and without a well-developed limb. Inflorescence an open anthela, with 5–10 primary rays not exceeding 15 cm in length and each bearing an umbelliform group of 3–6 spikes (shortest rays bearing only 1–2 spikes); involucral bracts 5–10, foliaceous, plane, erect or spreading, the largest 20–50 cm × 10–15 mm, exceeding the inflorescence; spikes 10–20 × 7–10 mm, oblong–cylindric with 5–40 spikelets spreading and spaced, the rachis slightly winged. Spikelet linear–lanceolate, 4–6 × 1–1.5 mm, slightly compressed, with 3–7 flowers, detaching as a unit from the rachis at maturity; glumes ovate–elliptic, 1.5–1.7 mm long, pale reddish-brown or yellowish-brown, with 2–3 prominent lateral nerves, apex rounded or emarginate. Flower with 3 stamens; style with 3 long branches. Achene obovate to oblong, 0.9–1.3 × 0.4–0.7 mm, greyish to black, with finely papillose surface.

## Distribution

fairly widely distributed in West Africa, from Guinea to Angola; rare in Gabon, found in Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

In swamps, swamp forests and at forest margins; at 0–1200 m altitude. Taxonomic note: recognizable by its large stature, its solitary stems arising from rhizomes resembling stolons and covered with scales, its very large anthela with prominent spikes and its rather small spikelets disarticulating at the base. Superficially, it resembles C. dives Delile fairly closely, from which it differs by the absence of stolons, the presence of a well-developed limb and larger spikelets that do not disarticulate.
