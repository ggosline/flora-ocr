# *Diplacrum capitatum* (Willd.) Boeckeler Planche 78, Figure 10a

**Genus**: [[Diplacrum]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Linnaea 38 : 435 (1874)

## Synonyms

- Scleria capitata Willd., Sp. pl., éd. 4, 4(1) : 319 (1805).
- Pteroscleria longifolia Griseb., Fl. Brit. W. I. : 579 (1864).

## Description

D. longifolium (Griseb.) C.B.Clarke in Durand & Schinz, Consp. fl. afr. 5 : 669 (1895). Perennial herb, fairly robust, with base usually producing long and fine stolons covered with scales; stems generally 30–50 cm (fertile part included) × 2–3 mm, triangular, glabrous. Leaves numerous, basal and cauline; sheath gutter-shaped, green to reddish-brown; blade 20–40 cm × 4–8 mm, linear, flat or with margin incurved, very scabrous on the margin and central nerve, especially toward the apex. Inflorescence composed of 2–6 sessile or shortly pedunculate heads, dense, formed of glomérules of sessile spikelets, each arising from one of the sheaths (except the lower one). Spikelet lanceolate, 2–3 × 1.5 mm, with a single flower; female flowers solitary, placed between 2 glumes on a receptacle with margin inserted between the glumes, the whole forming a sort of clip gripping the achene at maturity; glumes distinctly concave, 2.5–3 × 1.5 mm, light to dark brown, with dark spots or lines, central nerve winged, apex acuminate. Achene obovoid, 1–1.2 × 1–1.2 mm, white, weakly striated longitudinally, resting on a cupule 0.2–0.3 mm high, yellowish-brown and weakly lobed.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ghana, Nigeria, Gabon and also in tropical America; rare in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime (3 collections)

## Ecology

in swamps and mangroves; in Gabon, at 0–500 m altitude, up to 1200 m in East Africa. B: Haines & Lye (1983), Napper (1971, 1972). Bibliography
