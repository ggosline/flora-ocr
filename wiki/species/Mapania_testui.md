# *Mapania testui* Cherm.

**Genus**: [[Mapania]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Bot. Soc. France 77 : 278 (1930)

## Description

Fairly robust herb, with a thick rhizome of 1–2 mm; stem solitary, central, 25–40 cm × 1.5–2 mm, trigonous, green or reddish towards the base, glabrous. Leaves all basal; sheath lanceolate, 3–8 × 1 cm, greenish, fairly thick; blade linear to linear-elliptic, the largest of 15–40 × 1.5–2 cm, flat, fairly thick, trinerved, with base narrowed into a pseudo-petiole of 4–18 cm × 3–4 mm, apex abruptly acute. Inflorescence terminal or pseudo-lateral, in a brown, globose to hemispheric head, 1.4–2.2 cm wide, composed of 10–20 tightly packed and ± distinct spikes; involucral bracts 2, foliaceous, linear, 1–35 × 0.3–1.7 cm, the basal one longer than the stem, green and abruptly narrowed into a pseudo-petiole of 1–10 cm × 1.3–1.5 mm; spikes elliptic, 7–8 × 2–3 mm, acute; spicoidal bracts lanceolate, 4–5 × 1.4–1.6 mm, hyaline to brown, glabrous, apex acuminate, each subtending a spicoid with 2 male flowers and 1 female. Flower:

floral bracts 4, lanceolate, flat, brown to light brown, apex acuminate, the 2 lower ones of 3–4 × 1 mm, keel not winged, weakly hispid, the 2 upper ones of 2.5–3 × 1 mm, glabrous; style with 2 branches. Achene obovoid, 1.2–1.6 × 0.7–1 mm, often somewhat lenticular, pale brown to dark brown, longitudinally striated with 2 lateral ribs and 1 anterior.

## Distribution

endemic to Gabon, rare, found in Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo (9 collections)

## Ecology

primary or secondary forest, often along rivers and streams; at 250–700 m altitude. Taxonomic note: The form and size of the leaves of M. testui are variable. Close to M. mannii, but the latter has narrower leaves and involucral bracts, a smaller inflorescence with fewer spicoids. Under magnification, M. testui differs from M. mannii (and from all other African species) by its lower floral bracts distinctly longer than the upper ones. M. amplivaginata differs from M. testui by different involucral bracts, a less dense inflorescence and shorter spicoidal and floral bracts. b: Simpson (1992). Bibliography
