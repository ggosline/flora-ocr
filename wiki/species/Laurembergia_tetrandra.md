# *Laurembergia tetrandra* (Schott) Kanitz

**Family**: [[Haloragaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Laurembergia]]

*Flora Brasiliensis* **13**(2): 378 (1882).

## Synonymy

- *Laurembergia tetrandra* subsp. *brachypoda* (Hiern) A.Raynal, Webbia **19**: 694 (1965).
- *Laurembergia tetrandra* var. *numidica* (Batt. & Trab. ex Durieu) A.Raynal, Webbia **19**: 694 (1965).

## Description

Succulent herb, submerged or with a prostrate to ascending, mat-forming stem, to 25 cm high, sometimes woody at the base. Leaves usually opposite but often alternate on part or all of the stem; petiole 0–4 mm; blade linear to linear-oblong, rarely narrowly elliptic or narrowly obovate, 2–15 × 0.5–5 mm, apex rounded, margin entire or rarely with a few teeth, green to reddish-brown.

Inflorescence a fascicle of one pedicellate bisexual flower and up to 14(–21?) sessile or shortly pedicellate female flowers. Bisexual flower with pedicel 0.3–0.8(–1.8) mm; sepals triangular; petals oblong, about 1 mm, white to pink; stamens 4, filament 0.3–0.8 mm, anther oblong and 0.6–1 mm, pollen yellow; ovary ovoid, green to pinkish, with eight ribs, each bearing four tubercles, these sometimes united or becoming obsolete so that only two or three tubercles or the ribs remain; style short; stigmas papillose. Female flower similar but with pedicel 0–0.3 mm and without petals or stamens. Fruit ovoid, to 0.9 mm long, reddish, with whitish ribs and tubercles.

## Distribution

Widely but sporadically distributed in eastern South America, tropical Africa from Senegal to Ethiopia and south to Namibia, northern South Africa and Mozambique, and also Madagascar, Mauritius and coastal Algeria. In Gabon it is known from Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié and Ogooué-Maritime and should be sought in Estuaire and Nyanga.

## Habitat and ecology

Typically in shallow water in lakes, ponds or ditches during the rainy season and on exposed mud afterwards; at higher elevations also in peat bogs and wet grassland. Recorded at 0–400 m in Gabon and above 2100 m elsewhere. The treatment suggests migratory birds as seed dispersers.

## Notes

The treatment does not maintain Raynal's American subsp. *tetrandra* and African subsp. *brachypoda*: the supposedly diagnostic fruit-tubercle states occur on both continents. Variation in indumentum is also treated as continuous. Broad, distinctly toothed high-altitude plants called *L. mildbraedii* or var. *mildbraedii* may represent another species, but require further study.
