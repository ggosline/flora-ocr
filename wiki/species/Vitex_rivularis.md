# *Vitex rivularis* Gürke Planche 30(2-5)

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 297 (1903)

## Description

Tree reaching 18 m tall; branches almost glabrous except at nodes. Leaf: petiole (4–)10–20 cm, with a few spreading hairs accompanied or not by very short papillous hairs; leaflets 5 to 7, with petiolules 5–25 mm, clearly distinct from the blade, pubescent, blade elliptic to obovate-elliptic or narrowly ovate-elliptic, 8–18 × 4–6 cm, cuneate at base, apex fairly abruptly contracted into a long and fine acumen (2.5–3 cm on some leaflets), entire, upper surface dark green, dull, slightly scabrous or strigulose, glabrous except for the veins bearing appressed hairs and a few cilia on the midvein, lower surface subglabrous, sprinkled with numerous golden glands, veins with short straight appressed hairs (0.5 mm) accompanied or not by papillous hairs; secondary veins (8 to) 11 to 20 on each side. Inflorescence fragrant, formed of axillary dichasia, reaching 25 cm in length, of which 9–20 cm for the peduncle, ample, diffuse, very multiflorous, very much branched (7 to 9 levels of branching); axes puberulent, nodes with long fine pale tawny hairs. Flower: calyx 1.5–2(–2.5) mm long, of which 0.5 mm for the lobes, with numerous well-visible golden glands, not masked by short appressed hairs; corolla ± 4 mm long, whitish, with lower lip purple to bluish, shortly pubescent, with well-visible golden glands; ovary glabrous, but glandular at apex. Fruit obovoid, 20–30 × 18–20 mm, subtended by a cupuliform calyx.

## Distribution

West tropical Africa, from Guinea to Angola; in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

Dense forests; at 0–1200 m altitude

## Source note

This represents the first record of this species for the Gabon flora. The type of V. longipetiolata Gürke falls within the variation of V. rivularis Gürke. Bibliography: Aubréville (1956), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Huber et al. (1963), Lisowski (2009), Meerts (2020a, 2020b)
