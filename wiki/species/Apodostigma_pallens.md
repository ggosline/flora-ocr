Stipules with several teeth, one of them very acute, 1 mm. Petiole canaliculate, (3-)5–10(-15) mm long. Lamina thin or sometimes subcoriaceous, 3–10(-15) × 1.3–4.5(-7) cm; margin entire or with 6–15 teeth per side; base acute, sometimes briefly so; acumen 2–10 mm. Secondary veins 4–7 pairs ascending; fine venation often indistinct when dry.

## Discussion

From Senegal to Congo (Lecomte B85, Kitabi) and East Africa, and from Angola to Mozambique (Hallé N. 6509). A small form capurroniana Hallé has been described from Madagascar.

This species is highly variable in bark appearance, leaf texture and venation in particular. The ecology also varies: dry habitat on savanna edge or humid forest, or riparian along river or lagoon. These forms can behave locally as distinct species. The typical variety is to be sought at savanna edge in Gabon.

1b. var. dummeri Hallé, var. nov.

Differs from var. typica by large leaves and inflorescence ultimate axes appearing almost villous. TYPE: Dummer 3156, Uganda, 3–4. 1917 (holo-, P).

Variety remarkable for its often large leaves and for its inflorescences appearing villous: it is the fimbriated awns of the inflorescence axes, the multidentate bracteoles and the denticulate sepals that give this appearance.

Variety better known from Uganda, Zaïre (Kivu: Troupin 4388 and 4484, BR!) and Central African Republic.

In Cameroon, a very slight tendency toward the presence of denticules on the inflorescence awns appears in De Wilde W.c.s. 2523.

1c. var. buchholzii (Loesener) Hallé, stat. nov.

– Hippocratea ? buchholzii LOES., Bot. Jahrb. 19: 234 (1894); EXELL & MENDONÇA, CFA 2 (1): 16 (1954).

Liana very tall reaching 15–25 m. Leaf lamina somewhat larger than in the typical variety, 8–20 × 3.5–10 cm, often paler when dry, with often long acumen, margin entire. Secondary veins 3–6 pairs delimiting toward the center of the lamina 1 or 2 very extensive fields along the midvein.

Flowers green or orange. Carpels up to 5 cm in length. - Pl. 56, fig. 2, 22, p. 195.

This variety appears to favor the very dense and very humid forest regions of Côte-d'Ivoire, Ghana, Cameroon, Gabon and Angola.
