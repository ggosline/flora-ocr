# *Euadenia sp* .

**Genus**: [[Euadenia]]
**Family**: [[Brassicaceae]]

## Description

A specimen with simple leaves shows abnormal characters on the floral parts, particularly at the level of the androecium and the ovary. At first glance, it appears distinctly different, but it could be

considered as a strongly modified and partly abnormal specimen of E. trifoliata or E. alimensis.

Small shrub 1 m tall, branched from the base, emitting roots from the base apparently creeping from the trunk. The aerial stem is a simple and slender sympodium. Annual leafy shoot 1.5 cm long furnished with 3 leaves and terminating in a raceme.

Simple leaves shortly or briefly petiolate, broadly elliptic to obovate; blade of the upper leaves $ 23 \times 9.5 $ cm, apiculate; lateral nerves 8. Petiole of the lowest leaf 1 cm long, that of the highest 3.5 cm. Pluriflorous raceme fairly lax. Simple floral bracts, the lowest elliptic, shortly petiolate, $ 2.5 \times 0.6 $ cm, the highest filiform, 0.5–0.7 cm long, all persistent at anthesis. Upper sepal, $ 1.3 \times 2 $ mm, long attenuate; the other sepals are shorter and narrower. Petals incompletely expanded, pale yellowish green. Upper petals reaching $ 3.5 \times 0.4 $ cm, lanceolate acuminate, midvein well visible, lateral nerves short but distinct, tinged with reddish; lower petals, $ 2.5 \times 1.5 $ mm linear, acute, uninervate. Androgynophore 2–3 mm long. Fertile stamens 2, filaments 2 cm long, inclined, anthers 4 mm long. Staminodes 4 sometimes 3, grouped in 2 pairs connate at their base, fleshy, dividing into free filaments in their upper half; rudimentary anthers at most 0.5 mm long; there are 3 staminodes in the case where 2 adjacent filaments are completely fused; aberrant characters concerning the staminodes are found on this specimen. The pistil may be absent from certain flowers. Gynophore approximately 1.3 cm long. Ovary cylindric approximately 6 mm long, often curved in its apical region or ± abnormal in form, unicarpellate, with a single prominent placenta reaching almost the opposite wall of the cavity; ovules numerous. Stigma very slightly marked, obliquely developed or absent. – Map B, p. 7.

The petiole becomes increasingly long in the leaves that develop subsequently; this gradual change probably indicates that the process could lead to the appearance of trifoliolate leaves; similar simple leaves had already been observed in E. alimensis in Cameroon. The anomalies of the floral parts represent a limiting state of morphological characters fairly unstable at this level, within the genus.

This specimen originates from the scree of a slope; the plant appears to have developed ± as an epiphyte on a substrate covered with mosses (rocky or stony). These aberrant characters could be linked to an unusual biotope.
