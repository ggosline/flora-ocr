# *Cyperus triceps* Endl. Planche 62

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Cat. horti. Vindob . 1 : 94 (1842)

## Description

, nom. illeg. Perennial herb, forming tufts, with the base of the stems slightly swollen, bulb-shaped; old bulbs persistent; stems 5–30 cm × 0.5–1 mm, angular, glabrous, with longitudinal ridges, the base covered by old sheaths. Leaf: blade 5–15 cm × 1.5–3 mm, flat or incurved, densely scabrous on the margin and central vein, often with dark purple dots or lines. Inflorescence in an irregular and dense head, white or greenish, often triangular in outline, 4–9 mm wide, composed of a few distinct spikelets or more commonly, of a central oval spikelet with a few smaller spikelets at the base; involucral bracts usually 3–6, foliaceous, reflexed or spreading, the largest 2–8 cm long. Spikelet 2–2.5 mm long, one-flowered, with glumes spreading slightly when the achenes reach maturity; glumes elliptic, 1.5–2.2 mm long, whitish with brown dots near the glabrous and non-winged keel, with 3–4 prominent lateral veins, central vein of the upper glumes terminating in a short point. Flower with 1–3 stamens; style with 2 branches. Achene ellipsoid to obovoid, about 1.5 mm long, flattened, brown, finely papillose.

## Distribution

widespread in the tropical regions of Africa and Asia, except in the most humid regions; common in Gabon, in the Woleu-Ntem, particularly around Oyem, but not found elsewhere (8 collections)

CYPERUS L. subgenus PYCNOSTACHYS C.B.Clarke Fl. Brit. India 6 : 597 (1893). Perennial or more rarely annual herbs; stems usually 20–80 cm long. Basal leaves numerous, rare or absent. Anatomy of stems and leaves of eucyperoid type (that is, C3 type anatomy). Inflorescence in a lax anthela (rarely compact) with groups of digitate spikelets and generally large foliaceous involucral bracts. Spikelet with numerous flowers, 2–15 mm long, slightly compressed; axis persistent (spikelets not falling as a single unit); glumes 0.5–3.5 mm long, obtuse, acute or with the central vein prolonged into a straight or curved mucro. Flower with 1–3 stamens, connective often producing an apical appendage; style caducous, with 3 branches. Achenes sessile, trigonous, 0.4–1.5 mm long, with surface usually tuberculate or finely papillose. Medium-sized subgenus, comprising about 150 species from marshes and forests, usually in zones of high rainfall, growing mostly in tropical and subtropical regions; in Gabon 8 species.
