# *Peperomia pellucida* (L.) Kunth Planche 26.1

**Genus**: [[Peperomia]]
**Family**: [[Piperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Nov. gen. sp . (in 4°) 1 : 64 (1816)

## Description

Nov. gen. sp. (in 4°) 1: 64 (1816). Terrestrial herb, annual, erect or creeping, up to 30 cm tall, glabrous or somewhat pubescent; stem much branched, almost from the base. Leaves alternate; petiole 2–15 mm; blade broadly ovate-triangular, almost always as wide as long, 0.5–3.5 × 0.5–3 cm, with truncate, rounded or cordate base, with shortly acuminate-obtuse apex, membranous and translucent, bright green on the upper surface, yellowish on the lower surface, with 5 to 7 palmate veins. Inflorescence in numerous spikes (approximately 20 or more), solitary, terminal or opposite the leaves, 2–5 cm × ± ½ mm; peduncle 2–10 mm. Fruit globose, < 1 mm in diameter, papillose, dark brown to orange.

## Distribution

species native to the New World, today naturalized elsewhere and pantropical, notably common in tropical and equatorial Africa; in Gabon, known from the Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

terrestrial or lithophytic, often ruderal, considered a weed in humid, open or shaded environments, river banks, rocks in forest, periodically flooded marsh; in Gabon at 10–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1100 m. Uses: P. pellucida is commonly used in medicine in all tropical regions, including in tropical Africa. The aerial parts are used against pain, abscesses, acne, gout, headaches, kidney problems, cardiac arrhythmia and fatigue. In all tropical regions, the plant is used to treat boils, conjunctivitis and skin wounds. The plant is also commonly used as a condiment and consumed as a spiced leaf-vegetable, cooked or in salad. In Africa, it is sometimes cultivated for this purpose. It is also cultivated as an ornamental plant

## Vernacular names

cresson, herbe à couleuvre, herbe à couresse, pépéromia, salade soda, salade soldat (fr) ; cow foot, pepper elder, rabbit ear, shiny bush (angl.)
