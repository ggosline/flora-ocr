# *Salacia chlorantha* Oliver

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: FTA 1 : 375 (1868) ; HALLÉ, Monogr. Hippocr. Afr. occid., Thèse : 220 (1958) ; Mém. IFAN 64 : 223 (1962)

## Description

– S. macrocarpa Welw. ex Oliv., FTA 1: 373 (1868); Loes., Bot. Jahrb. 44: 166 (1910).

- S. macrocarpa WELW. ex OLIV. var. latifolia LOES., l.c. (1910).

- S. macrocarpa Welw. ex Oliv. var. angustifolia (Scott Elliot) Loes., l.c. (1910).

- S. macrocarpa WELW. ex OLIV. var. grandiflora LOES., l.c. (1910).

– S. angustifolia Scott Elliot, Journ. Linn. Soc. 30: 75 (1894). TYPE: Scott Elliot 5486, Sierra Leone (BM!, K!, P!).

- S. demeusei DE WILD. & TH. DUR., Ann. Mus. Congo belge 2 (1): 11 (1900), same type as S. macrocarpa WELW. ex OLIV.

- S. doeringii LOES., Bot. Jahrb. 44: 166 (1910), probable synonym (cf. HALLÉ, Mém. IFAN 64: 223, 1962). TYPE: Döring 265, Togo (not seen).

– S. dalzielii HUTCH. & Moss, FWTA 1: 453 (1928); Kew Bull.: 22 (1929); BRENAN, Kew Bull.: 220 (1950). TYPE: Dalziel s.n., Nigeria, Mt. Patti, 21.2.1905 (fl.) (K!).

– S. euryoides Hutch. & Moss, FWTA 1: 453 (1928); Kew Bull.: 21 (1929). Type: Scott Elliot 4870, Sierra Leone.

- S. ripicola Louis, nomen in herb. Louis 7721, Zaire (K!).

– S. senegalensis auct. non (LAM.) DC.; Wilczek, FCB 9: 200 (1960).

Liane 3 to 20 m tall, glabrous, containing rubber; branchlets rusty, early lenticellate. Leaves opposite or subopposite, sometimes nearly alternate. Petiole 4–10 mm, with straight or rarely slightly wavy canal margins. Blade green or brownish-green, more rarely brown when dry, discolorous, elliptic or oblong, 6–16 × 2.5–7.5 cm, with acute to obtuse base, rather briefly acuminate apex; margin dentate to entire, often with a small rim below. Secondary veins 6–8(10) pairs; fine network rather dense.

Inflorescences pluri- to multiflorous, axillary; floral glomerule short, ± divided on a variable peduncle, 0.5–5(7) mm long, or sessile. Pedicels 6–15(25) mm. Bud oblong, 4–5 × 2.5–3 mm. Flowers 6–10(12) mm in diameter, pale green or almost white or cream. Sepals green ± unequal to almost equal, subdeltoid to broadly semicircular, nearly entire, few-denticular or ± fimbriate. Corolla ± reflexed at end of anthesis, with oblong or ovate petals 4–6 × 2.5–3.5 mm, apex somewhat spoon-shaped. Disk truncate-conical, 0.9–1.3 mm tall, 1.5–1.2 mm broad at base, micropapilliferous (dark green zone becoming brown) over three-quarters of its height only: rehydrated material shows a pale corona 0.2 mm thick beneath the stamens. Filaments spaced, exceeding 2 mm; anthers somewhat taller than broad, 1.5 mm tall × 1 mm broad, with connectival apex prominent as a small apicule; locules non-confluent, juxtaposed at a very acute angle. Pistil narrowly pyramidal terminated by a punctiform stigma (sometimes expanding into three small lobes opposite the stamens). Ovary locules situated more than two-thirds above the level of the androecium, each containing 6–8 ovules ± distinctly biseriate.

Fruit bright orange, globose to briefly ellipsoid, 3–8.5 × 3–8 cm, smooth or weakly verruculous or mamillate, sometimes with a small tricostulate acumen, containing 6–13 seeds or more. Seed 15 × 10 mm. – Pl. 47, fig. 1–17, p. 165.

## Discussion

The related species S. senegalensis (Lam.) DC. (= Hippocratea senegalensis Lam., Ill. Gen.: 191, 1971) differs by its ovary locules more sunken with fewer ovules, 3–4, by its disk micropapilliferous to the apex, by its vegetative apparatus always rusty or reddish discolorous when dry, and by its adaptation to distinctly drier climatic zones; this Salacia has been found neither in Cameroon nor a fortiori in Gabon and its West African range does not appear to extend beyond eastern Liberia. – Pl. 47, fig. 18–24.

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Côte-d'Ivoire, Togo (Schröder 201, cited by LOESENER; Breteler 7291), Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo, Central African Republic, Zaire, Cabinda, Angola and Zambia. Scrub or dense forests.

DC.: 18, bud; 19, calyx seen from below; 20, flower, diam. 7 mm; 21, 22, disk, diam. 1.6 mm; 23, anther, 0.9 mm; 24, ovary locules (Jansen 2318).

It was not possible to maintain the subspecies recognized by us in 1962, particularly for West Africa. It would have been possible but without great significance to recognize a vast fragmentation of varieties or forms. The sepals are often strongly fimbriate in West Africa and nearly entire in Central Africa. Galled flowers are frequent, and not opening they remain as large somewhat conical buds on their somewhat thickened pedicel.

45 bis. Salacia owabiensis Hoyle

Kew Bull.: 186 (1934); HALLÉ, Monogr. Hippocr. Afr. occid., Thèse: 211 (1958); Mém. IFAN 64: 213, tab. 70 (1962).

Liane, sometimes sarmentose shrub, glabrous and containing rubber. Wood showing concentric scalloped rings of parenchyma in cross-section. Young branchlets cylindric smooth, enlarged at nodes. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole (6)8–13 mm, with straight canal margins. Blade olive-brown above and yellowish olive-green below when dry, elliptic, 6–16 × 3–8 cm, with acute base, sometimes very briefly so, with acuminate apex; margin entire ± denticular or sometimes dentate. Secondary veins (6)7–10 pairs arcuate ascending; fine network well visible when dry on both faces.

Fascicles 5–10-flowered or spherical multiflorous; inflorescences axillary, sessile or subsessile or pedunculate on 1–2(–4) mm. Pedicels 8–12(–15) mm, rather slender. Bud globose or briefly ovate, 3–4 mm. Flower nearly rotate, 7–9(–12) mm in diameter, yellow or yellow-green. Sepals 4, thin with nearly entire ± split margin, in 2 opposite pairs: the outer ones small, 1–1.8 mm, the inner ones large subpetaloid, 3 × 3.7–4 mm. Petals 5, ovate, about 4 × 3 mm, concave above beyond the proximal area. Disk brown, truncate-conical, 1.5–2.5 mm in diameter, 0.6–1 mm tall, sometimes constricted by a transverse median furrow, and crowned by 6 contact pits with the thecae. Stamens 2.5–3(4) mm, spaced but broad at base, with orange anther apiculate at apex, 0.7–0.9 mm broad, with juxtaposed thecae, dehiscence in an acute chevron; filament spiraled at end of anthesis. Pistil in narrow pyramid, 2–2.4 mm tall, with punctiform stigma; locules sunken for more than half their height, each with 2–5 ovules most often superposed.

Fruit spherical, 3–6 cm in diameter, irregularly muriculate over all or a large part of its surface, orange-red at maturity, with distinctly thickened peduncle; 2 or numerous seeds. - Pl. 48, fig. 1–10, p. 167.

Guinea, Liberia, Côte-d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria (Keay & Meikle 25661), Cameroon and Central African Republic (Sillans in Tisserant 1564, Salo, upper Sangha). To be sought in Gabon. Forests and very humid fringes.

This species well deserves to be distinguished, particularly by its calyx and fruit, from S. laurentii and S. pyriformis. It was confused with the latter by KEAY & BLAKELOCK (FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2): 632, 1958) and by IRVINE (WPG: 460, 1961).

45 ter. Salacia talbotii E.G. Baker

Cat. Talb. Pl. Niger: 19 (1913); HALLÉ, Monogr. Hippocr. Afr. occid., Thèse: 207 (1958); Mém. IFAN 64: 210 (1962).

Liane exceeding 5 m tall, with abundant rubber, entirely glabrous. Internodes smooth ochraceous, with epidermis somewhat wrinkled when dry. Petiole channeled, 7–14 mm. Blade rusty ochraceous elliptic, 8–22 × 4–8.5 cm, subcoriaceous, acute at base, with acumen 4–9 mm; margin nearly entire slightly rimmed. Midvein almost without relief on upper face; (5)6–8(9) pairs of secondary veins; reticulation apparent on both faces.

Inflorescences in sessile axillary glomerules, pauci- or pluriflorous (10 buds observed). Pedicel 4–7 mm. Buds ovoid, about 5–8 × 5–7 mm, rusty-orange when dry. Flower 12–16 mm in diameter, whitish-green or cream-white, orange when dry. Sepals 5 very unequal, with thinned brown margin cracked like that of the lower petals; altogether up to 14 perianth parts spreading or ± erect, the largest petals reaching 8.5 × 5.5 mm. Disk thick, 2 mm, constricted above its base, 4 mm broad above the constriction which is slightly wrinkled lengthwise; apex with a perigynous furrow. Stamens 3, with robust filament enlarged at base, 2 mm tall, white; anther pale yellow, elongate, 1.7 × 1.4 mm, with thecae disposed at a very acute angle, with connectival apex somewhat apiculate. Style triedric, attenuate, white, 3 mm tall. Ovary sunken, with narrow locules each containing 4 ovules aligned in a single vertical row.

Fruit bright orange and shiny at maturity, 7–10 × 4.5–5.5 cm, with slightly wrinkled pustulous and tricostulate integument, with slightly attenuate base, with briefly acuminate to rostrate apex, with thickened peduncle 7–12 mm long. Seeds 6–7 or more per fruit. - Pl. 48, fig. 11–21, p. 167.

The range of this species endemic to SE Nigeria reaches western Cameroon. River banks in dense high forest. Clearly related to S. pyriformis and confused with it by KEAY & BLAKELOCK (FWTA, ed. 2, 1: 632, 1958), despite its very original characters.
