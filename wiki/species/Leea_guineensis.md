# *Leea guineensis* G. Don

**Genus**: [[Leea]]
**Family**: [[Leeaceae]]
**Protologue**: G. Don, Gen. Syst. Gard. Bot. 1:712 (1831)

## Description

166 (1881). — Dewit, Fl. Congo belge et R.-U. 9 : 569 (1960).

Shrub 2–10 m tall, with erect stems sparsely branched, sometimes slightly sarmentose, swollen at the nodes, with numerous lenticels, with leaves clustered at the extremities. Leaves bi- or tripinnate. Rachis 30–60 cm long, ramifications 20–40 cm long, more or less canaliculate on the upper surface and furnished toward the base with small tubercles, glabrous, weakly pubescent at the insertions or distinctly pubescent. Petiole swollen and more or less sheathing, glabrescent or pubescent. Stipules 2–4 cm long and 1–3 cm wide, thick, rapidly caducous. Leaflets 3–5–7, of rather variable form on the same plant, generally more or less elliptic, often ovate-elliptic or elliptic-oblong, or even ovate or oblong, with base almost always cuneate, rarely obtuse, with apex acuminate, with dentate margins, 7–25 cm long and 3–11 cm wide, glabrous or more or less distinctly pubescent on the lower surface along the veins; 8–10 pairs of secondary veins more or less prominent, reticulum in slight relief on the upper surface; blade plane, fairly thick, discolorous, glossy.

Inflorescences opposite to the upper leaves, in broad corymbiform cymes 10–20 cm wide; peduncles and axes thick, cylindric sulcate, furnished with a few rugosities, more or less densely pubescent or glabrescent; bracts and bracteoles ovate-deltoid acute, 0.5–1.5 mm long and wide, glabrous or weakly pubescent, caducous. Flowers more or less 5 mm long, more or less 3 mm in diameter, with colour varying from coral red to orange yellow. Pedicel 1–2 cm long, cylindric, thickened under the calyx, weakly pubescent. Calyx 1.5–2.5 mm long and in diameter, thick, with deltoid acute teeth, generally glabrous or slightly pubescent. Corolla globose, more or less 4 mm long, glabrous, with oblong cucullate segments internally. Coronule white to pink in colour, 2–3 mm in height and diameter, with thick oblong lobes bidentate. Stamens with white filament, with oblong narrow connective. Ovary hemispherical, more or less 1 mm in diameter, glabrous; style 1.5–2.5 mm long; stigma capitate.

Berry red at maturity, discoid, compressed on the upper and lower surface, 8–10 mm in diameter, 6–7 mm in thickness, glabrous, furnished with the persistent calyx. Seeds generally 4 to 6, 4–5 mm long and 3–4 mm wide, with lateral faces plane.

Small shrub of the understory of secondary forest formations, becoming even ruderal and common on clearings and in plantations around villages; it is also encountered, but much less frequently, in more closed formations, at the margins and in forest galleries. The range of this species covers West and Central Africa, from Senegal to Tanganyika, as well as the northern half of Madagascar, the Comores and the Mascarene Islands.

The leaves are used in massage against rheumatism. A decoction of these leaves is drunk against gonorrhoea, sleeping sickness, etc. To render favourable the chiefs and other influential persons, certain customs recommend washing with a maceration of these same leaves. Among the Fang, after the death of a spouse, the widower or widow must wash obligatorily with this same lustral water. Moreover, in the ceremony of the nkal (ordeal poison) sorcerers use these leaves as an aspergillum to spray the assistants (according to A. Walker and R. Sillans).
