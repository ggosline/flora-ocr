# *Leplaea thompsonii* (Sprague & Hutch.) E.J.M.Koenen & J.J.de Wilde

**Genus**: [[Leplaea]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Pl. Ecol. Evol . 145(2) : 231 (2012)

## Synonyms

- Guarea thompsonii Sprague & Hutch., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1906 : 245 (1906).

## Description

Large tree reaching 35(–40) m in height; trunk up to 90(–140) cm in diameter; bark smooth, greyish; inner surface yellowish, exuding white latex. Leaf: petiole (5–)8–14(–18) cm, with subalate margins at the base, glabrous; rachis 12–28(–70) cm; petiolules (3–)6–15 mm, reaching 30 mm on the terminal leaflet; leaflets (7–)11–17, narrowly elliptic or elliptic to rectangular, fairly symmetrical, (5–)9–14(–28) × (2.5–)4–7(–9.5) cm, with obtuse to cuneate base, with obtuse or acuminate to mucronate apex, glabrous on both surfaces; secondary veins 10–16 pairs, veinlet network obscure above, slightly prominent below. Inflorescence solitary, lax, reaching 14 cm in length, puberulent. Functionally male flower: pedicel articulate, 1–7 mm; calyx 2 mm long, entire or rarely weakly 4–5-dentate, puberulent, glandular; petals 4–6, narrowly elliptic or rectangular, 7.5–9.5 × 2.5–3 mm, glabrous to variously hairy outside; staminal tube slightly urceolate, 5–7(–9) mm long, crenate at the apex, glabrous, with 7–8(–9) anthers; gynoecium sessile, ovary rudimentary ovoid, 3–5 × 1.5 mm, pilose; style 1–2 mm, glabrous. Functionally female flower very similar but larger in size; calyx 2–3 mm long; petals 10–12(–13) × 3.5–5(–7) mm; staminal tube 7–9(–10) mm long; ovary 5–6(–8.5) × 2–3 mm, 2(–3)-locular, with 2 superposed ovules per locule; style 3–3.5 mm. Fruit globose to globosely depressed, (2–)3–4.5 cm in diameter, glabrous, smooth, rose-violet, with late dehiscence. Seeds (1–)2–4(–6) per fruit, subreniform, 1.5–3 × 1.8 cm.

## Distribution

Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, fairly common and known from all provinces.

## Ecology

Primary evergreen forest, humid semi-deciduous forest; in Gabon, up to 700 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 900 m. Uses: Timber is valued for construction and is traditionally used for making canoes.
