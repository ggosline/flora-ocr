# *Tecomaria capensis* (Thunberg) Spach

**Genus**: [[Tecomaria]]
**Family**: [[Bignoniaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hist. Nat. Vég. Pha. 9 : 137 (1840) ; GENTRY, Ann. Missouri Bot. Gard. 60 : 961 (1973) ;

## Description

- Bignonia capensis THUNB., Prodr.: 105 (1794-1800).

- Tecoma capensis (THUNB.) LINDL., Bot. Reg.: tab. 1117 (1827).

Shrub or subshrub. Branches flexuous. Leaves opposite, pinnately compound, usually 7–11 leaflets, leaflets elliptic to suborbicular, apically and basally rounded or abruptly cuneate at the base, sessile, approximately 1.5 × 1 cm, dentate, membranous, puberulent at least along the main veins, usually with tufts of branched hairs in the axils.

Inflorescence racemose or racemosely paniculate. Calyx cupular, 5-toothed, 5–7 mm long, 4.5 mm wide, ± puberulent, ciliate with prominent circular glands in the upper half.

Corolla orange to scarlet-orange, tubular, curved, 3.5–5 cm long, 0.6–0.7 cm wide at the opening, mostly glabrous, lobes ciliate. Stamens ± equal in length; anthers exserted, thecae approximately 3 mm long, weakly divergent for half their length, filament slender attached at approximately 1 mm from the fused base of the thecae. Pistil 5.5–6.6 cm long, ovary oblong glabrous. Disk cupular-pulvinate.

Capsule linear, 5–11 cm long, 7–8 mm wide. Seeds in two rows, thin, biwinged with hyaline membranous wings. — Pl. 11, fig. 4, p. 47.

Native to South Africa and commonly cultivated mainly in subtropical regions and at higher altitudes in tropical zones. The genus is very close to Tecoma Juss., neotropical and may even be congeneric.

Although there are no collections made in Gabon, it is possible that it is cultivated there; it is in nearby Cameroon and elsewhere in tropical Africa.
