# *Mapania heteromorpha* (Nelmes) Lye Planche 110(3-5)

**Genus**: [[Mapania]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lidia 7 : 88 (2011)

## Synonyms

- Hypolytrum heteromorphum Nelmes, Kew Bull. 1954 : 522 (1955).

## Description

Fairly robust herb, with a short erect rhizome; lateral stems numerous, arising at the axil of lower leaves, erect, 15–60 cm × 1–3 mm, somewhat flattened or triangular–compressed, smooth to slightly scabrous in the upper part, green or sometimes purplish at the base. Basal leaves, numerous; sheath brown, with finer and darker margin; blade linear, plane or slightly folded, the largest 30–90 × 1–2 cm, margin scabrous at least near the apex, gradually narrowed to an acute apex. Inflorescence terminal, in a lax panicle, at most 4 cm wide, light to dark brown, comprising up to 20 spikes; several involucral bracts, often spreading, linear, usually brownish or sometimes the lower one green, shorter than the inflorescence, acute apex; spikes linear–lanceolate becoming cylindric at maturity, 8–25 × 1–4 mm; spicoidal bracts ovate, 2.2–2.8 × 1.5–1.8 mm, brown, glabrous, acute apex. Flower: floral bracts 2, lanceolate, 1.3–1.8 mm long, brown, keel not winged and distinctly hispid, acute to almost obtuse apex; style with 2 branches. Achene ellipsoid and slightly flattened at the top, 2–3 × 1.2–1.5 mm, acute to almost obtuse apex, lower part ± distinctly striated or longitudinally ribbed, light to dark brown, upper part tuberculate to almost smooth, pale to light brownish.

## Distribution

known from Guinea to the Democratic Republic of Congo, rare in East Africa; fairly widespread in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (19 collections)

## Ecology

swampy forests, sometimes along rivers and watercourses or at the edge of mangroves; in Gabon, at 0–700 m altitude, up to 1200 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: easily recognized by its lax lateral inflorescences. Other species with lateral inflorescences usually have spikes in dense head. However, in West Africa, the closely related species M. senegalensis sometimes also has a lax inflorescence. Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Nelmes (1955a)
