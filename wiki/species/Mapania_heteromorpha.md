# *Mapania heteromorpha* (Nelmes) Lye Planche 110(3-5)

**Genus**: [[Mapania]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lidia 7 : 88 (2011)

## Description

Rather robust herb, with a short erect rhizome; numerous lateral stems, arising in the axil of lower leaves, erect, 15–60 cm × 1–3 mm, somewhat flattened or triangular–compressed, smooth to slightly scabrous in the upper part, green or sometimes purplish at the base. Basal leaves, numerous; sheath brown, with finer and darker margin; blade linear, plane or slightly folded, the largest 30–90 × 1–2 cm, margin scabrous at least near the apex, gradually narrowed into an acute apex. Inflorescence terminal, in a loose panicle, at most 4 cm wide, light to dark brown, comprising up to 20 spikes; several involucral bracts, often spreading, linear, generally brownish or sometimes the lower one green, shorter than the inflorescence, apex acute; spikes linear–lanceolate becoming cylindric at maturity, 8–25 × 1–4 mm; spicoid bracts oval, 2.2–2.8 × 1.5–1.8 mm, brown, glabrous, apex acute. Flower: floral bracts 2, lanceolate, 1.3–1.8 mm long, brown, keel not winged and distinctly hispid, apex acute to almost obtuse; style with 2 branches. Achene ellipsoid and slightly flattened at the top, 2–3 × 1.2–1.5 mm, apex acute to almost obtuse, lower part ± distinctly striate or longitudinally ribbed, light to dark brown, upper part tuberculate to almost smooth, pale to light brownish.

## Distribution

known from Guinea to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, rare in East Africa; fairly widespread in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (19 collections)

## Ecology

swampy forests, sometimes along rivers and watercourses or at the edge of mangroves; in Gabon, 0–700 m altitude, up to 1200 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: easily recognized by its loose lateral inflorescences. Other species with lateral inflorescences usually have spikes in a dense head. However, in West Africa, the closely related species M. senegalensis sometimes also has a loose inflorescence. Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Nelmes (1955a)
