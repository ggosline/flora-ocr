Shrub or small tree reaching 15 m in height and 60 cm in girth; bark smooth, reddish-brown. Branches reddish-brown, smooth or ± rugulose, lenticellate, glabrous; branchlets initially pubescent with spreading hairs, then glabrescent. Leaves with petiole 2–5 mm long, appressed-pubescent to glabrescent; blade papyraceous to subcoriaceous, elliptic-oblong to ovate-oblong, 5.5–15 cm long, 2–5 cm wide, acute or ± longly acuminate at apex, obtuse to rounded at base; upper surface glabrous except on midvein ± pubescent, lower surface ± sparsely appressed-pubescent. Lateral veins 7–12 pairs, slightly prominent on lower surface.

Flowers whitish-yellow, solitary, rarely 2–3 fasciculate, subsessile or with pedicel not exceeding 5 mm in length; pedicel appressed-pubescent reddish-brown, bearing 2–5 ovate-lanceolate to semi-orbicular bracteoles, densely pubescent, reddish on exterior, glabrous on interior. Sepals green, triangular-ovate, 3–4 mm long, 2.5–3 mm wide, fused at base, obtuse or apiculate at apex, appressed-pubescent on exterior, glabrous on interior. Outer petals narrowly linear, grooved, enlarged and concave at base, 2–5 cm long, 3–4 mm wide, silky tomentellous on exterior, puberulent to glabrous on interior; inner petals shorter and narrower, enlarged and unguiculate at base reddish-brown, puberulent on both faces except at internal base. External staminodes oblong, 1.5 mm long, internal ones shorter. Stamens narrowly linear, 2 mm long, inserted on a crenulate receptacular extension surrounding the ovaries; anthers reddish-purple; connectives capitate, papillose. Carpels 5–13, 5 mm long; ovaries ovoid-cylindrical, 1.5 mm long, hairy; styles white, twice as long as ovaries, linear, terminated by a tuft of hairs. 8–10 uniseriate ovules.

Fruits with pedicel thick, 0.5–1 cm long; mericarps numerous, green externally, cylindric-oblong, 2–8 cm long, 0.8–1 cm wide, not apiculate, glabrescent, ± rugulose, obliquely constricted between seeds; stipes 0.7–1.3 cm long, glabrous. Seeds ellipsoid, reddish-brown, about 1 cm long, uniseriate; aril inconspicuous.

Type from Sierra Leone (holotype in herb. Dunal, MPU?; isotype in herb. DC., G!).

Species very widely distributed in Africa, from Sierra Leone to Sudan, and southward to Northern Rhodesia and Angola. It is common in periodically flooded and degraded forest, in forest galleries, and on river banks.

The wood is used in the making of paddles and the bark is used to make the walls of huts.
