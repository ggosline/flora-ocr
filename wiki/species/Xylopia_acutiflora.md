Shrub or small tree reaching 15 m in height and 60 cm in girth; bark smooth, reddish-brown. Branches reddish-brown, smooth or ± rugose, lenticellate, glabrous; branchlets at first pubescent with spreading hairs, then glabrescent. Leaves with petiole 2–5 mm long, appressed-pubescent to glabrescent; lamina papyraceous to subcoriaceous, elliptic-oblong to ovate-oblong, 5.5–15 cm long, 2–5 cm wide, acute or ± longly acuminate at apex, obtuse to rounded at base; upper surface glabrous except on the midvein ± pubescent, lower surface ± sparsely appressed-pubescent. Lateral nerves 7–12 pairs, little prominent on the lower surface.

Flowers pale yellowish-white, solitary, rarely 2–3 fascicled, subsessile or with pedicel not exceeding 5 mm in length; pedicel appressed-pubescent reddish-brown, bearing 2–5 bracts that are ovate-lanceolate to semi-orbicular, densely pubescent, reddish-brown on the outside, glabrous on the inside. Sepals green, triangular-ovate, 3–4 mm long, 2.5–3 mm wide, united at base, obtuse or apiculate at apex, appressed-pubescent on the outside, glabrous on the inside. Outer petals narrowly linear, grooved, enlarged and concave at base, 2–5 cm long, 3–4 mm wide, silky tomentellous on the outside, puberulent to glabrous on the inside; inner petals shorter and narrower, enlarged and ungulate at base reddish-brown, puberulent on both faces except at the inner base. External staminodes oblong, 1.5 mm long, inner ones shorter. Stamens narrowly linear, 2 mm long, inserted on a crenate extension of the receptacle surrounding the ovaries; anthers wine-red; connectives capitate, papillose. Carpels 5–13, 5 mm long; ovaries ovoid-cylindrical, 1.5 mm long, villous; styles white, twice as long as the ovaries, linear, terminated by a tuft of hairs. 8–10 ovules in a single series.

Fruits with pedicel thick, 0.5–1 cm long; mericarps numerous, green on the outside, cylindrical-oblong, 2–8 cm long, 0.8–1 cm wide, not apiculate, glabrescent, ± rugose, obliquely constricted between the seeds; stipes 0.7–1.3 cm long, glabrous. Seeds ellipsoid, reddish-brown, approximately 1 cm long, uniseriate; aril invisible.

Types from Sierra Leone (holotype in herb. Dunal, MPU?; isotype in herb. DC., G!).

Species very widely distributed in Africa, from Sierra Leone to Sudan, and southward to Northern Rhodesia and Angola. It is common in periodically flooded and degraded forest, in forest galleries, and at the edge of rivers.

The wood is used in the manufacture of paddles and the bark serves to make the walls of huts.
