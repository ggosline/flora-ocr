## Description

**Habit**: shrub 0.5–6 m tall or small tree 10–15 m, sometimes reaching 20–25 m and 30–40 cm in diameter. Stem straight, slightly fluted at the base. Bark grey to brown, thin and finely cracked, pink on the cut surface. Branches long, with terminal branchlets much-branched toward the outside. Branchlets and twigs glabrous, weakly two-winged along two opposite lines. Internodes striate when young. Foliage abundant.

**Leaves**: petiole glabrous, 3–15 mm long, canaliculate above, wrinkled beneath. Blade glabrous on both faces, bright green above, pale matt green beneath, oblong-elliptic, lanceolate or oblanceolate, 6.5–23 × 2–6(–10.5) cm; base cuneate to rounded; apex obtusely acuminate, mucronate. Midrib concave to prominent on the upper face, prominent and furrowed beneath. Secondary nerves 4–10 pairs, ascending, arcuate, anastomosing 2–4 mm from the blade margin, concave on the upper face, prominent on the lower face. Nervules forming a very loose reticulum, prominent on both faces.

**Inflorescence**: axillary multifloral fascicles. Flower bud globose.

**Flowers**: female, pentamerous, 3.5 × 3 mm, whitish, greenish or rosy. Calyx with sepals fused for 2/3 of their length, broadly ovate, glabrous on both faces, acute at the apex. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent at the middle of the internal face, narrowly ovate, 2 × 1 mm, fleshy on the upper 2/3 of their length, erect, strongly keeled, with verruculose margins. Stamens 10 in two cycles; filament 1.25 mm for the alternipetalous stamens and 1 mm for the others, strongly appressed against the internal face of the petals; anther 0.5 mm long, lateral longitudinal dehiscence slits; connective apiculate at the apex or not. Ovary superior, 0.5 mm long, with 3 locules, strongly adnate to a lobed disc 0.5 mm tall and 1 mm wide. Style conical, lobed, terminating in a trilobed stigma.

**Fruits**: solitary or grouped 2–3, axillary. Calyx accrescrent, red, with 5 slightly acute or obtuse lobes, deep sinuses extending to the base of the calyx. Drupe white or green with a hard mesocarp, black and lignified. Seed solitary, ellipsoid, with oleaginous yellow albumen. Embryo apical, 0.5 mm long.

## Types

LECTOTYPE: *Afzelius s.n.*, Sierra Leone (BM).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Senegal to Kasai. *H. parvifolia* is distributed over a wide area extending from Senegal through Guinea, Nigeria, Cameroon, to the Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Kasai.

**Gabon**: Ogooué-Ivindo (Oyem, Makokou, Bélinga), Ogooué-Lolo (Moanda, Franceville), Ogooué-Maritime (Lambaréné, Mayumba), Haut-Ogooué (Issala, Yao, Imeno).

## Habitat and ecology

Dense humid forest; abundant in dark understory on acid, non-inundated or partially inundated soil. Occurs in gallery forests and even in secondary, heliophilic, low-altitude formations.

## Vernacular names

Vounk (Boulou); Esamoko, Ngale Mesak (Boulou); Barokoul (Djinn); Messemokoué, Messimokoué, Tonso (Yaoundé); M'ban (Ndjolé); Vias Utok (Fang).

## Uses and properties

The hard wood, greyish-pink with fairly fine grain, is used as construction timber. It is also used to make bows, arrows, hunting and fishing traps, and tool handles. The bark is used for wounds of newly circumcised individuals. The fruit is edible.

## Notes

This species shows great variability in leaf and even fruit characters.
