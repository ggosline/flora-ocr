# *Heisteria parvifolia* Smith

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Heisteria]]

## Description

Shrub 0.5–6 m tall or small tree 10–15 m, sometimes reaching 20–25 m and 30–40 cm diameter. Trunk straight, slightly fluted at base. Bark grey to brown, thin and finely cracked, pink in section. Branches long, with terminal branchlets much branched towards the exterior. Branchlets and twigs glabrous, weakly two-winged along two opposite lines. Internodes striate when young. Foliage abundant. Petiole glabrous, 3–15 mm long, channelled above, wrinkled below. Lamina glabrous on both surfaces, bright green above, pale green dull below, oblong elliptic, lanceolate or oblanceolate, 6.5–23 × 2–6(–10.5) cm; base cuneate to rounded; apex acuminate obtuse mucronate. Midvein concave to prominent on upper surface, prominent sulcate below. Secondary nerves 4–10 pairs, ascending, arched, anastomosing 2–4 mm from the leaf margin, concave on upper surface, prominent on lower surface. Tertiary venation in very loose network, prominent on both surfaces.

Inflorescence a multi-flowered axillary fascicle. Floral bud globular. Flower pentamerous female, 3.5 × 3 mm, whitish, greenish or rosy. Calyx with sepals fused for 2/3 of their length, broadly ovate, glabrous on both surfaces and acute at apex. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent in the middle of the inner surface, narrowly ovate, 2 × 1 mm, fleshy in the upper 2/3 of their length, erect, strongly keeled, with verrucose margins. Stamens 10 in two cycles; filament 1.25 mm in alternipetalous stamens and 1 mm in the others, strongly appressed against the inner surface of petals; anther 0.5 mm long, fissures of dehiscence lateral longitudinal; connective apiculate at apex or not. Ovary superior, 0.5 mm long, three-locular, strongly adnate to a lobed disc 0.5 mm high and 1 mm wide. Style conical lobed, terminated by a trilobed stigma.

Fruits solitary or grouped by 2–3, axillary. Calyx accrescing red, 5-lobed, lobes not very acute or obtuse, sinuses deep extending to the base of the calyx. Drupe white or green with hard mesocarp, black and lignified. Seed solitary, ellipsoid, with oleaginous yellow albumen. Embryo apical, 0.5 mm long.

## Distribution

**Range**: West and Central African, from Senegal to Kasai.

**Cameroon**: Collected at Bipindi, near Mount Cameroun, along the Sanaga River, Eseka, Doumé, Deng Deng, Bertoua, Yaoundé, Mbalmayo, Kribi, Azem, Ndikiniméki, Gounso, Masok, Batouri, Yokadouma, Moloundou, Ebandé, Nanga Eboko, Yoko, Bétaré Oya, the Dja valley, Mopwo, and Moloundou.

**Gabon**: Lambaréné, Moanda, Franceville, Ndjolé, Oyem, Makokou, Bélinga, Booué, Mont Mela, Ipassa, Mayumba, Issala, Tsamba, Yao, Imeno, Franceville, the Ogooué rapids, and the Alima tributary.

## Habitat and ecology

Dense humid forest plant. Abundant in dark understory on acidic soil not inundated or partially inundated. Found in gallery forests and even in low heliophilous secondary formations.

## Vernacular names

Cameroon: vounk (Bobili); esamoko, ngale mesak (Boulou); barokoul (Djinn); messemokoué, messimokoué, tonso (Yaoundé); m'ban (Ndjolé); vias utok (Fang).

## Uses

The hard, greyish-pink wood with fairly fine grain is used for construction. It is also made into bows, arrows, traps for hunting and fishing, and tool handles. The bark is used for the wounds of newly circumcised individuals. The fruit is edible.
