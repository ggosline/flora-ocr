# *Mitragyna ciliata* Aubr. et Pellegr.

**Genus**: [[Mitragyna]]
**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Aubr. et Pellegr., Bull. S. B. Fr. 83 : 36 (1936)

## Description

Large tree 20-35 m tall, with diameter very rarely reaching 0.80 to 1 m, with bole 12-15(20) m, cylindrical, straight and without buttressing. Crown reduced, narrow, with few and tortuous branches, with dense foliage. Bark fissured, scaly, with yellow-pink fractured surface becoming red or violet by oxidation, very fibrous, 2-3 cm thick. Glabrous branchlet tips with subquadrangular internodes. Large oval stipules 3-8 cm long by 2.5-5 cm wide, somewhat bulging to subcarinate at the base, often glabrous or pubescent only near the base, very caducous on the flowering axes. Petiole 1.5-3(6) cm, glabrous, sometimes finely puberulent on the lower face. Limb rusty-coloured when dry, broadly elliptic or obovate, 9-32 ± 6-24 cm, glabrous, sometimes finely puberulent on the lower face; base rounded to subcuneate, rarely subcordate; apex rounded exhibiting a bifurcation of the midvein, ± 1 cm below the tip. 5-10 pairs of lateral veins with small axillary pubescent domatia on the lower face.

Terminal compound inflorescences. At the level of the last pair of developed leaves, 3 glabrescent peduncles 5-18 cm long are ordinarily grouped, bearing a pair of reduced leaves 3-6 cm long, and a group of fairly numerous ramifications sometimes disposed in pseudoumbels, sometimes ± scattered over short internodes. These ramifications are the elementary inflorescences, with a slender finely puberulent peduncle 2-5.5 cm, with a spherical capitulum of approx. 40-70 flowers, 7-12 mm in diameter and approx. 2.5 cm during anthesis. Flowers light yellow-green, nectariferous and very fragrant, inserted on a hirsute receptacle 3-4 mm in diameter and surrounded by very numerous cuneiform bracts 3-4 mm long, narrow and glabrous toward the base, dilated truncate and pubescent at the apex. Reddish glabrous ovary reaching with the calyx 3-4 mm high. 5 short ciliate calyx lobes, concealed by the bracteoles. Corolla 3-4 mm long with glabrous tube, with 5 valvate erect, yellowish lobes, prolonged or not by a small point, pubescent on the outside and also somewhat on the inner face. 5 sagittate stamens remaining concealed; at anthesis, all the pollen is driven outward by the stigma. White style exceeding the corolla by 2-3 mm, with a channelled stigma of approx. 1 × 1 mm. Ovary with 2 locules.

Fusiform fruits reaching 8 mm long on the capitulum, and exceeding by 2-3 mm the apex of the bracteoles. Persistent calyx with ciliate lobes bent inward. Numerous small seeds 1.5-? mm, oblong, unequally surrounded by an angular winged margin.

Type from Côte-d'Ivoire, Aubréville 877 (P!).

This species remained for a long time confused with M. stipulosa under the name M. macrophylla; its morphological differences are slight but well constant.

M. ciliata is a gregarious species of equatorial dense forest, which can be encountered in abundance in certain swampy depressions, at the edge of estuary or lagoon; the roots of these trees sometimes produce remarkable pneumatophores. These Mitragyna manage, despite the strangling grip of invasive plants that smother young trees, to dominate the exuberant species with rapid growth such as Macaranga, Parasoliers etc.; the species is distributed from Liberia to western Congo in the most humid forested regions.

Despite its generally modest diameter, and the susceptibility of its logs to rot, the wood of this species is interesting and is appreciated in various uses (joinery, peeling, canoes, paper pulp); it is light, greyish-pink or light brown, with fine grain, and resists insects well. Rational exploitation of this species requires antifungal protection (D. Normand).

The bark is febrifuge and is used in the composition of a remedy against angina pectoris.

Names: Tilleul d'Afrique (old settlers); Bahia (commercial name originating from Côte d'Ivoire); Abura (commercial name originating from Nigeria); Mouvougou (Bapounou); Mvuku-masi, Mvoukou (Bavili of Loango); Tobou (Eshira, Bavarama, Bavungu, Bapounou, Balumbu, Masangu); Epoukou (Mitsogho); Ivuku, Mbudi (Benzabi); Tobo (Baviya, Ngowé, Ivéa, Bavové); Moupoupougou (Bavili of Sindara, Mindumu); Mutumbiri (Baduma); Bélombé (Bakélé); Elélom, Elelom-nzame, Otsigué-nzam (Fang); Ntowo (Mopngwé, Galoa, Orungu, Nkomi); Guépoko-poko, Guéfuku (Apindji); Elombo (Benga); Usupu (Béséki); Supu Tobé (Bakota).
