# *Ongokea gore* (Hua) Pierre

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Ongokea]]

Bull. Soc. Linn. Paris 2: 1314 (1897).

## Synonymy

- *Aptandra gore* Hua, Bull. Mus. Hist. Nat. Paris 1: 315 (1895)
- *Ongokea klaineana* Pierre
- *Ongokea kamerunensis* Engl., Bot. Jahrb. 43: 163 (1909)
- *Schæpfianthus zenkeri* Engl. ex De Wild., Miss. Laur.: 337, nom. nud. (1907)

## Description

Dioecious tree, 8–40 m tall, with cylindrical and straight bole, without buttresses, sometimes lobed at the base. Crown narrow. Rhytidome grey to reddish-brown, cracked, detaching in small plaques on old trees. Bark thick, 1–2 cm, pulverizing on cutting, yellowish to ochre in cross-section becoming reddish-brown on drying, detaching from the sapwood by a surface proliferating in the wood by longitudinal ridges. Twigs 2-winged, compressed, grey or greenish. Petiole glabrous, 5–10 mm long, channelled above and ridged below. Lamina glabrous on both faces, dark green, slightly glossy on upper face, paler, matt green on lower face, elliptic, 4–11 × 2–5 cm; base cuneate or narrowed, apex shortly bluntly acuminate. Midvein concave above, strongly prominent below, striated. Secondary veins 6–10 pairs, ascending, slightly or not arched, anastomosing 1–2 mm from the lamina margin, effaced or slightly prominent on upper face, slightly prominent on lower face. Tertiary veins in a very lax, slightly prominent network on the lower face.

Inflorescence in a panicle of corymbs or umbels, many-flowered, axillary (axillant leaf quickly caducous). Inflorescence rachis 5–15 cm long, lateral branches of the inflorescence 1–3 cm long; umbel peduncle 0.5–0.7 cm long, in the axil of a large bract. Floral bud green, cylindric (sometimes square in cross-section), swollen at the apex, 1–2.5 mm long. Flower tetramerous, yellow or green. Calyx cupuliform with 4 glabrous sepals on both faces, fused at the base. Petals linear, glabrous on both faces, valvate with linear claw, reflexed and rolled, c. 3 × 0.5 mm; apex acute. Glands 4, alternipetalous, fleshy, yellow-green. Stamens 4, epipetalous, fused into a tube by the filaments and anthers; stamen tube 3 mm long; anthers 2-lobed, opening by annular dehiscence slits, flaps folding back toward the base of the lobes. Ovary superior, glabrous, unilocular with 1–2 pendulous ovules from an erect central placenta. Style slender, 2.5 mm long, terminated by a pointed stigma.

Fruit solitary, enclosed in an accrescent calyx surrounding the drupe almost entirely except at the apparent apex. Calyx opening by 2–3 valves. Exocarp smooth, yellow, areolate at the apex. Mesocarp fleshy, covering the endocarp except at the apex. Endocarp hard, 2 mm thick, striated longitudinally. Seed solitary, globose or ovoid, yellow. Albumen white, oily. Embryo apical or subapical, elongated.

## Distribution

**Range**: West Africa to Zaïre, extending from Côte d'Ivoire to the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

**Cameroon**: widespread localities including Douala region, Yaoundé, Bertoua, Kribi regions.

**Gabon**: Libreville region, Makokou (Ipassa), Tchibanga, Lastoursville, Poungi Essouma, Pera.

## Habitat and ecology

Dense evergreen forest, particularly inundated forests. Flowers December to June; fruits mature from January onwards.

## Uses and properties

The wood is yellowish, hard and homogeneous, used for construction, joinery and carpentry. The bark contains 16% catechic tannin; used as a purgative or to facilitate childbirth. The fruit contains 60% of a dichroic, drying oil with a fishy odor (potentially usable like linseed oil). Children use it as a spinning top. The seed is edible when fresh; serves as bait for hunting porcupines and palm rats.

## Notes

The holotype is cited as Dybowski 103 from Achouka, Central African Republic (deposited in P), but the species' main range documented by this treatment is West African, from Côte d'Ivoire through Cameroon and Gabon to Zaïre. This reference collection lies outside the Lower Guinea region but documents the species' wider distribution.
