# *Paropsiopsis decandra* (Baill.) Sleumer Planche 36

**Genus**: [[Paropsiopsis]]
**Family**: [[Passifloraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. Natl. Belg. 40 : 74 (1970)

## Synonyms

- Smeathmannia decandra Baill., Bull. Mens. Soc. Linn. Paris 1 : 304 (1882).

## Description

Small tree 3–6 m, or shrub; branchlets tomentose-ferruginous, plagiotropic. Stipules: glands 2 mm in length, flattened at apex. Leaf with petiole 1–7 mm; lamina narrowly oblong to elliptic or ovate, 2–3.5 times longer than wide, 7–24 × 4.5–7 cm, sometimes gradually diminishing towards the branch apex, apex acuminate, glabrous above except on the midvein, densely pubescent to tomentose and ferruginous below. Inflorescence with axillary flowers, solitary, often with 1 or 2 dormant buds and a few bracts at the base. Flower: sepals 4–5(–6), lanceolate-oblong, 20–25 × 5–8 mm, apex acuminate, membranous, pale green or white becoming brownish when dry; petals 4–5(–6), white to yellowish, becoming reddish-brown when dry; outer corona thin, 2–3 mm in height and enveloping the inner one which is fleshy, annular and 1 mm thick; stamens 7–11, adnate at the base, filaments 5–6 mm, anthers approximately 3 mm in length; ovary with (3–)4–6(–7) placentas, ovoid, 2.5–3 mm in diameter, pubescent; styles (3–)4–6(–7), stigmas sub-capitate. Fruit globose, approximately 2.5 × 2 cm, with thin pericarp. Seeds oval to ellipsoid, ± 5 × 2.5 mm, slightly flattened, finely tuberculate with approximately 10 pits on an imaginary meridional line.

## Distribution

from south-western Cameroon to Gabon, Angola (Cabinda) and western Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, common in the coastal zone (Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime and Nyanga)

## Ecology

primary and secondary forests, forest margins, distinctly distant from the coast; in Gabon and elsewhere at low altitude (up to 100 m). Flowers observed from September to March. Taxonomic note: The leaves vary greatly in size. Several previously recognized species have been placed in synonymy by De Vos & Breteler (2009). Only one other species known from the region (southern Cameroon) is P. atrichogyna J.M.de Vos & Breteler, with a glabrous ovary and filaments adnate to the ovary. b: De Vos & Breteler (2009), Sleumer (1976).
