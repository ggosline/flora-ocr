# *Tabernaemontana brachypoda* K.Schum. Figure 12(E)

**Genus**: [[Tabernaemontana]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 223 (1896)

## Description

Gabunia brachypoda (K.Schum.) Stapf, Fl. trop Afr. 4(1) : 137 (1902). Gabunia latifolia Stapf, Fl. trop Afr. 4(1) : 137 (1902). Tabernaemontana latifolia (Stapf) Pichon, Not. Syst. (Paris) 13 : 253 (1948). Liane with ramifications glabrous and smooth. Leaves of a pair equal or unequal, glabrous; petiole 4–20 mm; ochreae generally scarcely visible; blade broadly to narrowly elliptic to obovate, 5–18 × 2–8 cm, cuneate and decurrent on the petiole at the base, acuminate at the apex, with numerous tiny black dots scattered beneath; lateral veins in 5 to 8 pairs, generally scarcely visible. Inflorescences often in pairs, non-ramified, condensed, with 4–8 flowers; peduncle null or up to 2 mm, glabrous. Flower fragrant; bracteoles less than 1 mm long; pedicel 4–12 mm, glabrous; calyx with lobes 1.5–2 × 1–1.5 mm, fringed on the margin, without colleters on the inside; corolla completely white or greenish near the base, in the mature bud with rounded head, broadly ovoid or subglobular, sometimes the length of the bud; tube subcylindric, long 3.2–5 cm, wide 1.5–1.8 mm, often twisted especially near the base, reflexed at the level of the anthers and widening near the throat; the exterior glabrous or nearly so, the interior sparsely hairy, with lobes ± 2 cm × 3–5 mm; stamens with sessile anther, narrowly triangular, long 4–5 mm. Fruit pulp with distinct carpels up to 3.5 × 2.5 mm, yellow or orange, smooth, with aliform crests, with several seeds. Seed with shallow longitudinal grooves, with a minute honeycomb-like structure. Distribution Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Rio Muni, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola. Cabinda in Gabon, Ngouniée, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology Primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 750 m altitude, elsewhere up to 600 m.

## Source note

In the revision of Tabernaemontana (Leeuwenberg 1991), T. brachypoda is treated as a synonym of T. eglandulosa. Better knowledge of the fruits of T. eglandulosa s.l. has made comprehensible the re-establishment of T. brachypoda as a distinct species. B : Boiteau & Allorge (1981), Stapf (1902). BIBLIOGRAPHY
