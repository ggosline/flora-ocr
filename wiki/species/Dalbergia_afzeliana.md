# *Dalbergia afzeliana* G.Don var. afzeliana

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Gen. Syst. 2 : 375 (1832)

## Description

Liane or bushy shrub, sometimes defoliate at fruiting, reaching 5 m in height or 10 m in length and 15 cm in diameter at base, inodorous, generally without spines (rarely with a few spines on old stems), often bearing hook-like prehensile branchlets; bark dark brown to dark grey, with pale lenticels; young branchlets with short appressed pubescence (very rarely accompanied by long glandular hairs with swollen base, see note). Stipules very early caducous, falciform, 3–3.5 × 1–1.2 mm, with obtuse apex, with short appressed pubescence. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.5–1.2 cm; rachis 2.6–11 cm, very shortly pubescent; leaflets (9–)13 to 27, alternate and slightly unequal, with very short petiolule, ± 1 mm, limb elliptic to slightly obovate, symmetric, 0.5–1.6(–2.4) × 0.4–1 cm in upper leaflets and 0.3–1.7 × 0.2–0.7 cm in lower ones, rounded or obtuse at base, rounded or emarginate at apex, coriaceous, olive-green to olive-brown when dry with the lower face slightly glaucescent in adult leaves, with very short sparse appressed hairs on both faces, then rapidly glabrescent on the upper face; lateral veins barely distinct, veinlets invisible or almost so. Inflorescence in panicle often leafy at base, terminal or rarely axillary, (2–)4–25 cm in length, shortly pubescent; brac-

teoles early caducous, broadly elliptic, ± 1.2 × 1 mm, acute, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 0–1.5 mm; bracteoles caducous generally after flowering, elliptic to orbicular, 1.2 × 0.5–1.2 mm, pubescent; calyx pale green, with golden-brown appressed hairs (or rarely blackish) on the lobes and sometimes also on the tube, the latter narrow and slightly curved, 3–4 × 2–2.3 mm, becoming slightly ribbed when dry, teeth imbricate, 1.2–2.5 mm in length, equal or the lower one somewhat longer, the upper ones fused and rounded, the lateral ones rounded or acute, the lower one acute or rarely obtuse; corolla white or pale yellow, petals with claw shorter than calyx, standard strongly bent and folded lengthwise, with claw 1.5–2 mm and obcordate to elliptic limb 4.5–7 × 4–5 mm, rounded at base and distinctly bilobed at apex, wings exceeding the keel and concealing it largely, with claw 2 mm and very asymmetric obovate limb 4.2–6.5 × 2.5–3 mm, transversely wrinkled towards the base of the outer face and bearing a distinct basal auricle in hook shape, keel strongly curved, with claw 1.8–2.5 mm and limb 3–4.5 × 2–3 mm, with basal auricle in hook shape; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, with filaments 5.5–6 mm, fused on ⅔ of their length, ± straight at base and curved in their free part; ovary 1.7–2 × 0.8–1 mm, with dense appressed hairs, with 3 ovules, with curved stipe 2.7–3 mm; style linear, 0.8–1.5 mm, with punctiform stigma. Legume with pedicel 1–2 mm and stipe 6–12 mm, woody with hard wall, flattened and ellipsoid, 8.5–12 × 3.4–4.5 cm, obtuse at both ends, with ± effaced reticulation, glabrous, with a darker central zone around the single seed. Seed ellipsoid, flattened, ± 5.5 × 3 mm.

## Distribution

Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo; widely distributed in Gabon (Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Woleu-Ntem), but appears to be absent in the south-west of the country

## Ecology

secondary forests and regrowth, river banks, forest galleries and low forests on inselbergs; in Gabon up to 730 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 800 m. In Gabon, flowering from May to August and fruits from September to January. Use: In Côte d'Ivoire, the plant is used to treat leprosy

## Source note

The species comprises two varieties, of which only the typical variety is present in Gabon; var. parvifolia Cronquist is limited to the Democratic Republic of the Congo and differs by its smaller leaflets. D. congensis Baker f., described from Cabinda (Angola), is most probably a synonym of D. afzeliana; but this remains to be confirmed by examination of its type. Pellegrin (1949) separated the two species based on calyx pubescence and leaflet width; the study of more complete material shows that these characters are without value. A Gabonese specimen of D. afzeliana, Breteler et al. 13280, is atypical by the presence of long glandular hairs with swollen base on young branchlets; these hairs are similar to those of D. fischeri Taub., a related species from East Africa.
