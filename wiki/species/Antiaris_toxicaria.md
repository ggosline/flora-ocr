# *Antiaris toxicaria* Leschenault

**Genus**: [[Antiaris]]
**Family**: [[Moraceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Mus. Hist. Nat. Paris 16 : 478 (1810) ; HIERN, Cat. Welw. Afr. Pl. 4 : 1020 (1900) ; BERG, BJBB 47 : 310 (1977) ; 48 : 466 (1978)

## Description

combines A. usambarensis and A. welwitschii and reduces the latter to A. toxicaria var. welwitschii. It considers A. africana as a distinct species.

However, all representatives of Antiaris on the African continent appear as a single infra-specific taxon within A. toxicaria, a taxon for which the rank of subspecies seems to be the most appropriate.

subsp. welwitschii (Engler) C.C. Berg

– Antiaris welwitschii ENGL., Bot. Jahrb. 33 : 118 (1902).

Trees reaching 40 (–60) m in height, with or without buttresses; latex white or yellowish, turning pinkish or brownish; small leafy branchlets 1.5–5 cm thick, ± wrinkled at least when dry, with an epidermis generally desquamating in small scales, puberulent with erect or appressed yellowish to brown hairs, hirtellous, subtomentulose to hirsute, or strigulose. Leaf blade oblong to elliptic, or sometimes lanceolate, generally wider at the middle or above, ± asymmetric, (2–) 6–17 (–22) × (1.5–) 3–12 cm, subcoriaceous, shortly acuminate to apiculate, obtuse or subacute; base (subacute to) obtuse to (sub)cordate; margin subentire to denticulate toward the apex; upper surface scabrous to slightly scabrous or smooth, the midvein, especially its base, ± sparsely puberulent to hirtellous (to subtomentulose); lower surface sparsely puberulent with appressed hairs, to almost glabrous, or puberulent with erect to hirtellous to hirsute or strigulose hairs and often ± scabrous; veins slightly prominent to plane above, ± prominent below, the veinlets weakly or distinctly prominent, (5–) 7–14 pairs of secondary veins; tertiary veins often partially parallel. Petioles 3–9 mm long, with sparse to dense indumentum. Stipules caducous, 3–10 (–15) mm long, puberulent to pubescent to hirtellous to hirsute.

Inflorescences at the axil of leaves or on small (already) leafy branchlets, on short branchlets bearing bracteiform stipules; 1–2 ♀ inflorescences (on the lower nodes) and/or up to 8 ♂ inflorescences approx. ♂ inflorescences 16–12 (–20) mm in diameter, most often ± lobed, the margins deflexed at anthesis. Peduncle (4–) 6–14 (–18) mm long, densely puberulent to shortly velutinous, sometimes provided with bracts. Receptacle puberulent to shortly velutinous, bearing on its margin in 1 to 3 rows numerous (sub)ovate to oblong to elliptic, imbricate bracts, reaching 1 mm in length and lower down on the peduncle, some scattered bracts. Flowers several to numerous; perianth 1–1.5 (17) mm tall, with (2–) 3–5 (–7) free tepals, ± spatulate, flat, cucullate or subpeltate, puberulent; stamens (2) 3, 4, with filaments 0.2–0.4 mm long and anthers 0.8–1.8 × 0.4–0.7 mm, with rather narrow connective, apiculate or not. ♀ inflorescences 3–4 mm tall, approx. 3 mm in diameter, ellipsoid (to turbinate), sessile to pedunculate. Peduncle reaching 3 (or 6) mm in length, densely puberulent to shortly velutinous. Receptacle bearing up to 12, most often approx. 8, imbricate, reniform to ovate, puberulent bracts, arranged in several rows. Flower ♀ single, rarely 2; free part of perianth approx. 2 mm tall, quadripartite, puberulent; style reaching 1 mm in length; stigmas filiform to ribbon-like, (2–) 5–7 (–10) mm long, shortly puberulent.

Infructescences ellipsoid, sometimes almost ovoid or globose, 1–1.5 cm long, 0.8–1 cm in diameter, shortly or longly apiculate; receptacle fleshy, orange to scarlet, shortly brownish-velutinous (with inflated hairs, with scattered bracts); peduncle 3–12 mm long; pyrene reaching approx. 1 mm in length with crustaceous endocarp. Seed reaching 7–9 mm in length, with testa fused to the endocarp (at least on dry material), fairly finely vascularized sparsely in a suborbicular to oblong zone, not distinctly thickened, below the hilum; embryo often marked by the thickened part of the testa and the vascular bundles arising from it; cotyledons equal or nearly so.

## Discussion

In young specimens, small leafy branchlets hirtellous, subtomentulose, hirsute or (sub)hispid. Leaf blade often oblong to lanceolate, chartaceous to subcoriaceous (to coriaceous); upper surface hirsute to (sub)hispid, generally slightly scabrous because of persistent hair bases, sometimes almost glabrous except on the main veins, hirsute; lower surface hirsute to subtomentulose or (sub)hispid;

margin generally distinctly dentate; apex longly acuminate to subacute; veins, especially the main ones, prominent below.

A. toxicaria subsp. welwitschii can be subdivided into var. welwitschii, var. africana A. CHEV., and var. usambarensis (ENGL.) C.C. BERG. Var. africana inhabits drier forests and can be frequently encountered in forests bordering savannas and in forest patches within savannas, near rocks. This variety seems to be rare in Cameroon and absent in Gabon. Var. welwitschii can be found in humid forests. Since representatives of the genus Antiaris require light for their growth (cf. AUBRÉVILLE, FFCI, ed. 2, 1, 1959; VOORHOEVE, Liberian high forest trees, 1965), its presence in areas of dense evergreen forests seems to be due to accidental clearings. The 2 varieties are distinguished mainly by differences in the indumentum and venation of the leaves. But intermediate forms can often be found, especially when the distribution areas of these 2 varieties approach each other. Juvenile specimens cannot be distinguished.
