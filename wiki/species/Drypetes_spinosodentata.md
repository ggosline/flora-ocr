# *Drypetes spinosodentata* (Pax) Hutch.

**Genus**: [[Drypetes]]
**Family**: [[Putranjivaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr: 6(1): 688 (1912)

## Synonyms

- Cyclostemon spinosodentatus Pax, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 43(1-2): 76 (1909).

## Description

Shrub or tree reaching 15(–25) m in height; trunk up to 40 cm in diameter; bark pale brown; cut surface pale orange, without mustard oil odor; twigs 1.5 mm in diameter, fluted, glabrous; axillary buds 2.5 × 1.4, glabrous except for marginal cilia 0.2 mm. Stipules very quickly caducous, size unknown. Leaf: petiole 5–8 mm, canaliculate above, plicate when dry, glabrous; blade elliptic to narrowly elliptic or sometimes oblong, 14–30 × 4.3–8 cm, coriaceous, glossy above when fresh, glabrous, oblique at base, one side cuneate and the other rounded, abruptly acuminate for 2.1 cm at apex, with slightly recurved margin, bearing teeth spaced 1.4–2.2 cm apart and 1.5–3 mm long, spiny section of tooth 0.5–1 mm, directed forward; midvein prominent below, secondary veins 6 to 10, impressed above, raised below, tertiary and quaternary veins slightly raised on both faces, rather obscure. Male inflorescence ramiflorous, on cushions 1.5 × 0.5 cm, with numerous flowers on each cushion; bracts persistent, 2 × 0.5 mm, glabrous, becoming coriaceous and glossy after flower fall. Male flower with sweet odor; pedicel robust, 2.5–5 mm, glabrous; sepals 5, imbricate, suborbicular, 5–6 × 4.5 mm, yellowish green, very slightly wrinkled on outside, glabrous on inside; stamens 26 to 28, peripheral with respect to the disk, slightly surrounded by marginal lobes, filament 1.4–1.6 mm, glabrous, anther 2.8 × 1.3 mm, rounded at apex; disk plane to slightly concave, 4 mm in diameter, divided by very low ridges to form a reticulate pattern of 7 or 8 plates, very shortly papillose. Female flower unknown. Fruit globulous, 3.1–3.3 × 3.0–3.4 cm, orange, with sepals and stigma caducous; pedicel 3–4 mm.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, continental Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

Habitat: evergreen forests, apparently only on terra firme; in Gabon and elsewhere at 150–1000 m altitude.

## Source note

This species is easily identifiable by its glabrous coriaceous leaves with spiny teeth that give the species its name, however, the teeth are not always evident on certain specimens
