# *Fimbristylis cymosa* R.Br. Planche 90

**Genus**: [[Fimbristylis]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. : 228 (1810)

## Description

F. obtusifolia (Lam.) Kunth, Enum. pl. 2 : 240 (1837). Perennial herb forming tufts, with a short rhizome and numerous basal leaves; usually several stems 10–50(–90) cm long, rounded or triangular, smooth. Leaf: blade flat and thick, 3–25 cm × 1–4 mm, terminating abruptly in a rounded or truncate apex, with a short mucro, margin strongly scabrous at least near the tip. Inflorescence usually in a loose or compact compound anthela, with numerous small pedunculate glomerules of sessile spikelets, sometimes reduced to a spherical head of fairly tightly packed spikelets; involucral bracts poorly developed, shorter than the sessile groups of spikelets they subtend. Spikelet ovate-ellipsoid, 3–5 × 1–2 mm, pale brown, usually obtuse; glumes 1.3–2 mm long, apex rounded with prominent midvein, pale brown to yellowish-brown often with a broad transparent margin. Flower: style with 2 or 3 branches. Achene obovoid, biconvex to ± trigonous, 0.6–0.9 × 0.5–0.7 mm, generally rugose with cells in longitudinal rows, dark brown at maturity.

## Distribution

plant widespread on almost all coastlines of tropical regions; in Gabon, widely distributed in the Estuary and Ogooué-Maritime (8 collections)

## Ecology

sandy beaches, grassy areas on sandy soil, and salt marshes; near sea level only. Taxonomic note: recognized by its pedunculate glomerules of spikelets and by the obtuse glumes and blades with truncate apex terminating in a short mucro. The other African taxa with fascicles of spikelets are F. complanata and sometimes F. ferruginea. In the past, plants with a trifid style were named F. obtusifolia, while those with a bifid style were named F. cymosa. A plant bearing both flowers with 2-branched styles and flowers with 3-branched styles was found in Gabon. Bibliography: Goetghebeur & Coudijzer (1984a), Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1995, 1997)
