# *Paepalanthus lamarckii* Kunth

**Genus**: [[Paepalanthus]]
**Family**: [[Eriocaulaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. pl. 3 : 506 (1841)

## Description

Eriocaulon lamarckii (Kunth) Steud., Syn. pl. glumac. 2 : 276 (1855). Dupatya lamarckii (Kunth) Kuntze, Revis. gen. pl. 2 : 746 (1891).

Small herb, developing over time a rigid unbranched stem, surrounded by old leaves and fibrous roots, the new ones arranged in a loose rosette at the apex of the stem. Leaves linear to lanceolate, 2–3 cm × 1–3 mm, subacute and indurate at the apex, spongy, sparsely pilose to glabrescent. Inflorescence: scapes up to about thirty, gathered toward the apex of the stem, 2–7 cm, villous, 3-sulcate; sheath shorter than the leaves; capitulum subglobose, 3–4 mm in diameter, with an intrusive base at maturity, grey, densely villous, the young central flowers entirely hidden by white hairs; involucral bracts lanceolate to lanceolate-oblong, 1–1.2 mm long, acute, membranous and firm, greyish-brown, with a lighter central band, margin and external face bearing coarse villous spreading hairs; floral bracts angulate-obovate, dark grey with a lighter central band, coarsely villous pilosity above the midpoint. Flower 0.8–1 mm long. Male flower: sepals oblong-spatulate, concave, dark grey with a lighter central band, densely pilose at the subacute apex; petal tube borne on a stipe; rudimentary gynoecium formed of three elongate glands. Female flower: sepals at first resembling those of the male flower, subsequently indurate and curved, pushing the ovary above the petals; petals narrowly oblong-spatulate, equalling the sepals, hyaline, sparsely pilose. Seed ellipsoid, 0.3 mm long, light brown.

## Distribution

from Senegal to Liberia, the Republic of the Congo, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Tanzania (I. Mafia) and Madagascar; widely distributed in tropical America, from Belize to Brazil and the West Indies; in Gabon, known from the Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

humid soil near the sea; at low altitude. Taxonomic notes: This small species has not been found again in Gabon since a collection from Cape Lopez, at Akosso in 1894. It occurs sporadically across Africa in open humid stations on sand; it is probably under-collected. It can be distinguished immediately from Eriocaulon of comparable size by the strongly pilose scapes, the weakly elongate stem and the absence of septa in the leaves. The seed dispersal type is also different. The indurate, white and grey striated sepals of the female flowers curve outward, thus lifting the petals and ovary sheltered above the surface of the capitulum where they fall. The trilobed, empty calyces with a central cavity, remaining attached to the capitulum, are also another character allowing easy distinction of mature specimens from Eriocaulon, without the need for dissection. Bibliography: Lecomte (1908, 1909), Meikle (1968), Milne-Redhead (1949), Phillips (1997a, 2010)
