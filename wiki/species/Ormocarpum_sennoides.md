# *Ormocarpum sennoides* (Willd.) DC. subsp. hispidum (Willd.) Brenan & J.Léonard

**Genus**: [[Ormocarpum]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 24 : 104 (1954)

## Description

O. guineense (Willd.) Hutch. & Dalziel ex Baker f., J. Bot. 66, suppl. 1 : 109 (1928). Shrub 2 m tall; stems erect; branchlets reddish, ± hispid, glandular hairs present or absent. Stipules lanceolate, glabrous or pubescent, striate. Leaf: petiole and rachis pubescent-hispid, 1 and 5–8 cm, respectively; leaflets (7–)13 to 23, petiolule 1–2 mm, blade elliptic to oblanceolate, 1–2.5(–5) × 0.4–1.5(–2.5) cm, apex obtuse to emarginate; venation fine and hardly visible. Inflorescence in racemes 1–9(–13) cm long, with 3 to 6 flowers; peduncle hispid; bracts oval, 1 mm. Flower: pedicel 0.5–1 cm; bracteoles oval-lanceolate, 2–3 mm long, at some distance from the calyx; calyx 6–12 mm long, glabrous or bearing glandular hairs; corolla 1–1.5 cm long, white or yellow streaked with purple or red, standard suborbicular, 7–10 mm long, wings obovate-oblong, 8–12 mm long, keel obtuse at the apex, 10–15 mm long; ovary glabrous. Pod with apex produced into a point, with 2 to 6 ellipsoid articles 0.9–1.7 cm × 5–6 mm, attenuate at the extremities, longitudinally striate. Seed ellipsoid, 7 × 4 mm, flattened, reddish.

## Distribution

The most widespread species of Ormocarpum: from Africa to India, Southeast Asia and northern Australia; in Gabon, known from the Estuaire, Ngounié and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

Undergrowth of dry and secondary forests, forest galleries, edge of running water, rapids; in Gabon altitude unknown, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Uses: In Kenya, the leaves are applied against rheumatism and other pains; in Senegal, against headaches. In Asia, the uses are medicinal and ichthyotoxic, also used as a support plant and shade plant

## Source note

The subsp. zanzibaricum Brenan & Gillett is found in East Africa and the subsp. sennoides is found in India. B: Burkill (1995), Gillett (1966), Léonard (1954a, 1954b), Pellegrin (1949), Verdcourt (2000)
