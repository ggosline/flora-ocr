# *Trichoscypha oliveri* Engl. Planche 22(B)

**Genus**: [[Trichoscypha]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 1 : 425 (1881)

## Description

Tree 3–10 m tall or sometimes a large tree; branchlets densely puberulent with appressed hairs. Leaf up to 70 cm long; petiole ± semi-cylindric, 8–18 cm, with appressed hairs; rachis 9–48 cm, with appressed hairs; leaflets 7 to 17, petiolule 2–16 mm, with oval-elliptic to oblong blade, (7–)14–26(–37) × (3.5–)5–6.5(–12) cm, cuneate to rounded at base, acuminate over 0.5–2 cm at apex, with 12 to 20 pairs of lateral veins, midvein impressed and hairy above, often the lateral veins partly pubescent with scattered appressed hairs to ± glabrous below. Inflorescence terminal, with red hairs, ± dense, ± appressed, the male 5–60 cm long, the female 30–40 cm long. Male flower: pedicel 3–4 mm, with reddish pubescence; calyx with short lobes, ± spreading, ± deltoid, 0.5 mm long, hairy like the pedicel; petals with imbricate prefloration, reflexed, oval, 2.5–3 × 1.5–2 mm, red, glabrous or with scattered hairs on the outside; stamens ± as long as the petals, incurved; disk 2–2.5 mm in diameter, with ± appressed hairs. Female flower: pedicel 4 mm, pubescent; calyx pubescent, with spreading lobes, short, deltoid, 0.5 mm long; petals reflexed, ± 3 × 2.5 mm, red, with scattered appressed hairs on the outside; staminodes small; disk 2–2.5 mm in diameter, with ± appressed hairs; ovary 1–1.5 mm long; styles 3, divergent, 1 mm, pubescent. Fruit oblique, laterally compressed at least when young, ovoid, up to 3 × 2 cm, ± apiculate, brown, hairy to ± glabrous.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni) and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

primary and secondarized forest; in Gabon and elsewhere up to 550 m altitude

## Source note

In the synoptic revision of this genus for Lower Guinea and Congolia (Breteler 2004), this species was treated with synonyms including, among others, T. parviflora Engl. Additional material collected since has greatly improved our knowledge. Indeed, T. parviflora can now be recognized as a distinct species (for diagnostic characters see the key). There are a few collections of Trichoscypha with male flowers that closely resemble those of T. oliveri (especially for the form and hairiness of the disk), but differ from it by other characters, notably the hairiness of the branchlets and leaves, the width of the bracts and bracteoles and the color of the petals. They remain undetermined. Bib: Breteler (2004), Van der Veken (1966). Bibliography
