# *Brugmansia suaveolens* (Willd.) Sweet Planche 29

**Genus**: [[Brugmansia]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hort. suburb. Lond. : 41 (1818)

## Synonyms

- Datura suaveolens Willd., Enum. pl. 1 : 227 (1809).

## Description

Small tree or shrub, up to 6 m tall; branches erect or spreading, glabrous or puberulent. Leaf: petiole 2.4–9 cm, glabrescent to pubescent; blade ovate to elliptic-ovate, 11–24(–28) × (4–)6–14.5 cm, oblique and cuneate at base, acute to acuminate at apex, concolorous, glabrescent to pubescent. Inflorescence with solitary flowers. Flower: pedicel spreading to inclined at maturity, 2–6 cm, up to 7.5 cm in fruit, glabrous; calyx urceolate, swollen, (7–)9–12 cm long, generally glabrescent, sometimes pubescent, with (3–)5 triangular lobes, 1–2.6 cm long, acute at apex; corolla infundibuliform, 16–30(–35) cm long, white, pale green, pink or yellowish, with straight tube, not swollen, 11–15 cm long, with sinuate apex and terminating in 5 narrowly elliptic-ovate teeth, 3–20 mm long; stamens with filament free for 4–5.5 cm, anther 3–4 cm long, yellowish-brown to pale brown; ovary conical to obovate; style 11.8–25.5 cm. Fruit fusiform, 10–22 × ± 3 cm, green. Seed pyramidal or irregular, 8–12 × 5–8 mm, verrucose, but smooth on the ridges.

## Distribution

Native to southeastern Brazil, now widely planted in most tropical and subtropical regions and in greenhouses in botanical gardens. Present throughout sub-Saharan Africa; a single collection for Gabon from Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

often escaped from cultivation and naturalized in forests, near watercourses, along roadsides and in secondary habitats; in Africa at 500–1800 m altitude

## Vernacular names

stramoine en arbre, stramoine odorante (fr.) ; angel star, angel’s tears, angel’s trum pet, night bells, trumpet flower (angl.). Usage : B. suaveolens est planté comme arbre d’ornement dans les jardins publics et privés. En Amérique du Sud, les feuilles sont utilisées dans la médecine traditionnelle et les pratiques culturelles. Bi : Bennett (1992), Bikandu et al. (2020), Edmonds (2012), Heine (1963). B liographie
