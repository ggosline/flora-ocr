# *Strombosia grandifolia* Hooker fil.

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Strombosia]]

*Niger. Fl.* : 258 (1849).

## Synonymy

- *Lavalleopsis grandifolia* (Hook. F.) Van Tiégh., *Bull. Soc. Bot. Fr.* 43: 565 (1896)
- *Lavalleopsis densivenia* Engl., *Notizbl. bot. Gart. Berl.* 2: 287 (1899)
- *Lavalleopsis longifolia* De Wild. & Th. Dur., *C.R. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg.* 38: 186 (1899)

## Description

**Habit**: Tree or shrub 4–20(–30) m tall. Trunk generally 5–6 m high and 10–60(–80) cm in diameter. Bark smooth, slightly rough, grey-brown, reddish-brown or greenish, peeling in oblate plaques. Inner bark thin and rough, longitudinally striated. Crown broad, formed by 3–4 stout, diffusely branched branches. Young branchlets grey-green or light green, weakly longitudinally striated. Old branchlets almost entirely smooth, brownish. Internodes 2–2.5 cm long.

**Leaves**: Petiole glabrous, thick, subcylindrical, canaliculate above, finely striated below, 0.8–3 cm long. Blade papyraceous, glabrous on both faces, dark green or grey-green with shiny upper surface, dull lower surface, oblong-lanceolate, ovate, oblong-elliptic, sometimes elliptic or oblong; margins recurved; base cuneate, obtuse or even rounded; apex acuminate, apiculate and mucronate. Midvein concave above, prominent below. Secondary veins 4–7 pairs, ascending, curved, with inconspicuous anastomoses, concave above, prominent below. Tertiary veins fine, curved, parallel to each other, distant at most 2 mm from one another, anastomosing with each other, weakly prominent on upper face, prominent on lower face, paler below.

**Inflorescence**: Flowers grouped in multiflorous axillary fascicles (5–30 flowers) on a persistent greyish-white cushion formed by bractéoles. Pedicel finely longitudinally striated, 2–4 mm long. Bractéoles ovate, membranous, ciliolate on upper margin.

**Flowers**: Floral bud ovoid to globose, 2 × 1.5–2 mm. Flowers white-yellowish or greenish. Sepals united at base, deltoid, ciliolate on upper margin, apex obtuse. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent on upper third of inner face, valvate, red-veined, oblong-elliptic, 1.5 × 0.5 mm; apex acute, reflexed and papillose. Stamens 5, epipetalous with filiform filaments united along entire length to petals; anthers 2-locular, longitudinally dehiscent with ± lateral slits. Disk forming an indistinct calyptra above the ± pentalobate ovary. Ovary semi-inferior, ovoid or subglobose, 3–4-locular, each locule containing 1 pendulous ovule from an erect central placenta. Style short and grooved, glabrous, stout, terminating in a 3–5-lobed papillose stigma.

**Fruit**: Fruit pedicel strongly accrescent, surrounding the drupe almost entirely except at apex. Apical depression surrounded by remains of 5 sepals and containing style remains. Drupe ovoid or ellipsoid, 1–1.3 × 0.7–2 cm. Exocarp black, granulate. Endocarp hard, obovoid with horny albumen and depressed apex. Embryo solitary, apical, with thick radicle.

**Seedling**: The young seedling presents a characteristic swelling at the base of the hypocotyl. Cotyledonary leaves have a petiole less than 1 cm long.

## Distribution

**Range**: From Benin to Zaïre.

**Nigeria**: Biyé; Bipindi region; Yaoundé.

**Cameroon**: Loum; Douala region; Nanga Eboko; Bertoua region; Masok; Edéa region; Campo region; Kumba (Johann-Albrechtshöhe); Bipindi; Mimfia; Nlonako; Essong; Moloundou region.

**Gabon**: Libreville region; Ogooué River; Lake Ayem region; Tchibanga, Mayumbe forest; Lastoursville; Dibadiba; Nkoulounga.

## Habitat and ecology

Tree of the lower and middle storeys of rainforests. Frequent around lakes and along riverbanks, where it has the aspect of a shrub or small tree. Flowers practically throughout the year, with maximum flowering from December to March and July to August. Occurs up to 1100 m altitude.

## Properties and uses

Wood and sapwood are differentiated. Heartwood is red-violet with fine, very homogeneous grain; sapwood paler and thin. Wood provides good construction material, easy to saw and very durable. Used to manufacture railway sleepers and tool handles.

The species shows large variations in leaf form and dimensions, and fruit size can vary considerably between individual trees.
