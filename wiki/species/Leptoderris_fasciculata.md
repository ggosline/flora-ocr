# *Leptoderris fasciculata* (Benth.) Dunn

**Genus**: [[Leptoderris]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1910 : 390 (1910)

## Description

, syn. nov. L. fasciculata (Benth.) Dunn var. pauciflora (Pellegr.) Pellegr., Fl. Mayombe 1 : 90 (1924), syn. nov.

L. fasciculata (Benth. ) Dunn var. le-testui (Pellegr.) Pellegr., Lég. Gabon : 209 (1949), syn. nov. L. rutshuruensis Hauman, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 24 : 226 (1954). Large liane to lianescent shrub; branchlets puberulent. Stipules deltoid to broadly ovate, ± 2 × 2 mm. Leaf: petiole ± cylindric, 1–18 cm, puberulent; rachis 1–23 cm, puberulent; stipels caducous, ± 1 mm long; leaflets (3–)5 to 7(–9), with puberulent petiolule 3–8(–10) mm, blade obovate-elliptic to oblong or sometimes ovate, (6–)13–17(–20) × (2.5–)5–8(–12) cm, cuneate, rounded-obtuse to subcordate at base, rounded to shortly (on ± 0.5–1 cm) and obtusely acuminate at apex, with 5 or 6 pairs of lateral nerves, puberulent on midrib above, puberulent with brown appressed hairs below. Inflorescence reaching ± 50 cm long, puberulent to tomentellous; bracts and bracteoles narrowly ovate-triangular, 1–1.5 mm long. Flower: pedicel 1–2 mm; calyx turbinate, 4 mm long, lobes ± 1 mm long, puberulent, with shorter hairs outside than inside; corolla 9–11 mm long, white, standard elliptic, with claw ± 1 mm, not auriculate, wings narrowly oblong, as long as keel, with claw 2 mm, distinctly auriculate, glabrous or sparsely pubescent outside, keel petals slightly incurved, obliquely obovate, distinctly auriculate, with claw 2 mm, glabrous or sparsely pubescent outside; stamens as long as keel, anthers 0.5 mm long; pistil with 2 or 3 ovules, with short brown appressed hairs; style sometimes glabrous. Pod apterous, narrowly elliptic to oblong, 6–10 × 2.5–3 cm, puberulent, glabrescent, with 1 or 2 seeds. Seed not seen.

## Distribution

from Senegal in West Africa to central and eastern Africa; in Gabon, known from all provinces except Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

primary and secondarized forest; in Gabon up to 400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1400 m. Use: The plant is toxic to fish. Bibliography: Hauman (1954d), Hepper (1958), Jongkind (2003), Pellegrin (1949), Polhill (1971b).
