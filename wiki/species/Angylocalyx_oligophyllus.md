# *Angylocalyx oligophyllus* (Baker) Baker f. Planche 11.1–9

**Genus**: [[Angylocalyx]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: A. ramiflorus Taub., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 172 (1896)

## Description

in Rendle, Cat. pl. Oban. : 26 (1913). A. ramiflorus Taub., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 23 : 172 (1896). A. claessensii De Wild., Ann. Soc. Sci. Bruxelles 44 : 367 (1924). Shrub or small tree reaching 6 m in height; trunk barely branched. Leaf: pulvinus ± 1 cm long; petiole 5–6 cm; rachis reaching 30 cm; leaflets opposite or alternate, 3 to 7(–9), with blade ovate to oblong-ovate, 9–21 × 6–9 cm, base cuneate to rounded, apex ± long-acuminate; lateral veins in 3 to 5 pairs. Inflorescence cauline, 3–6(–10) cm long, glabrous, with ≥ 20 flowers. Flower: receptacle nearly cylindrical, at the base equal to the cup, then swelling somewhat into a sac 8–12 mm long, striated; calyx white-green with pale pink spots, with cup distinct from the receptacle and forming an angle of approximately 90°, 5–6 mm long; corolla white, with pale pink spots, sometimes violet-mauve, standard ovate, 13–17 mm long, greenish and auriculate at the base; stamens ± 12 mm long; ovary ± 6 mm long, pubescent, with 10 to 12 ovules; style 2 mm. Pod torulose, 11–17 × 1–1.5 cm, with long beak, glabrous, pale to bright yellow-orange, with 5 to 10 seeds; pericarp fleshy. Seed oblong-elliptic, 15–20 × 7–10 mm; hilum apical, furrow 1.5 × 6 mm.

## Distribution

from Ghana to Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

humid forests, primary and secondary; at 20–600 m altitude. Use: The wood is used for poles. The fruit pulp is edible

## Vernacular names

fira (fang)

## Source note

A. schumannianus Taub. is very close to A. oligophyllus, differs from it by its swelling at the base of the receptacle. Yakovlev (1968) distinguishes a var. congolensis Yakovl. very rare, with leaflets having broadly cuneate, straight or cordate blade at the base. In Gabon, all the material has leaflets cuneate at their base, it would then be var. oligophyllus, if one wanted to differentiate the Congolese taxon. b: Povydysh et al. (2010), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Toussaint (1953), Yakovlev (1968)
