# *Carpolobia alba* G.Don Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Carpolobia]]
**Family**: [[Polygalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gen. Hist . 1 : 370 (1831)

## Description

Shrub to small tree reaching up to 5(–10) m in height; branches tomentose, glabrescent or glabrous. Stipules absent or forming a dark glabrous glandular patch. Leaf: petiole (1–)1.5–2.5(–3) mm, pubescent; blade obovate-elliptic to ovate-elliptic, (1.5–)2–12.5(–15) × (1–)1.5–5(–6.5) cm, papyraceous, rounded to cuneate at base, ± acuminate or obtuse at apex, upper surface glabrous except for the midvein which is tomentulose-puberulent, lower surface pubescent to sparsely pubescent, with (3–)4–7(–9) pairs of secondary veins. Inflorescences 1–2 per axil, up to 3(–4) cm in length, with (1–)2–5(–7) flowers; peduncle 1.5–3 mm, ± pubescent; rachis pubescent; bracts and bracteoles narrowly oblong-lanceolate to subulate, 0.5–1 mm in length, pubescent, caducous. Flower: pedicel (3–)4–6(–7) mm, puberulent; sepals ovate-elliptic to obovate-elliptic, (3–)4–8 × 2–5(–6) mm, the upper one keeled, the two inner ones obtuse or acute, sometimes emarginate, glabrous or with brown external pubescence, denser towards the base and apex, ciliate; petals white, cream or yellowish, 12–18(–19) × 2–4(–5.5) mm, ciliate and with stiff pilosity, keel attenuate into a claw as long as itself, puberulent externally or only along the folds and towards the apex, puberulent internally near the apex, upper petals narrowly oblong, puberulent on both faces, the lateral ones narrowly obovate; stamens 11–14 mm in length, curved, glabrous; staminodes 0–2. Fruit ovoid to ovoid-globose, generally 3-lobed, smooth, 2–2.5 cm in diameter, shortly stipitate, apiculate, glabrous, yellow to orange at maturity. Seed flattened, ellipsoid to ellipsoid-ovoid, 6–12 × 3–8 mm, yellow to orange, generally covered with undulating hairs.

## Distribution

West and central Africa: Guinée-Bissau, Guinée Conakry, Sierra Leone, Libéria, Nigeria, Cameroun, Guinée Équatoriale, Gabon, République Centrafricaine, Congo (Brazzaville), République Démocratique du Congo and Angola; in Gabon: Woleu-Ntem, Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga, Ngounié, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Ivindo and Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

primary or secondary evergreen forest, semi-deciduous forest, gallery forest; at 0–1100 m altitude. Use: The fruits are consumed locally. The very hard wood is used as chewing sticks, as rat traps, house posts, walking sticks, tool handles, etc. The wood is also burned as a torch. The leaves and branches in infusion and sometimes a decoction of the bark are used as a stomachic. The roots are used as a tonic or aphrodisiac.
