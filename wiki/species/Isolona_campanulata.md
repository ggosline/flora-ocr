## Description

Engl.Monogr.Afr.Pflanzenf.6:83,tab.27 C (1901).– Pellegrin,Bull. Soc. Bot.Fr.94:387 (1947).– Keay,F.W.T.A.,ed.2,1,1:53(1947).– F.F.C.I.,ed.2,I:152,tab.44 (1960).– Keay et al.,Nigerian trees I:Aubréville,55 (1960).

Shrub or small tree 10–15 m tall, with thin bark, dark brownish, fissured, peeling in strips; branchlets dark, glabrous. Leaves with petiole 2–3 mm long; blade papyraceous, elliptic-oblong to oblanceolate, long-acuminate at apex, cuneate at base. Lateral nerves 6–10 pairs, very oblique, joined in arches quite far from the margin, prominent below.

Flowers axillary or supra-axillary, solitary or geminate, with slender pedicel 1–2.5 cm long, furnished with 3 small basal bracts, glabrous. Sepals green, ovate, acute at apex, 2–6 mm long, 1.5–3.5 mm wide. Corolla campanulate; yellowish-green; tube nearly as long as or equalling the corolla lobes, 7–11 mm long, swollen at base; lobes triangular, thin, regularly attenuate towards apex, acute, 7–14 mm long, 3–5 mm wide at base. Stamens subsessile, 1–1.5 mm long, anthers linear extrorse, connectives dilated-truncate above the anthers in the outer stamens, elongate in the inner ones. Ovary ca. 1 mm long, stigmas capitate, lobulate-plicate.

Fruits with pedicel 1.7–3 cm long, ± flexuous, with persistent calyx; syncarp yellow at maturity, narrowly ovoid, rounded-lobulate, 3.5–7 cm long, 1.8–4 cm wide. Seeds numerous, embedded in yellowish pulp, ellipsoid to subglobose, 1.2–1.5 cm long, 0.7–1 cm wide.

## Discussion

Species of Isolona most widely distributed in Africa, from Sierra Leone to Gabon; it appears, however, much more frequent in the understory of dense humid forest in West Africa than in the equatorial region. Tisserant and Sillans reported (Not.Syst. 15:326,1958) I. campanulata in the Central African Republic, but this is in fact a specimen of Isolona congolana (De Wild.et Th.Dur.)Engl.et Diels.

Species of secondary dense humid forest, uncommon, known from Gabon and Congo-Kinshasa (Mayombe).

Species of dense humid forest distributed from western Nigeria to the Central African Republic and southward to Angola, particularly polymorphic, highly variable in the size of leaves and flowers. Excellent descriptions made in the field by Father Tisserant in the Central African Republic and Letouzey in Cameroon enabled us to understand the variation in this species. These two collectors noted in particular the simultaneous variation in flower size on the same tree, in color during growth, in the position and size of the lobes relative to the corolla tube, all characters invoked to separate the species placed above in synonymy. Boutique, in the Flora of Congo, had already begun this regrouping by uniting the three species described from Congo-Kinshasa, and he noted at the same time their very great affinity with I. hexaloba. In fact it is for us a highly polymorphic species. We associate with it I. leucantha Diels with more uncertainty since it is known only from the type: Zenker 3038, on which only a few flower fragments remain. It was distinguished essentially by the white color of its corolla, yet we know that in the first stage, the corolla of I. hexaloba is white.
