# *Dalbergia bakeri* Welw. ex Baker Planche 36

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Oliv., Fl. trop. Afr. 2 : 235 (1871). Shrub or liana 1.5–5 m tall or reaching up to 10 m in length, with an aromatic fenugreek scent generally perceptible on dry material, unarmed and apparently without prehensile branches; bark dark grey or blackish, with pale lenticels (not always very prominent); young branches with short, spreading or appressed pubescence. Stipules very early caducous, falciform, 6–8 × 1.5–2 mm, with short appressed pubescence. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.3–1 cm; rachis 1.7–7 cm, with short pubescence like the young branches; leaflets 5 to 9 (sometimes up to 12 on young plants), alternate and unequal, with petiolule of 1–3 mm, blade elliptic or obovate, asymmetric (except for the terminal leaflet), 2.1–7.7 × 1.4–3.4 cm in the upper leaflets and 0.7–2.9 × 0.6–1.7 cm in the lower ones, acute or obtuse at the base, obtuse or acuminate at the apex, rather coriaceous, brownish when dry, with the lower surface distinctly glaucous, with very short appressed pubescence on both surfaces (often appearing glabrous to the naked eye); lateral veins 4 to 8, very faintly marked, veinlets invisible or very lax. Inflorescence in short panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary or on old branches, sometimes also terminal, 1–5 cm in length, with short dense golden-brown pubescence; bracts early caducous, broadly elliptic, ± 0.8 × 0.6 mm, pubescent externally. Flower: pedicel 1–5 mm; bracteoles persistent, broadly elliptic, 0.8–1.2 × 0.6–0.8 mm, rounded at the apex, pubescent; calyx 2.5–4 mm in length, pale green, with appressed pubescence, truncate or with very short obtusely triangular lobes; corolla white, petals with claw equalling or exceeding the calyx, standard bent at right angle, with claw 3–4 mm and limb obovate or wider than long 4–4.5 × 4.5–6 mm, bilobed at the apex, wings equalling or exceeding the keel, with claw 2.5–3.5 mm and limb ± rectangular 4.5–5 × 2–3 mm, slightly wrinkled transversely towards the base of the outer surface, with truncate or shortly auriculate base, keel slightly curved, with claw 3–3.3 mm and limb 4.3–4.5 × 1.7–2.3 mm, with slightly auriculate base; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments 5.5–6 mm, fused on the 4/5 of their length, straight at the base and curved in their free part; ovary ± 2.5 × 0.5 mm, glabrous (or rarely pubescent at the margins), with 3 ovules, with straight stipe 4 mm; style linear, 1.7 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 1–2 mm and stipe 6–8 mm, papyraceous, flattened and narrowly ellipsoid, 4.3–8 × 1.2–2 cm, reticulate over its entire surface, glabrous, with 1(–2) seeds. Seed reniform and flattened, ± 8 × 3 mm.

## Distribution

Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; frequent in Gabon in the coastal region (Estuaire, Nyanga and Ogooué-Maritime), it is also found very locally in the interior, in the Lopé National Park and on the crest of the Bélinga Mountains (Ogooué-Ivindo).

## Ecology

Particularly frequent in dry coastal thickets on white sand, also on forest/savanna edges, in low and open ridge forests, and in riparian forests of the Congo basin; 0–1000 m altitude in Gabon. Flowers and fruits from October to February. The plant is consumed by gorillas.

## Source note

D. bakeri is a fairly variable species. Plants from coastal regions generally have obtuse leaflets and long floral pedicels, those from the interior have rather acuminate leaflets and shorter pedicels. However, there are intermediates, and both types of leaflets can sometimes be observed on the same branch. D. bakeri has long been confused with D. micheliana (= D. bakeri var. acutifoliolata), which is also present in Gabon; see the note under that species. Contrary to the indications of Pellegrin (1949), D. pluriflora Baker f. is not a synonym of D. bakeri, but of D. grandibracteata De Wild. (Lachenaud & Maesen 2016). This latter species, widespread in the Congo basin, has not been recorded from Gabon to date. b: Cronquist (1954), Lachenaud & Maesen (2016), Pellegrin (1949). Bibliography
