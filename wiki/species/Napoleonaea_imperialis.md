# *Napoleonaea imperialis* P.Beauv.

**Genus**: [[Napoleonaea]]
**Family**: [[Lecythidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Napoléone : 1, pl. 1 (1804)

## Description

Shrub reaching 3–12 m in height; branches cylindrical, greyish. Leaf: petiole 3–13(–15) mm, black; blade elliptic-oblong to obovate, (12–)14–22(–24) × (4.5–)6–9 cm, cuneate or rounded at the base, acute to acuminate at the apex, coriaceous, with entire margins or coarsely dentate-undulate towards the apex, foliar glands arranged rarely at the base (often in pairs) otherwise arranged regularly 1–3.5 mm from the blade margin sometimes extending to the base of the acumen; lateral veins 8–11 pairs, forming a series of loops towards the blade margin and spaced at least 5–28 mm apart; network of tertiary veins scalariform to reticulate. Inflorescence axillary or ramiflorous; bracts reaching 3 × 4 mm, glandular. Flowers solitary or in fascicles, subsessile; calyx 16–20 mm in diameter, smooth, glabrous; first corolla 35–40 mm in diameter, purple but cream-white at the apex; second corolla cream-white; third corolla cream-white but purple at the base and apex; staminal corolla cream-white. Fruit subglobose to transversely broadly ellipsoid, 3–3.5 × 4–4.5 cm, orange.

## Distribution

Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko), Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; uncommon in Gabon

## Ecology

dense humid forest and secondary forest, on sandy soil; in Gabon up to 500 m elevation, elsewhere up to 800 m

## Vernacular names

Napoleon’s hat (Angl.) ; Napoleon’s hut (Allem.). alola afane (Fang). Usage : Le fruit est comestible. Le bois est utilisé pour des outils variés. L’espèce est parfois cultivée comme plante ornementale. Notes taxonomiques : Il existe parmi des spécimens de N. imperialis quelques échantillons dont les glandes nettement distinctes sont situées directement sur la marge du limbe. Ces échantillons qui ont des longues feuilles pouraient représenter une variété de N. imperialis. B : Hutchinson & Dalziel (1954), Liben (1971). i B liographie
