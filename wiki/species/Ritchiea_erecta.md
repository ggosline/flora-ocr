Small shrub up to 1 m tall, evergreen, branched or with single stem from base, plant glabrous except at level of floral parts. Young shoots yellowish-green matt or brown, finely scabrous (epidermal asperities), sometimes glabrous, vaguely striate. Older branches, up to 0.5 cm thick, glabrous, pale green-brown; lenticels numerous, elongate, pale brown, pulverulent, sometimes confluent, not forming corky ridges with age. Wood white fairly hard, pith white and narrow. Axillary buds solitary or 2–3 buds in series, tightly pressed against the subtending leaf, remaining dormant except one or a few near the branch apex.

Leaves simple or compound (1)–3(–5)–foliolate, present on the same branches, unevenly distributed on the axes, increasingly numerous towards the apex; some clustered just below the apex, the lower part of the branch being leafless or nearly so. Blade dark olive-green glossy above, somewhat paler below, papyraceous when young, coriaceous with age, the upper surface appearing smooth, polished and shiny under high magnification. Major veins prominent below, depressed above; the midvein wrinkled longitudinally on the lower surface, pale yellowish, shiny; lateral veins (3)–5–7(–10) evenly distributed on the midvein, gently curved, anastomosing in neat loops at the apex; veinlets in dense network clearly marked on both surfaces. Simple leaves shortly petiolate; blade broad and elliptic or obovate, 13–17(–20) × 6–9(–17) cm, acuminate, most often mucronate, base acute or obtuse, decurrent on the petiole 0.6–2.9 cm long. One-foliolate leaves rare, corresponding to an intermediate stage between simple and compound leaves. Three-foliolate leaves are most frequent, longly petiolate; base of leaflets unevenly stipitate. Five-foliolate leaves sometimes develop near 3-foliolate leaves;

terminal leaflet symmetric, obovate or elliptic, 10–20(–22) × 6–9(–12.5) cm, caudate or shortly cuspidate, blade longly attenuate into a petiolule-like base; lateral leaflets somewhat smaller 9–16 × 4.5–7(–9) cm, caudate to cuspidate, asymmetric, outer half elliptic with acute base, inner half obovate with attenuate base, clearly constricted into a petiolule similar to that of the terminal leaflet. Petiolules 1.5–5(–7) mm, deeply channelled on the upper surface especially near base, finely pleated and rugulose dark purplish-green, base strongly articulate; for the terminal leaflet, the length of the petiolule (including the stipitate base of the blade) is 6–13(–16) mm. Petiole glabrous, slender, dark green, grooved towards the top on the upper surface, base not entirely or weakly fused, not decurrent; for unifoliolate leaves its length is 1.7–3.3 cm; for trifoliolate leaves, 4–11.5 cm. Stipules inconspicuous reaching 0.5 mm, subulate or triangular, scariose marcescent.

Inflorescence a rigidly erect corymbose raceme, 30–40-flowered, terminal or subterminal, most often solitary or sometimes more in the apical region of the branch where 2 series buds each produce a raceme. Flowers very numerous and evenly distributed on the rachis 1.3–4.5(–6) cm, leafless at base, finely pubérulent, marked with dense and prominent grey scars after flower fall; its growth is limited. Floral bracts simple, elliptic or narrow and lanceolate, at most 1 cm long, sessile yellowish-green, thin, finely glandular along the margins and at the apex, median nerve clear; early caducous, they fall before anthesis most often. Stipules of floral bracts subulate or acute, at most 1 mm long, free, yellowish-green with sessile glands on the external surface, apically incurved, marcescent, taking on the appearance of a spine with age. Pedicel 1.5–2.6 cm long, slightly pubérulent, pale green, somewhat angular in cross-section at least towards the apex and where it broadens; base clearly articulate. Receptacle vaguely conical, 1.1–2 mm high just after anthesis and 3–4 mm wide, glabrescent (in fact there is very slight and very short pubérulence); margin with 4-angular outline; it becomes somewhat coriaceous and nearly flat at fruiting. Sepals nearly equal, broad, ovate or elliptic, (14)–18–25 × (6)–7–10 mm, acute or acuminate, yellowish-green, papyraceous, pubérulent, 3–5-nerved from the base, margin ciliolate, blade caducous.

Petals 8–12, entire, narrow, lanceolate to sublinear, 4–5 cm × (0.5–1)1–2(–3) mm, longly apiculate, often twisted, margin not undulate, blade glabrous or with scattered cilia along the margins, fragile, white or cream turning pale yellow on drying. The petals form groups of 2–3 alternating with the sepals. Androgynophore very short, at most 1.5 mm, robust, glabrous, striate, strongly exceeding the receptacle or not at all after anthesis. Stamens ca. 50–60, all fertile some desiccating very early (no staminodes); filaments of outer stamens 3–3.8 cm, those of inner ones 2–2.8 cm; all very fine and fragile, white then turning pale lilac at anthesis and brown on desiccating. Anthers linear, 1.5–2.5(–3) mm long, apiculate, dorsobasifixed, early caducous. Gynophore slender 1.5–2 cm × 0.75 mm at anthesis, then 2.2–3 cm × 1–1.25 mm at fruiting, glabrous, cylindric at base, striate towards the top, apical part 4-angular in cross-section; at fruiting, it lignifies, becomes cylindric and aligns with the pedicel or bends slightly downwards. Ovary cylindric to narrowly ellipsoid, rarely narrowly ovate, 2.5–5 × 1–2 mm, somewhat compressed, acute at base, furnished with 4 clearly prominent longitudinal veins continuous from base to apex, somewhat more pronounced at base; between these, the ovary walls have collapsed. Ovary unilocular with 2 parietal placentas; ovules numerous. Stigma sessile to subsessile, truncate, somewhat broader than the ovary apex, blackish with entire margin somewhat recurved.

Fruit fusiform or ellipsoid, 3–5 × 1–1.5 cm, obtuse or acute at both ends, vaguely 4-angular in cross-section, dirty yellow-orange at maturity; pericarp somewhat coriaceous, not bulging at the level of seeds, finely verrucose and faintly marked with 4 longitudinal ridges. Seeds, 1–5 per fruit suborbicular about 1 cm in diameter, somewhat laterally compressed; testa membranous, partially adnate to the inner wall of the fruit at maturity. — Pl. 7, p. 55 & Map E, p. 7.

## Discussion

Ritchiea erecta is very closely allied to R. simplicifolia; it differs by its clearly costate ovary, by the dense and regular arrangement of flowers, by the scabrous epidermis of young branches, by the unequal length of the stipitate base of leaflets and their polished and shiny upper surface, finally by the small number of lateral veins. Sterile specimens of R. erecta and R. macrantha can be confused given their terminal leaflet with long petiolule, but R. erecta is distinguished by more numerous lateral veins, shiny leaflets, scabrous young branches and unevenly spaced leaves.

There is wide variation in foliage in this species. Simple leaves are rare (or only rare in herbaria?). They are presumed to correspond to a juvenile stage of the foliage of an individual or of particular branches. Simple leaves are frequently encountered on poorly developed individuals or in the lower parts of plants; they would generally be absent from specimens with long and slender stems. The trifoliolate state always dominates and an ultimate transition to the 5-foliolate state rarely occurs. Unlike leaves, floral morphology varies very little.

The geographical distribution of this species is limited to the Gulf of Guinea: southern Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Fernando Po and Gabon. Its range follows hilly or mountainous regions close to coastal plains; this type of habitat is unknown in Gabon; elsewhere this species occurs in forest clearings or forest margins or along rivers, in rocky biotopes. Apparently rare in Gabon, it flowers in July; its flowers emit a slight odour (Walker s.n.). Fruit unknown from Gabon. No local name has been noted, from Gabon or elsewhere.
