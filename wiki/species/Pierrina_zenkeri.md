# *Pierrina zenkeri* Engler

**Genus**: [[Pierrina]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 43 : 376, tab. I, fig. A-P (1909); Pflanzenw. Afr. 1 (2) : tab. 602, fig. A-P (1910); 3 (2) : 473, tab. 219, fig. A-P (1921); LETOUZEY, Adansonia, ser. 2, 1 (1) : 138 (1961)

## Description

Pflanzenw. Afr. 1 (2) : tab. 602, fig. A-P (1910); 3 (2) : 473, tab. 219, fig. A-P (1921); LETOUZEY, Adansonia, ser. 2, 1 (1) : 138 (1961).

Pflanzenw. Afr. 1 (2) : tab. 602, fig. Q-U (1910); 3 (2) : 473, tab. 219, fig. Q-U (1921); LETOUZEY, Adansonia, ser. 2, 1 (1) : 138 (1961); see note 1 below.

Small shrub 0.5-4 m tall (reportedly reaching 10-15 m according to ZENKER), cauliflorous and ramiflorous; bark dark brownish. Young branches glabrous, furnished with 2 prominent ribs separated by a plane intercalary surface, the branch appearing subtetragonal and this form persisting on branches of previous growth; petiole not exceeding 5 mm in length. Blade papyraceous, oblong, sometimes elliptic, 4-7 × 8-20 cm (acumen not included); base acute or obtuse, slightly asymmetric; apex caudate-acuminate with narrow acumen, 2-5 × 10-20(-30) mm, with rounded apex, sometimes emarginate; margin dentate with obtuse, spaced teeth, rarely entire; 7-9 pairs of lateral nerves curved and anastomosing in loops 3-5 mm from the margin.

Inflorescences as isolated flowers or in pauciflorous pseudofascicles on stems and branches below the leaves, rarely among leaves and supra-axillary; pedicel orange 3-5 mm long, glabrous, with 2 lanceolate bracteoles near the base; floral bud subglobose then more elongate, even obovoid, up to 8 mm in height, obtuse or sometimes slightly acuminate. Calyx orange then reddish with green margin, 4 mm in diameter; corolla dark purple with silvery punctations and orange apex, opening into 4 lobes 10 mm long, curved outward, purple with white margin and spots internally; stamens 4 mm tall with white filaments and yellow anthers of the same length; ovary pale yellow, subglobose, 2 mm tall; style 4 mm long.

Capsule with pedicel 10 mm long, calyx bright red, of orange tint then bright red at maturity, subsequently dark brown and shiny, 1.5-2 × 5-7 cm, often constricted toward the middle, furrowed with approximately 4 meridians ± distinct; seeds reaching 4 × 12 × 20 mm. — PL. 45, p. 163.

## Discussion

TAXONOMIC NOTES:

1) Despite relatively numerous collections of Pierrina zenkeri Engl. from the Douala and Kribi regions, P. longifolia Engl. has not been refound to date and remains known only from the description and drawing published by Engler. It is possible to question the existence of this species because the entire leaf margin could be considered as an extreme case of a toothed margin (cf. Oubanguia africana Baill.), all other foliar characters being of low value, and the fruit figured for this species being found commonly in Pierrina zenkeri Engl. on the other hand; the character of the capsule, mentioned in one case (P. zenkeri) as 2-locular and in the other (P. longifolia) as 4-locular of which 2 seed-bearing, also appears of questionable value because the ovarian septa in fact disappear ± completely at the time of fruiting and the capsule should be considered unilocular it would seem. Pierrina longifolia Engl. is therefore placed here in synonymy with P. zenkeri Engl., pending new rediscoveries in the field. There are moreover very strong grounds for suspecting that Pierrina longifolia Engl. does not exist because the first isotype refound of this species (Zenker 3671, BM!), after multiple searches in various herbaria worldwide, manifestly consists of a sterile branch with dentate leaves of P. zenkeri Engl. and a sterile branch of a Moraceae corresponding to the figure provided by Engler in support of his diagnosis (cf. Bot. Jahrb. 43 : tab. 1, fig. Q, 1909); for the second known isotype (Zenker 3671, K!) with leaves and fruits, it seems difficult to distinguish it from Pierrina zenkeri Engl. 2) Confusions seem possible, in the absence of fruits, between Pierrina zenkeri Engl. and Brazzeia soyauxii (Oliv.) V. Tiegh. The branches of this latter species are not angular but furnished with a furrow, generally puberulent and not glabrous, the petiole is less individualized, finally the lateral nerves are 5-6 pairs in number.

Pierrina zenkeri Engl. is a small shrub known only from the understory of dense evergreen humid forest with Caesalpiniaceae in the regions of Mbanga, Douala, Edéa, Makak, Kribi, Nyabessan and Campo in Cameroon.
