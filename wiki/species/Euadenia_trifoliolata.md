Short shrub or small deciduous tree reaching 6 m in height but ordinarily much lower; trunk dark brown smooth, wood tender, pith narrow. Young branchlets olive-green shining or reddish, longitudinally grooved; older branchlets greyish-brown, cylindric, bark furnished with fine longitudinal fissures and rare oblong grey lenticels. Leaves 3-foliolate sometimes simple, borne on branchlets of the year, regularly spaced or more crowded at the 2 extremities of isolated branchlets; petioles long; limbs increasingly smaller towards the apex; maximum length of the lowermost 20 cm, that of the uppermost 4–9 cm. Leaflets lanceolate or lance-shaped (median), often obovate to ovate (lateral), olive-green on both faces when young, subsequently olive-green shining above and somewhat paler below tinged with reddish, membranous and fragile then papyraceous; stipitate base unequal depending on whether the leaflet is median or not. Median leaflet symmetric, (5–) 8 (–10) × (2–) 3 (–4) cm (lower leaves), becoming progressively smaller on upper leaves, acute or most often shortly cuspidate-acute; base narrowly attenuated narrowed into a petiolule. Lateral leaflets asymmetric, outer half ovate or lanceolate, inner half obovate to lanceolate, often shortly caudate; base oblique cuneate, abruptly attenuated into a stipe before the proper petiolule. Median petiolule 4–13 mm long (including the stipitate base of the limb), the lateral ones 2–7 mm long, all dark green vaguely channeled above, scarcely or not articulate at the base. Petiole slender, 2–12 (–18) cm long, often as long as the median leaflet, olive-green shining often reddish towards the base, weakly channeled on the upper face, distinctly articulate at the base; leaf scars well visible. Stipules difficult to see, setaceous, maximum length 0.5 mm, early withered.

Inflorescences in lax racemes, 10–20-flowered, 5–15 cm long, terminating short leafy shoots, axillary or subapical, sometimes borne at the apex of main branchlets; axis glabrous, rigidly erect, angular in section. Floral bracts simple, narrowly lanceolate to linear, 4–7 mm long, fragile greenish-yellow, falling before anthesis. Pedicels slender, glabrous, slightly curved at both extremities, exceeding 5 cm in the lower flowers. Sepals 4, narrowly lanceolate, long attenuate, membranous, pale greenish-yellow, caducous, unequal: the lower one 8–10 mm shorter than the others, the upper one 11–14 mm, the 2 lateral ones intermediate in size and equal to each other. Petals 4, greenish-yellow, sometimes reddish, turning brown when dry, caducous; the 2 pairs are very different in size and shape: upper petals reaching 6.5–7.5 × 0.6–0.7 cm, spatulate, acute or rounded at the apex, without mucro, midvein well visible, lateral veins rare and scarcely visible; lower petals 1.5–2.0 cm × 0.5–1.0 mm, small

and narrow, somewhat longer than the sepals, narrowly lanceolate, attenuate, uninerved, very fragile. Fertile stamens 4–5, filaments reaching 2.3 cm, rather thick, the basal part approximately 2 times wider than the gynophore which is truncate at the apex, inclined and whitish; anthers, narrowly oblong, 3–4 mm basifixed, early caducous. Staminodial appendage in 4 or 5 corpuscles, 1.0–1.4 cm × 1 mm, flat or concave in its lower half, sometimes entire, more often divided in its apical part, bifurcate or divided into several branches, sometimes almost from the base; rudimentary anthers, at most 0.5 mm long, labile. Gynophore slender, reaching 1 cm at the end of flowering, thickened and lignified at fruiting. Ovary narrowly cylindric, obtuse at the apex, acute or acuminate at the base, glabrous; stigma sessile to subsessile, tumidly capitate, with entire margin. Ovules numerous on 2 parietal placentae.

Fruit oblong, obtuse at both extremities, reaching 2.5 × 1.5 cm; before their maturity, the fruits are pale orange-greenish with green spots; the ripe and dry fruits are dark brown to blackish and covered with grey spots; pericarp coriaceous, somewhat wrinkled. Seeds very numerous densely agglomerated, not uniseriate, diameter 7–8 mm, suborbicular-reniform in lateral view, somewhat compressed laterally, hilar fissure short and narrow, elaiosome absent; extremity of the cotyledons developed and rounded, that of the radicule short and sharp; testa coriaceous, smooth, pale cinnamon brown. – Map, B, p. 7.

## Type

Isert s.n^{o}, Ghana (holo-C)

## Discussion

This species is recognized by its short and squat fruit (cf. Aubréville, 1936) by its relatively small greenish upper petals and by its unequally stipitate leaflets on each leaf. The foliage is rapidly deciduous, falling from the shoots of the year itself.

Only two collections come from Gabon, both from the North-Eastern part. This species lives in clearings, forest edges, on river banks and along small streams in forested zones. It is most often encountered on rocky soils and in rugged terrain. The specimens observed were flowering in August and October; none from Gabon are known to be in fruit.

Its overall distribution extends from Northern Gabon to North-Western Côte-d'Ivoire; it is found along the Gulf of Guinea in Cameroon and Nigeria, as well as in Benin and Togo. It appears to be rare in Gabon.
