# *Sida ovata* Forssk. Planche 25

**Genus**: [[Sida]]
**Family**: [[Malvaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. aegypt.-arab. 116 : 124 (1775)

## Description

Woody herb, up to 1 m tall, much branched, trailing or erect; young branches puberulent. Stipules linear, subequal, the widest ones often divided at the apex into two subequal elongate parts, 0.5–1 cm long. Leaf: petiole 3–5 mm; limb obliquely ovate to broadly elliptic, apex obtuse, 0.9–5 × 0.5–3 cm, base rounded, margin crenulate-serrulate, both surfaces covered with long simple hairs. Flower axillary, solitary; pedicel up to 5 mm, articulate at 1 mm from the apex; calyx rather open, especially in fruit, 5–9 mm long, with triangular pilose lobes; petals about 10 mm long, yellow to orange; staminal tube 2 mm long, filaments free for half their length, anthers numerous; ovary with (7–)10 carpels; style divided into 5 branches of 2 mm, at the same level as the anthers. Fruit with trigonous mericarps, indehiscent, about 3.5 mm long, with reticulate faces, not or briefly apiculate with two awns.

## Distribution

Dry zones of Africa, up to India; in Gabon uncommon, known only from Ogooué-Lolo.

## Ecology

Savannas, forests, roadsides; in Gabon at 400 m, elsewhere at 0–2350 m. Flowers observed in April and September. B: Berhaut (1979), Exell & Meeuse (1961), Hauman (1963), Verdcourt (2009)
