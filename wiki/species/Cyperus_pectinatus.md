# *Cyperus pectinatus* Vahl Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. pl . 2 : 298 (1805)

## Description

Perennial herb in tufts, without leaves, with few or numerous stems arising from a short rhizome, sometimes stoloniferous, with numerous roots, light brown to reddish brown, sometimes spongy; stems 30–120 cm × 0.6–2 mm, with rounded angles, glabrous. Leaf: sheath dark grey to purple; young stems erect then often bending outward at maturity and finally the inflorescence re-roots on the ground or in floating mud and then gives rise to a new tuft; blade often reduced and triangular or absent. Inflorescence in solitary head, 1–4 cm wide, formed of 3–20 reddish-brown sessile spikelets; involucral bracts 1–2, resembling the stem and generally shorter than the inflorescence. Spikelet lanceolate, 5–25 × 2–6 mm, light to dark reddish-brown, flattened, with 15–40 flowers (the spikelets forming continuously, their size is highly variable within the same head); glumes 4–5 mm long (2–4 mm for the lower glume of the spikelet), ovate, keeled, reddish-brown with usually the central nerve trinerved, green and very strongly scabrous; apex obtuse to acute. Achene lanceolate, 3.5–4 × 1.0–1.2 mm, somewhat flattened, apex long-acuminate; surface smooth. Seed dark, surrounded by yellowish cork tissues, allowing the achene to float.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical Africa and Madagascar; rare in Gabon, found only in Haut-Ogooué and Woleu-Ntem (2 collections)

## Ecology

in swamps, on the borders of lakes and along rivers, sometimes floating; in Gabon, probably at 400–600 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–1700 m. Taxonomic note: recognized by its leafless stems and its solitary head inflorescence. It resembles only C. colymbetes Kotschy & Peyr., which has a thicker triangular stem and larger involucral bracts. C. colymbetes is known neither from Gabon nor from West Africa, but further east. Cyperus L. subgenus Mariscus (Vahl) C.B.Clarke, J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 21: 34 (1884). Perennial or annual herbs; stems 5–500 cm long, triangular, rarely cylindrical. Leaves usually with well-developed blade. Anatomy of stems and leaves of the chlorocyperoid type (that is, with C4 type anatomy). Inflorescence in loose or dense anthela, with few or many distinct spikes, ovoid to cylindrical, sessile or pedunculate, but sometimes inflorescence reduced to a solitary spikelet or in dense head formed of many sessile spikelets. Spikelets ± flattened, dehiscent or indehiscent; glumes ovate, generally 1–8 mm long, densely imbricate. Flower with 1–3 stamens; style usually with 2–3 branches. Achenes sessile, trigonous, more rarely biconvex and flattened dorsiventrally, surface usually finely papillose or fairly smooth. Large subgenus of almost 300 species from dry grasslands, swamps, lake borders, marine shores, brackish marshes and forests, widely distributed especially in tropical and subtropical regions; in Gabon 32 species
