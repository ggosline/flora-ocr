## Description

Tree or shrub reaching 4 m in height. Leaf: petiole 1–13 mm, with 3 or more pairs of axillary colleters; limb elliptic to narrowly obovate—sometimes as long as broad or—±— cm, subcordate or cuneate at the base or decurrent on the petiole, sometimes unequal, acuminate at the apex, glabrous or pubescent on both faces; secondary nerves 7 to 20 pairs. Inflorescence —–± cm, lax with few or numerous flowers; peduncle —± cm, rather slender, bracteate; persistent bracts as long as the calyx lobes, without colleters. Fragrant flower; pedicel 2–17 mm; calyx with lobes 1.1–± mm, glabrous or pubescent on both faces, finely ciliate at least at the apex, alternating with groups of colleters in a single flower, at total corolla with reddish or violaceous points or bands in 5 groups, adult bud 6–12 mm long, with lobes

Seed 7–10 × 4–6 × 3.5–5.5 mm; testa reticulate on the hilar side. Distribution from Cameroon to the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon all provinces. Ecology: under primary and secondary forest in Gabon to m altitude; elsewhere to m. Use: The root plays a crucial role in bwiti ceremonies, especially as a hallucinogen. A decoction of roots can also be taken as a tonic to combat fatigue, endure hunger and thirst. It also has a reputation as an aphrodisiac, as does a decoction of leaves. In the United States and in Europe it was used to interrupt drug dependence without causing addiction. In traditional medicine, the latex is taken to calm toothaches. It is also used as an ingredient for arrow poison. Taken in small quantity, iboga raises sleep
