# *Clerodendrum speciosissimum* Van Geert ex Drapiez

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Hort. Belge 3 : 322 (1836)

## Description

Shrub or small tree erect, reaching 4 m in height; stem often fistulose and fetid when damaged, sometimes myrmecophilous, sometimes solid; branches quadrangular, with dense short pubescence. Leaves decussate; petiole 2–25 cm, densely puberulent; blade ovate, ovate-rounded to almost deltoid, 7–20(–35) × 6–17(–26) cm, 1–1.5 times longer than wide, generally cordate at base, acute to shortly acuminate (the distal half of the blade sometimes almost lanceolate) at apex, undulate or irregularly denticulate-dentate, chartaceous when dry, sparsely puberulent above, densely below. Inflorescence terminal, in thyrse, erect, lax, rounded and often subcorymbiform, (9–)14–30(–38) × 10–25 cm, composed of red and decussate branches of divaricate cymes, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1–1.5 cm; calyx campanulate, 4–9(–12) mm long, with lobes 2–5 mm long, acuminate at apex, slightly accrescent in fruit, scabrous; corolla bright orange-red to red or vermilion, tube (1.3–)2–2.3(–2.7) cm long, slender, externally scabrous, with oval lobes, weakly spatulate, 0.8–1.4 cm long; stamens exserted 2–3 cm. Fruit 7–8 mm wide, green, turning bright blue.

## Distribution

Western Malesia, from New Guinea to the Solomon Islands and Vanuatu, introduced in cultivation and naturalized in numerous tropical regions. In Africa: Ghana, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Gulf of Guinea islands and Tanzania; its presence in Gabon is reported by Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), but without indication of province.

## Ecology

cultivated as an ornamental plant, escaped from cultivation and naturalized, considered by some as an invasive weed; no altitude recorded in Gabon, elsewhere up to 600 m. Use: A decorative plant for gardens.

## Vernacular names

flaming glorybower, Java glorybower, pagoda flower (angl.). B : Bramley (2019), Huber et al. (1963), Randall (2012), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). i B liographie
