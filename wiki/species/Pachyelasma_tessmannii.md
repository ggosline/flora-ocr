# *Pachyelasma tessmannii* (Harms) Harms

**Genus**: [[Pachyelasma]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Harms, Engl. Bot. Jahrb. 49 : 428 (1913)

## Synonyms

- Stachyothyrus Tessmannii Harms, Engl. Bot. Jahrb. 45 : 303 (1910).

## Description

Large trees. Large glabrous leaves, bipinnate, up to 35 cm in length, generally with 3 pairs of opposite pinnules (2-5). Leaflets 9-15, asymmetric, oblong to oblong-lanceolate, acute or obtuse at the base, rounded and emarginate at the apex, alternate, 4-10 cm in length by 1.5-3.5 cm in width, glabrous. Secondary veins 10-15 pairs, very obscure and irregular.

Robust spiciform racemes, solitary or fasciculate reaching 20 cm in length, very floriferous, glabrous. Flowers red, approximately 9 mm, ill-scented. Pedicels 2-3 mm in length. Receptacle cup-shaped thickened at the centre by a yellow disk.

Sepals 5, ovate to suborbicular, thick, ciliolate. Petals oblong 4.5-6 mm in length by 2-3 mm in width. Stamens 10, glabrous with red filaments and small blackish anthers with developed connective; 5 large ones opposite the sepals; 5 smaller ones alternate to the sepals. Ovary glabrous shortly stipitate; ovules 15-20 in two rows.

Very strong black woody pods, erect vertically above the crown, 15-35 cm in length by 3.5-4 cm in thickness and 2-2.5 cm in width, glabrous; rectangular section reinforced at the 4 angles by marked longitudinal ridges; transversely partitioned. Seeds 10-18, each in a chamber surrounded by viscous pulp. Seeds with very hard testa, approximately 2 × 0.85 × 0.6 cm. Cotyledons bright yellow surrounded by a translucid albumen.

## Vernacular names

Mekogho

## Type

Tessmann 819, Campo, Guinée espagnole

## Discussion

One of the largest and most majestic of the trees of the Congolese primary forest, capable of reaching 60 m in height. The crown is very developed and spread in an umbrella shape. The trunk can reach a very large diameter. It is tall, straight, cylindrical; sometimes deformed in very large trees (de Saint-Aubin). The base is flared, especially in large trees. Bark smooth, but rugose in old trees; section reddish, with granular texture, brittle. Wood reddish-brown, hard and heavy.

Species widespread from southern Nigeria, Cameroon, to the Congolese basin and Ubangi.

It is rare in Gabon, more frequent to the east of Lastoursville.
