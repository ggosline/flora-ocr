# *Blighia sapida* Koenig

**Genus**: [[Blighia]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Bot. 2 : 571, tab. 16, 17 (1806)

## Synonyms

- Cupania edulis Schum. & Thonn., Danske Vidensk. Selsk. Skrift 3 : 210 (1828).

## Description

Tree 15–20 m tall and 60–80 cm in diameter; crown globose (LETOUZEY), or umbellate; young branchlets tomentose yellow, striated-channeled, 3–4 mm in diameter, then whitish glabrous. Leaves 25–35 cm, concolorous, with obovate leaflets with cuneate base, briefly acuminate, with 12–14 pairs of veins, often also rounded, without apiculum at the apex, 9–12 × 6–8 cm, with 8–9 pairs of veins; basal leaflets small, 6 × 5 cm.

Inflorescences in simple axillary racemes, 10–20 cm long; cymules sessile with 4–6 flowers; bracts 1 mm. Flowers yellow-greenish; pedicel 5 mm; sepals 2–3 mm, tomentellous outside, hairy inside; petals 3–4 mm, funnel-shaped in their lower half; scale dentate hairy, shorter than the lanceolate limb, hairy on both faces; disk puberulent, entire crown-like externally, marked by internal notches between the filaments.

Fruit initially yellow-greenish (LETOUZEY), vermillion scarlet (WALKER and SILLANS) at maturity, pendulous, pyriform; aril yellow edible, taste of nuts. Seed black; on herbarium specimens, the aril has almost disappeared; on fresh material, it envelops the seed to mid-height. The dried seed measures 20 × 12 × 6 mm, the hilum cordate, 10 mm wide, obliquely truncate at the base; wood semi-hard, yellow-reddish.

Tree spontaneous in Senegal, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Côté d'Ivoire, Ghana, Haute Volta, Togo, Dahomey, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Islands Saô Tomé and Príncipe. Introduced in India and America.
