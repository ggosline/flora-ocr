# *Adenia mannii* (Mast.) Engl. Planche 25.5, 29

**Genus**: [[Adenia]]
**Family**: [[Passifloraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 14 : 375 (1891)

## Description

Sub-herbaceous vine, reaching 5 m in length; stem smooth. Stipules minute or absent, narrowly triangular. Leaf with petiole 0.5–2.5 cm; lamina elliptic to oblong, up to 17 × 8 cm, base obtuse to rounded, apex obtuse to acute, papyraceous; two auricular glands at the base of the lamina, 1.5–3 mm in diameter, laminar glands (0–)1–8, sub-marginal; sterile tendrils scarcely branched, up to 20 cm. Inflorescence arranged along the main stem, generally with a tendril, the male bearing up to 30 flowers, the female up to 10 flowers; peduncle 2–10 cm; bracts and bracteoles triangular, 0.5–1 mm, acute at the apex. Male flower with pedicel 5–20 mm and stipe 0.5–3 mm, cylindric, 15–23 mm in length; hypanthium broadly cupuliform, shallow, ± 5-chambered, septa up to 1.5 mm in height; sepals connate into a tube 8–12 mm, with triangular lobes 4–10 mm in length, margins sub-entire, sub-obtuse to acute at the apex; petals oblanceolate, 6.5–12 mm in length, apex obtuse to acute and distinctly lacerate; corona composed of fine filaments 1–2 mm in height, glands of the hypanthium 1–1.5 mm in length; stamens inserted at the base of the hypanthium, filaments 4–7 mm, connate for up to 1.5 mm, anthers 5–6 mm, with apicule 0.3 mm; pistillode 0.5 mm in length. Female flower resembling the male flowers, but with pedicel 15–23 mm and stipe 2–10 mm; hypanthium 2 mm in height; sepals in a tube 6–15 mm, lobes 5–7.5 mm in length; petals filiform, 4–10 mm in length, entire; staminodes 3.5–7 mm; ovary ovoid, ± trigonous, 6–8 × 4.5 mm, gynophore ± 0.5 mm; styles connate for ± 0.25–2.5 mm, with branches 1.5–2.5 mm, stigmas sub-reniform, 3–4 mm in diameter, woolly-papillose. Fruits 1–6 per inflorescence, broadly fusiform, (3–)6-angular, 2.5–3 cm in length, with coriaceous to woody pericarp, gynophore 1 mm. Seeds 20–30 per capsule, oblong-ovoid, 5–7 mm in length, with 9–13 fossules on a meridian line.

## Distribution

from Sierra Leone to Ghana and from Cameroon and Gabon to Uganda; very common in Gabon, present throughout the country

## Ecology

primary and secondary forests, swamp forests, forest galleries; in Gabon and elsewhere, up to 1000 m altitude. Flowers and fruits year-round. b : De Wilde (1971, 1975). Bibliography
