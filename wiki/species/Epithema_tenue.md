# *Epithema tenue* C. B. Clarke

**Genus**: [[Epithema]]
**Family**: [[Gesneriaceae]]
## Description

in DE CANDOLLE A. & C., Mon. Phan. 5 (1): 181 (1883); BAKER J. G. & CLARKE C. B., FTA 4 (2): 501 (1906); HUTCHINSON, FWTA 2: 237 (1931); EXELL, Cat. Vasc. Pl. S. Thomé: 258 (1944); BURTT, FWTA, ed. 2, 2: 383 (1963); FL. Cam. 27: 6, tab. 1 (1984); HALLÉ F. & DELMOTTE A., Adansonia, ser. 2, 13 (3): 273 (1973).

– Epithema thomense HENRIQ., Bol. Soc. Brot. 10: 145 (1892).

– E. graniticolum A. CHEV., Mém. Soc. Bot. Fr. 2 (8): 189 (1912).

Soft succulent herb, annual but with life cycle embracing 2 rainy seasons and one dry season; highly variable in size, up to about 25 cm tall, but sometimes only about 4 cm with cleistogamous flowers. Small and medium-sized flowering plants usually having a simple leaf near the ground on the stem, and above it a stem with a pair of smaller leaves and a terminal pedunculate inflorescence. Larger plants bearing additional inflorescences or flowering stems at the axil of leaves or from the base of the plant (see notes and references below). All axes shortly pubescent, some hairs glandular, others curved. Stem below the first leaf 1 to 6 cm. First leaf broadly ovate to suborbicular, rounded at apex, deeply cordate at base, varying from 4 × 4.5 cm to 20 × 19 cm, sparsely shortly pilose on both surfaces, margins crenate-dentate, ascending veins: petiole 2 to 13 cm. Flowering stem of main axis, long of 4 × 10 cm below the pair of leaves. Leaves opposite varying from 2.5 × 2 cm to 8 × 7 cm, shortly petiolate, otherwise similar to the first leaf.

Main inflorescence terminating the flowering stem, others in axillary position. Peduncle 0.5 to 0.7 cm long. Inflorescence in scorpioid cyme with flowers in pairs; axillant leaf, navicular, up to 2 cm long, crenate-dentate on margins, with rather stiff hairs on the exterior. Pedicel short, thick, 2 mm long, somewhat longer in fruit. Calyx obconic, tapering on the pedicel at base, 5.5 to 8 mm long, divided to the middle into 5 lanceolate, triangular lobes; tube sparsely hairy externally; lobes rather densely surrounded by stiff hairs, most of them curved; inner surface of lobes with very shortly stipitate glands; lobes trinerved, veins united in a well-marked hydathode below the apex. Flowers pale blue or whitish, only one or two opening at the same time. Corolla tube (in chasmogamous flowers) 7 to 9 mm long; lower lobes about 3 mm long; the two upper ones 1.5 mm long. Corolla of cleistogamous flowers about 2 mm long; lobes very undeveloped very small. Disc shortly cylindric, enveloping the ovary, split dorsally. Stamens 4 rising towards the corolla opening; filaments united in their lower part to form a thin membranous blade; the two posterolateral ones fertile, free part of filament coiled, anthers coherent face to face; sterile filaments of anterolateral stamens well developed, projecting forward on each side of the anthers. Ovary small rounded, with an apical tuft of erect hairs; style thin, held in position in its upper part by the fertile stamens, stigma strongly papillose, projecting just beyond the anthers; ovary completely unilocular, with parietal placentas, the most inferior part of the placentas hanging free from the ovary wall, without ovules; numerous ovules on long funicles.

Capsule thin-walled, subglobose, surrounded by the persistent calyx and somewhat fleshy; dehiscent by a circumscissile operculum. Seeds about 0.5 mm long, very numerous, light brown, marked with spiral ridges, formed by the longitudinal walls of the elongated cells of the testa, the cross walls not very visible. - Pl. 1, p. 9.

## Discussion

The description of this species from pressed herbarium specimens is not at all satisfactory: in particular, it was not possible to describe in detail the minute and delicate cleistogamous flowers.

HALLÉ F. & DELMOTTE A. (l.c.) gave a more elegant account of the life and growth mode of E. tenue. During the first rainy season, the plant does not flower, and its single large leaf is an accrescing cotyledon. This dies late during the dry season. When the rains resume, it is the third leaf that forms (the third leaf is epicotylar: it is already present as a fully formed rudiment). From a complex of "meristems" on the stem of this leaf, the main flowering stem develops as an axillary inflorescence. Below the meristematic complex, the "stem" is actually formed by intercalary growth of the petiole and the fused plumule axis. The adult plant which has a simple leaf, a flowering stem with a pair of opposite leaves and a terminal inflorescence, undoubtedly shows the growth mode of the species. Larger plants show additional inflorescences and flowering stems: more details are given by HALLÉ & DELMOTTE in the cited article. WEBER A. (l.c.) found that a similar growth mode is characteristic of Asian species and he analysed the floral system in terms of TROLL's inflorescence concepts. He also gives some details on floral morphology.

Like Streptocarpus nobilis C.B.CL., Epithema tenue has many biological aspects of a weed, although both retain their ecological requirements. They produce numerous small seeds, and when they germinate in an unfavorable situation, they are able to persist as very small plants, to flower cleistogamously and to produce fruits. In good conditions, they can grow to a larger size over considerable time and then produce chasmogamous flowers.
