# *Strombosia pustulata* var. *pustulata*

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Strombosia]]

## Description

Tree (10)–25–35(–40) m tall. Trunk straight and slender, 0.4–1 m in diameter. Rhytidome smooth, grey-brown to pinkish, exfoliating in circular plates 3–4 cm in diameter, whitish when cut, 4 mm thick. Bark smooth or scaly, whitish, greyish to reddish. Young branches weakly bi-winged, longitudinally striated and densely leafy, becoming smooth and blackish when mature. Petiole glabrous, 8–14 mm long, channelled above, wrinkled below. Lamina glabrous on both surfaces, pustulous on the upper surface, dull grey-green on both surfaces, elliptic, oblong-elliptic, oblong or lanceolate-oblong, 5.5–11.5 (–25) × 2–6.5 (–18.5) cm; base attenuate (sometimes rounded), apex acute obtuse mucronate. Midrib ± convex on the upper surface, prominent and wrinkled longitudinally. Secondary nerves 5–9 pairs, ascending, arched, attenuating within a few millimetres of the lamina margin, with poorly distinct anastomoses, slightly or not concave on the upper surface, weakly prominent on the lower surface. Nervilles mostly entirely indistinct on both surfaces.

**Inflorescence**: Axillary fascicles with few flowers on a cushion of bracts. Pedicel wrinkled longitudinally, glabrous, 2.5–3 mm long. Bracteoles scattered on the pedicel, broadly lanceolate, 0.5 mm long, upper margin ciliolate, apex obtuse.

**Flowers**: Bud globose. Flower whitish to yellowish, pentamerous. Sepals valvate, broadly lanceolate, 0.75–1 mm long, apex rounded or obtuse, upper margin ciliolate. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent on the inner surface and margins, linear or narrowly oblong, 3–5 × 1–1.5 mm, apex acute. Stamens 3–4 mm long; filament filiform strongly appressed against the inner surface of petals, 2.5–3 mm long; anther narrowly ellipsoid, 0.75–1 mm long. Pistil 4 mm long; ovary surrounded by an ovoid disc with 5 grooves, 0.75–1 × 0.5–1 mm, semi-inferior, 1-locular containing 3–5 pendulous ovules from a short, thick, erect central placenta; style 3 mm long, terminating in a stigma with 3 or 5 lobes.

**Fruit**: Drupe surrounded by the accrescent pedicel except at the apex bordered by the remains of the sepals in the form of 5 teeth or lobes, ellipsoid, 12–30 × 10–25 mm. Endocarp thin and granular. Seed solitary, ± fused to the endocarp. Albumen white, very abundant.

## Distribution

**Range**: West and Central Africa, from Senegal to Zaïre.

**Nigeria**: Lagos.

**Cameroon**: Kumba; Song Bong SE of Ezéka; 50 km south of Badjob, 60 km SW of Ezéka, left bank of Nyong; Douala; Goura; Muyuka; Ndingue-Koungoulou-Lomié trail, Dja valley; ESE of Campo; Ottotomo; Mimfia; Bipindi.

**Gabon**: Mondah; Libreville area (Ebimanghâ); Makokou; Libreville; Haute Ngounié; Ramboué; Nkoulounga.

## Habitat and ecology

Common tree of dense evergreen forest. Also found along watercourses. Flowers from August to September; fruits mature from September onwards.

## Vernacular names

African boxwood; *inessoma*, *nsoma* (Fang); *oyé m'vone*, *m'ba n'zogo*, *mbazork* (Libreville Fang); *mbeza* (Bakota); *mouguiba* (Eshira); *mbang-mbazoa*, *m'ban m'bazoa*, *bazoa* (Boulou, Yaoundé); *musebi*, *mutsebi* (Bavungu, Bapunu); *bonbongo* (Bibaya Pygmy of Ndingue).

## Properties and uses

Wood and sapwood are poorly differentiated. Sapwood is white-yellowish. Hardwood is whitish to brownish, difficult to use as timber, though it is used to make house posts. Pounded bark mixed with grains of grains of paradise relieves muscle aches and kidney pain.

## Source

Flore du Gabon, Volume 20: 139–141.

## Notes

The source notes that the type specimen (Rowland s.n., Lagos, Nigeria, deposited in K) has been compared with parts of the syntypes of *S. glaucescens* Engl., and no significant differences were found to separate the two species. *S. pustulata* Oliv. is thus retained as the valid name due to its priority.
