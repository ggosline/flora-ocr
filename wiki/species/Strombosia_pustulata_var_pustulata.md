# *Strombosia pustulata* var. *pustulata*

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Strombosia]]

## Description

**Habit**: tree (10)–25–35(–40) m tall. Bole straight and slender, 0.4–1 m in diameter. Bark smooth, grey-brown to pinkish, exfoliating in circular plates 3–4 cm in diameter, whitish in section 4 mm thick. Inner bark smooth or scaly, whitish, greyish to reddish. Young branchlets weakly biwinged, longitudinally striate and densely leafy, becoming smooth and blackish at maturity. 

**Leaves**: petiole glabrous, 8–14 mm long, canaliculate above, wrinkled beneath. Blade glabrous on both faces, pustulate on upper face, dull grey-green on both faces, elliptic, oblong-elliptic, oblong or lanceolate-oblong, 5.5–11.5(–25) × 2–6.5(–18.5) cm; base attenuate (sometimes rounded), apex acuminate obtuse mucronulate. Midrib ± convex on upper face, prominent and longitudinally wrinkled. Secondary nerves 5–9 pairs, ascending, arcuate, attenuating a few millimetres from the margin, with poorly distinct anastomoses, little or not concave on upper face, weakly prominent on lower face. Nervules mostly entirely indistinct on both faces.

**Inflorescence**: axillary fascicle of few flowers on a bract-bearing cushion. Pedicel longitudinally wrinkled, glabrous, 2.5–3 mm long. Bracteoles scattered on the pedicel, broadly lanceolate, 0.5 mm long, upper margin ciliolate, apex obtuse. Flower bud globose.

**Flowers**: whitish to yellowish, pentamerous. Sepals valvate, broadly lanceolate, 0.75–1 mm long, apex rounded or obtuse, upper margin ciliolate. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent on inner face and along the margins of inner face, linear or narrowly oblong, 3–5 × 1–1.5 mm, apex acute. Stamens 3–4 mm long; filament filiform, strongly appressed against the inner face of the petals, 2.5–3 mm long; anther narrowly ellipsoid, 0.75–1 mm long. Pistil 4 mm long; ovary surrounded by an ovoid disc with 5 grooves, 0.75–1 × 0.5–1 mm, semi-inferior with 1 locule containing 3–5 pendulous ovules from a short thick erect central placenta; style 3 mm long, terminating in a stigma with 3 or 5 lobes.

**Fruit**: drupe surrounded by the accrescent pedicel except at the apex bordered by sepals in the form of 5 teeth or lobes, ellipsoid, 12–30 × 10–25 mm. Endocarp thin and granular. Seed solitary, ± fused to the endocarp. Albumen white, very abundant.

## Types

**Holotype**: *Rowland s.n.*, Lagos, Nigeria (K!).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Senegal to Zaire (Democratic Republic of the Congo). A very common tree of dense evergreen forest; also encountered along watercourses.

**Gabon**: Ogooué-Ivindo (Makokou); Haut-Ogounié; Ogooué-Maritime (Libreville region).

## Habitat and ecology

Dense evergreen forest and riparian forest. Flowering from August to September; fruits ripen from September onwards.

## Vernacular names

Buis d'Afrique; inessoma, nsoma (Fang); oyé m'vone, m'ba n'zogo, mbazork (Fang of Libreville); mbeza (Bakota); mouguiba (Eshira); mbang-mbazoa, m'ban m'bazoa, bazoa (Boulou, Yaoundé); musebi, mutsebi (Bavungu, Bapunu); bonbongo (Pygmy Bibaya of Ndingue).

## Uses and properties

The wood and sapwood are poorly differentiated. The sapwood is white-yellowish. The hard wood, whitish to brownish, is a building wood that is difficult to use; however it serves to make house posts. The pounded bark mixed with maniguette pepper seeds relieves muscular soreness and kidney pain.

## Notes

This variety was synonymized with *S. glaucescens* Engl. after comparison of the type with parts of the syntypes; *S. pustulata* Oliver remains the valid name by priority. The treatment notes that *S. glaucescens* is not sufficiently distinct from *S. pustulata* to warrant separation as a distinct species.
