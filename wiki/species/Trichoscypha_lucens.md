# *Trichoscypha lucens* Oliv. Planche 16, 21(G)

**Genus**: [[Trichoscypha]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 1 : 444 (1868)

## Description

Tree reaching 29 m in height, more rarely a lianescent shrub or liana; trunk up to 80 cm in diameter; branchlets puberulent to tomentellous. Leaf: petiole subcylindric, (0.5–)2–12 cm; leaflets (9–)11 to 21(–27), the basal ones sometimes very close to the branch, petiolule 2–15 mm, with oblong lamina, (2–)10–25(–36) × (1–)4–7(–12) cm, cuneate to rounded or subcordate at the base, acuminate for 1–1.5 cm at the apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, with 10 to 16 pairs of lateral veins, ± pustulous and glabrous above in general, except for the impressed and pubescent midvein, glabrous or sparsely puberulent below. Inflorescence subapical or arising below the leaves, in panicles reaching 35 × 25 cm, brown, velutinous. Male flower: pedicel (0–)1–4 mm, puberulent; sepals almost free to half-fused, ± 1 mm long, glabrous to sparsely hairy; petals slightly imbricate, erect to spreading, elliptic, 2–3 mm long, glabrous; stamens slightly longer than the petals, glabrous, anthers 0.5–1 mm long; disk velutinous; pistillode absent. Female flower slightly larger than the male flower; pedicel 1–2 mm; petals erect to reflexed; staminodes shorter than the petals; pistil 2.5 mm long, ovary velutinous; stigmas 4, shortly (≤ 0.5 mm) stipitate. Fruit subellipsoid, up to 3 × 2 cm, pink to dark red at maturity, densely to sparsely hairy.

## Distribution

well distributed in tropical Africa, from Liberia to Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo towards East Africa and in the southeast to Zimbabwe; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

primary and secondarised forest; in Gabon up to 640 m altitude, elsewhere at 50–2000 m

## Uses

The fruits are edible. B: Breteler (2001, 2004). Bibliography
