# *Millettia zechiana* Harms

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 36 : 36 (1907)

## Description

Shrub or tree, up to 10 m tall; branchlets glabrous or nearly so. Stipules caducous. Leaf: petiole 2–5 cm; rachis 17–22 cm, slightly canaliculate; stipels absent; leaflets (9–)13 to 17(–19), petiolule 2–4 mm, blade oblong to lanceolate or ovate, 4–12 × 2.5–5 cm, base acute to obtuse, apex long-acuminate, acumen rounded, glabrous above, with sparse pubescence below especially on the prominent veins in 7 to 10 pairs. Inflorescence axillary and terminal, 10–23 cm long, scarcely branched, puberulent, densely flowered; brachyblasts 1–3 mm long; bracts circular, ± 1–2 mm. Flower: pedicel 3–4 mm; bracteoles circular, ± 1–2 mm; calyx brown-ferruginous, tube 6–9 mm long, lobes 1–2 mm long, the 2 upper ones very short and connate; corolla pink-violet or sometimes white, standard with broadly ovate blade, 18–35 × 15–20 mm, densely silky, with golden to brown appressed hairs on the back, spotted green at the base, wings with oblique-oblong blade, ± 28 mm long, auriculate, glabrous, keel with obovate blade, ± 26 mm long, apex connate, silky with golden to brown hairs on the back; stamens ± 30 mm; disk short; ovary linear, ± 30 mm, silky; stigma small, capitate. Pod linear, sometimes becoming narrowly obovate, flattened, 10–14 × 2–2.5 cm, with 3 to 6 seeds, with short hairs to glabrescent, reticulate, brown, with attenuate base, apex rounded with a small curved beak. Seed flattened, oblique-rounded, ± 15 × 10 × 3 mm, brown.

## Distribution

From Guinea to Cameroon, in Equatorial Guinea and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

ripicoline thickets, secondary forest, savannas, roadsides; in Gabon at 490 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–800 m. Use: Several medicinal uses in West Africa and Cameroon (Banzouzi et al. 2008, Burkill 1995). Planted as a living hedge in West Africa. Bibliography: Banzouzi et al. (2008), Burkill (1995), De la Estrella et al. (2010), Hepper (1958).

sect. TRUNCATICALYCES Dunn J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 41: 137 (1912). Trees, shrubs or sometimes lianes; branches lenticellate, fistulose. Leaves: stipels absent; leaflets with blade sparsely pubescent to velvety below. Inflorescences axillary or sometimes terminal, in pseudoracemes (with flowers in subsessile fascicles) or panicles. Flowers: calyx truncate or with lobes up to 0.5 mm long; standard glabrous or hairy only towards the apex, not auriculate; stamens monadelphous or sometimes diadelphous; disk present, annular. Pod pubescent to velvety. A section with 6 species, distributed from Nigeria to Uganda, of which 3 are present in Gabon and one to be sought. Bibliography: Baker (1929), Dunn (1912), Mollel & Adema (2006).
