# *Lijndenia barteri* (Hooker f.) Bremer

**Genus**: [[Lijndenia]]
**Family**: [[Melastomataceae]]
**Protologue**: Nord. J. Bot. 2 : 124 (1982); Jacq.-Fél., Fl. Cam. 24 : 174 (1983)

## Description

— Memecylon barteri Hook. f., FTA 2 : 462 (1871).

— Spathandra barteri (Hook. f.) JacQ.-Fél., Adansonia, sér. 2, 18 (2) : 226 (1978).

Shrub to small tree of 8 to 10 m; branches rounded. Leaves subcoriaceous; petiole 5 to 10 mm; blade varying from narrowly elliptic-lanceolate and 3 × 8 cm, to broadly elliptic and up to 7 × 14 cm, base rounded, or broadly cuneate and cuneate on the petiole; acumen short, obtuse; upper surface granulate; 3 veins, prominent below; 2 submarginal ones finer, curved upward, sometimes scarcely evident; the transverse veins few, oblique, ± bifurcate or reticulate, scarcely visible.

1 to 5 cymes per axil, on leafy and leafless nodes; varying from 1 to 4 cm in total length, with stipe 0.5 to 2 cm; 2 to 3 times divided; bracts ovate, ± cucullate, persistent on all articulations (sometimes caducous on the lowest ones), including immediately below the flowers

1. Glomerate in species from Madagascar and Asia.

which are sessile. Flowers subglobose or obovoid in bud; hypantho-calyx campanulate, ±2.5 × 2 mm; sepalar lobes developed, semi-ovate to triangular, with thin margin, imbricate and largely overlapping in still young bud. Petals white, subcircular or broadly semi-ovate, clawed, up to 2 × 2 mm. Stamens with anther 1.2 × 1 mm; connective short, conical-obtuse, not saddled, gland very reduced and situated toward the apex, or absent; filament slender, flexuous, 2 to 4.5 mm; epigynous chamber deep, without membranous septa; style linear, 2.8 to 3.5 mm long, stigma truncate to discoid. Ovary slightly prominent at the centre, occupying only one-third or one-half the height of the hypantho-calyx; ovary locule 2-ovulate.

Fruit with pedicel 1 mm, globose, 5 mm in diameter, calyx lobes applied to the ovary. — Pl. 51, p. 143.

## Type

Barter 2152, Nigeria (holo-, K!)

## Discussion

Well-characterized species. Occurs from Senegal (Casamance) to Zaire and, probably, as far as Mozambique. In Gabon, it is to be sought in deciduous thickets rather than in evergreen forest.

## Specimens examined

Klaine 3263, Libreville (mars).
