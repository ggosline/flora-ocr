# *Pilea microphylla* (L.) Liebm.

**Genus**: [[Pilea]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kongel. Danske Vid. Selsk. Skr., ser. 5, naturv.-math. Afd. 2 : 302 (1851)

## Synonyms

- Parietaria microphylla L., Sp. pl. 2 : 1492 (1753).
- Pilea muscosa Lindl., Coll. bot. 1 : t. 4 (1821).

## Description

Annual herb (or possibly short-lived perennial), with erect or procumbent stems, up to 30 cm tall, generally much-branched and forming mats, succulent, glabrous or rarely with a few scattered hairs. Stipules minute, up to 1 mm long, caducous. Leaves of a pair very unequal; petiole 0.5–5 mm, glabrous; blade elliptic to ovate or more often obovate, 3–8 × 0.5–4 mm, base rounded to cuneate, apex rounded to obtuse, with entire margin, smooth and glabrous on both surfaces, with obscure lateral veins, with linear cystoliths very prominent in the epidermis above. Inflorescence in small cymose fascicles and pedunculate or subsessile, 1 or 2 together in the axils, 1–4 mm long, generally with a few male flowers and 5 to 10 female flowers (or entirely female). Flower: sessile or on a pedicel reaching up to 0.5 mm; perianth 0.5–0.8 mm long. Male flower: generally longer than the female, 3-merous; tepals subequal, with a subapical swelling. Female flower with one long tepal and two lateral ones smaller. Fruit ovoid, 0.5–0.8 mm long, smooth, brown.

## Distribution

Probably indigenous only to Central America, but today introduced and widely naturalized in the tropical and subtropical regions of the World; in West Africa, from Senegal to Nigeria, Cameroon and Democratic Republic of the Congo, widely distributed in East Africa, from Kenya to South Africa (Kwazulu-Natal); in Gabon, known only from Ngounié

## Ecology

Sometimes cultivated in gardens, or also as a weed, in humid places, naturalized on stone walls and between paving stones, sometimes between rocks bordering streams; in Gabon at about 100 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–1000 m

## Source note

This species is documented with a collection from Mouila, in Ngounié. Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961) report a species which they name Parietaria debilis Forst., and state that it is common on rocks, old walls, cemeteries and villages. They cite numerous vernacular names and the use of this plant. Parietaria debilis is a species of montane forests and afroalpine vegetation, at 1100–4400 m altitude. It is then unlikely that the plants of Raponda-Walker and Sillans from Gabon so named represent this species and in the absence of collections, it is difficult to identify it with certainty, but their indication of habitats corresponds very well to those where Pilea microphylla is found. If such is the case, P. microphylla is much more widespread in Gabon than what the single collection indicates. It remains uncertain whether the vernacular names and uses cited by Raponda-Walker & Sillans apply entirely or partly to P. microphylla. B: Letouzey (1968), Friis (1989a, 1989c). Bibliography
