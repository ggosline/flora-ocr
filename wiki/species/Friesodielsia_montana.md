## Description

Sarmentose shrub or liane with black bark, reaching 4-7 m in length and 1.5 cm in diameter; branchlets glabrous, ultimate twigs puberulent.

Leaves with short petiole, 3-6 mm, puberulent; blade obovate-oblong to oblong, 5-20 cm long, 2.5-8.5 cm wide, rounded to subcordate at base, obtuse or shortly acuminate at apex; upper surface glabrous, lower surface sparsely covered with appressed hairs, denser on the prominent midvein. Lateral nerves 8-15 pairs; nervules parallel.

Flowers violet, extra-axillary, solitary or in unipare cymes of 2-3 flowers, with very short peduncle, 1-2 mm; pedicel 2-4 cm long, appressed-pubescent, bearing towards the base a very small lanceolate, pubescent bract. Floral buds subglobose. Sepals green, triangular-acute to lanceolate, 2.5 mm long, 1 mm wide, densely pubescent on the outside. External petals purple, elliptic-ovate, narrowed at base, 10-20 mm long, 6-12 mm wide, densely puberulent on the outside and on the inner margins, with well-visible longitudinal nerves, reflexed at anthesis; inner petals triangular-ovate, cordate-auricate and unguiculate at base, about 10 mm long, 8-9 mm wide, glabrous, yellow, tinged with red at the base. Stamens 42-50, cuneiform, 1 mm long, 1.5 mm wide, thecae oblong extrorse, connectives enlarged-flattened above the anthers. Carpels 11-12, 2 mm long, 0.5 mm wide, ovaries oblong, cylindric, densely pubescent, stigmas sessile, small, capitate, glabrous. Ovules 2 (3), lateral.

Fruits with slender pedicel, 3-5 cm long; mericarps cylindric, stipitate, red at maturity, strongly contracted between the seeds 1-3; articles ellipsoid-oblong, 11-14 mm long, 5 mm wide, puberulent to glabrous, the terminal one apiculate; stipites 3-5 mm long. Seeds oblong, ± 10 mm long, 4.5 mm wide.

## Discussion

F. montana is very close to F. discostigma (Diels) van Steenis, a species known only from Cameroon. The latter possesses thicker leaves, a shorter floral pedicel, 1 cm only, somewhat larger sepals, obovate petals and especially carpels with puberulent disciform stigmas, and 5 ovules.

It is a species of wet dense riparian forest, widespread from southern Nigeria to the Central African Republic and Congo-Kinshasa.

This species is particularly recognizable by the bluish appearance of the lower surface of the leaf blade, the russet-hirsute piloseness of the ultimate twigs, and the size of the flowers. On a specimen from the Central African Republic, Sillans had described it as a new species of Popowia: P. Mangenotii. Comparison of the holotype with that of Friesodielsia grandiflora (Boutique) Steen., described a few months earlier, revealed to us the conspecificity of these two taxa.

After proposing a concolor form for specimens whose lower leaf surface is brown, like the upper surface, Sillans rightfully abandoned this form, observing that there exists a complete range of intermediates between the bluish colour, very characteristic of the lower surface, and the brown colour when dried.

During the printing of this volume, Mr. Verdcourt kindly communicated to us the results of his research on this species and we owe to him having drawn our attention to the identity of F. grandiflora and Popowia Enghiana.

F. Enghiana belongs to the group of West African species: F. obanensis, F. hirsuta, F. velutina, which Sprague and Hutchinson (1916) already considered as 'another type', in assigning them to the genus Oxymitra. The form and arrangement of the inner petals, not connivate at the apex, as well as the stamens with lateral thecae, indeed distinguish these four species from the type of Friesodielsia represented in the two other Gabonese species. The species Enghiana is moreover particularly remarkable for its stamens with very short anthers, and connective truncate above them, slightly prolonged towards the centre of the flower.

F. Enghiana is a liane of wet dense forest, very common in the understory, where it is most often encountered in the form of a small sterile under-shrub, with large young leaves clearly bluish on the lower surface. It is distributed in Sierra Leone, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Gabon and Congo-Kinshasa.
