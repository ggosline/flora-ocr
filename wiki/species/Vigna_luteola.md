# *Vigna luteola* (Jacq.) Benth. Planche 127

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: V. oblonga auct., Baker in Oliv., Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 206 (1871), non Benth. (1844)

## Description

in Mart., Fl. bras. 15 : 194 (1859). V. oblonga auct., Baker in Oliv., Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 206 (1871), non Benth. (1844). V. marina auct., Baker in Oliv., Fl. trop. Afr. 2 : 206 (1871), non (Burm.) Merr. (1917). V. marina (Burm.) Merr. subsp. oblonga auct., Padulosi, Bull. Jard. Bot. Nat. Belg. 122 (1993), non s.s. Perennial herb, trailing or climbing, practically glabrous. Stipules 3–4 × 2 mm, biauricululate at base. Leaf: petiole 3.5–8 cm; rachis 1–1.5 cm; stipels oblong, 1.5–2 mm long; leaflets with petiolule 2–4 mm, blade ovate-oblong, 4–10 × 1.5–5 cm, rounded at base, acuminate to acute at apex, glabrous with some hairs on the main vein below. Inflorescence: peduncle glabrous, (3–)6–18 cm; rachis 1.5–5 cm; bracts caducous, ovate-lanceolate, ± 1.5 mm × 0.5 mm. Flower: bracteoles at base of calyx, ovate, ± 1.5 × 1 mm; calyx glabrous to sparsely pilose, tube 2–4 mm long, lobes deltoid, 1–1.5 mm long; corolla yellow, 12–17 mm long, standard suborbicular, ± 13 × 17 mm, emarginate at apex, wings obovate, auriculate and clawed, 16 × 5 mm, keel curved, 15 × 9 mm, with a short beak; ovary with 7 to 12 ovules; style extended beyond the internal stigma by a short beak. Pod pendulous, subcylindric, somewhat curved and torulose, 4–8 cm × 4–6.5 mm, terminated by a short beak, with 7 or 8 seeds, black. Seed ellipsoid, 3–6 × 3–4 × 2.5 mm; hilum nearly central, 1.5 mm long; strophiole slight, central.

## Distribution

Pantropical; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Nyanga, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Vegetation of marine and lacustrine coastal areas, sands, Papyrus swamps and swamp forests; in Gabon at 0–500 m elevation, elsewhere up to 2200 m. Use: An excellent forage for goats and sheep. Roasted seeds could replace coffee among the Fang. In Ethiopia and Malawi, use as a vegetable is reported, the boiled seeds being edible. Children in Malawi dig up the tuberous roots and chew them to extract sweet juice.

## Vernacular names

haricot sauvage (fr) ; hairypod cowpea, deer pea (angl) ; adong (fang)

## Source note

The description by Pellegrin (1949) and Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961) of V. oblonga refers to V. luteola. The latter, from Atlantic Ocean beaches, was often determined as V. marina (Burm.) Merr., because of its thicker leaflets (halophyte syndrome), but V. marina is found only on the coasts of the Indian Ocean and Pacific Ocean and its seeds are larger, 5.5–7 × 4.5–6 × 4–5 mm.
