# *Dalbergia hostilis* Benth. Planche 40

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: J. Linn. Soc. 4, Suppl. : 33 (1860)

## Description

Shrub or liana reaching 10 m in length and 6 cm in diameter, inodorous, furnished with strong spines (1–3.5 cm in length) on the trunk and old branches, and often also with prehensile hook-like branches; bark brown with pale lenticels; young branches with very short crisp pubescence. Stipules very early caducous, falciform, ± 5 × 1.5 mm, densely pubescent. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 1–2.4 cm; rachis 3.4–11.6 cm, shortly pubescent or glabrescent; leaflets 10 to 21, alternate and slightly unequal, with petiolule of 1–3 mm, limb elliptic or slightly obovate, symmetric or nearly so, 1.1–3.8 × 0.5–1.7 cm in the upper leaflets and 0.7–2.8 × 0.3–1.6 cm in the lower ones, acute or rounded at the base, rounded or emarginate at the apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, olive green to dark brown when dry, with short appressed and very sparse pubescence on both faces or sometimes glabrous on the upper face; lateral veins 8 to 14, very inconspicuous and scarcely distinct from the veinlets, veinlets in dense dark network and generally very apparent on the lower face (except on old leaves), with meshes of ± 0.3 mm. Inflorescence in a panicle often leafy at the base and branched into contracted cymes, terminal or rarely axillary, with very numerous flowers, (2.5–)10–30 cm in length, with short and dense pubescence; bracts caducous after flowering, broadly elliptic, ± 0.5 × 0.3 mm, pubescent externally. Flower very small; pedicel 0–0.5 mm; bracteoles persistent, orbicular to elliptic, 0.4–0.7 × 0.3–0.4 mm, pubescent; calyx pale green, with scattered appressed hairs, tube 1.5–2 × 0.7–1 mm, teeth imbricate and unequal, the upper and lateral ones rounded 0.3–0.5 mm in length, the lower one longer and acute, 0.5–1 mm in length; corolla white, petals with claw shorter than the calyx, standard 2.5–2.7 × 1.5–2 mm, slightly and very obliquely bent, ± folded lengthwise, with very short claw (± 0.3 mm) and obcordate limb with auriculate base, wings equalling the keel, with claw of 0.8 mm and elliptic or rectangular limb of 1.5 × 0.7 mm, with truncate base, smooth externally and furnished with an internal thickening on the upper margin, keel almost straight, with claw of 0.5–0.7 mm and limb of 1.8 × 1 mm, with truncate base and furnished with a pocket on the upper margin; stamens 9, monadelphous, filaments 1.7–2 mm, fused on ¾ of their length, ± straight at the base and curved in their free part; ovary ± 1 × 0.5 mm, glabrous or rarely with very sparse appressed hairs, with 2 ovules and stipe of 0.5 mm; style absent or nearly so, the stigma oblique-laminate. Legume with pedicel of 0.5–1 mm and stipe of 4–8 mm, papyraceous, flattened and narrowly ellipsoid, 3.6–7.7 × 1.1–2.1 cm, acute at the base, acute or rounded at the apex, with slightly depressed reticulation and with a paler zone well differentiated around the seed (slightly concave on one side and convex on the other), glabrous, never glaucous, with 1 or 2 seeds. Seed reniform and flattened, ± 6 × 3.5 mm.

## Distribution

Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, recorded only in Woleu-Ntem, in the vicinity of Oyem and Mitzic

## Ecology

Forest galleries, shrubby savannas, forest regrowth, and particularly dry thickets on rocky outcrops; altitude not recorded in Gabon, elsewhere up to 1020 m. In Gabon, flowering in February and July. Use: None recorded in Gabon. In Côte d'Ivoire, the plant is used in the treatment of eye ailments. Other uses are recorded elsewhere in Africa (Burkill 1995), but it is difficult to know whether they truly relate to this species, or to D. gilletii (see Notes)

## Source note

D. hostilis has long been confused with D. gilletii, although the two species show numerous differences (Lachenaud & Maesen 2016 and specific key). The illustration of D. hostilis in the Flora of Central Africa, and most of the specimens cited in that work (Cronquist 1954) actually represent D. gilletii; the same is true for part of the West African specimens cited under D. hostilis by Hepper (1958). b: Adam (1971), Cronquist (1954; see notes), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hepper (1958; see notes), Lachenaud & Maesen (2016), Pellegrin (1949)
