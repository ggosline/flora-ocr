Annual herbs of soft texture, reaching 30 cm in height, densely branched from the base. Main stem erect, soon arrested in growth by the dominance of basal branching, the lowest of which are decumbent and curve upward at their tips. Stems rather slender, striate, finely pubescent, green or faintly tinged with violet near their base. Hairs without glands, spreading, slender, reaching 1.5 mm in length, whitish, hyaline, with a broad and swollen base on the ribs.

Leaves trifoliolate, long-petiolate, progressively smaller toward the stem apex; leaflets thin, dark olive above, somewhat paler below, sessile or subsessile, acute and mucronulate, unequal; median leaflet symmetric, broadly lanceolate to subrhombic, 5.5 × 2.5 cm, with an acute base; lateral leaflets somewhat smaller, disymmetric, subrhombic, outer half often ovate, inner half often obovate. Midvein prominent above, depressed below; lateral veins 5–7. Lower surface of leaflets bearing a few hairs especially on the principal vein and others; upper surface glabrous or with regularly spaced hairs between glabrous veins. Petioles 3–5.5 cm long for the lower leaves, progressively shorter toward the stem apex, faintly canaliculate on the upper surface, with sparse pubescence on both faces. Stipules very small, glandiform, ovate.

Flowers solitary in the axils of leaves, never forming developed racemes; flowers and vegetative shoots alternating at the level of lower axils while the upper ones bear only flowers. Pedicels 1–2.5 cm long at flowering, slender and ascending, 1.8–2.5 (–3.6) cm at fruiting, much shorter than the subtending leaf in the case of lower flowers, while they exceed it near the apex of large shoots; pedicels very finely puberulent above and most often glabrous below. Sepals narrowly lanceolate to subulate, subequal, free, 2–2.5 mm long for a maximum width of 0.5 mm, dorsal face finely puberulent, green or tinged with violet along the margins, somewhat spreading at anthesis, early deciduous. Petals white, pale pink or sometimes tinged with violet, white ones 6–10 × 1.5–3 mm, acute or briefly mucronate, lower half gradually narrowed to a basal claw equal to or slightly shorter than the sepals; these petals are ascending; the upper (inner) pair bears a faint, white or yellowish spot bordered with violet near the claw. Androgynophore absent. Stamens 6, not exserted, all fertile, inclined, the lower pair somewhat larger than the others; filaments fine and gracile, glabrous, whitish and hyaline, 4–6 (–9) mm long, free from the base, deciduous; anthers linear, 1.5–2.5 (–3) mm long before dehiscence, dorsibasifix, early deciduous. Gynophore 7–12 mm long, shorter than the pedicel at fruiting (1/3–1/2 its length), glabrous or sometimes bearing a few very small hairs, slender, directed in line with the pedicel. Ovary linear, somewhat swollen on its sides, attenuate at both ends, glabrous with sometimes a few glands and a few very small hairs; stigma subsessile, truncate.

Capsule linear, thin, 45–55 × 3–4 mm, often attenuate at the base, acuminate at the apex, green at maturity, straight or slightly curved, spreading horizontally or slightly nodding at maturity; replum glabrous; valves glabrous or sometimes bearing very small hairs along 7–8 distinct, abundantly anastomosed nerves; these nerves are much narrower than the sections they delimit; fruit walls uniformly thin at each point, no rib-like thickening among the nerves. Style subnull or reaching 2.5 cm in length, glabrous, straight; stigma truncate, very small but somewhat wider than the apex of the style. Seeds suborbicular-reniform in lateral view, somewhat compressed laterally, 1.75 mm in diameter; hilar slit reaching the middle of the seed, most often covered by a deciduous, whitish, hyaline elaiosome of soft texture; seed coat dark brown to blackish at maturity, ornamented by a dense system of barely marked longitudinal striations and by prominent transverse ridges generally continuous except for a few that are interrupted, upper surface bearing very fine puberulence that disappears with age. – Pl. 2, p. 19.

## Discussion

This species is closely related to C. iberidella Welw. ex. Oliv. and to C. schimperi Pax. All three very probably derive from a common ancestor from which evolution has led to the production of three taxa that are probably still interfertile. However, the number of specimens combining the characters of two of them is truly small: their chances of crossing are greatly reduced by the differences in their geographic distributions and respective ecological requirements. It is not without interest to note that the few specimens of supposed hybrids, or hybrid descendants, come from localities with disturbed soils. There is very little chance that C. rutidosperma and C. schimperi have ever met in nature. This latter species, limited to high altitudes, is unknown in Gabon. Atypical characters sometimes encountered in C. rutidosperma always lead to considering the possibility of gene exchange with C. iberidella. It is not easy to clearly distinguish taxa between which hybridization and introgression phenomena have probably occurred. Consequently, specimens showing only relatively weak expression of aberrant characters were classified with typical C. rutidosperma; those in which these aberrant characters were distinctly masked were treated separately as "intermediate forms" between C. rutidosperma and C. iberidella. This latter category is represented in Gabon by only a single collection.

Typical specimens of C. rutidosperma have white or pale pinkish petals and a glabrous ovary. The atypical characters are: more abundant hairiness on the stems and leaflets, pubescent capsules, more vividly colored petals with the upper (inner) petal more distinctly spotted near the base. Specimens showing atypical characters are more frequent at higher altitudes and do not exist in coastal regions. The majority of collections were made near the coast where the species is probably common at least near inhabited places; the few interior localities are very widely dispersed. This species prefers humid, humus-rich sandy soils; it may be nitrophilous; it has been reported as a frequent weed of roadsides and parks in cities on the west coast of Africa. It often inhabits semi-shaded places but is also found in full sun. C. rutidosperma is quite common in the western coastal regions of Africa from Senegal to northern Angola; still frequent in the Congo Basin, it has also occasionally been collected in East tropical Africa. It has been introduced as a weed in coastal stations of very diverse tropical regions (Central America, Antilles, Sri Lanka, Malaysia).

Spécimen intermédiaire entre C. rutidosperma et C. iberidella :

Ce spécimen ressemble à C. rutidosperma par son feuillage, le gynophore allongé (9-10 mm) et glabre, la taille modeste des pétales (5-6 mm), les filets staminaux longs de 5-6 mm, légèrement inclinés, les anthères longues de 1,5 mm. La capsule porte un style court mais net, long de 1-2 mm ; les valves sont relativement pauvres en nervures (5 à 8). Ce matériel diffère du C. rutidosperma typique par son ovaire elliptique et poilu, par l'indument du pédicelle, la pilosité des nervures du fruit, le petit nombre d'anastomoses sur les valves du fruit et les épaississements longitudinaux de la paroi du fruit toujours visibles quoique

faiblement marqués (ceci est peut-être lié à la jeunesse du fruit). Les pétales sont rosés dans leur moitié supérieure. Les pédicelles portent des poils rougeâtres et les sépales sont teintés de rouge. De plus, les crêtes transversales des valves sont généralement interrompues et dépourvues de pubérulence. Ces caractères aberrants indiquent une affinité avec C. iberidella. Ces spécimens peuvent être des hybrides, des descendants d'hybrides ou le résultat de divers croisements avec les formes parentales (back-cross). Des cas semblables ont aussi été rencontrés au Cameroun. Un examen superficiel peut conduire à confondre ce matériel avec C. schimperi. Il en diffère cependant par un gynophore long et glabre, par son style distinct, la nervation assez pauvre des fruits et des anthères plus longues. (Pour une description détaillée de C. schimperi, on se reportera à la Flore du Cameroun).

C. rutidosperma et C. iberidella croissent en général dans des stations différentes car leurs exigences écologiques ne sont pas les mêmes ; leurs répartitions respectives, par conséquent, coincident rarement. On peut les rencontrer ensemble, occasionnellement, dans la partie Est du Gabon, sur un sol modifié, dans des stations rudérales, etc. Malheureusement cette intéressante récolte ne porte ni indication précise de localité ni données stationnelles.
