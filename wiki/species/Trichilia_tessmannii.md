# *Trichilia tessmannii* Harms Planche 22

**Genus**: [[Trichilia]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 46 : 162 (1911)

## Description

T. le-testui Pellegr., Not. Syst . (Paris) 9(1) : 20 (1940). Tree 10–20(–30) m tall; trunk cylindrical, up to 40 cm in diameter; cross-section cream-rose colored, exuding a little yellowish latex; old branches with distinct scars of fallen leaves and bark with rhytidome exfoliating in thin rectangular rusty plates. Leaf: petiole 5–14(–17) cm, tomentose-hirsute, tawny; rachis (2–)7–25(–30) cm; petiolules 1–5(–10) mm; leaflets (5–)9–15(–21), opposite to subopposite, narrowly elliptic to oval or obovate, (3–)7–17(–20) × 2–6(–8) cm, obtuse to subcordate at base, acuminate-acute at apex, glossy, densely villous and with prominent venation beneath; lateral veins 12–26 pairs, distinct, straight and parallel, depressed above. Inflorescence in the axil of terminal leaves, (3–)9–20(–26) cm long; bracts villous, caducous. Functionally male flower: pedicel up to 7 mm; receptacle 1–2.5 mm long; calyx 4–9 × 4–6 mm, tomentose outside; petals narrowly rectangular, 9–20(–29) × 2–3 mm, yellow, velvety outside; staminal crown (7–)12–18 mm long, with free portion of filaments 4–7 mm, densely villous inside and on margins, rest nearly glabrous, anthers obovate, 1.3–2 × 0.7–10 mm; pistillode filiform, 11–15 mm long, villous. Functionally female flower similar; anthers reduced; ovary ovoid, ribbed at base, 3 mm in diameter, densely villous, 3(–4)-locular; style 6.5–10 mm, stigma capitate. Fruit subglobose to pyriform, 2–4 cm in diameter, with a distinct stipe 0.5–1 cm, superficially 3(–4)-lobed, tomentose. Seed approximately 2 × 1–1.5 cm, with black, glossy testa, modified for the greater part into a red-orange sarcotesta.

## Distribution

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Angola (Cabinda) and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, rather rare and known from Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

restricted to dense humid lowland forests, not abundant; in Gabon and elsewhere, up to 500 m altitude.

## Uses

The wood has been used for construction. The bark has been used as a laxative and against stomach problems. Taxonomic note: This species shows extremely variable indumentum; from nearly glabrous to a dense layer of hairs. B: Aubréville (1959), Burkill (1997), Staner & Gilbert (1958), de Wilde (1968).
