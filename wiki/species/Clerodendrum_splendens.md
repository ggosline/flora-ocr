# *Clerodendrum splendens* G.Don Figure 3(E, F)

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Edinburgh Philos. J. 11 : 349 (1824)

## Description

C. splendens G.Don var. gilletii (De Wild. & T.Durand) B.Thomas, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 68 : 58 (1963). Robust liane, sarmentose, or shrub reaching 5 m in height; stem subcylindrical to subquadrangular, faintly striate, sometimes twisted, solid or hollow, pale brown to dark brown or reddish, glabrous, generally with pseudospines of 3–7(–13) mm. Leaves generally opposite, some subopposite, but not decussate; petiole 0.5–1 cm, glabrous; blade narrowly to broadly ovate or narrowly to broadly elliptic, 4.5–11.5(–18.5) × 3.5–7(–13) cm, rounded to cordate or rarely subcuneate at base, broadly acuminate at apex, with triangular acumen of ± 1(–1.5) cm, entire, coriaceous, glabrous except for nerves beneath. Inflorescence terminal or very rarely axillary, lax, paniculate, 5–11(–14.5) × 6–18 cm; cymes with 10 to 20 flowers, distant by (2–)4–6(–8) cm, often the two pairs of apical cymes distant by < 0.5 cm and the lowest distant by 3–5 cm; peduncle 1–6(–22) cm, pubescent; bracts foliaceous, up to 6 × 4 cm; bracteoles filiform to narrowly elliptic-ovate, 1–8 mm long. Flower: pedicel 3–6(–12) mm; calyx with campanulate tube, 3–5 × 3–4 mm, with triangular lobes, 3–5 × 1–3 mm, shortly acuminate, glabrous, green, pink to pink-purple at maturity; corolla with tube 1–1.8 × 0.1–0.15 cm, red, with obovate lobes, 0.5–1.1(–1.3) × 0.4–0.8 cm, red or scarlet; stamens exserted by 1.5–3.3 cm. Fruit ± 10 × 12 mm, black, glossy.

## Distribution

Tropical West Africa, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Angola and Burundi; introduced to Bangladesh and the Caribbean; in Gabon: all provinces. Conservation status: preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC) by Pollard in Cheek et al. (2004)

## Ecology

Secondary forests, forest margins, swamp forests, Raphia swamps, roadsides, broken ground, on disturbed soils; in Gabon at 5–600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1400 m. Uses: This beautiful ornamental climbing plant is used for trellises and pergolas. The populations of the Republic of the Congo use the leaves to produce a black coloring matter, as for C. umbellatum

## Vernacular names

bleeding heart vine, flaming glorybower, pagoda flower (angl.); bedembwe (saké) ; belbayem (fang) ; elelem (makina). B : Burkill (2000), Huber et al. (1963), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Vande weghe et i B liographie al. (2016)
