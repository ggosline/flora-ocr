## Description

Bulb subglobose, 1–4.5 cm in diameter, often with a prolonged rhizomatous portion. Leaves present simultaneously with flowers, arranged in a basal rosette, submerged, shiny, dark green, often semi-transparent, unable to support themselves and floating like ribbons in the current, ribbon-shaped, reaching 140 cm in length, 1–5 cm wide; blade distinctly undulate or not, with a more or less prominent midvein, margin slightly denticulate.

Flowering scape erect, green, paler towards the base, 20–75 cm long. Bracts membranous, pale whitish-green, erect or drooping, subtending 1–5 flowers sessile to subsessile, faintly or pleasantly scented, emerging a few cm to 30 cm above the water surface. Perianth tube greenish, erect, 11–18 cm long; segments pure white or with a cream dorsal stripe, spreading or drooping, narrowly lanceolate, 5–9 cm long, 0.9–1.6 cm wide. Filaments white, curved, arranged radially, slightly shorter than the segments. Anthers dark green, greenish-brown to black, 1–2 cm long. Style greenish, exceeding the anthers.

Fruit at water level or submerged, green or reddish, shiny, globose, with a beak of highly variable length, 0.5–2 (–6) cm. Seeds green, irregular, 5–20 per fruit.

## Distribution

**Range**: West Africa from Guinea eastward to Cameroon, and south to Zaïre (now Democratic Republic of the Congo).

**Gabon**: Mboundou (37 km SE of Makokou), Abanga, Monts de Cristal (Sanga River), Kanango region (Kambo).

## Habitat and ecology

Submerged in running water, not very deep, firmly rooted in sandy or rocky soil between rocks. Occurs from sea level to about 650 m altitude. Associated with littoral forest, biafran forest, and congolese forest types. Different ecological forms are recognized: a coastal form with strongly undulate leaves mostly broader than 2 cm with a very prominent midvein, and a more common continental form at low to higher altitudes with leaves less than 1.5 cm wide, non-undulate and lacking a prominent midvein. These characters remain constant when plants are cultivated in aquariums under equivalent conditions, indicating they are genetically fixed. Transitional forms occur in nature.

## Notes

The lectotype is Mann 1416 from Fernando Pô, housed at K with isotypes at P. The treatment recognizes two distinct ecological forms differing in leaf morphology and width, interpreted as ecotypes without formal taxonomic rank rather than as infraspecific varieties.
