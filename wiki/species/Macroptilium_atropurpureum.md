# *Macroptilium atropurpureum* (Moç. & Sessé ex DC.) Urb. Planche 83.1–7

**Genus**: [[Macroptilium]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Symb. antill. 9 : 457 (1928)

## Synonyms

- Phaseolus atropurpureus Moç. & Sessé ex DC., Prodr. 2 : 395 (1825).

## Description

Herb procumbent; stem reaching 120 cm, densely hairy with retrorse hairs. Stipules 4–5 × 2 mm, distinctly nerved. Leaf: petiole 2.5–5 cm; leaflets (1–)3, with rhomboidal to ovate blade, 2.5–4 × 2–2.5 cm, with shortly lobed base, with acuminate apex, lateral lobes of lower leaves with square base, leaves further along the plant with ovate to narrowly ovate blade and sometimes 1-foliolate, with light and short pubescence above, grey tomentose below; lateral nerves in 4(–6) pairs. Inflorescence: peduncle 10–25 cm; rachis 4–8 cm; bracts acute, ± 5 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 2 mm; bracteoles acute, ± 3 mm in length; calyx with tube 5–6 mm in length, lobes acute, free for ± 3 mm; corolla curved in bud, dark purplish red, wings densely coloured, standard reflexed, obovate to rounded, with claw 6 mm, blade ± 11 × 11 mm, wings 23 × 10 mm, keel petals linear-naviculate, ± 23 mm in length; ovary 10 mm in length, with numerous ovules; style 11 mm. Pod linear, slightly reflexed at apex, 7–10 × 0.3–0.5 cm, apex acute/attenuate, with up to 12(–15) seeds, grey-tomentose. Seed oblong-ellipsoid, ± 4 × 3 × 2 mm, brown, marbled with black; hilum central, 1 mm in length.

## Distribution

of American origin, cultivated and naturalized in tropical Africa, Asia, Australia and the Pacific, in Africa, especially in the East and South; to be sought in Gabon

## Ecology

fallows, fields, herbaceous savannas, roadsides; in Africa up to 1650 m in altitude. Use: Used as fodder and green manure, also against erosion

## Vernacular names

macroptilium à fleur pourpre, siratro (fr.) ; purple bean, purple bushbean, siratro (angl.). b : Anonymous (1975), Lewis et al. (2005), Mackinder et al. (2001). ibliographie
