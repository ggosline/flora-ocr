# *Manniella gustavi* Rchb.f.

**Genus**: [[Manniella]]
**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Otia Bot. Hamburg. 2 : 109 (1881)

## Synonyms

- Manniella gustavi Rchb. f. var. picta De Wild., Not. Fig. Ut. Inter. Congo 1: 153 (1903).

## Description

Stem < 100 cm, glabrous at base, glandular above. Leaves forming a rosette, 4.5–16 × 2.5–7 cm, lamina elliptic to broadly elliptic-acuminate, silvery green spotted on the upper surface. Spike 24–38 cm, 20–80-flowered. Flowers pink to pinkish-brown. Dorsal sepal ovate, obtuse; lateral sepals fused at the base to the column foot and to the dorsal sepal; free part oblong to spatulate, subobtuse, falciform. Petals oblong, slightly asymmetric. Labellum borne on a long and narrow claw fused to the sepals, rectangular to broadly ovate in appearance, channeled, neither lobed nor constricted, very thick and pubescent at the centre, outer surface finely ciliate, basal appendages digitate, thick. – Pl. 36, p. 79.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Guinée, Côté d'Ivoire, Liberia, Ghana, Nigeria, Guinée équatoriale, Sao Tome, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Centrafrique, Congo (Kinshasa), Ouganda, Tanzania

## Ecology

alt. 0–1200 m. Primary forest, dense humid lowland forest and montane foothill forest

IV. NEOTTIOIDEAE Lindl.

Coll. Bot. App. (1826).

Veil absent. Stem calamiform or two-leaved. Leaves convolute, plicate to subplicate, sometimes absent. Inflorescence terminal. Gynostemium erect. Staminodes digitate, partly fused to the filament or with the apex of the style and filament forming a dorsal clinandrium. Rostellum similar to the 2 lateral lobes of the stigma, or triangular and subacute, erect, sometimes producing a sticky semi-liquid viscidia. Hamulus and tegula absent. Anther erect to suberect, ovate or elliptic, mobile. Pollinia pulverulent, cylindric to ovate.

Relictual group divided into 2 tribes totaling 7 genera, the majority from North-temperate zones. In tropical Africa, two genera, Diceratostele Summerh. and Epipactis Zinn., have been encountered in Cameroon, whereas only the first was collected in Gabon.
