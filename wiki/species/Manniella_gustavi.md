# *Manniella gustavi* Rchb.f.

**Genus**: [[Manniella]]
**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Otia Bot. Hamburg. 2 : 109 (1881)

## Description

- Manniella gustavi Rchb. f. var. picta De Wild., Not. Fig. Ut. Inter. Congo 1: 153 (1903).

Stem < 100 cm, glabrous at the base, glandular above. Leaves forming a rosette, 4.5–16 × 2.5–7 cm, lamina elliptic to broadly elliptic-acuminate, silvery green spotted on the upper surface. Spike 24–38 cm, 20–80-flowered. Flowers pink to pinkish-brown. Dorsal sepal ovate, obtuse; lateral sepals fused at the base to the foot of the column and to the dorsal sepal; free part oblong to spatulate, subobtuse, falcate. Petals oblong, somewhat asymmetrical. Lip borne on a long and narrow claw fused to the sepals, rectangular to broadly ovate in appearance, channeled, neither lobed nor constricted, very thick and pubescent in the center, external surface finely ciliate, basal appendages digitate, thick. – Pl. 36, p. 79.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, Ghana, Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, São Tomé, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Central African Republic, Congo (Kinshasa), Uganda, Tanzania

## Ecology

alt. 0–1200 m. Primary forest, dense humid lowland forest and montane foothills forest

IV. NEOTTIOIDEAE Lindl.

Coll. Bot. App. (1826).

Veil absent. Stem calamiform or bifoliate. Leaves convolute, folded to subfolded, sometimes absent. Inflorescence terminal. Gynostemium erect. Staminodes digitate, partly fused to the filament or with the apex of the style and filament forming a dorsal clinandrium. Rostellum similar to the 2 lateral lobes of the stigma, or triangular and subacute, erect, sometimes producing a semi-liquid sticky viscidium. Hamulus and tegula absent. Anther erect to suberect, ovate or elliptic, mobile. Pollinia pulverulent, cylindric to ovate.

Relict group divided into 2 tribes totaling 7 genera mostly from North-temperate zones. In tropical Africa, two genera, Diceratostele Summerh. and Epipactis Zinn., have been encountered in Cameroon, whereas only the first has been collected in Gabon.
