Medium-sized tree reaching a height of 30 m. Trunk cylindrical, elevated, fairly straight, furnished with alate buttresses at the base, with a diameter varying from 40 to 90 (120) cm. Bark smooth, grey to greenish-grey (light brown to light reddish-brown in KEAY, ONOCHIE and STANFIELD, l.c), with rhytidome exfoliating in irregular plates leaving reddish depressions, cross-section thick 5-8 mm, dark pink to garnet-red.

Sapwood yellowish. Large oblique branches. Crown wide and rather flattened; foliage fine. Young branchlets olive-green with pale brown lenticels becoming brown on the living plant; ± angular; shortly densely puberulent yellowish on the dry state ± ridged. Older branchlets, brown on the dry state, glabrous, longitudinally wrinkled. Axillary buds globose ovoid, densely puberulent. Stipules densely pubescent, very narrowly triangular, 3.5-6 mm long, caducous. Petiole glabrous to finely puberulent, sometimes with lenticels, yellowish to orange on the living plant and dry state, 3-6 cm long; glands solitary or geminate between the midpoint and the base; flat or weakly keeled above. Rachis shortly pubescent beneath; dark brown beneath and yellowish above to orange on both surfaces on the living plant; ± cylindric to dorsiventrally flattened to ± winged; 12-24 cm long; apex with 1-2 glands between the insertions of the upper pinnae, upper surface keeled; lower surface sometimes with lenticels. Pinnae 12-22 pairs, oblong, opposite, sub-opposite to almost alternate (at the base). Pinnae axis finely pubescent, 3.5-8.5 cm long, lower surface hemicylindric, upper surface keeled, prominent gland at the apex between the terminal pinnules above. Leaflets 10-32 pairs, sessile, glabrous on both surfaces; light to dark green glossy above and pale green beneath on the living plant, glossy brown above and green beneath on the dry state; asymmetrically elliptic-oblong, 5-9 × 1.25-2.5 mm; base unequal: proximal margin auricate truncate and distal margin rounded; apex obtuse to weakly retuse (sometimes acute). Midrib prominent on both surfaces. Basal secondary nerves prominent above, very slightly or not prominent beneath; that of the proximal 1/2, long ± parallel to the margin of the blade. Other secondary nerves scarcely or not visible. Tertiary nerves not visible.

Inflorescences in solitary pendulous terminal capitula or in panicles of pendulous terminal capitula. Peduncle glabrous, up to 32 cm long. Rachis claviform, cream-white on the fresh plant, cylindric base 1.5-2.5 cm long, ellipsoid oblong apex 2-3.5 cm long. Bracts numerous, densely rusty pubescent externally at the apex, narrowly obtriangular or spatulate, 5-7.5 mm long, base ± membranous, folded into a groove, ± pubescent on the apex of the dorsal keel and on the margins, apex cucullate, apex truncate apiculate (overlapping apices giving a scaly appearance to the young immature capitulum). Basal flowers orange on the living plant, sterile. Pedicel fleshy glabrous 1.5 mm long. Calyx zygomorphic 4.5-6 mm long, gamosepalous; 2 dorsal lobes connate, sub-semicircular to very broadly oblong, pubescent externally, 0.75 × 1 mm long, reflexed internally; ventral lobes free, pubescent externally, ± 0.5 mm long. Corolla with 5 petals exserted from the calyx, glabrous except for a few hairs at the apex and on the dorsal keel, very narrowly lanceolate, 7-8 mm long; connate at the base to the stamens for 2 mm. Stamens with undulate filaments 1.5 cm long, connate into a tube at the base for 2.5-5 mm, anthers absent. Pistil reduced to a short point. Apical flowers red on the living plant. Pedicel fleshy 1.5 (2.5) mm long. Calyx gamosepalous, pubescent externally at the apex 8-11 mm long; 2 dorsal lobes ± free, oblong, 1.5 × 2 mm, 3 ventral lobes free 1.5-2 mm long. Petals glabrous, 9.25-12 mm long, very narrowly lanceolate, free apical lobes 2 mm long. Stamens 11.5-17.5 mm long, staminal tube 5-8 mm long. Pistil with stipe 0.5-2 mm long; ovary very laterally compressed, obtriangular, a few hairs at the apex or glabrous, asymmetric, 3.5-4.5 mm long, containing 25-26 ovules; style 7-10.5 mm long, weakly widening at the apex, stigma scarcely visible tubular.

Fruits pendulous grouped on the persistent capitulum. Pod black and ± glossy on the dry state, red on the living plant, flattened, straight or twisted, elliptic, (18-) 23.5-37.5 × 2.5-4.5 cm; stipe 5.5-7.5 cm long; venation of the lateral faces most often very marked (sometimes scarcely or not visible); sutural ridges prominent. Seeds elliptic oblong, 1.7 × 1 cm, light beige, without visible areole. – Pl. 5, p. 29.

## Discussion

This species has a range extending from Guinea to Zaïre. It is a plant of the dense humid forest domain; it is particularly abundant along riverbanks.

## Uses

The wood is usable for carpentry. The bark serves for the treatment of wounds. The fruits would be used to trap "squirrels".
