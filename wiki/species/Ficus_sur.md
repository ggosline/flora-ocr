## Discussion

Trees up to 25 (-30) m tall. Leafy branchlets 2-5 mm thick, densely brownish white puberulent, sometimes with longer hairs on the nodes, glabrescent or nearly glabrous, reddish brown when dry, periderm sometimes peeling. Leaves arranged in spirals. Lamina elliptic to ovate or oblong, or sometimes suborbicular, 4-20 (-32) cm long, 3-13 (-16) cm wide, papyraceous to coriaceous, apex acuminate to acute, base subobtuse, obtuse, truncate or subcordate, margin coarsely and obscurely undulate dentate to subentire, upper surface puberulent only on the base and lower half of the midvein and on the proximal part of the lower lateral veins, or glabrous, sometimes shortly scabrous, lower surface yellowish brown tomentose to puberulent, more densely also on the main veins with longer hairs, or glabrous; veins plane above, prominent below, 5-8 pairs of lateral veins, including 1-2 pairs of basal veins, glandular spots in the axils of the basal veins, smaller in the axils of the other lateral veins. Petiole 1.5-9 cm long, 0.1-0.2 cm thick, ± densely brownish white or white puberulent or tomentose, glabrescent, or nearly glabrous. Stipules free, 1-3.5 cm long, with long white appressed hairs or pubescent in the middle, very shortly puberulent to glabrous toward the margin, or face nearly glabrous but margin ciliate, caducous, occasionally subpersistent.

Figs 1-3 on the nodes of branched leafless ramified branchlets (reaching 50 cm), on the trunk and/or main branches; peduncle 0.5-2 cm long, 1-3 mm thick, ± densely yellowish white puberulent or ± glabrescent; basal bracts 3, free or basally fused, sometimes caducous, broadly ovate, approximately 2 mm long, 2 mm wide, yellowish white puberulent; receptacle obovoid to subglobose, often ± globose depressed, often ± distinctly stipitate (at least when dry), 2-3 (-4) cm in diameter when fresh, 0.5-2.5 cm when dry, sparsely yellowish white puberulent to glabrous, sometimes densely puberulent to velvety, yellowish or reddish at maturity, sometimes with lighter spots.

Pistillate flowers with 2-4 tepals, often of irregular shape and split, often caudate and narrow, partially fused or free, stigmas free, seed-bearing flowers (sub)sessile, style 2-2.5 mm long, stigma elongate; gall flowers subsessile or with pedicel reaching 3.5 mm, stigma shorter and frequently truncate. Fruits ellipsoid, oblongoid or subovoid, 1.5-1.8 mm long; gall fruits ellipsoid to obovoid, approximately 2 mm long. Staminate flowers sessile, subtending bracts 2-3 mm long, often cucullate, perianth approximately 2 mm long, stamens 2, filament short, anther approximately 1.3-1.5 mm long, often apiculate, pistillode often present. - Pl. 37, p. 137; pl. 38, p. 139.

Pollinators: Ceratosolen capensis GRANDI (Ethiopia, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe-Rhodesia), (?) "Cuckoo wasp": Ceratosolen flabellatus GRANDI (Cameroon, Guinea, Ivory Coast, Uganda).

F. sur is very widely distributed and is highly variable in leaf shape, leaf dimensions, and in the presence and density of pubescence.

The form with pubescent figs (or still pubescent on the lower leaf surface), formerly recognized as a distinct species (F. mallotocarpa) or as a variety of F. capensis (var. mallotocarpa) is also found in Cameroon. Morphological differences between lowland and montane material cannot be recognized.

The species is distributed from Senegal to Ethiopia (extending to Yemen) and southward to South Africa. It is also present in the Cape Verde islands, Fernando Po, São Tomé, and Príncipe. It is most often encountered in ± open vegetation such as savannas (often wooded savannas), in forest galleries (rather in the form of small trees), in secondary forests, and also in submontane forests (often as large trees). It is found up to an altitude of 1800 m.
