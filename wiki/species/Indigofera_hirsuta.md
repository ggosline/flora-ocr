# *Indigofera hirsuta* L. var. hirsuta Planche 71

**Genus**: [[Indigofera]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 751 (1753)

## Description

Herb or annual suffrutex, reaching 1–1.6 m in height; stem erect to procumbent, branched; indumentum hirsute brown conspicuous, hairs biramose, but very asymmetrical, almost simple. Stipules setaceous, 5–12(–18) mm. Leaf: petiole 7–16 mm, petiole and rachis pilose; stipels present; leaflets 5 to 9, opposite, with blade elliptic-obovate to elliptic, 2–4.5 × 1–2.5 cm, the terminal one larger, base cuneate, apex rounded, pilose, whitish on both faces. Inflorescence in dense pseudoracemes of (5–)10–25(–30) cm in length, in the axil of well-developed leaves; peduncle 3–7(–11) cm; bracts 3–7 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 0.5–1 mm; calyx 4–6.5 mm in length, equalling or exceeding the corolla, hirsute, cleft almost to the base into setaceous lobes; corolla red to pink, standard elliptic, 4–5 × 2–2.5 mm, apex emarginate, with white pubescence on the back, keel with upper margin hairy; stamens 4–5(–6) mm. Pod straight, reflexed, ± tetragonous in section, 12–20 × 1.8–2.5 mm, with 5 to 7 seeds, hirsute with brown or grey hairs, valves with well-developed sutures. Seed almost cubical, 1.3–1.8 × 1.2–1.5 mm, brown, distinctly foveolate.

## Distribution

Asia, Africa and Madagascar, introduced to the Americas and the Pacific; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

cultivated land and fallows, open disturbed forest, sandy savannas, inselbergs; in Gabon at 5–450 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1350 m. Flowers and fruits in December, January and July. Use: Plant used as cover crop, green manure and fodder in mixture with grasses and other legumes, but also notorious as a weed. The leaves are used against ulcers. In the Batéké Plateaus, it is used as an ichthyotoxic

## Vernacular names

indigotier hérissé (fr.) ; hairy indigo, rough hairy indigo (angl.) ; sókè (nkomi) ; manami (bapunu)
