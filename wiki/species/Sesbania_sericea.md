# *Sesbania sericea* (Willd.) Link

**Genus**: [[Sesbania]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. hort. berol. alt. 2 : 244 (1822)

## Description

Semi-woody herb 1–3 m tall; branchlets with silky pubescence at first. Stipules rapidly caducous, linear-acute, 6–7 mm, with narrow margins, silky towards the middle. Leaf 12–23 cm long; petiole 5–10 mm; rachis finely pubescent; stipels minute; leaflets in 12 to 18(–26) pairs, with petiolule 1 mm, limb oblong, 1.5–2.3 × 0.5–0.6 cm, base slightly oblique, apex rounded, mucronate, glabrous to pubescent and generally with black dots above, below with silky pubescence. Inflorescence 3–8 cm long. Flower: pedicel 0.5–1 cm; calyx campanulate, tube 3–4 mm long, teeth 1 mm long, glabrous but with pubescent margins; corolla yellow, standard cordate, wider than long, 6–8 × 9–10 mm, apex emarginate, with or without brownish spots on the back, appendages entirely adnate to the claw, wings obovate, unguiculate, 9 × 4 mm, keel ± 8 mm long, the claw as long as the limb; stamens ± 9 mm, curved; ovary and style glabrous. Pod linear, slightly curved, 15–16 × 3 cm, slightly torulose, with 15 to 30 seeds, with thickened margins, mucronate by the style. Seed rounded-rectangular, 3–4 × 2 × 1.5–2 mm, reddish-brown or olive green spotted with black dots.

## Distribution

Tropical Africa, from Senegal to Somalia in the East and Angola in the South, also in Arabia and introduced to Sri Lanka and tropical America; in Gabon, known from the Estuaire

## Ecology

Marshy soils, river edges; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 800 m. Flowers in March, November and December; fruits in March

Use: The foliage serves as green manure. The whole plant is ichthyotoxic for brackish-water fish.

## Vernacular names

coronilla sericea, papagallo, papagayo (angl.). B : Cronquist (1954), Gillett (1963, 1971c), Ipor & Oyen (1997), Pellegrin (1949). i B liographie
