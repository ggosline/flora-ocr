# *Scyphocephalium ochocoa* Warb.

**Genus**: [[Scyphocephalium]]
**Family**: [[Myristicaceae]]

## Synonyms

- Myristica Mannii Benth. et Hook., Icon. 3e série. 3 : 49 (1877).
- Scyphocephalium Mannii (Benth.) Warb., Not. bot. gart. Mus. Berl.: 98 (1895).
- Ochocoa gaboni Pierre, Bull. soc. linn.: 1257 (1896).

## Description

— Warburg, Monograph of the Myristicaceae: 244 (1897).

Tree reaching 20-35 m tall with a trunk 60-80 cm in diameter. Leaves with blade most often cordate at the base, rarely acute, measuring 22-24 cm long by 6-7 cm wide, with acuminate apex, upper surface glabrous and glossy, lower surface at first tomentose-ferruginous then glabrous. The petiole measures 1 cm long by 3 mm thick; it is subpubescent; there are 18-25 (35) pairs of secondary nerves.

Male inflorescences axillary 3-4 (6) cm with tomentellous and caducous bracts at the ramifications; subspherical umbels 15 mm in diameter comprising approximately 50 flowers 3-5 lobed tomentose on the outside, glabrous and red on the inside; 2 bracts surround the young umbel. Female inflorescences arranged as the male inflorescences: umbels grouped in trichotomous cymes. Fruits subspherical 30-35 mm in diameter, covered with golden fawn tomentum, grouped by 3-4.

Types: Soyaux: 301, 31 Oct. 1881 ♂ (B, K, Petersb., P) Gabon, Munda. Klaine: 72, 24 Apr. 1896 ♂ (B, P) Gabon, Libreville.

Chevalier notes that it is a very common tree in the forests near Libreville, that the flowers have red sepals on the inner face, that the inflorescences and the undersurface of the leaves are rubiginous, the wood white with brown-orange heart with lustrous sheen.

The geographical range extends over Gabon and Cameroon. The vernacular names collected are as follows: Ochoco according to Klaine, Sogho or Sogo according to Aubréville, Gauchotte and Chevalier (Fang), Zambalombé (Bayaka), Ilombé (Bavili), Ossoka (Galoa). Moreover Walker and Sillans (Useful plants of Gabon: 306) cite: Ossoko (Mpongwé, Galoa, Nkomi, Orungu, Ivéa), Soko (Bavové), Isoko (Bakota), Musoko (Ngowé), Otsoko (Mitsogo, Apindji, Simba), Sokwé (Bakélé, Béséki), Soghe (Fang), Musuku (Eshira, Bavili, Banzabi, Bapunu, Masangu, Baduma), Musaga (Balumbu), Disuku (Bavarama, Bavungu), Nsuku (Loango), Motsuku, Ontsogo (Mindumu), Bohoko (Benga).

## Uses

The light wood, semi-hard, is used for heating (boats, lime or brick kilns) or the construction of pirogues. The condimentary seeds serve as bait in porcupine traps. Decoctions of the bark are a remedy against blennorrhagia.

3. CSELOCARYON ¹ Warb.

— Warburg, Notizbl. bot. gart. Mus. Berl.: 99 (1895).

Trees with glabrous branches; leaves coriaceous with few secondary nerves (7-10 pairs); blade often acuminate, petiole 1-3 cm. Inflorescences in isolated umbels or grouped in simple racemes or in ramose panicles; these umbels are surrounded by an involucre that can comprise 2 bilobed bracts enveloping the flowers almost completely but very early caducous, detaching circularly to leave only small discs terminating the branches; flowers without bracts, arranged in umbels, on these small discs; perianth cupuliform 3-4 parted; male flowers with a column bearing 3-5 adnate anthers. Infructescence comprising several fruits with enlarged perianth and accrescent pedicel. Seed presenting a large central cavity in the ruminate albumen (hence the name); aril laciniate in interlaced ribbons at the apex of the seed.

Initially created based on the study of fruits, the genus was soon precisely defined in its description by Warburg thanks to new collections from Cameroon. Monospecific at first: C. Preussii Warb., the genus was enriched by the description of new species: C. Klainei Pierre in 1900 (manuscript); C. cuneatum Warb. in 1904; C. oxycarpum Stapf (F.T.A. 6, 1: 164) in 1909; C. botryoides Vermœsen in 1923, finally C. Staneri Ghesq. in 1936. However, according to Hutchinson and Dalziel, C. oxycarpum = C. cuneatum; according to Engler, C. cuneatum = C. Klainei; according to Gilbert and Troupin, C. Klainei = C. Preussii and C. botryodes = C. Staneri; only two species remain: C. Preussii Warb. and C. botryodes Verm. Examination of the plants in the Paris Museum allows the following remarks: the types of C. oxycarpum from Guinea are specimens with fruits; these latter measure 25 mm long by 10 mm in diameter, attenuate at the base and mucronate at the apex, grouped by 3-5 in an isolated umbel on a long peduncle (4-5 cm)

Male plants from Ivory Coast brought near these types by the shape of the panduriform leaves (like that of C. botryodes) have umbels grouped in simple racemes like C. botryodes. The typical specimens of C. botryodes on the other hand have larger fruits, 30 mm long by 15 mm in diameter and ellipsoid, without thinning at the base or mucron at the apex and grouped by three in isolated umbels.
