# *Cyperus tisserantii* Cherm. Figure 5a

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Arch. Bot. 4, Mém. 7 : 18 (1931)

## Description

C. margaritaceus Vahl var. tisserantii (Cherm.) Kük. in Engl. & Diels, Pflanzenr . IV. 20 (101) : 285 (1936). C. niveus Retz. var. tisserantii (Cherm.) Lye, Nordic J. Bot. 3 : 231 (1983). Perennial herb, small to medium-sized, growing in dense tufts; rhizomes formed by the aggregation of ovoid swellings at the base of stems; rhizome, if present, short, woody, approximately 3 mm thick; stems 5‒30 cm × 0.6‒1 mm, angular, longitudinally striated, glabrous, with base covered by layers of leaf sheaths not decomposed into fibres. Basal leaves, numerous; new sheaths green to light reddish-brown, but almost entirely concealed beneath layers of old dead sheaths brown to blackish; blade filiform, < 20 cm × 0.3‒0.7 mm, often canaliculate, almost glabrous, but sometimes scabrous on the margin just below the rounded apex. Inflorescence usually formed of an irregular fascicle of 3‒10 sessile spikelets digitate, sometimes accompanied by a solitary spikelet just below the main inflorescence, subtended by its foliaceous bract; involucral bracts 3‒5, foliaceous, spreading, similar

a b Achene elliptic, 1.5‒1.6 × 0.9‒1.2 mm, distinctly triangular with at least one concave face, black at maturity, smooth.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical and subtropical Africa; rare in Gabon, found only in Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime (2 collections)

## Ecology

in dry grassy areas and seasonally burned savannas; in Gabon, 0‒500 m altitude, up to 2100 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: very close to C. margaritaceus and C. niveus, but differs from them by its leaves only 0.3‒0.7 mm wide. C. tisserantii is frequently collected at a very young stage, as the white heads are very visible in the middle of burned black grasses. B : Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972). Bibliography
