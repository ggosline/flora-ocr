# *Staurogyne alboviolacea* R. Benoist var. alboviolacea

**Genus**: [[Staurogyne]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: R. Benoist, Not. Syst. II : 151 (1944)

## Description

Perennial herb, erect, attaining about 60 cm in height, with round, glabrescent stem. Petiole 2–3 cm; leaf blade ovate-lanceolate, 40–110 × 23–49 mm. 7–11 pairs of lateral veins, glabrous above, puberulent below.

Terminal inflorescences, in spikes 5–13 cm long, glandular-pubescent. Flowers subopposite in the lower parts, ± alternate in the upper parts of the inflorescence; bracts spatulate-lanceolate, 6–8 (10) × 1–1.5 mm, green, violet below (Hallé and Cours 5978) trinerved, ciliate at the margins and along the midrib, often bearing on the outer abaxial surface a few glands, at most 6–7, disciform, with a diameter of 0.3 mm; bracteoles appressed to the calyx and perfectly simulating two lateral sepals, pubescent, nearly the same size, 7.5–10 × 1 mm, as the posterior sepal. Calyx composed of unequal sepals; the posterior one larger, lanceolate, very acute, 10 × 1 mm; lateral sepals smaller, triangular-lanceolate, 3 × 0.3 mm; anterior sepals 7 × 0.5 mm; all are pubescent-ciliate, with cilia reaching a length of 2 mm (!). Corolla white, with labellum tinted violet, 10 mm long, with tube 7 mm long, infundibuliform in the lower third; a ring of fairly dense hairs at 2 mm above the base; light pubescence on the inside, above this ring and to the base of the limb; posterior lobes forming an upper lip, ovate-triangular, emarginate at the summit, concave; lateral lobes 1.8 × 0.7 mm, forming with the anterior lobe 2 × 2.5 mm a lower lip. Stamens inserted in the upper third, just above the ring of hairs of the corolla; anthers bilocular. Disk cupuliform; ovary glabrous, with 6–7 ovules in each locule.

Fruit 4–5 mm. Seed 1.2 mm diam.

## Discussion

Gabon, Congo-Léopoldville and Central African Republic.

1 bis. Staurogyne alboviolacea R. Benoist var. gracilior Heine, nov. var.

From var. alboviolacea it differs by epiphytic habit, more gracile dimensions, very short inflorescences (3–6 flowers), corollas white throughout (not violet).

Small plant, epiphytic, very much branched, low (to 10 cm high); branches sparingly pilose; internodes 1–3 cm long; petioles 4–5 mm long; leaf blade ovate, 15–25 × 7–13 mm, rounded at apex, attenuate or subrounded at base, secondary veins 4 on each side, blade above furnished with some rigid subulate hairs, very short, below pubescent at the veins; inflorescences short, sparingly pilose, to 1.5 cm long, 4–6 flowered; flowers to 1.5 mm pedicellate; bracts lanceolate, 5.5 mm long, 0.7–1 mm wide, glabrous; bracteoles linear-lanceolate, 4.5 mm long; longer sepals 4.5 mm long; two lateral sepals shorter, to 1.2 mm long; corolla to 9 mm long, entirely white; fruit to 5 mm long.

Gabon: N. Hallé 3543 (P, holotype of the variety) Bélinga (fl. and fr. 19 Dec. 1964), small Acanthaceae with white flowers, epiphyte in riparian forest near savanna, after the lowering of the Ivindo.

Small epiphytic plant, very much branched, to 10 cm high, with slightly pubescent branches; internodes 1–3 cm long. Petioles 4–5 mm long. Leaf blade ovate, 15–25 × 7–13 mm, rounded at apex, above with some rigid subulate, very short hairs; below pubescent along the veins, 4 pairs of secondary veins.

Inflorescences very short, somewhat pubescent, to 1.5 cm long, 4–6 flowered. Flowers pedicellate to 1.5 mm; bracts lanceolate, 5.5 mm × 0.7–1 mm, glabrous; bracteoles linear-lanceolate, 3–5 mm long. The two lateral sepals much shorter, masked by the two bracteoles which resemble them; lateral sepals reaching only 1.2 mm long. Corolla to 9 mm long, entirely white.

Fruit to 5 mm long.

The floral characters correspond exactly to var. alboviolacea, but by its dimensions and habit it appears very different, so that one could easily consider the corresponding plants as representing a distinct species. It must be noted, however, that certain Acanthaceae (for example in the genera Asystasia and Dischistocalyx) can assume an epiphytic habit, which changes their usual appearance considerably. It seems highly probable that such a case has occurred in the plants we are describing based on the sole collection by N. Hallé. The taxonomic position could eventually be modified with more abundant material. Plants from Oubangui-Chari (Tisserant 2313, 2613) appear clearly to be intermediates between var. alboviolacea and gracilior; their corollas are also entirely white according to the collector's notes.
