- P. hildebrandtii HARMS, Bot. Jahrb. 26: 261 (1899).

- P. bussei HARMS, l.c. 33: 164 (1902).

Trees reaching a diameter of 60 cm. Thin alate buttresses, forming an angle of $ 45^{\circ} $ with the trunk. Bark surface spotted with grayish white. Bark section pinkish red, tender, sticky to the touch on living wood. Crown plateau-shaped with fine foliage. Branchlets glabrous, blackish, ribbed-wrinkled, scaly, with brown lenticels. Petiole puberulent, 5-12.5 cm long, base bearing above an elliptic gland or 2 narrowly elliptic ± cupular non-prominent glands, flat or ± weakly prominent above, flattened dorsiventrally, lateral margins with weakly alate ridges. Rachis puberulent, 13-27.5 cm long, upper face carinate; lateral margins alate reflexed toward the lower base carinate (when dry); terminal mucro early caducous, 1 large gland or 2 smaller ones (then separated by a small carina) between the terminal pinnae, then glands of decreasing size between the following 2-3 pairs of pinnae. Pinnae 7-12 pairs, opposite to subopposite, elliptic-oblong. Pinna axis puberulent, 8-15.5 cm long, upper face carinate, bearing glands between the terminal pairs of leaflets; lateral margins alate folded toward the lower face ± carinate. Leaflets opposite, 22-31 pairs, sessile or subsessile. Blade glabrous on both faces, dark brown above and paler brown below when dry, oblong asymmetric, reaching $ 20 \times 5 $ mm, asymmetric base: proximal margin ± auriculate, distal margin attenuate truncate, apex obtuse to subobtuse, margin finely pubescent especially at the auricle. Principal median nerve, ± sinuose, very prominent paler above and prominent below. Secondary veins prominent on both faces, a basal one ± parallel to the lower blade margin, anastomosing at 3/4 or 4/5 of the blade length. Tertiary veins in loose reticulum, prominent on both faces.

Inflorescence in a pendulous capitulum. Peduncle 15.5-30.5 cm long; ribbed-furrowed, with russet scales exfoliating then light brown lenticels. Capitulum axis claviform 4.5 cm long; cylindric at the base, 2-3 cm long; ellipsoid at the apex 2-2.5 cm long. Bract spatulate, 13 mm long; apex enlarged pubescent externally, reflexed inward; dorsal median carina pubescent. Basal flowers neutral or ♀. Pedicel thick, glabrous, 1.5-1.8 mm long. Calyx glabrous except on the external face of the lobes, cylindric tubular, 8.75 mm long; dorsal lobes semicircular, ± 1.5 mm long; ventral lobes irregular reaching 1.5 mm. Corolla as in ♀ flowers, ± 8 mm long; lobes not prominent relative to the ventral calyx lobes. Stamens 10.6 mm long as in ♀ flowers, anthers oblong 1.25 mm. Central pistillode. Apical flowers orange-red. Pedicel thick, glabrous 2.5-3.25 mm long, ribbed. Calyx zygomorphic, 11-11.5 mm long; pubescent externally on the lobes; dorsal lobes semicircular, 1.5-2.25 × 2-2.5 mm, ventral lobes unequal, median one 1-1.5 mm long, lateral ones shorter. Petals glabrous, narrowly linear lanceolate, 12-12.5 mm long, adnate to the staminal tube at the base, lobes free, 7-9 mm long, some hairs on the margin. Stamens adnate into a tube for 7-8.25 mm, filaments 17-20 mm long; anther basifixed 1.25 mm long. Pistil with stipe 3-3.5 (-4) mm long, ovary with very fine russet micropubérulence, asymmetrically obtriangular, 3.5-4.5 mm long, containing 25-28 ovules; style slender 8-10.5 mm long, with scarcely distinct stigma.

Fruits 3-4 pendant per capitulum. Pods linear glabrous, black at maturity, ± glossy when dry, 33-66 cm long, 1-1.6 cm wide; sutural margins thickened ± sinuous; lateral faces little or not innervated, compressed between the seeds; base with stipe 8-15 cm long. Seeds 8-19, brown, 9-17 × 4-11 mm; thin testa; areole not visible. - Pl. 6, p. 33.

## Discussion

This species is known from semi-deciduous dense forests and forest galleries. It is found in Côte d'Ivoire, then from Cameroon to Zambia. In eastern Africa and in eastern Zaïre, the leaflets are much larger (corresponding to P. bussei Harms); but this is the only distinct character and there exist many intermediate forms that do not warrant the maintenance of separate taxa. In Gabon, this species is encountered especially on the edge of dense forest.

Arbres, arbustes, lianes, rarement herbacées ; épineux ou inermes. Feuilles bipennées, rarement phyllodes. Pennes pauci- ou multifoliolées, alternes ou opposées. Glandes présentes ou absentes sur les feuilles.

Inflorescences variées : grappes, épis capituliformes, panicules ; axillaires ou terminales. Fleurs ♢, polygames, ou andromonoïques ; actinomorphes. Calice valvaire, au moins dans le bouton (sauf Dinizia, d'Amérique tropicale), sépales libres ou soudés. Étamines aussi nombreuses ou deux fois plus nombreuses que les pétales ; libres ou soudées à la base ; sommet du connectif avec ou sans glande ; anthère médifixe. Disque présent ou absent ; libre ou soudé aux étamines. Pistil sessile ou stipité ; ovaire à 2-n ovules.

Gousse très variable en forme ; déhiscente ou indéhiscente ; ligneuse, ou chartacée papyracée. Graines aréolées ou non.

Pollen composé sans hétéromorphie, sans pores proximaux (sauf Dichrostachys p.p., Leucaena p.p.) avec des apertures distales et subdistales face à face par 2 ou 3 (4 dans Amblygonocarpus, Tetrapleura, etc.). Pollen simple avec des apertures habituellement équatoriales et composées ; avec un tectum traversé par quelques canalicules ; avec une exine lisse.

Cette tribu est la plus importante des Mimosoïdées : 36-37 genres pour 650-725 espèces dont 400 à 500 pour le seul genre Mimosa. Par contre les genres pauci- ou monospécifiques sont nombreux.

Au Gabon, 15 genres sont recensés.
