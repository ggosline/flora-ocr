# *Cyperus sesquiflorus* (Torr.) Mattf. & Kük. subsp. sesquiflorus

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kyllinga sesquiflora Torr., Ann. Lyceum Nat. Hist. New York 3 : 287 (1836)

## Description

in Engl. & Diels, Pflanzenr . IV. 20(101) : 591 (1936). Kyllinga sesquiflora Torr., Ann. Lyceum Nat. Hist. New York 3 : 287 (1836). Kyllinga odorata Vahl, Enum. pl . 2 : 382 (1805), non C. odoratus L. (1753). Perennial herb, densely tufted, with stems arising from a usually short rhizome (< 8 cm), creeping; stems 10–80 cm × 0.7–2.8 mm, trigonous, striate, glabrous, base usually swollen and covered with hardened scales often torn, but rarely becoming fibrous. Leaf: lower sheaths without or with a very short lamina; lamina flat or rolled, scabrous on the margin and central vein, the largest 7–40 cm × 2–5 mm. Inflorescence as a head 7–15 × 5–17 mm, composed of a central ovoid to ovoid–cylindric spike and lateral spikes generally much smaller, very rarely formed of a single spike; involucral bracts 4–6, foliaceous, usually spreading or reflexed, the main one 3–15 cm long; spikes whitish, becoming pale brownish. Spikelet 3–4.5 mm long, with 2 flowers, but often producing only a single achene; glumes elliptic, 3–4 mm long, whitish with reddish dots and often with a greenish central vein and 2–5 lateral veins, central vein with keel distinctly denticulate, apex generally acuminate. Achene obovate to elliptic, 1.2–1.5(–1.9) × 0.7–0.9 mm, blackish and finely tuberculate.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical Africa, but not yet recorded from Gabon where it should be sought

widely distributed in the tropical regions of Africa and Asia; scattered in Gabon, found in the Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

shaded edge of wooded areas, along forest tracks, in montane forests and bamboo groves where light intensity is high; up to 2600 m altitude
