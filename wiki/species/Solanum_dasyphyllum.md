# *Solanum dasyphyllum* Schumach. & Thonn. Planche 39, Figure 7(L)

**Genus**: [[Solanum]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Beskr. Guin. pl. 126 [146] (1827)

## Description

S. duplosinuatum Klotzsch var. semiglabrum C.H.Wright in Thyselton-Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 4(2) : 244 (1905). S. dasyphyllum Schumach & Thonn. var. semiglabrum (C.H.Wright) Bitter, Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg., Beih. 16 : 191 (1923). S. dasyphyllum Schumach. & Thonn. var. inerme Bitter, Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg., Beih. 16 : 192 (1923). Perennial herb or shrub, up to 1 m tall, densely prickly; branches erect to spreading, sparsely to densely pubescent, with stipitate stellate hairs, armed with straight prickles. Leaf: petiole 0–4 cm, generally decurrent on the stem, sparsely to densely pubescent, armed with 0 to 5 prickles; lamina elliptic, 10–35 × 6–20 cm, attenuate to cuneate at the base, acute to obtuse at the apex, with margin moderately to deeply lobed, often doubly lobed, with 4 or 5 broadly triangular lobes per side, acute to obtuse at the apex, discolorous to concolorous, bearing 10 to 15 prickles on each face, sparsely to densely pubescent with simple hairs above, densely pubescent with stipitate stellate hairs below. Inflorescence lateral, 4–7 cm long, simple, with 5 to 10 heterostylous flowers (andromonoecious plants); peduncle 0–3 mm; axes sparsely to densely pubescent, armed with 0 to 30 prickles. Flower 5-merous; pedicel 0.7–3 cm, sparsely to densely pubescent, armed with 0 to 30 prickles; calyx 8–30 mm long, pubescent, unarmed or with a few prickles, with triangular lobes, 0.7–2.5 cm long, acute to long acuminate at the apex; corolla subrotate, 1.5–6 cm in diameter, pale mauve to purple, sometimes white, divided to one-third, with spreading, broadly triangular lobes, 0.2–2 × 0.7–2.5 cm, moderately pubescent below; stamens equal, filament glabrous, anther 6.5–7.5 mm long; ovary puberulous to minutely glandular hairs in the upper third; style 0.8–1.3 cm, with glandular hairs at the base. Fruit spherical to broadly ellipsoid, 2.5–4 cm in diameter, yellow at maturity, with 50 to 100 seeds. Seed reniform, 2.8–4.5 × 2–3.5 mm, orange to black.

## Distribution

Common in the west, centre and east of subsaharan Africa, in a latitudinal band between approximately 15°N and 10°S, present in Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

principally in forest habitat, but also in savannas, grasslands or wastelands, often close to water points; in Gabon collected on the seashore, elsewhere up to 1600 m altitude. Use: The roots, leaves, fruits and seeds are used in traditional medicine, presumably for uses similar to those of the cultivated sister species, S. macrocarpon (see the treatment of this species below)

## Source note

As a wild progenitor species of a cultivated species, S. macrocarpon (gboma), the taxonomic status of S. dasyphyllum remains debated (see Vorontsova & Knapp (2016) for a detailed discussion). Here we have chosen to distinguish the cultivated species from the wild species because (1) these two species are morphologically distinct (see below S. macrocarpon), and (2) the separation between these two species makes it possible to account for the difference in the selection regimes at work in the cultivated species and in the related wild species.
