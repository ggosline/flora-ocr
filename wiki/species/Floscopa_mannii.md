# *Floscopa mannii* C.B.Clarke Figure 3(A, B)

**Genus**: [[Floscopa]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]

## Description

in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phan. 3 : 268 (1881). Annual herb; stem creeping, ± 30 cm, rooting at the nodes, internodes ± 5 cm; roots fibrous. Leaves arranged along the stem, with 2 or 3(–4) apical leaves subverticillate; sheath ± 5 mm long, fairly open, not enveloping the stem, covered with pilose pubescence, also covering the pseudopetiole and the base of the limb; pseudopetiole distinct, ± 1–1.5 cm; limb ovate to elliptic, 3–6.5 × 1.5–3 cm, base rounded, apex acute to obtuse, glabrous, upper surface bright green, lower surface greyish green. Inflorescence terminal, in a compound thyrse, ± lax, 3–4.5(–7) cm in diameter, with scattered glandular pubescence; cincinni 5 to 20, (0.5–)1.5–4 cm long, often bifurcate, not directed in the same direction, floriferous almost to the base, with up to ± 15 flowers, reddish. Flower bisexual, 4–6 mm in diameter; pedicel 2 mm, with a few spreading hairs; sepals elliptic, 2–2.5 mm long, concave, with a few spreading hairs, glutinous or not, green on the outside, whitish inside; petals glabrous, white, sometimes tinged with pink, the upper ones rhomboidal, ± 3 mm long, the lower one linear, ± 3 mm long; lower stamens with filament ± 5 mm, anther pale yellow; upper stamens subequal to the lower ones, sometimes the median one absent or reduced, filament ± 4 mm, white, anther bilobed, yellow; style ± 4 mm, white, with a small globose stigma, pale yellow. Fruit subglobose, ± 2 × 1.5 mm, truncate or emarginate at the apex, corniculose, green when young. Seed broadly ovoid, truncate at base and apex, with 15 to 20 prominent ribs.

## Distribution

Rare species, found in Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Río Muni) and Gabon; in Gabon in the Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

primary humid lowland forest, stream margins, muddy places; in Gabon at ± 500 m altitude, elsewhere from 50 to 700 m. B: Brenan (1968b), Cabezas et al. (2014), Sosef et al. (2006), Vande weghe et al. (2016). Bibliography
