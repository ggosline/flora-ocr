# *Coula edulis* Baillon

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Coula]]

Adansonia, ser. 1, **3**: 61 (1863).

## Description

**Habit**: tree 15–30 m tall; trunk 60–80 cm in diameter; first branches at 5–6 m height. Densely branched, canopy dense with small, pendant branchlets, russet-pubescent at the extremity. Bark grey-ash, ± light, fissured.

**Leaves**: petiole glabrous above, ferruginous indumentum beneath or glabrous, 1–3 cm long, canaliculate above. Blade glabrous (except on young leaves), bright green above, russet beneath when fresh, glossy brown on upper surface, pale brown beneath when dry, oblong, oblong-elliptic to elliptic, 10–25 × 4–10 cm; base cuneate or rounded, margins recurved; apex obtuse-acuminate. Midrib concave above, prominent beneath, longitudinally striate. Secondary nerves 10–13 pairs, ascending, slightly arcuate, reaching the leaf margin, concave on upper surface, prominent beneath. Nervules anastomosing amongst themselves, little distinct on both faces.

**Inflorescence**: axillary raceme, many-flowered, 2–7 cm long. Floral pedicel approximately 2 mm long, short, thick, russet-pubescent, at the axil of a bract.

**Flowers**: hermaphrodite, cream or slightly reddish. Sepals fused into a cup with undulate upper margin and russet external pubescence. Petals free, erect or exserted-reflexed, valvate, pubescent externally, 2–2.5 × 1.5 mm. Stamens gynobasic, four times more numerous than petals, arranged in 4 cycles: 4 central epipetalous stamens, 8 lateral epipetalous stamens and 4 alternipetalous stamens; filament ± strongly adnate in the grooves on the inner surface of the petals, variable in form and size; anther with 2 locules bearing lateral dehiscence slits. Disc adnate to the ovary. Ovary superior, with 4 uniovulate locules. Style short, conical, ending in a trilobed stigma.

**Fruit**: drupe globose or ellipsoid, glabrous, 2–3 × 1.5–3 cm. Endocarp woody rugose, 3–6 mm thick.

**Seed**: globose, with oleic-starchy albumen. Embryo apical.

## Types

**Holotype**: *Aubry Lecomte s.n.*, Gabon (P).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Sierra Leone to Zaire (Democratic Republic of the Congo).
**Gabon**: collected at Ndjolé, Libreville, Makokou, Mondah, Mouyamba, Fernan Vaz, and in the valley of the Ngounié.

## Habitat and ecology

Common in the understories and forest floor of dense humid forest; appears to prefer clayey soils. Flowering occurs from January to July; fruits ripen from January onwards.

## Properties and uses

The wood is used in construction for house posts and as support for bark forming partitions. Bark powder is used for dressing ulcers. In decoction, it stimulates appetite and combats anaemia; as an enema, it is antidysenteric. The seed is edible and eaten raw; an edible oil may be extracted from it. It is sometimes crushed after cooking to make oil cakes.

## Notes

The bark, leaves, ovary wall, disc and pericarp contain secretory cavities filled with yellow-brown resin that darkens upon exposure.
