# *Coula edulis* Baillon

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Coula]]

Adansonia, ser. 1, 3 : 61 (1863).

## Synonymy

- *Coula cabra* De Wild. & Th. Dur., Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg. 38 : 189 (1899).
- *Coula edulis var. cabra* (De Wild. & Th. Dur.) Léonard, Bull. J.B. Brux. 18 : 147 (1947).
- *Coula utilis* S. Moore, Journ. Bot. 58 : 226 (1920).

## Description

Tree 15–30 m tall, trunk 60–80 cm diameter. First branches at 5–6 m height. Branched tree with dense foliage and small drooping branchlets, pubescent with rusty hairs at the tips. Bark greyish ash-coloured, ± pale, fissured. Petiole glabrous above, with ferruginous indumentum below or glabrous, 1–3 cm long, channelled above. Lamina glabrous (except on young leaves), bright green above, rusty below when fresh, shiny brown on upper face, light brown below when dry, oblong, oblong-elliptic to elliptic, 10–25 × 4–10 cm; base cuneate or rounded, margins recurved; apex obtusely acuminate. Midvein concave above, prominent below, longitudinally striated. Secondary veins 10–13 pairs, ascending, slightly curved, reaching the leaf margin, concave on upper face, prominent below. Tertiary veins anastomosed with one another, scarcely distinct on both faces.

**Inflorescence**: Axillary many-flowered raceme, 2–7 cm long. Floral pedicel c. 2 mm long, short, thick, pubescent with rusty hairs, at the axil of a bract.

**Flowers**: Hermaphrodite, yellowish-white or slightly reddish. Sepals united in a cupule with undulate upper margin, with rusty external pubescence. Petals free, erect or reflexed exserted, valvate, pubescent externally, 2–2.5 × 1.5 mm. Stamens gynobasic, 4 times more numerous than the petals, distributed in 4 cycles: 4 central epipetalous stamens, 8 lateral epipetalous stamens and 4 alternipetalous stamens; filament ± strongly appressed in the grooves of the inner face of the petals, variable in form and size; anther with 2 thecae with lateral dehiscence slits. Disc adnate to the ovary. Ovary superior, 4-locular, uniovulate per locule. Style short, conical, terminated by a trilobed stigma.

**Fruit**: Globose or ellipsoid drupe, glabrous, 2–3 × 1.5–3 cm. Endocarp woody, wrinkled, 3–6 mm thick.

**Seed**: Globose with oleaginous-starchy albumen. Embryo apical.

## Distribution

**Range**: Sierra Leone to Zaïre.

**Cameroon**: Akonolinga (Nkolemewout), Douala, Eséka region, Yaoundé region, Ebolowa, Lolodorf, Bipindi.

**Gabon**: Libreville, Ndjolé, Mouyamba, Fernan Vaz, Makokou, Port Gentil region, Mondah forest, Ngounié valley region.

## Habitat and ecology

This common species of the understory or understorey of dense humid forest appears to prefer clayey soils. Flowering occurs from January to July; fruits mature from January. Bark, leaves, ovary wall, disc and pericarp contain secretory pockets with yellow-brown resin becoming black.

## Properties and uses

Wood is used in building houses as posts and supports for bark forming partitions. Bark powder serves in dressing ulcers. In decoction, it stimulates appetite and combats anaemia; as an enema, it is antidysenteric. Seeds are edible and eaten raw; an edible oil can be extracted from them. They are sometimes crushed, after cooking, to make cake.
