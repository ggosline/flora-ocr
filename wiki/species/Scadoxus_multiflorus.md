## Description

Herb, 25–80 cm, with a bulb attached to a distinct rhizomatous portion. Leaf bases forming a distinct false stem, often red to brown or purple-spotted in the lower parts. Leaf blade lanceolate to ovate, acute or acuminate with an attenuated base, 10–27 × 4–9 cm. Leaves most often developed after anthesis.

Flowering scape lateral, 15–55 cm, often spotted like the false stem. Inflorescence semi-globose to globose, reaching 16 cm in diameter, with ± 20–100 scarlet to bright red flowers. Pedicels 1.5–3.5 cm. Perianth tube 0.5–1.5 cm; segments linear, 1.3–2.7 cm long and 0.6–2 mm wide, with 3 nerves. Anthers red or yellow.

Berry orange to red, 0.5–1.2 cm in diameter.

## Distribution

**Range**: Widely distributed across almost the entire Sudano-Zambezian zone from Senegal to Somalia and Yemen to the east, and to the Transvaal and Natal to the south. *S. multiflorus subsp. multiflorus* avoids more or less the humid lowland forests of the Guineo-Congolian regions. It does, however, penetrate the western part of this region (Ghana to Sierra Leone) as subsp. *longitubus* (C.H. Wright) Friis & Nordal and the coastal forests of Natal as subsp. *katherinae* (Bak.) Friis & Nordal.

**Gabon**: Mt. Bana, between Ogooué and Cameroon.

## Habitat and ecology

The subspecies is heterogeneous, occupying various habitats: grassland, wooded grassland, wooded country, riparian vegetation, mountain forest, and mountain grassland. In forests, it is very rare; when found in forests, it always occurs on granite outcrops. It is also dispersed north of the 6th parallel, especially along the western hill chains. In very dry zones it is represented by a very slender form, called "*Haemanthus rupestris*," but to which no taxonomic rank is given, as transitional forms are observed toward the more robust southern forms.

## Notes

The type was collected from Sierra Leone and sent to England; original material is lost. The lectotype is the original plate accompanying Martyn's description (1795), as designated by Bjørnstad & Fris and reproduced in Norw. Journ. Bot. 19: 194 (1972). The subspecies represents a heterogeneous complex occupying diverse ecological zones, with forms in very dry areas that are notably more gracile but not formally recognized taxonomically.
