# *Alectra sessiliflora* (Vahl) Kuntze Planche 28, 56(L)

**Genus**: [[Alectra]]
**Family**: [[Scrophulariaceae]]
**Protologue**: Rev. gen. pl . 2 : 458 (1891)

## Description

Annual herb, 15–50(–60) cm tall; stem simple or branched with numerous paraclades. Leaf: petiole 0–3 mm; blade narrowly ovate to ovate 14–30(–55) × 8–18(–28) mm, rounded or cordate at the base, acuminate at the apex, with subentire to crenate or dentate margins, hispid to glabrescent. Inflorescence dense, leafy or with foliaceous bracts; lower bracts 17–19 × 7–12 mm. Flower: pedicel 0–1.5 mm; bracteoles 3–5 mm long; calyx (6–)7.5–8.5(–9.5) mm long, glabrous to ciliate on the nerves and margins of the lobes, tube 4–5 mm long, lobes (2–)3.5–4.5 mm; corolla 13–14 mm long, yellow to dark orange, with red-purple or red venation, tube 9–10 mm long, lobes ± 4 mm long; stamens unequal, the abaxial ones with hairy filaments 6.5 mm long and anthers with apiculate thecae 1.6 mm long, the adaxial ones with glabrous filaments 4 mm long and anthers 1.4 mm long; ovary 2.5–2.8 mm long; style 8–8.5 mm. Fruit spherical, 5.5 × 5.5 mm.

## Distribution

widespread throughout Africa, in Madagascar and Mauritius to India, Burma (Myanmar), Thailand, Philippines, Taiwan and China; in Gabon, observed in the Ngounié

## Ecology

marshes, herbaceous savannas and ruderal areas; in Gabon, at 640 m, in Rwanda, up to 2950 m

## Uses

The flowers as well as the roots (haustoria) have been used to dye textiles and to color wood. The species also has several medicinal applications. Taxonomic note: Hepper (1960) distinguished 3 varieties which are linked by intermediate forms. For this reason we have not attempted to distinguish varieties here. For Gabon, the typical variety and var. monticola (Engl.) Melchior have been recorded. Bibliography: Fischer (1996, 2006b), Ghazanfar et al. (2008), Hepper (1960, 1963), Jansen (2005), Mielcarek (1996), Philcox (1990)
