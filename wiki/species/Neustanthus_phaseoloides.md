# *Neustanthus phaseoloides* (Roxb.) Benth. var. javanica (Benth.) A.N.Egan & B.Pan

**Genus**: [[Neustanthus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Phytotaxa 218(3) : 207 (2015)

## Description

Plate 100 Phytotaxa 218(3) : 207 (2015). Neustanthus javanicus Benth. in Miq., Pl. Jungh. 2 : 235 (1852). Pueraria javanica (Benth.) Benth., J. Linn. Soc. Bot. London 9 : 125 (1865). Pueraria phaseoloides (Roxb.) Benth. var. javanica (Benth.) Baker in Hook.f., Fl. Brit. India 2 : 199 (1876). Vine or trailing herb, often covering vegetation in a dense mat, rooting at the nodes; stem with spreading, long and ferruginous hairs, glabrescent. Stipules triangular to ovate, 4–11 × 2–3 mm, striate, pubescent. Leaf: petiole 3–11 cm; stipels narrow, 3–7 mm in length; leaflets with petiolules of 2–5 mm, limb ovate or rhomboidal, 3.5–17.5 × 2.5–16 cm, striate, pubescent on both surfaces, the lateral ones oblique, 4–14 × 3–12 cm, base cuneate to truncate, apex acuminate to acute, sometimes obtuse. Inflorescence 15–36(–46) cm in length, pubescent, with peduncle longer than the rachis; bracts 2–5 × 1–2 mm. Flower: pedicel 2–6 mm; bracteoles lanceolate, 3–5 × 1–2 mm, striate, hairy; calyx pubescent externally, tube 3–5 mm, upper lobes divided at the apex, 2–4 mm in length, lateral lobes obtuse, 1.5–2.5 mm in length, lower lobe lanceolate, 3–6 mm in length; standard obovate, 10–23 × 8–18 mm, mauve-purple or blue sometimes spotted with green or yellow or striped with purple to pink, apex emarginate, base with claw and short auricles, wings narrowly obovate, 9–20 × 3–6 mm, keel triangular-clawed, 10–21 × 4–6 mm; ovary 10–15 mm in length, pubescent, with ± 20 ovules; style puberulent below the stigma. Legume cylindrical, 5–12.5 × 0.3–0.5 cm, with 10 to 20 seeds, grey to blackish, valves coiling at maturity. Seed cylindrical, 2.5–4.5 × 2–3.5 × 1.5–2 mm, brown or grey, with finely papillate testa; hilum 1 mm in length.

## Distribution

Of Asian origin, but introduced by humans to all tropics where it is often naturalized; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem, but probably present in all provinces

## Ecology

Roadside and railway margins, plantations, secondary forest in transition to savanna, in scrub, should also be found in oil palm plantations, etc.; in Gabon at 0–250 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Flowering in June and August; fruits in October and November. Use: Cover crop and fodder plant par excellence. The tubers are edible, but rarely reported for this use

## Vernacular names

kudzu tropical, puero (fr.) ; tropical kudzu (angl.) ; puero (angl. d’Australie)

## Source note

The varieties phaseoloides and subspicata (Benth.) A.N.Egan & B.Pan, with acute lateral lobes of the calyx, are rather Asian in distribution, but sometimes introduced elsewhere.
