# *Voacanga africana* Stapf Planche 121 ; Figure 14(E-G)

**Genus**: [[Voacanga]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. Linn. Soc. , Bot. 30 : 87 (1894)

## Description

Shrub or small tree reaching 4 m in height; branches glabrous to pubescent. Leaf: imparipinnate, leaflets 9–23, petiolules of 0.5–1.5 cm, somewhat variable in shape and size, obovate to narrowly elliptic, 0.8–8 × 0.4–4.2 cm, cuneate at base or decurrent on the petiole, acute to acuminate at apex, glabrous on both surfaces or (partially) pubescent below; secondary veins 8 to 22 pairs. Inflorescence generally with long peduncle, of 2–8 cm, generally strongly ramified, bracteate, generally all fallen before opening of the first flower. Flower: pedicel of 3–25 mm; calyx with tube with scattered but clearly visible colleters, globose, 0.5–2 times as long as the tube of 1.5–3 mm, imbricate in bud generally half reflexed; corolla yellow to white, in bud 1.5 cm, lobes reaching 0.4 to 0.75 of the length of the bud, forming an almost conical head, tube subcylindrical, twisted almost from the base to the throat, lobes slightly shorter, slightly longer than the calyx, lobes erect, long of 2–4 mm, generally with small hairs from the throat to the base of the lobes, lobes 1.4–2.5 times as long as the tube, of 15–30 × 7–15 mm; stamens exserted over 0.5–1.2 mm or sometimes just included, anther long of 4–5 mm; pistil long of 7–12.5 mm. Fruit: free mericarps, often only one developed, obliquely subglobose, often laterally compressed, long of 2.5–8 cm, green; wall of 5–15 mm thickness. Seed: obliquely ellipsoid, long of 1–1.5 mm, finely tuberculate, aril yellow to orange. Distribution: Tropical continental Africa from Senegal to Mozambique and in the islands of the Gulf of Guinea; in Gabon: all provinces.

Ecology: Secondary forests and gallery forest in Gabon, up to 400 m altitude elsewhere up to 1500 m.

Use: [Uses are mentioned here with some doubt, see the notes below.] Different parts of the plant have medicinal uses throughout its range. The latex or a decoction or infusion of the stem bark, leaves or roots are applied to wounds, boils or sores and are used to treat nosebleeds, eczema, fungal infections and scabies. They are also absorbed to treat cardiac problems, hypertension and rheumatic affections. The latex is applied to the teeth to treat caries and in drops in the eyes, it treats ophthalmia. In Europe, tabernine, an alkaloid easily converted into vincamine, is extracted from the seeds, widely used in geriatrics. Seed extracts are also used in medicines intended to treat cardiac diseases, to lower blood pressure and to treat cancer. The latex has been employed to falsify Hevea rubber, and it serves as glue to capture birds. The wood considered of inferior quality is used as firewood. From the branches, arrows and knife handles are made. In Nigeria, a thread is made from it which is mixed with cotton thread and other fibres to make mats. In Tanzania, the species is planted as an ornamental plant because of its fragrant white flowers. Vernacular names: voacanga, African voacanga (fr.), smallfruit wildfrangipani (eng.).

## Source note

There is great variation in this species across its extensive range. It is therefore possible that more than one species is included under this name, for which reason there are no synonyms indicated above.
