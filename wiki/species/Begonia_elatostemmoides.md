# *Begonia elatostemmoides* Hook.f.

**Genus**: [[Begonia]]
**Family**: [[Begoniaceae]]

## Description

Herb 40(–80) cm tall, densely tomentose to pubescent. Stipules ± persistent, ovate or triangular, 3–11 × 0.5–4.5 mm, dentate-ciliate at the apex. Leaf: petiole 2–10(–20) mm; blade asymmetric, obovate, ovate or elliptic to rhombic, (2–)5–13(–15) × 1–6 cm, obliquely cordate at the base, one auricle more developed than the other, acuminate at the apex; margin almost entire to doubly dentate-ciliate, densely hirsute on both faces. Inflorescence with 1–3(–6) ♂ flowers and one ♀ flower; peduncle robust, 1.5–6 mm; bracts persistent, narrowly triangular, 1.5–6 × 0.2–2.5 mm, dentate-ciliate. ♂ Flower: pedicel 4–12 mm; tepals elliptic to ovate or obovate, 3–9 × 2–6 mm, pubescent on the outside, ciliate, white, ± transparent, the base and margin pink; stamens 9–25. ♀ Flower: pedicel 0.5–5 mm; tepals 5.5–10 × 2–7.5 mm; ovary narrowly ellipsoid with 3 unequal wings, 4–15 × 2.5–7 mm (wings included); styles 3, bifid at the apex, stigmas lunulate. Fruit solitary, on a peduncle 5–10 mm, elliptic, 6.5–16 × 4–12 mm, pubescent, with a beak reaching 1 mm.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Democratic Republic of the Congo

## Ecology

dense humid primary or secondary forests; between 30 and 950 m altitude. Uses: the leaves are eaten as a vegetable. A decoction of the leaves is used to treat cough. B: Akendengué & Louis (1994), Wilczek (1969a). BIBLIOGRAPHY KE EWALD
