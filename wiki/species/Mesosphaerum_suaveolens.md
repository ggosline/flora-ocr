# *Mesosphaerum suaveolens* (L.) Kuntze

**Genus**: [[Mesosphaerum]]
**Protologue**: Revis. gen. pl. 2 : 525 (1891)

## Synonyms

- Hyptis suaveolens (L.) Poit.. Ann. Mus. Hist. Nat. 7 : 472 (1806).

## Description

Annual or short-lived perennial herb, erect, up to 2.5 m tall, with a strong unpleasant odor; stem hollow, branched, viscid hispid with glandular and non-glandular hairs. Leaf: petiole 5–60 mm; blade ovate to orbicular, 2–6 × 1.5–7 cm, cordate to rounded, truncate or shortly cuneate at the base, sometimes oblique, acute at the apex, regularly or irregularly dentate, slightly to densely hispid on both surfaces. Inflorescence composed of axillary cymes, lax, unilateral, with up to 6 flowers; peduncle 0–15 mm; bracteoles linear, 1–3 mm long. Flower: pedicel 1–5 mm; calyx turbinate-campanulate, 4–5 mm long at anthesis and 8–12 mm in fruit, pilose and with sessile glands, with subulate teeth, hairy around the throat; corolla 10–12 mm long, blue to violet, with white tube. Fruit: achenes usually 1 or 2, oblong-ellipsoid, emarginate at the apex, 3.8–4 mm long, gray-black, finely tuberculate except at the base.

## Distribution

Pantropical weed widely distributed, native to central and southern America; in Africa widely distributed, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Ogooué-Maritime. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC); invasive in some regions.

## Ecology

Open areas and disturbed zones; in Gabon at 60 m altitude, elsewhere up to 500 m. Use: Used in Congo against sexually transmitted diseases. Bibliography: Burkill (1995), Harley & Pastore (2012), Morales (2011), Morton (1963), Paton et al. (2013)
