# *Albizia laurentii* De Wildeman

**Genus**: [[Albizia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Miss. Laur.: 92 (1905); Ann. Mus. Congo b., Bot., sér. 5, 2: 217 (1907); l.c., 3: 189 (1910); BJBB 4: 84 (1914); BJBB 5: 246 (1916); Pl. Bequaert. 3: 50 (1925); Th. & H. Dur., Syll.: 188 (1909); PELLEGRIN, Légum. Gabon: 13 (1948); GILBERT & BOUTIQUE, FCB 3: 173 (1952)

## Description

l.c., 3: 189 (1910); BJBB 4: 84 (1914); BJBB 5: 246 (1916); Pl. Bequaert. 3: 50 (1925); Th. & H. Dur., Syll.: 188 (1909); PELLEGRIN, Légum. Gabon: 13 (1948); GILBERT & BOUTIQUE, FCB 3: 173 (1952).

Tree 5–25 m tall. Bole short, diameter 20–40 cm. Bark thick, 5 mm; rhytidome fissured light grey; section orange. Young branches glabrous to puberulent. Older branches glabrous, longitudinally wrinkled, with numerous brownish lenticels. Petiole pubescent, 1.5–3.5 cm long, upper surface ± plane (especially toward the distal end) with a very prominent gland on the upper half, lower surface sometimes terminating in a narrowly triangular, pubescent, acute mucro, early caducous. Pinnae 1 pair. Pinna rachis pubescent, 0.5–2 cm long, channelled above and bearing toward the apex 1 or more often 2 very prominent glands. Leaflets 12 pairs per pinna, subsessile or petiolulate (petiolule reaching 2 mm, pubescent above (especially the inner edge) and glabrous below). Lamina glabrous on both surfaces, dark green to black above and green below when dry; asymmetrically elliptic-lanceolate, 5.5–14 × 2–5 cm, base asymmetric: proximal edge rounded and distal edge attenuate; apex attenuate-mucronate. Midvein eccentric, prominent on both surfaces, sparsely pubescent at the base becoming glabrous to subglabrous toward the apex of the upper surface, sparsely pubescent on the lower surface. Secondary veins 10–20 pairs, ascending, not arched (except the basal ones), prominent on both surfaces; glabrous or very sparsely pubescent below. Tertiary veins in a reticulate pattern with polygonal or elongate meshes, distinctly prominent above, ± strongly prominent below.

Inflorescences in capitate spikes solitary in the axils of terminal leaves or grouped in terminal panicles. Spike peduncle pubescent, longitudinally sulcate, 1.5–3 cm long, apex slightly claviform. Basal bracts pubescent, very narrowly triangular, apex acute. Interflorals glabrous on both surfaces, margins pubescent, very narrowly triangular. Basal flowers pentamerous, white in life, sessile. Calyx cylindric, distinctly but not densely pubescent with erect hairs; ± 3 mm long, free lobes narrowly triangular, 0.25–0.5 mm long, apex acute. Petals pubescent like the sepals but more abundantly at the apex of the free lobes, ± 10.5 mm long; fused into a tube for ± 9 mm; free lobes lanceolate, ± 1.5 mm long, apex acute. Stamens numerous, bright red or dark in life, ± 27 mm long; fused into a tube for ± 22 mm; free filaments ± 5 mm; anther ellipsoid, 0.3 mm long. Pistil obscurely stipitate; ovary glabrous to pubescent, 1.2 mm long with 8 ovules; style long and slender, ± 2.6 mm. Central flower sessile. Calyx cupuliform, sparsely pubescent, ± 2 mm long; free lobes triangular with acute apex. Corolla with petals fused into a cylindric tube ± 6.2 mm long, shortly pubescent externally, ± 8 mm long; free lobes triangular, ± 2 mm long. Stamens numerous, fused into a tube ± 7 mm long, not exsert; free filaments reflexed, ± 5 mm long. Pistil with ovary pubescent on the ventral suture, 1 mm long, with 10 ovules; style ± 8 mm long.

Pod weakly coriaceous, very shortly and sparsely pubescent, elliptic-oblong, 7–13 × 1.5–2.5 mm, base stipitate; lateral surfaces with venation showing long transverse meshes, not very prominent; apex rounded, weakly apiculate. Seeds orbicular to elliptic, 7–8 mm long, areole slightly displaced from the micropyle, same shape as the seed. – Pl. 34, p. 137.

## Type

Laurent E. & M. S.n., Zaire, Forestier central, lac Tumba (BR)

## Discussion

PL. 34. – Albizia laurentii De Wild.: 1, general aspect × 2/3; 2, 3, pinna, apex × 2; 4, leaflet, lower surface, detail × 2.5; 5, inflorescence × 1.5; 6, bracteole × 30; 7, external basal flower × 5; 8, pistil × 8; 9, idem × 20; 9, central flower × 5; 10, pistil × 8; 10, idem × 20; 11, fruit × 1; 12, seed × 4. (1–4, Hallé N. & Le Thomas 199; 5–10, Hallé N. 3978; 11–12, Leemans 263 (Zaïre)).

This species is Guinean; its range extends from Cameroon to Zaïre. It is found along watercourses, in swampy or periodically flooded forests. Flowering occurs from June to November.

## Uses

the wood is used to make axe handles
