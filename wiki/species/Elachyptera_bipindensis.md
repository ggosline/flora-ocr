# *Elachyptera bipindensis* (Loesener) Hallé ex Wilczek

**Genus**: [[Elachyptera]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: FCB 9: 140 (1960);

## Description

- E. bipindensis (LOES.) HALLÊ, Bull. IFAN, sér. A, 21 (3): tab. 22, fig. B (1959), nom.

, nom. nud.; Bot. Jahrb. 34: 103, fig. 1B (1904), descr., ; EXELL & MENDONÇA, CFA 2 (1): 16 (1954).

– Hippocratea rubrocostata PIERRE, nomen in mult. herb.

Liane exceeding 10–30 m in height; wood not known. Leaves opposite, rarely subopposite. Petiole 5–9 mm, canaliculate. Blade chartaceous elliptic, (3)5–12 × 2–6.5 cm; base acute; apex acuminate for 7–15 mm; margins entire or with a few rare indistinct denticules toward the apex. Secondary veins 5–8 pairs moderately ascending.

Inflorescences many-flowered, axillary or terminal, in cymes or panicles of cymes, 2–15 cm long, sometimes interrupted by leaves. Axes dark reddish-brown when dry, 4-angled, almost subwinged; bracts 0.5 mm. Pedicels 0.8–1.5 mm. Bud green, ovoid, approximately 1 mm. Flower very fragrant, green with white corolla then cream becoming yellow, 3–3.3 mm in diameter. Sepals short, ovate, approximately 0.5 mm broad. Petals oblong, 1.5 × 0.8 mm, with one axial vein, margin somewhat irregularly crenate. Disk cupuliform, 0.8 mm in diameter, with margin split under each stamen and somewhat sinuous from contact with the anthers in the bud. Filaments narrow, deeply inserted, 0.6 mm long. Pistil subpyramidal; style erect with 3 small lobes opposite the stamens; ovary 3-locular, ovules 2 per locule.

Mericarps solitary or 2–3 together, 4.5–6 × 3–3.7 × 0.8 cm, ovate. Integument reddish-brown, rugose, rather thin. Seeds 32–44 × 20–25 × 2.3 mm, including the atrophied wing 5–10 × 5 mm. Cotyledons fused or ± distinctly adherent. Funicular tubules thick and subcylindrical on a cylindrical base; at the base of the valves, scars 2–2.5 mm high. – Pl. 53, fig. 15–26, p. 185; pl. 54, fig. 15–27, p. 187.

## Discussion

Cameroon, Muni, Gabon, Congo (Sita 1526), Cabinda, Zaïre. Riparian liane of the most humid dense forests.

This species is very variable, especially in its habit and leaf dimensions; it adapts to extremely diverse ecological conditions, humid or very dry. The distribution range is wide but discontinuous in West Africa.

Casamance (Berhaut 6284), Mali, São Tomé (De Wilde J.J. 447), Cameroon, Central African Republic (Tisserant 1472), Zaïre, Angola, Ethiopia (Jansen 6369); Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Zanzibar (Sacleux 1174), Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Transvaal. A sterile and doubtful specimen was discovered in Mauritania (Monod 16125). To be searched for in Gabon.
