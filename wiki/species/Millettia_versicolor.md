# *Millettia versicolor* Welw. ex Baker

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Oliver, Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 219 (1871). Shrub or tree, reaching 20(–30) m in height; branchlets finely striate, glabrous; buds somewhat flattened. Stipules caducous. Leaf: petiole 4–8 cm; rachis 9–19 cm; petiolule ± 5 mm, pilose; stipels subulate, 1–3 mm; leaflets 9 to 13, with oblong-lanceolate blade, 9–18 × 4–8 cm, base obtuse, apex acuminate, glabrous above, with fine appressed hairs below; lateral veins in 9 to 19 pairs, prominent below. Inflorescence terminal, in ample panicle, 20–40 cm in length; branchlets 6–13 cm, with appressed hairs; bracts caducous. Flower ± 2 cm in length; pedicel ± 4 mm; bracteoles caducous, ovate, ± 2.5 mm in length, acute; calyx with tube 3–4 mm in length, lobes rounded, 3–4 mm in length, rusty-pubescent; corolla pink to violet, standard broadly elliptic to suborbicular, 20–22 × 14–16 mm, base subcordate, with 2 callosities, without auricles, apex somewhat emarginate, dorsum pilose and shiny, glabrous and white abaxially, wings oblong, blade ± 16 × 7 mm, with claw 4 mm, auriculate, with violet hairs along the midvein, keel obovate, blade ± 14 × 10 mm, with claw 8 mm, glabrous; stamens diadelphous (9 + 1) but the vexillar stamen adnate to the others, ± 22 mm in length; ovary 12 mm in length, pilose, with 4 or 5 ovules; style 7 mm, glabrescen. Pod oblong, (8–)10–20 × 2–4 cm, base attenuate, apex obtuse and acuminate, with 1 to 5 seeds, velvety, rusty, with woody valves. Seed elliptic-oblong to quadrangular, flattened, 20 × 13 mm, brown; hilum 3 mm.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from all provinces except Estuaire

## Ecology

galleries, secondary and dry forests, forest margins; in Gabon, as elsewhere, at 10–460 m altitude. Flowers and fruits for most of the year. Uses: A very ornamental species and often planted in villages. The golden or black-brown wood provides posts and fence wood for houses, used for framing and boards, it is easy to turn and to polish. The bark is vermifuge. A decoction of leaves is used in baths against syphilis. A tea of the bark is well recommended for general health (Towns & van Andel 2014). The tree provides a poison for hunting and fishing (Banzouzi et al. 2008)
