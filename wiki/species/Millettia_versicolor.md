# *Millettia versicolor* Welw. ex Baker

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Oliver, Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 219 (1871). Shrub or tree, reaching 20(–30) m in height; branchlets finely striated, glabrous; buds somewhat flattened. Stipules caducous. Leaf: petiole 4–8 cm; rachis 9–19 cm; petiolule ± 5 mm, villous; stipels subulate, 1–3 mm; leaflets 9 to 13, with oblong-lanceolate lamina, 9–18 × 4–8 cm, base obtuse, apex acuminate, glabrous above, with fine appressed hairs below; lateral nerves in 9 to 19 pairs, prominent below. Inflorescence terminal, in ample panicle, 20–40 cm long; branchlets 6–13 cm, with appressed hairs; bracts caducous. Flower ± 2 cm long; pedicel ± 4 mm; bracteoles caducous, ovate, ± 2.5 mm long, acute; calyx with tube 3–4 mm long, lobes rounded, 3–4 mm long, reddish-brown pubescent; corolla pink to violet, standard broadly elliptic to suborbicular, 20–22 × 14–16 mm, base subcordate, with 2 callosities, without auricles, apex slightly emarginate, dorsal surface villous lustrous, glabrous and white abaxially, wings oblong, lamina ± 16 × 7 mm, with claw 4 mm, auriculate, with violet hairs along the midline, keel obovate, lamina ± 14 × 10 mm, with claw 8 mm, glabrous; stamens diadelphous (9 + 1) but the vexillary stamen adnate to the others, ± 22 mm long; ovary 12 mm long, villous, with 4 or 5 ovules; style 7 mm, glabrescent. Pod oblong, (8–)10–20 × 2–4 cm, base attenuate, apex obtuse and acuminate, with 1 to 5 seeds, velvety, reddish-brown, with woody valves. Seed elliptic-oblong to quadrangular, flattened, 20 × 13 mm, brown; hilum 3 mm.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of Congo, Democratic Republic of Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from all provinces except Estuaire.

## Ecology

gallery forests, secondary and dry forests, forest margins; in Gabon, as elsewhere, at 10–460 m altitude. Flowers and fruits most of the year. Uses: A very ornamental species and often planted in villages. The golden or black-brown wood provides posts and fencing wood for houses, used for timber and planks, it is easy to turn and polish. The bark is vermifuge. A decoction of leaves is used in baths against syphilis. A tea of the bark is well recommended for general health status (Towns & van Andel 2014). The tree furnishes a poison for hunting and fishing (Banzouzi et al. 2008).
