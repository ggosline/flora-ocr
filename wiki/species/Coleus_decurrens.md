# *Coleus decurrens* Gürke Figure 4(A)

**Genus**: [[Coleus]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 19 : 215 (1894)

## Synonyms

- Coleus elatus Baker in Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 5 : 427 (1900).
- Plectranthus decurrens (Gürke) J.K.Morton, Bot. J. Linn. Soc. 58 : 267 (1962).

## Description

Herb or shrub, (0.15–)0.3–2 m tall; stem erect, glabrous to sparsely pubescent toward the base, becoming densely pubescent on the inflorescence axis with colored non-glandular hairs. Leaf sessile; limb elliptic, (8–)15–30 × 6–10 cm, long and narrowly decurrent for 5–15 mm, acuminate at the apex, crenate to dentate, nearly glabrous but with hairs on the veins and brown or pale sessile glands scattered on the lower surface. Inflorescence terminal, generally with several verticillasters separated by internodes reaching 20 mm; cymes sessile, 3-flowered; bracts narrowly elliptic-ovate to ovate, reaching 5 mm in length, entire. Flower: pedicel 4 mm; calyx tubular, slightly curved upward in fruit, 4–6 mm long, 10–12 mm in fruit, sparsely pubescent, with pale or brownish sessile glands, upper lip ovate, mucronate at the apex, decurrent, lateral lobes deltoid, acuminate at the apex, lower lobes divided to the base, narrowly triangular, subulate at the apex, slightly curved upward; corolla 15–20 mm long, orange-yellow, nearly glabrous but with pale sessile glands, tube sigmoid, 8–10 mm long, lower lip 8–10 mm long, deeply cucullate and enclosing the stamens; stamens with filaments fused at the base. Fruits: achenes ovoid, slightly flattened, 1.5 mm long, brown, finely reticulate due to the pattern of epidermal cells.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko, Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda and Angola; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

river margins or open forest stations; in Gabon at 250–580 m elevation, elsewhere at 30–1250 m. B: Morton (1963), Morales (2011). Bibliography
