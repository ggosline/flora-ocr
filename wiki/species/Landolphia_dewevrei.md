# *Landolphia dewevrei* Stapf Planche 45(7-19) ; Figure 6(H)

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 4(1) : 52 (1902)

## Description

Fl. trop. Afr. 4(1) : 52 (1902). Large liana; branches hairy or glabrous. Leaf: petiole 3–9 mm; lamina narrowly elliptic to elliptic or obovate, 2–4 times as long as wide, 0.8–12 × 0.4–4.3 cm, subcordate to acute at the base, always decurrent on the petiole, acuminate or sometimes acute or obtuse at the apex, dark green, glabrous, but hairy on the midvein below; secondary veins 8 to 19 pairs, straight or slightly curved, often more visible above, tertiary venation reticulate. Inflorescence terminal, long of 6–13 cm with 6 or more flowers; pedicel 0.3–2.3 cm, sometimes tendril-shaped and longer, hairy to glabrous. Flower fragrant; pedicel 1–5 mm; bracteoles at calyx; calyx with unequal lobes, 2–4 times as long as wide, 1.6–4 × 1–2.5 mm, glabrous or partially hairy, often with visible membranous margin and keeled; corolla whitish-green, partially pale red or cream, with tube 3.6–6 mm long, 1–2.3 mm wide at the level of the anthers, towards the throat with thickened wall on 4–7 mm, the exterior hairy on 0.3–2.8 mm from the base, the interior villous at the level of the anthers, otherwise glabrous, with ovate to oblong lobes, 0.6–1.3 times the length of the tube, 1.7–4.4 times as long as wide, 1.9–4.2 × 0.8–1.1 mm, at least partially hairy; stamens inserted at 0.54–0.71 of the length of the tube, included 0.2–1.1 mm, anther 0.8–1.2 mm long; pistil 3–4.5 mm, ovary glabrous at the base with longer and denser pilosity towards the apex; style 0.4–1.9 mm, glabrous except at the base. Fruit subglobose to ellipsoid, 2.3–3.8 × 1.5–2.8 cm, rounded at the apex and at the base, greenish dull, becoming violet or brown at maturity, often with circular brownish spots (0.1–1 mm diameter) often fused, with 15 to 35 seeds; wall 1–6.5(–10.5) mm thick; sclerified layer 0.5–1.5 mm thick. Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Angola; in Gabon: Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forests, gallery forests in Gabon, up to m altitude; elsewhere at 0–1400 m. B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953). BIBLIOGRAPHY
