# *Boehmeria virgata* (G.Forst.) Guill. subsp. macrophylla (Hornem.) Friis & Wilmot-Dear

**Genus**: [[Boehmeria]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Blumea 58(2) : 161 (2013)

## Description

var. molliuscula (Blume) Friis & Wilmot-Dear Plate 24 Blumea 58(2): 161 (2013). Boehmeria macrophylla Hornem., Enum. pl. hort. hafn. 2: 809 (1815), pro parte, non B. macrophylla D.Don (1825), nec B. macrophylla (Thunb.) Siebold & Zucc. (1846). Boehmeria platyphylla D.Don, Prodr. Fl. Nepal.: 60 (1825), pro parte. Shrubs or perennial herbs, monoecious, up to 3 m tall; stems simple, aqueous or rather robust, or long branches more slender, often overhanging and arising from a woody rootstock, rarely short, slender and well branched; branches glabrescent, pubescent or tomentose, particularly on the terminal parts, with spreading hairs. Lateral stipules, ± free, lanceolate to narrowly triangular, 6–9 mm long, membranous, brown, glabrous or puberulent on the nerves. Leaves opposite, generally the two of the same pair unequal, the larger up to twice the smaller; petiole 2.5–15 cm, glabrous, pubescent or tomentose; blade narrowly ovate to narrowly elliptic, 4–20(–25) × 2.5–12(–15) cm, almost symmetrical, base subcordate, truncate, rounded or cuneate, apex acuminate, sometimes almost caudate, the extremity reaching 1.5 cm, margin serrate, with 15 to 35 teeth on each side, sometimes absent in the lower third, coriaceous, dark green on both faces, upper side glabrescent, scabrid, pubescent or tomentose, with visible punctiform cystoliths, lower side glabrescent to tomentose; venation almost symmetrically triplinerved, the basal pair of lateral nerves prominent, reaching the distal third before looping with the upper lateral or median nerves, rarely terminating in the 5 to 10 distal teeth, upper lateral nerves in 3 to 6(–7) pairs, tertiary venation scalariform. Inflorescence unisexual, rarely with a few flowers of the opposite sex, solitary in the upper axils, the male and female quite comparable, appearing as a discontinuous spike, 5–50 cm long; glomerules dense and sessile, distant 2–6 mm apart, each provided with a short caducous bract, the male ones 2–4 mm in diameter, the female ones 3–5 mm in diameter. Male flower sessile or subsessile, 4-merous, ± 1 mm in diameter; tepals with a ± pronounced dorsal gibbosity, apiculate, often with hooked hairs. Female flower larger in fruit, sessile; perianth tubulous glabrescent or pubescent, with 2 clearly marked apical teeth, ± 1 mm long; stigma exserted, 1–2.5 mm. Fruit included in the persistent perianth, ± 2 mm long, glabrescent to pubescent.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa, from Sierra Leone and Senegal to Ethiopia, southward to Angola, Zimbabwe and Mozambique and also to the Comoros Islands, Madagascar, Mauritius and Réunion Island. The species as a whole is present from tropical Africa, Madagascar and the Mascarene Islands, extending to southern China, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea, Australia and the eastern Pacific, to the Society Islands, the Marquesas Islands and the Austral Islands. In Gabon, var. molliuscula is common and widely distributed, in Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forests, particularly in clearings, along forest edges and riverbanks; in Gabon at 200–800 m, elsewhere (both var. molliuscula and the species as a whole) up to 2100 m.

## Source note

The leaves of all specimens seen for Gabon are coriaceous, relatively small, dark green above, paler below and not truly rough, thus in spite of the five informal forms cited under A to E and described by Letouzey (1968), all the Gabonese material must be referred to var. molliuscula. In the cited literature, identifications under the synonyms of this taxon are to be taken as B. virgata subsp. macrophylla var. molliuscula. B. virgata subsp. macrophylla is distributed in Africa and Asia; subsp. virgata is localized mainly in the eastern part of the species' range, particularly in the Pacific islands.
