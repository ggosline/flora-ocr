# *Lophira alata* Banks ex C.F.Gaertn. Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Lophira]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Suppl. carp. 1(1) : 52, t. 188 (1805)

## Description

Suppl. carp. 1(1) : 52, t. 188 (1805). L. procera A.Chev., Bois Côte Ivoire : 154 (1909). Tree reaching 60 m in height; trunk cylindrical, up to 160(–180) cm in diameter, slightly thickened at the base and without buttresses; bark scaly, rust-brown to orange-brown, peeling in numerous long vertical and narrow bands; branches erect. Stipules lanceolate, 4–6(–9) × 1.5–2 mm, acuminate. Leaf glabrous: petiole 0.5–3 cm (that of young trees up to 4.5 cm); blade oblong-obovate, 8–45 × 3.5–12 cm, ratio 2–4, cuneate at the base, rounded to emarginate at the apex, papyraceous, with entire margin, blade of seedlings and young trees often much narrower and with acute apex; midvein prominent on both faces, lateral veins anastomosing, apparent on both faces. Inflorescence loose, pyramidal, 12–20 cm in length, glabrous. Flower: pedicel 1–2 cm, articulate near the apex; sepals unequal, the 2 outer ones ovate-lanceolate, 10–12 × 4–5 mm, acute at the apex, the others broadly ovate, 9–10 × 6–7 mm, obtuse at the apex; petals obcordate, 12–17 × 10–14 mm; stamens with filament 3–7 mm, anthers narrowly oblong, 4–6 mm in length, yellow to orange; ovary conical, 8–10 × 2–2.5 mm, grooved, verrucose, with 8 to 16 ovules; stigmas 2.5 mm in length. Fruit conical, approximately 3 × 1 cm, glabrous; sepals unequally winged, one narrowly semi-ovate and 8–12 × 1.5–2.5 cm, the other narrowly subelliptic and 3–6 × 0.7–1.5 cm. Seed ovoid, 1.5–1.7 × 0.7–0.8 cm.

## Distribution

From Guinea-Bissau to Ghana and from Nigeria to the Central African Republic, the Republic of the Congo and the Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, common, but not yet collected in Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Lolo and Nyanga

## Ecology

primary and secondary evergreen or semi-deciduous forest, on ridges, but also on humid rock faces or even periodically flooded areas, in forest edges and gallery forest, on sandy or sandy-clay soil; in Gabon, up to 600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Uses: L. alata is one of the most exploited timber species of tropical Africa. Thanks to its hardness and resistance, the wood is highly valued in heavy construction for extreme conditions. It is also used in joinery, sporting articles, toys, agricultural tools, turning and hardboards. It is a good firewood that provides excellent charcoal. In traditional medicine in Gabon, bark decoction is applied locally for kidney complaints and is applied as an enema against menstrual pain, the beginning of hernia or stomach problems. The seeds contain an oil, which is sometimes used in cooking or as an ointment and for soap manufacture
