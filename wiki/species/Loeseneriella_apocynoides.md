General distribution, all varieties combined: Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Togo (Warnecke 404, L), Nigeria,

Cameroon, Muni (Tessmann 132, K), Gabon, Central African Republic, Congo (Pauwels 4391, Mbamou Island), Zaïre, Angola, Uganda, Tanzania, Upper Katanga.

Contrary to our opinion of 1958, it was appropriate to recognize a broader status for this species and to apply the name L. apocynoides var. guineensis (Hutch. & Moss) N. Hallé to the more abundant materials from lower Ivory Coast. In equatorial Africa the typical variety is distinctly dominant.

a. var. apocynoides

High liane capable of exceeding 10-30 m in height. Young branchlets rust-brown, puberulent. Wood excavated by radiating grooves: 4 for 1-1.5 cm in diameter, then, by dichotomy of wood sectors, 8, 16 then approximately 32 for 4 cm in diameter, grooves of unequal depth and ± continuous. Stipules reduced pubescent, juxtaposed on certain nodes, spaced without transverse fold on others. Petiole pubescent 3-8 mm. Limb ± discolorous, ovate 4-13 × 1.5-6 cm, entire or more rarely somewhat crenate, pubescent below, hairs simple rust-colored pluricellular, especially along the margins, and especially at the juvenile stage; extremities obtuse or rounded, the apex sometimes slightly acuminate. Secondary veins, 5-9 pairs.

Inflorescences in slender axillary cymes, 1.5-9 cm long, multiflorous, sometimes grouped in terminal panicles; axes and small bracts rust-tomentose as are the pedicels 0.5-3 mm long. Bud pubescent ± conical. Flowers greenish, grayish or yellowish, sometimes somewhat rusty, 3-8 mm in diameter. Sepals puberulent or villous, deltoid ± elongated, 0.5-1.5 mm. Petals narrowly triangular 2-5.5 × 0.7-2 mm, with external face tomentose to hirsute, with internal face tomentellose to velvety, clearly valvate in prefloration. Gynoecium short and massive; the glabrous disk presents a thin and very narrow cupular rim which may reduce to a small non-cupular rim (Hallé N. 1596). Stamens inserted contiguously approximately 0.2 mm above the disk; filament trapezoid, 0.5-0.7 × 0.6-0.8 mm; anther 0.2-0.3 mm wide. Ovary somewhat convex, pubescent around the base of the style; the latter is glabrous, cylindroconical truncate, approximately 0.3 mm high. Ovules 5-10 per locule.

Mericarps 4-7 × 2-4 cm, most often elliptic, rust-colored puberulent then ± glabrescent. Seeds 30-36 × 12-15 mm; cotyledonary portion 20 × 7 mm; cotyledons fused. – Pl. 71, fig. 1-4, 6-18, p. 249.

## Discussion

This variety groups together numerous forms, with bud ± elongated or attenuated, with pubescence ± rusty and ± villous, with disk ± cupuliform, with ovules ± numerous; it is difficult to define the limits of these forms. The typical form has long and very attenuated buds, approximately 7 ovules per locule, and a petal with internal pilosity shorter and much finer than the external pilosity.

All these forms as well as the variety that follows prefer riparian stations, temporarily flooded forests; occasionally also in forests on slopes or on ± rocky summits.
