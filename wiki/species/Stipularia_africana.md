# *Stipularia africana* P. de Beauv. (P.L. 33)

**Genus**: [[Stipularia]]
**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Palisot de Beauvois, Fl. Oware, 2 : 26, t. 75 (1807)

## Synonyms

- Sabicea africana (P. de Beauv.) Hepper, Kew Bull. 13 : 292 (1958).

## Description

Highly variable plant, erect, herbaceous and little-branched, or ‡ woody, reaching 0.50 to more than 3 m in height; the base of the stem is well-lignified and reaches 6 cm in diam., in the larger forms which are rather ramified. Apices glabrous or ‡ pubescent. Stipules red and finally brown desiccated persistent, ovate ± acute 15-30 mm wide, glabrous or only pilose at the base. Petiole 1-6(10) cm long, sometimes pubescent notably on the margins of the channel. Blade discolorous glabrous above, white felted below, elliptic or elongately obovate (10)12-34 × 4-15 cm; base attenuate acute; apex ± briefly lanceolate. Midvein generally glabrous above and below, but sometimes pubescent. 15-22 pairs of secondary veins.

Inflorescences sessile enveloped by an involucre ± voluminous 2-4 cm tall, entirely glabrous or ± pilose toward the base, with margin (3)4 lobed, pink or red erect and serving as a receptacle to a small marsh. Flowers numerous with staggered flowering, sessile, inserted between bracts ± broadly laminate exceeding the young buds. Ovary glabrous surrounded at the base by stiff bristles. Calyx tubular with 5 pubescent lobes. Corolla white, glabrous, opening above the marsh which covers the buds, with tube ca. 2.5-3 cm long, with 5 lobes, with dense and short internal pubescence. Stamens exserted in the throat of the brevistyle flower, they are conversely deeply hidden in the longistyle flower. Pollen tetraporose with well-distinct pores. Disc tubular elevated. Style glabrous 3-5-lobed. Ovary with (2)3-5 locules.

Infructescence ± encumbered with decomposing debris. Ripe fruit bright red 15-17 × 11-14 mm, with pedicel sometimes accrescent. Pulp white or pinkish. Seeds brown 0.6 mm; testa with very fine stretched reticule (fig. 1 p. 18).

## Type

P. de Beauvois (P!, ex herb. Richard), « derrière le Galbar » (Calabar), « au-delà du royaume d'Oware » (Warri), Sud Nigeria

## Discussion

River banks, ± open marshy grounds, the feet often in water; species generally gregarious. From Sierra Leone to the Congo.

Fruit with sweet edible pulp according to Walker.

Vernacular names according to Walker and Sillans: Irondowélé (Nkomi, Orungu); Dirundubèlè (Ngowé); Diyanga (Eshira).
