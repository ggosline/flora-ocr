# *Barleria alata* S. Moore

**Genus**: [[Barleria]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: S. Moore, J. Bot. 18 : 266 (1880), excl. Welwitsch 5194 et 5195.—C.B. Clarke, F.T.A. 5 : 158 (1899), excl. syn. B. lancifolia T. Anders. et Welwitsch 5194, 5195.—S. Moore, Journ. Bot., Suppl. 2 : 135 (1930)

## Synonyms

- Barleria lancifolia auct. : Hiern, Cat. Afr. Pl. Welwitsch, Dicotyl. IV, 816 (1900), excl. Welwitsch 5194, 5195; non T. Anders. J. Linn. Soc., Bot., 7: 28 (1863).

## Description

, excl. Welwitsch 5194 et 5195.—C.B. Clarke, F.T.A. 5 : 158 (1899), excl. syn. B. lancifolia T. Anders. et Welwitsch 5194, 5195.—S. Moore, Journ. Bot., Suppl. 2 : 135 (1930).

Herbaceous or suffrutescent plant, branched, reaching approximately 0.80 m in height, finely pubescent especially in the inflorescences, woody at the base; stems subquadrangular, with internodes 5-10 cm long. Petioles winged 1-5 (7) cm long; leaf blade up to 9 × 4.5 cm, ovate-acuminate, cuspidate; acumen approximately 8 mm long; base triangular, attenuate-acute decurrent along the entire length of the petiole; translucent when dry, blackish above, lighter olivaceous-greyish below; on young leaves, the upper surface bears relatively thick unicellular hairs. Nerves below covered with some very small hairs. 4-5 pairs of lateral nerves.

Inflorescences elongate, up to 20 cm long, with peduncle 3-4 cm long; the lower bracts resemble leaves, pubescent, with winged petiole reaching 1.2 cm long, and blade 2.5 × 1 cm; upper bracts sessile, linear-subulate, 10-12 mm long, pubescent. Flowers subsessile, solitary axillary, with pedicel at most 2 mm long, pubescent. Calyx pubescent, with numerous multicellular glandular hairs, especially at the margin, up to 14 mm long, lateral sepals subulate-linear, 9 × 0.7 mm; posterior sepal distinctly obovate 13 × 7 mm with ± 5 nerves; anterior sepals fused into a single lobe scarcely smaller than the posterior sepal and very slightly emarginate at the apex, obovate, 12 × 7 mm, with ± 5 palmate nerves. Corolla bluish-white or very pale mauve, reaching up to 35 mm long, slightly pubescent outside; tube 12 mm long and approximately 6 mm in diameter; lobes 16-19 mm long, rounded. Ovary slightly pubescent, 3 mm long; style glabrous 28 mm long.

Fruit slightly pubescent $ 18 \times 5 $ mm. Seed strongly flattened, ovate-elliptic, $ 3 \times 4 \times 0.3 $ mm; testa yellowish-brown, with very marked concentric rugose rings.

## Source note

This species has a rather complex taxonomic history. The contradictory and imprecise indications of the authors require the following clarification.

S. Moore, having based his species on heterogeneous material (Welwitsch 5147, 5148, 5169, from Golungo Alto, and 5194, 5195, from Punto Andongo), gives a description relating especially to the specimens from Golungo Alto which represent the majority of the cited specimens. The two specimens from Punto Andongo, Welwitsch 5194 and 5195, however represent another species not yet well known in 1880. This is B. lancifolia T. Anders. (Journ. Linn. Soc., Bot., 7 : 28, 1863), of which only the holotype was known at the time (herbarium of Trinity College in Dublin, Ireland). S. Moore had not seen this specimen, and knew only the description of the species. He had nevertheless well indicated in the discussion of his B. alata that this B. lancifolia was very close to his species. C.B. Clarke, in the Flora of Tropical Africa, placed B. lancifolia in synonymy with B. alata S. Moore, retaining the latter name contrary to nomenclatural rules. This fact seems nevertheless to indicate that he had considered this species as doubtful, perhaps not truly distinct from B. alata; this is what he expresses better in the discussion of another species (B. damarensis T. Anders., l. c. 169, 1899), where he speaks of B. lancifolia "supposed equivalent" to B. alata. It is evident that he had not seen, either, the type specimen of B. lancifolia, and the description he gives is quite that of B. alata in the sense of S. Moore. A year later, Hiern gave, in (Catal. of. Afr. Pl. Coll. by Fr. Welwitsch, Dicot. IV : 816, 1900) the corrected nomenclature, that is to say Barleria lancifolia T. Anders. for the plants described by S. Moore. Not at all satisfied with the synonymy established by Clarke, Moore discusses (Journ. Bot. 1902 and 1907) the position of B. lancifolia after having seen and studied for the first time the Dublin type. He specifies the differences between these species, particularly the characters of the corollas, very useful for distinguishing them; they are very well described and discussed from 1902 onwards. But the original material of B. alata is heterogeneous, containing some plants belonging to B. lancifolia, and this fact was not recognized by S. Moore. It was only during a new revision of these same materials for the preparation of the Flora of Gabon that we became aware of this fact.

In a study on the genus Barleria in South Africa (A.A. Obermeijer, Ann. Transvaal Mus. 15 : 147, 1933), B. alata figures in the synonymy of B. lancifolia and the type specimens are neither discussed nor cited in this work, where the name Barleria alata was, probably, simply copied as a synonym from earlier works. Miss A.A. Obermeijer had nevertheless studied the type specimens of B. alata, preserved in the British Museum, on which are found some small notes in her hand. These same specimens also bear other small notes (also in pencil) very probably in the hand of S. Moore. These notes reveal not only the doubts of a very competent botanist about the synonymy established by C.B. Clarke, but also the fact that Miss Obermeijer had very well recognized the heterogeneity of the material grouped under the name Barleria lancifolia in the British Museum. It is clearly evident that she was able to distinguish the two species without being able to recognize their true identity, nor escape the vicissitudes of the synonymy established by C.B. Clarke, her revision being moreover limited to the Barleria of South Africa.

In a more recent revision of the genus for South-West Africa (P.G. Meyer, Mitt. Bot. Staatssammlung München 2 : 381, 1957) the name B. alata no longer figures in the synonymy of B. lancifolia, a species very widespread in the region treated. Finally, the characters indicated in the keys allow identification of the plants that were the subject of confusion neither in the work of A.A. Obermeijer nor in that of P.G. Meyer.

In conclusion, B. alata S. Moore must be recognized as a distinct species. The reasons for this have already been well set out by S. Moore in his note on B. lancifolia in 1902. Moreover, the ranges of distribution of the two species are quite different: B. lancifolia more or less xerophilous species, is from Angola and South Africa; B. alata more or less mesophilous species, is from Gabon, Congo, Cabinda and Angola.
