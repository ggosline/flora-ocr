# *Cyperus squarrosus* L. Planche 46

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Cent. pl . 2 : 6 (1756) ; Sp. pl. éd. 2, 1 : 66 (1762)

## Synonyms

- Mariscus squarrosus (L.) C.B.Clarke in Hook.f., Fl. Brit. India 6 : 623 (1893).

## Description

Annual herb, slender, with solitary or clustered stems and numerous thin roots; stems 3–20 cm × 1–2.5 mm, triangular with sharp edges, glabrous. Leaf: sheath green to violet-purple, rather wide; blade 2–10 cm × 1.5–3 mm, plane or V-shaped, glabrous or with slightly scabrous margin. Inflorescence an anthela of 1–5 × 1–4 cm, composed of 1–2 sessile spikes and 1–6 spikes on peduncles 0.5–4 cm; involucral bracts 2–4, foliaceous, erect or spreading, the largest 2–8 cm × 1.5–4 mm; spikes 5–15 × 5–12 mm, formed of numerous spreading spikelets. Spikelet linear, 3–7 × 2–3 mm, flattened, green to reddish-brown, bristling with the recurved tips of the glumes, with 6–15 flowers; glumes elliptic, 1.4–2 mm long, ranging from yellow to reddish-brown, with 3–5 nerves on each side of the central green nerve and terminating in an arched point. Flower with 3 stamens; style with 3 branches. Achene obovate or oblong, 0.6–0.7 × 0.2–0.3 mm, triangular, brown to dark grey, finely papillose, disarticulating at its base but retained by the persistent glumes, so that it falls with the spikelet.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in the tropical regions of Africa, America and South Asia; rare in Gabon or overlooked, found only in Ogooué-Maritime (1 collection)

## Ecology

in open grassy areas (grasslands), often on sandy soil; in Gabon, near sea level, but up to 2000 m altitude elsewhere. Taxonomic note: easily identified by its annual habit and by the strongly bristling spikes/spikelets. The other annual Cyperus with bristling spikelets in Gabon, C. cuspidatus, has spikelets in digitate fascicles, not in spikes. B: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972). ibliography
