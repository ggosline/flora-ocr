# *Mesosphaerum pectinatum* (L.) Kuntze Planche 18

**Genus**: [[Mesosphaerum]]
**Protologue**: Revis. gen. pl. 2 : 525 (1891)

## Synonyms

- Hyptis pectinata (L.) Poit., Ann. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat. Paris 7 : 474, t. 30 (1806).

## Description

Annual or short-lived perennial herb, 1–2.5(–4.5) m tall, with strong unpleasant odour; stem well-branched, pubescent with hairs directed downward. Leaf: petiole 20–50 mm, shorter toward the apex; blade narrowly to broadly ovate, 2–8 × 1–5 cm, rounded to truncate at base, acute to obtuse at apex, slightly and irregularly dentate, pubescent on both surfaces, more densely so beneath. Inflorescence terminal and on short lateral shoots, candelabrum-shaped; scorpioid cymes, usually in pairs from a short common peduncle, with (10 to)20 to 40 flowers, unilateral, the entire calices erect giving a pectinate appearance; bracts and bracteoles linear to setaceous, 2–4 mm long. Flower: pedicel 0.5 mm; calyx 2–2.5 mm long and 5.5 mm in fruit, pubescent, with a tuft of white hairs visible in the throat, with cylindric tube, erect, becoming weakly urceolate in fruit, with subulate lobes somewhat longer than the tube; corolla 2.5–3.5 mm long, whitish to pale mauve, with median lobe of lower lip sometimes darker or streaked. Fruit: 4 achenes, oblong-ellipsoid, 0.9–1 mm long, black, smooth.

## Distribution

Widespread, native to Central and South America; in Africa, including Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known only from three specimens from Estuaire (approximately from Libreville), collected in 1900. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

Disturbed soils, clearings with secondary vegetation; in Gabon at 0–100 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Use: Used in Nigeria as a flavoring for soups and as a laxative. B: Burkill (1995), Harley & Pastore (2012), Morales (2011), Morton (1963), Paton et al. Bibliography (2013)
