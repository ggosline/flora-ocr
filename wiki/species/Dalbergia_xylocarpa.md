# *Dalbergia xylocarpa* O.Lachenaud Planche 51

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Symb. Bot. Ups. 38 : 190 (2016)

## Description

Liane or shrub (occasionally becoming a tree?) 4–20 m tall, inodorous, unarmed, but sometimes with hook-like prehensile branchlets; trunk reaching 20 cm in diameter; bark brown or dark grey with small pale lenticels; young branchlets glabrous or with short appressed pubescence. Stipules very early caducous, narrowly falciform, 2.5–5 × 1–1.2 mm, acute or obtuse at apex, with short appressed hairs. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.5–2.8 cm; rachis 1.5–7.8 cm, glabrous or shortly pubescent; leaflets 3 to 7, alternate and very unequal, with petiolule 2–7 mm, blade elliptic or slightly obovate, symmetric, 4–12.8 × 1–4.8 cm in upper leaflets and 1.4–7 × 1–4.8 cm in lower leaflets, rounded or acute at base, acuminate at apex, ± coriaceous, brownish when dry and slightly glaucescent on lower face, glabrous on both faces or pubescent on lower face with straight hairs of ± 0.5 mm, appressed or erect; lateral veins 6 to 10, slightly more prominent than the veinlets, ascending, joined in arches near the margin, veinlets in concolorous network, fairly dense and prominent on lower face. Inflorescence in short panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary or on old branchlets, 1–2 cm long, with short appressed or ± spreading hairs; bracts persistent after flowering, broadly triangular, ± 0.5 × 0.5 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1–2 mm; bracteoles caducous after flowering, broadly ovate, ± 0.5 × 0.3 mm, pubescent; calyx pale green, with short appressed and rather sparse hairs, tube 1.5–2.2 × 2–3 mm, teeth valvate ± equal (or rarely absent), triangular, (0–)0.3–1 mm long, acute; corolla white or pinkish, petals with claw equalling or scarcely exceeding the calyx, standard obliquely bent, with claw 2–2.3 mm and elliptic or suborbicular blade 3.5–4.5 × 3.3–4 mm, bilobed at apex, wings equalling the keel, with claw 2.3–3 mm and elliptic or rectangular blade 3.5–4.5 × 1.5–1.7 mm, smooth externally, truncate at base, keel strongly curved, with claw 2–2.5 mm and blade 3–4 × 1.5–2 mm, truncate or shortly auriculate at base; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments 5–5.5 mm, connate for ¾ of their length, straight at base and curved in their free part; ovary ± 1.5 × 0.7 mm, glabrous, with 2 ovules and straight stipe of ± 3 mm; style linear, 1.3 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 2–3 mm and stipe 4–7 mm, woody, thick, ellipsoid or discoid, 2.2–4.6 × 2–3.4 cm, fairly flat when young (< 5 mm thick) then strongly convex and reaching 10–14 mm thick at maturity, slightly reticulate, glabrous, with 1 seed; pericarp 1–5 mm thick, with outer layer formed of fairly soft cork tissue without woody fibres, endocarp very hard of ± 0.2 mm thick. Seed reniform, very thick, ± 26 × 17 × 10 mm at maturity.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Rio Muni), Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo (Mayombe); in Gabon, widely distributed in the coastal sedimentary basin (Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Maritime), extending into the interior up to the Ngounié valley and the environs of Ndjolé

## Ecology

Flooded forests by lakes or rivers, and more rarely forest edges on firm ground; in Gabon and elsewhere at 0–150 m altitude. Flowering March to May; immature fruits May to November, ripe in December

## Vernacular names

enguméguihé (Fang)

## Source note

D. xylocarpa is highly variable in the pubescence of the leaflets: most specimens have glabrous leaflets, but some show erect hairs on the lower face of the blade, and others appressed hairs (this latter variant is difficult to separate from D. pachycarpa without pods). The pods are also quite variable in shape (discoid to ellipsoid). They remain ± flat for a long time, and only swell late during maturation. The species is very close to D. semiapplanata, difficult to separate in the absence of fruits, but whose distribution area appears distinct. These two species were long confused with D. monetaria L.f., but the latter is present only in tropical America (Lachenaud & Maesen 2016). D. xylocarpa is also close to D. pachycarpa from which it differs mainly in the pods. D. xylocarpa is recorded here for the first time from Cameroon, based on the specimen van Andel et al. 4242 (WAG) from the Kribi region.
