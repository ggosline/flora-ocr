# *Alysicarpus vaginalis* (L.) DC. Planche 8.1–8

**Genus**: [[Alysicarpus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. 2 : 353 (1825)

## Description

A. ovalifolius (Schumach.) J.Léonard, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 24 : 88 (1954). A. violaceus (Forssk.) Schindl., Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg . 21 : 13. (1925), pro parte .

Flowers geminate with 1 primary bract reflexed and 2 secondary bracts (× 5). – 4. Standard, internal face (× 5). – 5. Wing, internal face (× 5). – 6. Half of keel, internal face (× 5). – 7. Androecium (× 5). – 8. Pod (× 2). – Alysicarpus glumaceus : 9. Pod (× 5). (1, 8 : Vermoesen 1364 ; 2–7 : M. Laurent s.n. (B-G); 9 : Bredo 1964bis ). Drawing by J.M. Lerinckx, Meise Botanical Garden (©).

Annual or perennial herb, often creeping, 15–60 cm in height; stem branched. Stipules 5–20 × 1–3 mm. Leaf: petiole 2–10(–15) mm; stipels 0.5–1.5 mm; leaflet with oval-elliptic to lanceolate blade, 0.5–5.5 × 0.3–2.5 mm, highly variable on the same stem, base cordate, apex acute to emarginate, mucronate, glabrous and reticulate above, scabrous and hairy along the veins below. Inflorescence in racemes or loose or dense panicles, 2–13 cm in length, with puberulent axis, with 3 to 13 pairs of flowers; bracts caducous, oval-acuminate, 4–5 × 1.5–2.5 mm, glabrous. Flower: pedicel 1–2 mm, puberulent; bracteoles 1.5–2.5 mm in length; calyx puberulent, tube 1.5–2.5 mm in length, lobes not imbricate, triangular, 3–4 × 0.5–1 mm, acuminate, yellow-violaceous, with 1 to 5 veins and the central one more prominent than the lateral ones; corolla red-violaceous, standard 4–6 × 3–4 mm, darker, wings 4–6.5 × 1–1.5 mm, keel 4–6.5 mm in length; ovary linear, ± 3 mm in length, style ± 3 mm. Pod 12–25 × 2–2.5 mm, not or scarcely constricted between the articles, articles (2–)4 to 8, the first equalling the calyx, each 2.5–3(–4) × 2 mm, puberulent, reticulate-anastomosed, green turning black, distal article equalling the others but pointed. Seed ellipsoid, 1.6–2.1 × 1.2–1.5 × 1 mm, reddish-brown or pale brown tinged with brown; hilum subcentral.

## Distribution

Tropics of the Old World, introduced to America, Australia and the Pacific; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Nyanga and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

Coconut plantations, along roads, ruderal stations, savannas, swampy and rocky savannas; in Gabon at 100–200 m altitude, elsewhere up to 900 m. Use: Good forage and cover plant, but also considered a weed

## Vernacular names

alyce clover, buffalo clover, one-leaf clover (angl.)

## Source note

For Asia, Adema (2003) considers the species A. ovalifolius and A. vaginalis as conspecific, because of overlapping characters. Previously, A. ovalifolius was distinguished as an annual species, with loose inflorescence (flowers shorter than the internodes) and pod not partitioned, whereas A. vaginalis was perennial, with dense inflorescence (flowers longer than the internodes) and partitioned pod. If these differences are fairly clear, for example for Beninese material (van der Maesen 2006a), two entities could be discerned. However, in Gabon, and also in East Africa (Verdcourt, 1971b), the material is more variable. In conclusion, we corroborate Adema's view (2003). b : Adema (2003), Halim & Pengelly (1992), Léonard (1954a), Pellegrin (1949), Rapondaibliography Walker & Sillans (1961), Verdcourt (1971d)
