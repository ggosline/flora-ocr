# *Asystasia africana* (S. Moore) C. B. Clarke

**Genus**: [[Asystasia]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: C.B. Clarke, F.T.A. 5 : 134 (1899), et in Hiern, Cat. Afr. Pl. Welwitsch 1 : 818 (1900); Journ. Bot. 48 : 134 (1905)

## Description

, and in Hiern, Cat. Afr. Pl. Welwitsch 1: 818 (1900); Journ. Bot. 48: 134 (1905).

$ = \text{Isochoriste africana S. Moore, Journ. Bot. 18: 309 (1880); Lindau, in Engl. et Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. IV 3B: 326 (1895).} $

≡ Styasasia africana (S. Moore) S. Moore, Journ. Linn. Soc., Bot. 37: 195 t. 2 (1905). — R. Pilger et K. Krause, in Engl. et Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam., Nachtr. zu II-IV, 4: 286 (1915).

Shrub or subshrub, more or less sarmentose, forming tufts (Hallé 2781), or dense green bushes (Hallé 2805), reaching a height of 2 m (the original description indicates only 80 cm in height and refers to a herbaceous plant); stems of old specimens woody, cylindrical, with brown striated bark; young stems glabrous, with across the nodes a pubescent band with small short hairs directed upward. Petioles 3–7 mm long (original description up to 3 cm long). Leaf blade ovate-lanceolate, 5–7 × 1.7–3 cm, finely pubescent when fresh, glabrescent; adult leaves pubescent below along the midrib only, when dry dark greyish green above, somewhat glossy, olive or yellowish green below, matt; 4 nerves on each side.

Inflorescences axillary and terminal at the apex of the plant, up to 8 cm long, simple, very finely pubescent, particularly at the nodes. Flowers opposite, not arranged unilaterally; bracts subulate-lanceolate, 1.5–2 mm long (in the original description, most probably in error, indicated as 1.5 cm long), base 0.6 mm wide, finely ciliate at the margins toward the base; bracteoles not shorter, similar but somewhat narrower, pedicels reaching 3 mm long, finely pubescent. Calyx 3–4 mm long; sepals equal, lanceolate, 2.5–3.5 mm long, finely pubescent with glandular hairs. Corollas white, approximately 20 mm long, bilabiate, with cylindrical tube 6 × 2.5 mm; posterior lip 9 mm long with two lobes fused almost to the apex which is emarginate for 1 mm; anterior lip almost twice as wide as the posterior lip, i.e. 7.5 mm, with 3 acute triangular lobes approximately 2.5 mm long; central part of the anterior lip very swollen and striped with oblique dark red lines (Hallé 2781; the original description refers to flowers "rosei, nervosi"). Stamens didynamous, with long filaments 13 mm, and short filaments 11 mm, glabrous, fused in pairs at the base; anthers approximately 2 mm long, when alive violet-purple, with lobes slightly superimposed, 1.5 mm long, apiculate and furnished with a hymenoid or membranous triangular-apiculate appendage toward the base (according to in vivo notes of Hallé 2805). Disc thick, yellowish, approximately 1 mm high; ovary glabrous, 2.5 mm long; style glabrous 10 mm long; stigma capitate.

Fruit glabrous, reaching 32 mm long, with cylindrical stipe 20 × 2 mm; swollen part 5 mm wide and 3 mm thick, apiculate. Seeds orbicular-angular, strongly flattened, approximately 4 mm in diameter; testa with regular network of furrows and elongate warts arranged in more or less concentric lines.

## Type

Welwitsch 5073, Pungo Andongo, Angola (BM, holotype!)

## Discussion

Angola, Gabon.

A variety parviflora S. Moore of Isochoriste africana (S. Moore) S. Moore was described from the Lake Victoria region in East Africa (Journ. Linn. Soc., Bot. 37: 195, 1905). This variety is particularly interesting because it was described with characters that approach the Gabonese material described above; its collector A.G. Bagshawe had noted: "straggling shrub... flowers white with red streaks on the midlobe of the lower lip"; these indications can be compared with those mentioned above. The flower dimensions do not allow the Gabonese material to be associated with this var. parviflora.
