# *Ochna afzelii* R.Br. ex Oliv. Planche 20

**Genus**: [[Ochna]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 1 : 319 (1868)

## Description

O. congoensis (Tiegh.) Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 239 (1903). O. gilletiana Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 239 (1903). O. afzelii R.Br. ex Oliv. subsp. congoensis (Tiegh.) N.Robson, Bol. Soc. Brot., sér. 2A, 36 : 33 (1962). O. afzelii R.Br. ex Oliv. subsp. mechowiana (O.Hoffm.) N.Robson, Bol. Soc. Brot., sér. 2A, 36 : 34 (1962). Flower. Drawing by T. Adam (1–3) and Christine Darter (4), reproduced with permission from Aubréville (1950) and Keay et al. (1960).

Shrub or tree up to 40 m tall; branchlets with blackish, greyish or dark brown bark, densely furnished with small round white lenticels; buds ovoid, 4–5 × 3 mm, with caducous scales. Stipules narrowly elliptic-ovate, 2–3 × 0.5 mm, entire, caducous. Leaf: petiole 2–6(–10) mm; lamina obovate to elliptic, 3–15 × 1–6 cm, cuneate at the base, obtuse to acuminate at the apex, but rounded at its tip, coriaceous, with margin serrate to crenate; secondary nerves prominent on the upper surface. Inflorescence fasciculate to shortly racemiform, with rachis 1–4 mm and 2- to 6-flowered. Flower: pedicel 1–2 cm, articulate at the base; sepals elliptic to elliptic-ovate, 4–6 × 2–4 mm in flower, reaching 18 × 8 mm in fruit; petals obovate, unguiculate, 5–13 × 2–8 mm, white to pale yellow, sometimes pinkish or with reddish lines; stamens numerous, filament 3–4 mm, anthers 1.5 mm long, with longitudinal dehiscence; ovary with (5–)6 to 8(–9) carpels; style 3–7 mm, entire. Fruit with pedicel elongating to 3 cm; receptacle 3–4 × 6–7 mm; drupels erect, ovoid, 6–9 × 4–7 mm. Seed straight; cotyledons equal, accumbent.

## Distribution

from Guinea to Sudan, Uganda and Tanzania to the east and to Angola and Zambia to the south; in Gabon, only in the eastern part of the country, in the provinces of Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, forest galleries, wooded or shrubby savanna; in Gabon, at 300–700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1650 m. Taxonomic note: Sometimes several subspecies have been recognized, but we have followed the opinion of Bamps & Farron (1967) for whom this species is highly variable and the variation continuous. b : Bamps & Farron (1967), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hutchinson, Bibliography Dalziel & Keay (1954), Robson (1963), Verdcourt (2005)
