# *Garcinia conrauana* Engl.

**Genus**: [[Garcinia]]
**Family**: [[Clusiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 40 : 562 (1908)

## Description

Dioecious tree, up to 25 m tall; trunk up to 35 cm in diameter; bark scaly; latex yellow; branches tiered horizontally; branchlets angular. Leaves opposite, glabrous; petiole 7–22 mm, deeply canaliculate above, foveola apparent, 2–5 mm long; blade ovate to elliptic or broadly elliptic, 7–24 × 4–15.5 cm, cuneate to obtuse at base, shortly acuminate to obtuse at apex, coriaceous; midvein depressed above, prominent below, lateral veins numerous, 9–12 pairs distinct, slightly prominent below, reticulation indistinct, black secretory canals indistinct, subparallel to midvein. Inflorescence terminal, male flowers in pauciflorous fascicles borne on a peduncle 3 × 3 mm, female flowers solitary. Flowers unisexual, tetramerous; pedicel 3–5 × 2–3 mm, glabrous; sepals subequal, ovate to broadly elliptic, 10–14 × 7–10 mm, pink, finely papillose; petals obovate, 10–18 × 7–14 mm, white to yellowish. Male flower: staminodial phalanges with very numerous stamens, filaments partially free, anthers globose. Female flower with 4–6 free staminodes; ovary 4-locular, subglobose, 6–7 mm in diameter, glabrous; stigma sessile, 4-lobed, 5–6 mm in diameter. Fruit almost sessile, subglobose to ovoid, 5–11 × 4.5–9 cm, smooth, glabrous, becoming dark purplish-black. Seeds 1–2, ovoid, 4–6 × 2–3.5 cm, orange.

## Distribution

Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime, and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Coastal forest, submontane forest (often in stands), on sandy soil, on ridges; in Gabon, 0–1020 m elevation, elsewhere up to 1250 m. Taxonomic note: In the past, this species was sometimes confused with G. kola. This is explained by the fact that Engler (1908), in describing this species, cited two specimens (Conrau 54 and Zenker 2552) representing two different species. In 1959, Pellegrin resolved the situation by choosing Conrau 54 as lectotype. B: Engler (1908), Pellegrin (1959), Sweeney (2008). Bibliography
