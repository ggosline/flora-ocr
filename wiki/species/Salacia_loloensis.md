Shrub 2-4 m tall, exceeding 3 cm in diameter, lacking latex. Young branches smooth, olivaceous. Old bark greyish-black, somewhat rugose. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole 6-10 mm, with slightly open channel and straight margins. Blade oblong-elliptic, base ± acute and apex narrowly rostrate-acuminate, 12-20 mm, discolorous, often olive-brown above when dry, (10-)13-27 × 4-9 cm, margin entire or slightly dentate. Midrib bearing a fine double ridge above near the base. Secondary veins 10-16 pairs, regular and slightly ascending; fine reticulation moderately evident.

Axillary glomerules, sessile, 1-5-flowered. Pedicel variable, 4-7-22 mm, sometimes very slender. Bud subglobose, 2.5-4 mm in diameter. Flower wheel-shaped, pale yellow or salmon-yellow, sometimes pale green. Calyx 3.5 mm in diameter, with quincuncial, very unequal sepals, ± broadly rounded, the two outer smaller, the largest 2-2.5 mm wide, margin entire, narrowly rebordured above. Petals 5, ± reflexed, orbicular, subunguiculate, 2.5-3 × 3-4 mm, margin entire, narrowly rebordured above. Disc cushion-shaped, annular, finely crenate in apical view, bearing about thirty radiating striae above, diameter 1.9 mm, thickness 0.2 mm; perigynous groove deep, bearing 3 spaced filaments. Stamens about 1 mm long, lying on the disc at anthesis; anther 0.4 mm wide with transverse dehiscence line barely interrupted. Ovary paler than the disc and ± striolate with brown, style short and stigma punctiform; 3 locules, deeply positioned, each with 2 superposed ovules.

Fruit ovoid or ± fusiform, smooth, lacking ribs, glaucous green before maturity, becoming orange or bright red, sometimes blotched with marblings, $ 3.5-7 \times 2.5-3.5 $ cm. Pericarp thin, $ 1 $ mm when dry. Seeds $ 2-5(6) $ per fruit; seed up to $ 3 \times 2 $ cm, cotyledons fused, white in section. - Pl. 20, fig. 10-14, p. 77 ; pl. 41, fig. 4-5, p. 143.

## Discussion

Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo. Forest understory species, in dense sometimes flooded forest.

This fairly variable species is a very close relative in its floral characters to S. mayumbensis Exell & Mendonça, which is distinguished by its smaller, more oblong and more dentate leaves, and by its often pod-like fruits with more verrucose surfaces. S. loloensis also presents certain forms (P. van Meer 783, Akure, Nigeria, with pale violet buds) with rather small, finely dentate leaves, which have a small ovoid fruit (2 × 1.5 cm) on a pedicel 12-15 mm long; these are related to S. pallescens Oliv., which has more clearly dentate leaves, darker flowers, and fruits that are both smooth globose and marred. The incomplete material of this difficult group remains of doubtful determination.

The variety marmorata Loes. (which we prefer provisionally to regard as a simple variation) shows marbling on its fruit resembling that very characteristic of S. pallescens.
