# *Drypetes staudtii* (Pax) Hutch.

**Genus**: [[Drypetes]]
**Family**: [[Putranjivaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 6(1): 688 (1912)

## Description

Tree up to ±20(−25) m tall; trunk up to ±50 cm in diameter, straight, furrowed, fluted; bark smooth, dark green to greyish, lenticellate; slash with remarkable odour; branchlets strongly furrowed, angular, orange or reddish-brown, glabrous; buds glabrous. Stipules caducous, linear, up to ±5.9 mm long, glabrous, with a few scattered lateral protuberances. Leaf: petiole 5.6–7.8–9.6(−13.5) mm, smooth, canaliculate above, glabrous; blade oblong, (21–23–29(−40) × (7.3–7.5–11(−13.5) cm, coriaceous, asymmetric at base, one side frequently rounded, the other cuneate to obtuse, shortly acuminate at apex, with margin subentire to sparsely serrulate, mainly in the distal half of the leaf, plane to slightly recurved, glabrous on both faces, bright light green above, paler beneath; venation pinnate, reticulate and slightly raised on both faces, midvein glabrous, secondary veins (9 to 11 to 13(to 17), ascending, slightly curved and anastomosing well before the margin, impressed above and prominent below, yellowish to reddish when dry. Male inflorescence cauliflorous and ramiflorous, sometimes axillary on leafy branches, with up to ±11 flowers; bracts oval, (2.5–2.8–3.4(−4) mm long, whitish with dark veins, with subentire margin, sometimes with scattered lateral protuberances, glabrous. Male flower remarkably fragrant; pedicel robust, (1.2–8–20(−22) mm, sparsely and finely hairy; sepals (4 or 5(or 6), imbricate, suborbicular, (3.5–6.2–7.2(−7.6) × (3.3–5.2–5.8(−6.2) mm, hooded at apex, greenish to yellowish-cream, glabrous or weakly puberulent on the outside, puberulent on the inside, without marginal cilia; stamens (4 to 20 to 24(to 30), arranged around the disc margin and penetrating the disc towards the centre, filament (1.6–1.7–2.9(−3.1) mm, anther (1.4–2.1–3.3(−3.5) mm long, glabrous; disc slightly convex, (1.4–2.5–5.5(−6.5) mm in diameter, rugose, somewhat plicate, with lobed margin, exceeding the filaments, glabrous; pistillode up to 2.4 mm or absent. Female inflorescence as the male, with up to ±8 flowers; bracts (2.7–2.8–3.3(−3.8) mm long, as in the male. Female flower: pedicel robust, (6–7–9(−10) mm, puberulent, glabrescent; sepals (4 or 5), (5.4–5.7–8(−10) × (6.3–6.5–9.5(−12.5) mm, as in the male; disc annular, (5.6–6.8(−7.1) mm in diameter, glabrous; ovary globose and somewhat lobed, (3–)5-locular, glabrous; style (0.9–1.2–2.8(−3.9) mm, 4 or 5 times branched, partially fused, with obtriangular to flabelliform stigma, (2.2–3–4.6(−5.2) × (3–3.3–4.7(−5.6) mm, Fruit globose, (22–26–29.5(−30.5) × (30.5–31–32.5(−35.5) mm, yellow at maturity, hard, without persistent sepals, glabrous, with 3 to 5 seeds; pedicel (6.3–6.6–10 mm, glabrous. Seed 12–20 mm long.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Estuaire and Woleu-Ntem

Habitat: primary or secondary evergreen forests; in Gabon at 180–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1,200 m.

## Source note

D. staudtii has been collected mainly in the Estuaire and south of Woleu-Ntem. Its leaf blades are large, with subentire to serrulate margin and rather asymmetric at base (one side frequently rounded). The main veins often retain a yellowish-reddish colour after drying, and the branchlets are strongly furrowed. Other Gabonese species with large leaves, such as D. aylmeri, D. moliwensis and D. similis, have a very slightly oblique leaf base, as well as very different flowers and fruits.
