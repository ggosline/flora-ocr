# *Erythrina vogelii* Hook.f.

**Genus**: [[Erythrina]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Niger Fl. : 307 (1849)

## Description

Shrub or tree 2–24 m tall; trunk with rough, grey bark; branches and petioles furnished with conical or hooked spines. Stipules caducous. Leaf: petiole 3–6 cm, grooved above, slightly thickened at the base; rachis 1–2 cm; stipels represented by glands 2 mm in diameter; leaflets with petiolule 3–6 mm, blade ovate, (2–)5–10 × 3–5 cm, base rounded to broadly cuneate, apex rounded or scarcely pointed; lateral veins 5 to 7 on each side, alternate except the lower pair. Inflorescence in terminal pseudoracemes, spike-like, 15–25 cm long, often in the leafless state or with the first leaves; bracts caducous, 5–9 mm long. Flower: pedicel 4–6 mm; calyx oblique, 12–18 mm long, with very short pubescence, with ventral slit, apex with 3 to 5 lobes finger-shaped or teeth 1–2 mm long; corolla bright red or sometimes orange, 3–5 cm long, standard obovate, 3–5 × 1.4 cm, remaining closed and covering the wings and keel 5 mm long, keel petals united; stamens diadelphous (9 + 1), the vexillar free, exceeding the standard; ovary half the length of the stamens. Pod moniliform, curved, 8–15 × 1–1.2 cm, opening on one side, twisting, exposes the seeds. Seed ovoid, 8–10 × 5 × 7 mm, bright red or orange; hilum central, white.

## Distribution

Ivory Coast, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko) and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Estuaire and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

Riparian forest, secondary forest, coastal forest, openings; in Gabon up to 200 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Use: The utility of the wood seems limited. It is traditionally used for the making of floats for fishing, brake pads, tiles and small articles

## Source note

E. vogelii is very close to E. senegalensis DC., of wide distribution and known from West African savannas, and there are doubts about their distinction as species. The habit of E. vogelii being generally more imposing, and the calyx lobes are surmounted by small fingers or teeth 1–2 mm, whereas E. senegalensis has a calyx with undivided lobes. Known from Gabon by two specimens, one of which, Chevalier 33589 (P, WAG) lacks flowers. B. Krukoff determined it at Paris as E. senegalensis, but we consider it rather as E. vogelii, the height being indicated as 20–24 m. b: De la Estrella et al.
