# *Dischistocalyx grandifolius* C. B. Clarke

**Genus**: [[Dischistocalyx]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: C. B. Clarke, F.T.A. 5:61 (1899)

## Synonyms

- ? D. Buchholzii Lindau, Bot. Jahrb. 20 : 14 (1894).
- D. insignis Bremek., Bot. Jahrb. 73 : 139 (1943).

## Description

(≡ Acanthopale Buchholzii (Lindau) C. B. Clarke, 1. c. 64, 1899).

Robust plant, suffrutescent, climbing along trunks or even having an epiphytic habit; approximately 1.80–2 m in height, reaching up to 3–4 m as an epiphytic plant (Hallé 2360); nearly glabrous except for a few very small hairs on the veins of the dorsal face of the leaves and on some parts of the flower.

Stem cylindrical, glabrous, reaching in old woody individuals up to 8 mm in diameter; bark, when dry, greyish; young stems blackish and striated. Petioles 10–45 mm long, striated and blackish. Leaf blade oval (or obovate) elliptical, 10–25 × 4.5–10 cm, acute at the base and acuminate at the apex, decurrent over 1–1.5 cm towards the petiole, slightly and irregularly crenate at the margin, glabrous above, very finely or scarcely pubescent along the veins below; pubescence violet when fresh; upper surface of blade dark green when fresh, olive-blackish or nearly black when dry; lower surface, violet-tinged when fresh, olive-brownish or even pale brown when dry; 10–16 pairs of lateral veins; veins prominent below, midvein impressed above in large leaves.

Terminal strobilose inflorescences, bracts dark blackish-red or deep garnet when fresh, brown-blackish when dry, very finely pubescent; the lower bracts foliaceous, sessile and with very prominent veins, approximately 3.5–5 × 1.5–3 cm; the other bracts elliptic-acuminate without prominent veins, generally wider than the foliaceous bracts and distinctly keeled, rounded at the acumen which is somewhat inclined outwards, 2–4 × 2–3.5 cm; the bracts are generally very caducous, especially the lower foliaceous ones; bracteoles very small, linear-lanceolate, 3–4 mm long, glabrous. Flowers subsessile, approximately 3 ± developed per axil. Calyx nearly glabrous; sepals ciliate at the margin towards the apex, purple or violaceous when fresh, reaching 22 mm long; the three posterior sepals are fused over 3/4 of their length, the two anterior ones entirely free, approximately 3–3.5 mm wide. Corolla bright violet (Hallé 2360), 48 mm long, forming at the base an almost white cylindrical tube 16 × 2 mm; above this the corolla is infundibuliform, 15 mm in diameter; veins of the corolla ciliate on the outside; diameter of the spread corolla 46 mm; lobes rounded, flattened and slightly emarginate, 22 × 11 mm. Filaments of the stamens inserted in the narrow part of the corolla but descending almost to the base of the tube; stamens long with free portion of 15 mm; the short filament and the long filament corresponding

are fused above their insertion over approximately 6 mm long; filament of the short stamens 8 mm. Anther 6 mm long; filament of the long stamens finely glandular-pubescent on the upper 2/3 and along the connective; connective of the short stamens only slightly ciliate at the apex. Style finely pubescent, 40 mm long; stigma lobe lanceolate, 4.5 mm long, curved over the upper anthers. Ovary 4 mm, glabrous, disc 1.5 mm high.

Fruit glabrous, 18–20 × 2 mm, with approximately 12–16 ovules per locule (rarely all well developed). Seed flattened, lenticular, 1.5 mm in diameter, finely reticulate, furnished with the characteristic ring of hygroscopic hairs of the genus, with dark brown surface.

## Type

Bates 511 (K, holotype!)

## Source note

The plants comprised here under the name D. grandifolius are most probably conspecific with D. Buchholzii Lindau, ≡ Acanthopale Buchholzii (Lindau) C. B. Clarke, described from Gabon: Buchholz s. n. (fl. 27–VIII–1874), with no other indication. The type material being destroyed and not represented in other herbaria, it is preferable not to accept this name for the present species, especially since Lindau's description indicates for D. Buchholzii dimensions that are distinctly smaller than those of the species as it is accepted above. It is nevertheless important to note that D. Buchholzii Lindau is the oldest published name in the genus Dischistocalyx for a plant truly belonging to this genus. The two other species described in 1894 by Lindau, namely D. laxiflorus and D. confertiflorus (1. c. p. 13) actually belong to the genus Acanthopale C. B. Clarke. Given that Ruellia thunbergiiflora T. Anders, the type species of the genus Dischistocalyx, was transferred to this genus only in 1899 by C. B. Clarke, this species quite probably still comprises other taxa described from Nigeria and Cameroon whose type materials no longer exist (lost in Berlin in 1943 and not represented by isotypes). Moreover, the variability of these plants and the very insufficient quantity of herbarium specimens do not permit an adequate monographic study of this genus.

Distribution in Cameroon: Zenker 1881, Bipindi; road from Bipindi to Dihani (fl. and fr. 24–28 June 1918); E. Annet 417.
