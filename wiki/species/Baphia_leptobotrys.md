# *Baphia leptobotrys* Harms

**Genus**: [[Baphia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 26 : 282 (1899)

## Description

B. leptobotrys Harms subsp. silvatica (Harms) Soladoye, Kew Bull. 40 : 346 (1985). Small tree sometimes lianescent shrub, up to 18 m tall; trunk up to 40 cm in diameter; branchlets glabrous or nearly so, puberulent when young. Stipules ovate-triangular to elliptic, 2–10 × 1.5–4 mm. Leaf: petiole with distinct pulvinus, 2–4 cm, ± glabrous; leaflet with ± elliptic-oblong limb, (4–)7–20(–24) × (2–)4–7(–9) cm, rounded to cordate at base, apex rounded to acuminate over 0.5–2.5(–4) cm, coriaceous to papyraceous, glabrous or nearly so on both surfaces, pubescent when young and very quickly glabrescent; lateral nerves in (5–)6 or 7 pairs. Inflorescence in lax axillary raceme, up to 15-flowered, glabrous to sparsely hairy; bracts elliptic, 1.5–3 mm long. Flower: bracteoles situated at apex of pedicel against calyx, spreading or reflexed, oblong-elliptic, 1.5–5 mm long; pedicel 10–14 mm, ± glabrous; calyx spathate, 8–12 × 8–11 mm, glabrous or with a few hairs at apex; standard ± circular, 15 × 15–18 mm, bilobed at apex, wings falcate, 15–17 × 5–7 mm, with claw 2–3 mm, keel petals falcate, 12–13 × 6 mm, with claw ± 1 mm; stamens 7–10 mm, anthers 1.5–2 mm long; pistil 9–10 mm long, ovary pubescent, with 6 ovules. Pod oblong, 12–20 × 1.5–3 cm, glabrous or with a few appressed hairs, with 2 or 3(–4?) seeds. Mature seed 16–20 × 13–16 mm, brown.

## Distribution

South-East Nigeria, Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forest; in Gabon at 450–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m

## Source note

The synonym B. silvatica is treated as a subspecies of Baphia leptobotrys by Soladoye (1985). The characters used to separate the two taxa, namely the habit of the plant, the texture of the leaves and the length of the bracteoles, are too variable to maintain this subdivision. Bibliography: Pellegrin (1949), Soladoye (1985).
