# *Eriocaulon cinereum* R.Br.

**Genus**: [[Eriocaulon]]
**Family**: [[Eriocaulaceae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr . : 254 (1810)

## Description

, ‛ sieboldtianum'. E. amboense Schinz, Bull. Herb. Boiss. 4, App. 3 : 35 (1896). E. heudelotii N.E.Br. in Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 8(2) : 258 (1901). E. stuhlmannii N.E.Br. in Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 8(2) : 259 (1901). Small fugacious herb in tufts. Leaves forming a dense tuft, linear to acicular, 1–3 cm × 0.2–2 mm, fine, clearly septate, apex setaceous. Inflorescence: scapes often numerous, 4–13 cm × 0.3–0.4 mm, 5-furrowed, with sheath equalling ± the leaves; capitulum subglobose to ovoid, 2–5 mm across, grey and straw-coloured or becoming darker, with bracts very loosely erect; involucral bracts multiseriate, obovate-oblong, 1.3–2 mm long, rounded then lacerate, pale or grey, scarious, harder towards the base; floral bracts lanceolate-oblong to linear-oblong, 1.5–2 mm long, finely scarious, pale with a dark central band or sometimes blackish, glabrous or some internal bracts weakly keeled with rare fine hairs on the keel, the external ones obtuse, the internal ones progressively more acute; receptacle glabrous or sparsely pilose. Flowers 3-merous, pedicellate. Male flowers: sepals fused into a spathe with tridentate apex, dark grey, glabrous; petals included in the calyx, minute, glandulous; stamens 6, anthers cream-white, 0.1 mm long. Female flowers strongly reduced and generally formed of the gynoecium alone; sepals 2 or 3 or absent, filiform, caducous; petals absent, position recognizable by a node on the ovary stipe. Seeds broadly ellipsoid, 0.3 mm long, yellowish-brown, shiny, smooth.

## Distribution

India and China, Japan, South-East Asia and Australia, probably not native to Africa where it is known from scattered stations in West Africa: from Senegal to Chad, Tanzania and tropical South Africa; introduced elsewhere in warm regions as a rice field weed; to be sought in Gabon. Ecology: marsh margins, seeping waters and temporary lowland marshes; up to 1500 m altitude. Taxonomic notes: A bibliographic reference citing the presence of E. cinereum in Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006) is based on a determination error. Nevertheless, this widespread weed could well be collected in Gabon as an introduced plant, particularly in disturbed wet habitats. It is well distinguished by its minute white anthers and its reduced female flowers. Anthers of a pale colour are relatively rare in Eriocaulon and are generally visible without dissection of the capitulum. The dense tuft of acicular leaves is also characteristic.
