35a. var. nitida

Liane of (1)3-30 m, devoid of rubber, with wood exceeding 6 cm in diameter, marked in cross-section by 8 radiating grooves from 15 mm in diameter; bark with an orange layer; internodes smooth. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole 7-12 mm with straight canal margins. Blade elliptic (6)8-18(20) × 2.5-9 cm, acute or rounded at the base, ± acuminate at the apex, entire or moderately dentate (well-dentate on juvenile plants), olivaceous concolorous and slightly glossy. Secondary veins 6-8 pairs.

Inflorescences axillary (1)2-10(20)-flowered, with peduncle 3-12 mm, with small ramifications often contracted. Pedicel 5-13 mm, articulated very clearly above its base. Bud subglobose 2.5-3.5 mm in diameter. Flowers green or yellow-green, urceolate, 4-6 mm in diameter. Sepals 5 slightly unequal, about 1.5 mm wide, fleshy gibbous, ovate to subdeltoid, furnished with a few small marginal glands. Petals 5 semi-erect 2.5-3 × 1.5-2.3 mm. Disc cup-shaped sometimes pentagonal, about 2 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm in height, with thin margin, erect, slightly sinuous marginally. Stamens 3 erect 2-2.7 mm, with filament narrow at the base, with yellow anther 1 mm wide, with 2 locules arranged at 90°, with apex notched, apiculate or biapiculate. Pistil pyramidal about 2 mm with truncate stigma. 3 locules half-sunken (mean level as the base of the stamens), containing 4-8 ovules each.

Fruit ovoid or fusiform 4-13 × 2.5-5.5 cm, orange or bright red at maturity, with thick exocarp (about 3 mm when dry), smooth and ± mammillate, containing 4-10 seeds. Cotyledons fused, white. - Pl. 40, p. 141 ; pl. 41, fig. 12, 13, p. 143.

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo, Zaire.

This species of dense humid forests can persist in secondary formations, and its regrowth sometimes flowers on recent temporary roads.

As this is the type variety of LOESENER's species, obovata is an illegitimate name; the other varieties recognized by LOESENER are not based on material from Bipindi.

This name can be given to the Cameroonian and Gabonese plants in particular which are the most vigorous and with flowers with long pedicels as does not appear to exist in West Africa.
