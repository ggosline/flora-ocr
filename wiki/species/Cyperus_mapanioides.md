# *Cyperus mapanioides* C.B.Clarke Planche 68

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: C. dichromeniformis Kunth var. major Boeckeler, Flora 62 : 549 (1879)

## Description

in Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 8 : 340 (1902). C. dichromeniformis Kunth var. major Boeckeler, Flora 62 : 549 (1879). C. mapanioides Kunth var. major (Boeckeler) Kük. in Engl. & Diels, Pflanzenr. IV. 20(101) : 230 (1936). Perennial herb, tall, slender, with a thick creeping rhizome and a few to numerous tightly packed stems; rhizome 1–5 cm × 2.5–4 mm, covered with brown to blackish scales, often breaking down into fibres; stems 12–50 cm × 1–4 mm, triangular or winged, glabrous but often scabrous beneath the inflorescence. Leaf: sheath light reddish-brown to purple; blade 10–25 cm × 5–12 mm, flat, with three principal veins, scabrous on the margin and on the veins, at least near the apex. Inflorescence in compact, whitish anthela, 1–3 cm wide, composed of 10–20 spikelets, the young ones almost hidden among the 5–8 large spreading bracts; main involucral bract 10–20 cm × 4–10 mm, scabrous on the margin and veins. Spikelet linear–lanceolate, 7–12 × 2–4 mm, with 8–25 flowers; glumes oval–lanceolate, 2.5–3 mm long, grey, with numerous strongly scabrous veins and margin, central vein scabrous at least at the base, terminating in a thickened apex or slightly excurrent. Flower with 3 stamens; style with 3 branches. Achene ovate, 1.4–1.6 × 1–1.1 mm, strongly triangular, dark brown, glossy, smooth or finely papillose.

## Distribution

widely distributed in the forest regions of tropical Africa; scattered in Gabon, found in Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga and Ngounié (8 collections)

## Ecology

in forest, often along paths, trails and at forest edge, also in degraded forest, always in well-drained habitat; in Gabon, at 100–600 m altitude, up to 1800 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: easily identified by its forest habitat and compact whitish inflorescence subtended by numerous large foliaceous bracts. B: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972). Bibliography
