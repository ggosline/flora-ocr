# *Allophylus bullatus* Radlkofer

**Genus**: [[Allophylus]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sitzungsber. Bayer. Akad. Wiss. München 38 : 223 (1908)

## Description

Small tree (10–12 m) to large tree; branches rounded, at first pubescent, greyish-yellowish at flowering, then glabrescent at fruiting, fairly thick, 5–6 mm in diameter. Leaves goffered, the midvein showing undulations in the plane of symmetry, the secondary veins prominent below and sunken above, the tertiary veins prominent on both surfaces.

Flowers yellowish; sepals not nerved. Fruits green, blackening when dry. PL. 7, 7–10, p. 41.

## Type

Mann 1184, Cameroun (holo-, K; iso-, B, W)

## Discussion

Collected at more than 2000 m altitude on Mount Cameroon, only in fruit, the plant had been classified as Schmidelia abyssinica Hochst. by Hooker F. (Journ. Linn. Soc. 7: 189, 1864). Rediscovered in June 1908 by MILDBRAED (3448) in the same region at 2200 m altitude, the plant is distinguished from A. abyssinicus by RADLKOFER who created A. bullatus, without knowing the flowers. Since then, several specimens have been collected with flowers and fruits by English collectors: Maitland 685, 690, 1089, Keay FHI 28487 (K) and French collectors, cited below. Despite these differences, the appearance of herbarium specimens is very similar: purplish-blackish colour, dentition, consistency, pubescence, goffering of leaves, etc.; many characters are common. Moreover their growth at high altitude (1800–2400 m in Abyssinia, Congo, Tanzania, 600–2500 m in Cameroon) could lead one to unite all these plants.
