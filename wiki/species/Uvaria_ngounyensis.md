# *Uvaria ngounyensis* Pellegrin

**Genus**: [[Uvaria]]
**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc.Bot.Fr.96 :172 (1949);id., Mém.31 : 59 (1949)

## Description

Vine (?) with reddish-brown hirsute branchlets, with fasciculate flexuous hairs and very numerous branches. Young leaves hirsute on the lower face, covered over the entire limb surface with a thick felt of stellate and fasciculate hairs. Adult leaves with thick, very short petiole, 2-3 mm long, reddish pubescent-fasciculate; limb subcoriaceous, elliptic-oblong, 6.5-14 cm long, 3-5 cm wide, attenuate at the apex into an acumen 1-1.5 cm long, rounded at the base slightly asymmetric; upper face pubescent with simple and fasciculate hairs; lower face with long soft stellate hairs on all the nerves and nervules, leaving the limb apparent. Lateral nerves 10-12 pairs, ascending-arched, united in arches near the margin. Network of nervules lax, very prominent on the lower face.

Flowers solitary, supra-axillary, with pedicel 12-18 mm long, hirsute-reddish. Bracteoles semi-orbicular, 3-5 mm long, reddish hirsute on the exterior. Floral bud ovoid-subglobose. Calyx with sepals fused enveloping the young bud completely, separating into three semi-orbicular lobes in the opened flower, 5-6 mm long, 7-8 mm wide, fused at the base for 2 mm in height, hirsute-reddish on the exterior. Petals subequal, 10-12 mm long, densely reddish velutinous on the exterior, glabrescent on the interior, the outer ones suborbicular, the inner ones slightly unguiculate at the base. Stamens numerous, 4-seriate, oblong, 1.5-2 mm long, with linear, lateral thecae, connectives enlarged-flattened above the anthers, slightly depressed at the centre, puberulent. Carpels hairy with short hairs, approximately 3 mm long, ovary ovoid, 1.5 mm long, styles obconic, stigmas involute. 8-10 ovules biseriate.

Fruits unknown.

## Type

Le Testu 6o76,Ndingui,Haute Ngounyé,Gabon,fl.,sept.(holotype P!; isotype BM!)

## Discussion

Gabonese species known only from the type, easily identifiable, even in the absence of flowers, by the venation and pubescence of the leaves.
