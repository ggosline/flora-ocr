# *Volkameria inermis* L. Planche 31

**Genus**: [[Volkameria]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. : 637 (1753)

## Description

Clerodendrum inerme (L.) Gaertn., Fruct. sem. pl. 1 : 271 (1788). Shrub or vine reaching 1(–2) m in height; stem solid, unarmed, finely wrinkled to glabrous. Leaves decussate, opposite or more rarely ternate; petiole (3–)6–10(–20) mm; lamina ovate to elliptic or obovate or narrowly elliptic-ovate, (3–)4.5–7.5(–12) × (1.6–)3–4.5(–9) cm, cuneate to rounded or shortly attenuate at the base, shortly acuminate to acute or obtuse or slightly retuse at the apex, entire, ± glabrous, sometimes with slight brown pubescence on the midvein, often with black, brown or pale punctiform glands, somewhat fleshy, penninerved. Inflorescence terminal, thyrsoid, lax, 5–6(–10) × 5–6 cm, puberulent, with branches distant 1.5–2 cm apart; cymes with 3 to 6 flowers; peduncle (1.4)–2.1(–5) cm. Flower actinomorphic; pedicel 5–13 mm; calyx with tube 3–5(–7.5) mm long, with broadly triangular lobes, accrescent, 1(–1.5) × 1–2.5 mm and 4–10 mm wide in fruit, glabrous, crimson, purplish red, pink or even white or greenish white, brown or dark brownish at maturity; corolla white, sometimes tinged with purple or pink, with tube 2.3–3 cm long, with oblong-elliptic or obovate lobes, (6–)8–11 × 4–7 mm; stamens exserted 2–3(–5) cm. Fruit subglobose to obovoid, 7–15 mm long, black, brown or dark brownish, shiny.

## Distribution

from India and Sri Lanka to southern China and Australia and in the western Pacific, indigenous/introduced status as far north as Micronesia and Hawaii, and south to Samoa and Tonga; introduced in French Polynesia; naturalized in Tunisia; in Africa, introduced here and there; in Gabon: Estuaire – apparently the first occurrence for sub-Saharan Africa. Conservation status: widely distributed and preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

coastal zones, mangroves, swamps, in Africa introduced or adventive, known in Gabon only from Libreville beach, at ± 1 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–100 m. Uses: The plant is locally used as medicine, notably in India, and is commonly cultivated as an ornamental plant and hedge plant or on beaches to combat erosion. B: El Mokni et al. (2013), Leeratiwong & Chantaranothai (2014), Wearn & Mabberley in Bibliography (2019)
