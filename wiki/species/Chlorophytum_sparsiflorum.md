# *Chlorophytum sparsiflorum* Baker var. sparsiflorum

## Description

, syn. nov. C. deistelanum Engl. & K.Krause, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 134 (1911). C. grewenii Engl. & K.Krause, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 136 (1911). C. ledermannii Engl. & K.Krause, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 137 (1911). C. comosum non (Thunb.) Jaques var. sparsiflorum (Baker) A.D.Poulsen & Nordal, Bot. J. Linn. Soc. 148 : 15 (2005). Plant slender to moderately robust, 25–40 cm in height, greenish after drying ; rhizome short ; roots 1–2 mm in diameter, with distal fleshy tubers reaching 2.5 cm in length, whitish. Leaves in rosettes, more or less sheathing, not petiolate ; blade gradually narrowed towards the base, 15–40 × 3–7 cm, apex acute to acuminate, dark green above, pale green below. Inflorescence (peduncle included) 20–60

Flowers 1–3 per node ; pedicel 3–6 mm, articulate towards the middle or in the upper half ; tepals 5–8 × 1.5–2 mm, white, sometimes slightly greenish or yellowish, 3-nerved ; filaments fusiform, papillose, 2–4 mm, anthers 2–2.5 mm in length ; style straight or somewhat curved. Pseudo-vivipary frequent. Fruit 3–7 × 4–8 mm, truncate to slightly emarginate at the base. Seeds cupuliform, 1.5–2.5 mm in diameter.

## Ecology

in primary and secondary forest, sometimes along rivers, but also as a "weed" in plantations ; at approximately 130–900 m in altitude

primary forest, often on rocks or in river bed on sandy soil ; at 30–400 m in altitude

Taxonomic note : A particular specimen with a blade measuring 12 cm, a petiole of 4 cm and an inflorescence of 5 cm was collected at Sibang ( Bogner 686 ). Field observations should be made to determine whether this form should be considered as a particular taxon. B : Poulsen & Nordal (2005). Bibliography

## Distribution

from eastern Ivory Coast to Cameroon, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda. In Gabon, known from the provinces of Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem. Taxonomic note : Plants resulting from pseudo-vivipary seem to flower more rapidly than those from seeds. Consequently, flowering specimens from vegetative reproduction have smaller leaves and shorter peduncles than the description above. This form has often been named C. laxum R.Br. originally described from Australian material ; this name should not be used in Gabon. B : Hepper (1968).
