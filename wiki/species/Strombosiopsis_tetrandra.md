## Description

**Habit**: shrub or large tree 4–30 m tall and 15–70(–100) cm in diameter. Bole
most often low-branching, sometimes tall and straight. Trunk with a lobed base,
irregularly fluted above. Crown broad, with numerous branches; foliage dense.
Rhytidome grey, smooth and soft, exfoliating in plates 0.1–0.6 mm thick. Bark
red when fresh, exuding a sticky resin that hardens in air. Internodes
1.5–2 cm long. Young branchlets angular or 2-winged.

**Leaves**: petiole glabrous, canaliculate above, striate beneath, 1–2.2 cm
long, sometimes swollen in its distal portion. Blade glabrous on both faces,
dark brown and weakly glossy above, light matt chestnut beneath, oblong to
oblong-lanceolate, sometimes ovate, 7–28 × 4–11.5 cm; base obtuse or rounded,
apex obtuse or weakly apiculate, mucronate. Midrib concave above, prominent
beneath. Secondary nerves in 6–10 pairs, ascending, arcuate, anastomosing in
arches 2–4 mm from the margin, little distinct above, distinct beneath.
Tertiary nerves ± parallel to one another, practically indistinct above and
concave beneath. Quaternary nerves forming a reticulum with square, rectangular
or polygonal meshes.

**Inflorescence**: short racemes or many-flowered fascicles (6–25 flowers),
axillary or extra-axillary. Rachis short and thick (2–5 mm long), furnished
with a collar of bracteoles marking the insertion of the pedicels. Pedicel
2–3 mm long, longitudinally striate. Flower bud broadly ellipsoid or
cylindrical, the apex obtuse.

**Flowers**: white, cream-white or yellowish. Calyx of 4 fused green sepals,
valvate, ± ciliolate on the membranous upper margin, fleshy, deltoid, the apex
subacute or obtuse. Petals valvate, free except at the base, glabrous on both
faces, elliptic, 3–6 × 1–2.5 mm, fleshy. Stamens 4, epipetalous, 3.5 mm long,
strongly appressed against the inner face of the petals; filament short,
0.5 mm, flattened; anther introrse, with 2 narrowly elliptic locules, the apex
obscurely obtuse. Ovary semi-inferior with a ± quadrangular disc bearing 4
fleshy lobes, glabrous, 4-locular. Style short, conical, ending in a 4-toothed
stigma.

**Fruit**: pedicellate; pedicel 3–11 mm long, longitudinally striate,
accrescent, surrounding the fruit for almost its whole length. Drupe solitary,
ellipsoid, 2 × 1.4 × 1.4 cm, the free apex marked by 4 calycine teeth or lobes
surrounding a depression. Exocarp green when fresh, brownish to black when dry,
± fluted. Endocarp hard and lignified, granular externally.

**Seed**: solitary, the albumen red-brown.

## Types

LECTOTYPE: *Staudt 137*, Cameroon (B, destroyed; iso-, P!).

## Distribution

**Range**: Nigeria to Zaire (Democratic Republic of the Congo).
**Gabon**: widespread — Ndjolé, Moanda, the Ngounié valley (Lebamba),
Lambaréné, Abanga, Bélinga, the Komo, the Monts de Cristal, Tchibanga, Mouila,
Nkoulounga.

## Habitat and ecology

A straight-boled tree in dense humid forest; in especially wet forest its habit
becomes that of a low, bushy tree. Also found in inundable forest and gallery
forest. Flowers from November to May. Collected to about 700 m.

## Properties and uses

The whitish or yellow-brown wood is hard and used in joinery. It serves for
construction, being weather-resistant, and the trunks of young trees can be
used as piles for houses. The bark is used to treat wounds; boiled, it enters
into the preparation of a remedy for scabies. The resin is used to coat pottery,
giving it a brown varnished colour. The fruits, pounded, are said to be a
stupefying poison used in fishing.
