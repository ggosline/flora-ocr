# *Strophanthus gratus* (Wall. & Hook.) Baill. Planche 103 ; Figure 11(H)

**Genus**: [[Strophanthus]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hist. pl. 10 : 171 (1889)

## Description

Vine reaching 25 m in height, but more rarely a shrub of 2–3 m; exudate clear or white; stems of older plants often with corky striations; branchlets glabrous. Leaf: Spirolobe ± ± mm, limb ovate to elliptic, sometimes obovate, of ± to ± cm, brown di to cuneate at the base, acuminate at the apex, glabrous, secondary nerves in pairs, almost straight or curved, forming an angle of ± with the median. Inflorescence condensed, with to flowers, glabrous, bracteate of ± to ± mm. Flower fragrant; pedicel 4–13 mm; calyx with subequal or unequal lobes (the inner ones longer and wider), 7–18 × 3–13 mm; corolla with tube 25–45 mm long, white and becoming yellow near the base, reddish or violet near the throat, widening at 33–55% of its length into an upper part cylindric or slightly infundibuliform, ± mm wide at the throat, glabrous on the exterior, scabrous on the interior, crown with subulate lobes 5–15 × 1.5–4 mm, fleshy, pink becoming violet, corolla lobes circular, 14–35 × 15–32 mm, unequal at the base, emarginate to rounded and apiculate at the apex, white with a violet band on the right exterior side, the exterior becoming reddish or violet over the entire surface, the interior white turning yellow; stamens exserted, anther 17–23 mm long; style 16–22 mm. Fruit: follicles divergent, 23–41 × 3–4.3 cm, attenuate to an obtuse apex; exocarp thick and hard, slightly sulcate, glabrous, lenticellate. Seed 12–20 × 2.5–4.5 mm, glabrous, finely puberulent; beak glabrous for 6–15 mm, bearing a coma for 23–47 mm of its length; coma petal-like, reflected, ± mm long. Distribution from Senegal to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, to Gabon, Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga and Ogooué-Lolo. Ecology: Forest and forest edge in Gabon, up to m altitude, elsewhere up to m. Use: The seeds, highly toxic, are used locally to prepare arrow poison. The plants are cultivated for seeds intended for export to Europe for medicinal use. The plant is ornamental for its flowers.

## Vernacular names

strophanthus glabre du Gabon (fr.). B : Beentje (1982, 2008a, 2021), Burkill (1985), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961). IBLIOGRAPHIE
