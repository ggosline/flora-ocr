# *Pleiocarpa mutica* Benth. Planche 90

**Genus**: [[Pleiocarpa]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hooker’s Icon. Pl. 12 : 71, t. 1181 (1876)

## Description

Shrub or small tree, glabrous or pubescent. Leaves opposite, sometimes ternate (in plants from Liberia and Ivory Coast); petiole 5–13 mm; blade broadly to narrowly elliptic to oblong or ovate, as long as wide or ± as long as wide, 0.5–2 × 0.7–1.5 cm, obtuse to acute and decurrent on the petiole at the base, smooth and shiny on both faces; secondary nerves 9 to 12 pairs forming an angle of 40–60° with the median and a submarginal nerve; tertiary venation obscure below. Inflorescence axillary or ramiflorous, rarely terminal. Flower very fragrant; pedicel 0.5–2 mm; calyx with lobes 1.3–2(–2.4) × 0.7–1.9 mm; corolla pure white, 15–27 mm long in bud, forming a relatively large obovoid head, representing 50–70% of the bud length, apex rounded; tube subcylindric, 10–22 mm long, 1–3 mm wide above the base, narrowed to 0.4–1.8 mm width below the insertion of the stamens, again widened at the level of the anthers to 0.9–1.8(–2.5) mm, with lobes petaloid and recurved, later ovate to elliptic or narrowly oblong, 2–3 times as long as the tube, 1–2 times as long as wide, 3–5 × 1–2 mm; stamens included, inserted in the upper part of the tube, anther 2–3 times as long as wide, 1–2 mm long; pistil 9.2–18 mm long, ovary with 5 distinct carpels (sometimes 3 or 4 observed in flowers of the same inflorescence or the same branch), with 1 ovule per carpel; style 8–16 mm long. Fruit yellow to bright orange, with 3 to 5 distinct mericarps, ovoid to obovoid, sometimes slightly incurved, 4–7 × 3–5 × 2–4 mm, rounded to pointed at the apex, smooth, sometimes finely ridged at the ends, finely lenticellate, often in clusters, always containing 1 seed. Seed subglobulous to obovoid, 3–5 mm long.

## Distribution

from Sierra Leone to Gabon; in Gabon: Estuaire and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forest; in Gabon above 500 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–600 m. Uses: In Ivory Coast a decoction of bark is taken to treat stomach complaints and against leg oedema. A decoction of root bark is used against kidney diseases and malaria. Bark ground in palm wine is taken as a laxative. The wood is used to make combs, paddles, pestles and various other small objects.

## Vernacular names

abre huileux (fr.)

## Source note

Among the three Gabonese collections cited under P. mutica by Omino (1996), two are now identified as P. robusta (Breteler 6558 and 13338), while the third is P. brevistyla (Breteler & van Raalte 5635). Three other specimens, Mcpherson 17968, Nguema Miyono 1879 and Wieringa 853, two of which were collected after Omino's revision, now represent P. mutica for Gabon. P. mutica seems to reach its southern limit in the Cristal Mountains in the northwest of Gabon. B: Jongkind & Lachenaud, Nyunà, Omino. BIBLIOGRAPHY
