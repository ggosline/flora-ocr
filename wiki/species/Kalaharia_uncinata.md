# *Kalaharia uncinata* (Schinz) Moldenke Planche 16 ; Figure 4(E)

**Genus**: [[Kalaharia]]
**Protologue**: Phytologia 5 : 132 (1955)

## Synonyms

- Clerodendrum uncinatum Schinz, Verh. Bot. Ver. Brandenb. 31 : 206 (1890).

## Description

K. spinescens (Gürke) Gürke var. hirsuta Moldenke, Phytologia 3 : 418 (1951). K. uncinata (Schinz) Moldenke var. hirsuta (Moldenke) Moldenke, Phytologia 5 : 132 (1955). K. uncinata (Schinz) Moldenke f. rubra Moldenke, Phytologia 23 : 210 (1972). Erect herb, spreading shrub or small tree 0.3–1.5 m high, often forming tangled masses up to 2 m wide; stem subcylindric to angular, solid; branches pale yellowish to reddish-brown, slightly to densely pubescent, with whitish hairs and glands, armed with curved ± orange-brown spines, conspicuous and reaching 6 mm, and straighter spines derived from the peduncles after flower fall, reaching 15(–24) mm. Leaves (sub-)opposite; petiole 2–6 mm; lamina narrowly elliptic-ovate to broadly ovate, broadly elliptic or ± orbicular, 0.5–3.2(–4.7) × 0.3–1.8(–2.5) cm, rounded to broadly cuneate and often oblique at the base, shortly apiculate or subacute at the apex, entire, densely pubescent and glandular throughout. Inflorescence axillary; cymes uniflorous; peduncle 0.8–1.2(–1.8) cm; bracts foliaceous, in pairs, linear, 4–8 mm long. Flower zygomorphic; pedicel 1–4 mm; calyx 8–9 mm long, with dense white hairs; corolla scarlet or orange-red, densely hairy outside, tube 6–13 mm long, lobes oblong to obovate-elliptic, (6–)10–25 × (3–)6–10 mm; stamens long-exserted (1–)1.5–2 cm. Fruit 8–15 mm in diameter, pubescent, black when ripe.

## Distribution

Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola, tropical eastern and southern Africa to South Africa (Cape Province); Gabon: Haut-Ogooué. Conservation status: Widely distributed and preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC).

## Ecology

Forest margins, shaded herbaceous stations, savannas; in Gabon at 395–600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2100 m.

## Vernacular names

baka-nlele (loango)

## Source note

When leaves fall, the hardened petiole bases can catch on clothing, thus favouring seed dispersal. Bi: Moldenke (1955c), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Schinz (1890), Verdcourt (1992). Bibliography
