# *Microberlinia brazzavillensis* A. Chev.

**Genus**: [[Microberlinia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: A. Chevalier, Rev. Bot. appl.: 588, pl. 15 (1946)

## Description

Zingana (Pl. 72, p. 287)

PL. LXXII. — Microberlinia bisulcata Chevalier : 1, flowering branch × 2/3; 2, leaflet seen from above × 1.5. — Microberlinia brazzavillensis Chevalier : 3, flowering branch × 2/3; 4, leaflets seen from above × 1.5; 5, flower × 4; 6, open flower × 4; 7, large petal × 4; 8, petal × 4; 9, fused sepals × 4; 10, sepal × 4; 11, fruit × 2/3.

Trees. Leaves with 10–12 pairs of opposite leaflets. Foliaceous stipules, approximately 1 cm long, caducous. Rachis 6–7 cm, channelled and slightly winged between the nodes, at first hairy then glabrous. Leaflets oblong, with rounded or very slightly emarginate apex, sessile, with base truncate parallel to the rachis, unequal, the posterior part being the narrowest, not auriculate, 10–16 × 5–7 mm.

Terminal pubescent russet panicles. Pedicels 1–1.5 mm. Bractlets ovate, velutinous tawny, approximately 1 cm. Tubular receptacle 8 mm. Calyx lobes 5–6 mm, glabrous. Petals oblong-spatulate, unguiculate, approximately 5 mm, glabrous, the upper one slightly larger, inserted at the throat of the receptacle. Stamens 10, inserted at the same level as the petals; the upper one free, the other 9 fused at the base for approximately 3 mm. Ovary stipitate, hirsute.

Woody pod up to 18 cm × 5 mm, becoming glabrous, marked with a very prominent longitudinal nerve, running close to the upper suture itself thickened. Seeds flattened, black, elliptic, approximately 20 × 14 mm.

## Type

Chevalier 27377 (P) pays Batéké, Brazzaville

## Discussion

Zingana is a large tree that can reach a large diameter. Buttresses little developed at the base. Trunk straight, cylindrical, fairly short. Crown very developed and dense. Bark reddish, scaly, with fibrous section, pink. Wood pale yellowish-brown with dark brown veins.

This species is found in Gabon only on the sandy soils of the shores of the Fernan Vaz lagoons where it forms clusters and sometimes almost pure stands. It exists in the Congo in the Brazzaville region.

## Vernacular names

Izingâni (Fernan Vaz); Zingana (Nkomi)
