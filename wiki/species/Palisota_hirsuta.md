Seed subhemispheric, round to triangular in transverse section, 2.7–3 mm in diameter, covered by a fleshy layer (papyraceous when dry); testa dark violet-brown, finely striped to partially and superficially reticulate; hilum large, punctiform; embryotegue dorsal.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon; in Gabon in Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

understory of mature and secondary forest, often along roads; in Gabon from 470 to 550 m elevation, elsewhere up to 640 m. Flowering from November to March and in June; fruiting in February and March

## Source note

Distinguished from all other Palisota species by the combination of its decumbent habit, its long, spreading and russet pubescence on the vegetative parts, its dense, terminal and solitary inflorescence, 2–3 times longer than broad, its pedicels and sepals with glandular pubescence and its mauve flowers with bearded staminodes bearing yellow hairs. Leaf subsessile, in pseudowhorls; sheath with long flexuous tawny hairs; blade narrowly obovate, ± 25–35 × 6–9 cm, upper surface glabrous, lower surface pubérulent, margin and midvein pubescent above and especially below, with appressed tawny or reddish hairs. Inflorescence terminal and axillary, lax, (12–)20–40 cm in length; central axis yellowish, with ± 50 to 100 cincinni spread perpendicular, 2–4 cm long, white, the basal part of 2–2.5 mm sterile, with ± 20 flowers, slightly elongate during fruiting; apical flowers (± 10) male and/or not opening. Flower white and violet; pedicel 1.5 mm; sepals spreading, ovate-elliptic, 3.5 × 1.5 mm, with rounded apex, glabrous, white or with a pink or violet tip; petals similar to sepals, but 5 × 2.5 mm; stamens: the 2 upper ones with filament filiform 1 mm and basifixed anther, erect, 2 × 0.5 mm, yellow, the lower one projected forward, with firmer filament 6.5 mm, white, with dorsifixed anther, 1 × 0.5 mm, violaceous; staminodes 3 mm in length, white; ovary broadly ovoid, 0.5 mm in length, glabrous; style 1.5 mm, stigma flattened. Fruit globose, 5–8 mm in diameter, mauve with reddish tint, then shiny, dark blue to black at maturity, with ± 14 seeds. Seed broadly conical, finely nerved with ± 15 irregular ridges, whitish-blue, waxy, with distinctly round and concave operculum.

### Distribution

Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Río Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

### Ecology

secondary forests, edges, fallows, fairly common; in Gabon up to 900 m elevation, as elsewhere. Flowering and fruiting throughout the year. Usage: The stems are ground with Tephrosia and Strophanthus to serve as a fishing poison. Shavings of the stems are used to cicatrize wounds, particularly those of the umbilical cord. Against kidney ailments, the heated leaves are applied to the lumbar region. Cooked with peanuts, they are intended to purify the milk of nursing mothers. The stems soaked in a bottle of water exposed to the sun yield a beverage used against gonorrhea
