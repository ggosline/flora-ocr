Seed subhemispheric, rounded to triangular in cross-section, 2.7–3 mm in diameter, covered by a fleshy layer (papyraceous when dry) ; testa brownish-violet, finely striate to partially and superficially reticulate ; hilum broad, punctiform ; dorsal embryotegium.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon ; in Gabon in the Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

understory of mature and secondary forest, often along roadsides ; in Gabon from 470 to 550 m altitude, elsewhere up to 640 m. Flowering from November to March and in June ; fruiting in February and March

## Source note

Distinguished from all other species of Palisota by the combination of its decumbent habit, long, spreading, russet pubescence on the vegetative parts, its dense, terminal and solitary inflorescence, 2–3 times longer than wide, its pedicels and sepals with glandular pubescence and its mauve flowers with bearded staminodes bearing yellow hairs. ### Distribution

Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Río Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo ; in Gabon in the Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

### Ecology

secondary forests, forest edges, fallow land, fairly common ; in Gabon up to 900 m altitude, as elsewhere. Flowering and fruiting year-round. Uses : The stems are ground with Tephrosia and Strophanthus to serve as a fishing poison. Shavings of the stems are used to cicatrize wounds, particularly those of the umbilical cord. Against kidney pain, heated leaves are applied to the lumbar region. Cooked with peanuts, they are used to purify the milk of nursing mothers. Stems soaked in a bottle of water exposed to the sun give a beverage used against gonorrhea
