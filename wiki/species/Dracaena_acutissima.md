# *Dracaena acutissima* Hua Planche 2, Figure 1(B, C)

**Genus**: [[Dracaena]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Hist. Nat. Autun 10 : 669 (1897)

## Synonyms

- Pleomele acutissima (Hua) N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1914 : 277 (1914).

## Description

Shrub to small tree, up to 4 m tall; trunk generally simple. Leaves spiral, overlapping and uniformly distributed, concealing the stem, not forming distinct rosettes; blade sessile, linear, up to 40 × 1.3 cm, widening towards the base, channelled above, base sheathing embracing the stem over clearly more than the circumference, apex gradually narrowed into a mucro 1–1.5 cm, soft, flexible, coriaceous, slightly thick, uniform dark green; central and lateral nerves indistinct above, prominent below; prophylls frequent at the base of young shoots and in the inflorescence. Inflorescence erect, thyrsoid spiciform, up to 30 cm long, with densely scabrous and rugose axes, green to pale violet, with several well-spaced glomérules, each generally with more than 10 flowers; bracts beneath the glomérules, caducous, narrowly triangular, up to 20 × 3 mm, scabrous; floral bracts ovate, up to 1 mm long, scarious. Flower: pedicel up to 5 mm; perianth 8–16 mm long, pale violet to white, longitudinally striped with violet on the outside, lobes as long as the tube; stamens not seen; ovary ovoid, up to 2 × 1 mm; style reaching the apex of the lobes. Fruit ± 15 × 9 mm, entire or deeply 2- or 3-lobed, lobes ellipsoid, divergent, smooth and shiny, yellow, receptacle up to 5 mm long. Seed ovoid, ± 8 × 6 mm, with angular ventral face.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, in the Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

Gallery forest edges, riverine forests, in semi-shade on sandy soil near rivers; in Gabon at 350–600 m altitude, elsewhere at 150–800 m. Flowering recorded in March, July and September. Uses: The stems are cut by the Batékés and placed in houses as an insecticide. The fruits are eaten by porcupines.

## Vernacular names

bongo (batéké). B : Baker (1898), Damen et al. (2018), Kikufi et al. (2016), Mwachala (2005). i B liographie
