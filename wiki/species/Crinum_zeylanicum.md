## Description

**Bulb**: subglobose, reaching 15 cm in diameter, often with a very large neck; bulbs frequently propagate through vegetative multiplication, constituting large populations.

**Leaves**: present with flowers, in basal rosette, spreading or erect, of somewhat firm texture due to the presence of vascular sclerenchyma (appearing as white woolly fibres when a leaf is torn); leaves non-petiolate, clasping, narrowly lanceolate to ensiform; length depending on stage, reaching 75 cm though often shorter; width 1.5–6 cm; outer leaves without apex as if torn (this is due to the fact that leaves are perennial: they wither to the base each dry season, and all except the outer ones regenerate from the base, but then without intact apex; only the new inner leaves have intact acute apices); midvein thickened; longitudinal veins dense, without distinct transverse veins; margin scabrous, undulate.

**Inflorescence**: flowering scape green or tinged reddish, thick, 15–65 cm long. Bracts green or tinged reddish, erect, subtending (1–) 3–6 (–9) sessile flowers.

**Flowers**: perianth tube greenish to reddish, curved, 9–12 (–15) cm long; segments white with a distinct dorsal stripe, pink to purple, lanceolate, 9–11 (–13) × 2–3.2 cm, the inner slightly wider than the outer; during the day bell-shaped with apical parts reflexed, more open during the night. Filaments white, declined, reaching about 2/3 of the length of the segments. Anthers black, curved, approximately 0.7 cm long. Style reddish at the apex, exceeding the anthers.

**Fruit**: red or greenish, tinged red, subglobose, 3–5 cm in diameter, without or with a short beak of less than 5 mm. Seeds bright green, narrowly packed and irregularly compressed, 15–45 per fruit.

## Distribution

**Range**: Widely distributed in tropical Africa, from Guinea to Ethiopia (extending to India).

**Gabon**: Libreville, Sibang.

## Habitat and ecology

*Crinum zeylanicum* appears to prefer zones of "periforest savanna" and "semi-deciduous forest" that have been cleared by humans or subjected to human influence; it is thus common in abandoned cultivations, roadsides, and secondary scrub. It grows on clayey, black, brown, or red soils.

## Notes

The type specimen is from West Africa, with no preserved specimen recorded. The lectotype designated by Nordal (1977) is tab. 5, fig. 2 in Ehret, Plant. et Pap. rar. depict. (1748). The extensive synonymy reflects the long history of this pantropical species in cultivation and scientific description.
