# *Vigna mungo* (L.) Hepper

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 11(1) : 128 (1956)

## Synonyms

- Phaseolus mungo L., Mant. 1 : 101 (1767).

## Description

Herb erect; stems glabrescent to hirsute with white hairs 1–3 mm long. Stipules narrow, lanceolate and arched, 7–15 × 2–5 mm, prolonged below the insertion and spurred at the base, glabrous except for ciliate margins. Leaf: petiole 4–18 cm; rachis 5–35 mm; stipels linear 2–6 mm; leaflets with petiolule 3–5 mm, blade ovate-deltoid to ovate-rhomboidal, 4–12 × 3–8.5 cm, rounded to subacute at base, acuminate at apex, covered with long hairs on both faces. Inflorescence with peduncle 3–8 cm; rachis 0.3–1.5 cm; bracts ovate-lanceolate, 4–7.5 mm long. Flower: bracteoles 4.5–7 mm long; calyx glabrous, tube 2.5–3 mm long, lobes triangular-lanceolate, 1–2 mm long; corolla 9–13 mm long, bright yellow, standard asymmetric, ± 12 × 16 mm, emarginate at apex, with on the inner face a unique central callosity, wings obovate, 8–11 mm, keel falcate, 9–11 mm, with a rostrum leading to a keel incurved over 250–300°, the left petal with a spur of 4–5 mm on the side supporting the left wing in horizontal position; ovary with 6 to 10 ovules; style prolonged beyond the stigma by a beak ± 0.8 mm long. Pod erect to spreading, subtorulous, 4–6 cm × 5 mm, terminated by a curved rostrum, dark brown to blackish, with brown-rusty hairs. Seed with brown and farinaceous-reticulate or brown to greenish and smooth tegument, 4–6 × 3–4 mm; hilum surmounted by a strophiole formed of two parallel prominent edges.

## Distribution

of Indian origin, cultivated especially in India; in Gabon, cultivated at least in the 19th century

## Ecology

agricultural situations with alternating humid and dry seasons, warm regions; cultivated up to 1800 m altitude. Uses: The seeds are consumed as a dried vegetable, alone and in various preparations. In India, papadum wafers are prepared with its flour. Sprouted seeds are also consumed and green pods are consumed as a cooked vegetable. It also serves as forage and green manure. In traditional medicine, the seeds are used for their draining, cooling and astringent properties

## Vernacular names

haricot urd, urd (fr.) ; black gram, urd bean, urad bean (angl.). B : Jansen (2006), Tateishi (1985), Verdcourt (1971c). i B liographie
