# *Vigna mungo* (L.) Hepper

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 11(1) : 128 (1956)

## Description

Kew Bull. 11(1) : 128 (1956). Phaseolus mungo L., Mant. 1 : 101 (1767). Erect herb; stems glabrescent to hirsute with white hairs, 1–3 mm long. Stipules narrow, lanceolate and curved, 7–15 × 2–5 mm, prolonged below the insertion point and spurred at the base, glabrous except for ciliate margins. Leaf: petiole 4–18 cm; rachis 5–35 mm; stipels linear 2–6 mm; leaflets with petiolule 3–5 mm, blade ovate-deltoid to ovate-rhomboidal, 4–12 × 3–8.5 cm, rounded to subacute at the base, acuminate at the apex, covered with long hairs on both surfaces. Inflorescence with peduncle 3–8 cm; rachis 0.3–1.5 cm; bracts ovate-lanceolate, 4–7.5 mm long. Flower: bracteoles 4.5–7 mm long; calyx glabrous, tube 2.5–3 mm long, lobes triangular-lanceolate, 1–2 mm long; corolla 9–13 mm long, bright yellow, standard asymmetric, ± 12 × 16 mm, emarginate at the apex, bearing on the inner face a single central callosity, wings obovate, 8–11 mm, keel falcate, 9–11 mm, with a rostrum leading to a keel incurved over 250–300°, the left petal bearing on the side a spur 4–5 mm supporting the left wing in horizontal position; ovary with 6 to 10 ovules; style prolonged beyond the stigma by a beak ± 0.8 mm long. Pod erect to spreading, subtorulous, 4–6 cm × 5 mm, terminated by a curved beak, dark brown, with reddish-brown hairs. Seed with dark brown and farinose-reticulate or brown to greenish and smooth testa, 4–6 × 3–4 mm; hilum surmounted by a strophiole formed of two raised parallel edges.

## Distribution

Of Indian origin, cultivated especially in India; in Gabon, cultivated at least in the 19th century

## Ecology

Agricultural situations with alternating humid and dry seasons, warm regions; cultivated up to 1800 m altitude. Use: The seeds are consumed as dried vegetables, alone and in various preparations. In India, papadum cakes are prepared with its flour. Sprouted seeds are also consumed and green pods are eaten as a cooked vegetable. It also serves as fodder and green manure. In traditional medicine, the seeds are used for their draining, cooling and astringent properties

## Vernacular names

haricot urd, urd (fr.) ; black gram, urd bean, urad bean (angl.). B : Jansen (2006), Tateishi (1985), Verdcourt (1971c). i B liographie
