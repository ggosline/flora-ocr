# *Raphia gabonica* Mogue, Sonké & Couvreur Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Raphia]]
**Family**: [[Arecaceae]]
**Protologue**: Phytokeys 111 : 19 (2018)

## Description

Palm with solitary stem, 3.5–7 m, 23–30 cm diameter, covered with old leaf sheaths and long weakly curved fibres, 1–2 mm width, black. Leaves 7 or 8; sheath 0.8–1.4 m long; petiole 1.7–4 m; rachis 5–7 m, spines absent on upper surface; blade comprising 170 to 195 pairs of linear leaflets, 110–120 × 4.5–5 cm, in irregular arrangement, the basal leaflet filiform. Inflorescences 5, pendulous, 1.2–1.8 m long; prophyll tubular; peduncle 20–25 cm; rachis 1.5–1.6 m, covered with numerous bracts; primary flowering branches 17–40 cm, those of second order, 20 to 40 on each side, directed toward the apex, 2.5–7 cm long, 2–5 mm diameter, slender, longer toward the base of the inflorescence than at its apex. Male and female flowers poorly known (old flowers seen only). Fruit spherical to ovoid, 4 × 3.5 cm, apex concave or rounded, rostrum absent or very short, with 11 to 12 rows of scales, containing 1 to 3 seeds, bright red at maturity. Seeds spherical.

## Distribution

endemic to Gabon, in the north of Ngounié. Preliminary conservation status: Endangered (EN)

## Ecology

humid tropical forest, growing on terra firme, in hills, or along rivers and streams; between 70 and 250 m altitude

## Source note

R. gabonica is the only Raphia endemic to Gabon. Its very localized distribution in unprotected forests makes it a species at risk of extinction. With R. regalis, it is the only known terra firme species; other Raphia are generally confined to swampy areas or riverbanks. R. gabonica is easily recognizable by the small number of leaves (7 or 8), the absence of spines on the upper surface of the rachis, its solitary stems covered with a network of fine curved fibres, its pendulous inflorescence with its slender flowering branches and its spherical to ovoid fruit, non or slightly rostrate.
