# *Coleus bojeri* Benth. Planche 10

**Genus**: [[Coleus]]
**Protologue**: Labiat. gen. spec . : 52 (1832)

## Synonyms

- Solenostemon latifolius (Hochst. ex Benth.) J.K.Morton, J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 58 : 271 (1962).

## Description

S. bojeri (Benth.) Guillaumet & A.Cornet, Adansonia , n.s., 15 : 525 (1976).

Plectranthus bojeri (Benth.) Hedge, Fl. Madag. 175 : 218 (1998).

Annual or short-lived perennial herb, sometimes noted as succulent, reaching 1 m in height; stem erect or ascending, sometimes creeping at the base, sometimes distinctly purple, pubescent. Leaf: petiole 5–50 mm; lamina ovate to subtriangular, 8–50 ×

Calyx (× 3½). – 5. Achene (× 10). (1-5 : Ghesquière 4316 ). Drawing by Albert Cleuter, Meise Botanical Garden (©).

20–30 mm, cuneate to truncate at the base, but attenuate on the petiole, acute to rounded at the apex, crenate to dentate, sometimes marked with purple, pubescent or sparsely pubescent and with reddish sessile glands on the lower surface. Inflorescence terminal, generally loose, with several distant verticillasters; cymes sessile, rarely with very short peduncle, reaching 25 mm in length, with 5 to 10 small flowers; bracts erect above, slightly cucullate, 2–6 mm long. Flower: pedicel 2–5 mm; calyx 1–2 mm long, pubescent, with red sessile glands, 3–5 mm long in fruit, tubular, slightly constricted at the throat, upper lip elliptic, lateral lobes oblong, rounded at the apex, lower lip with fused teeth, linear, 0.5 mm long, not closing the calyx throat; corolla 5–10 mm long, pale blue to violet, tube sigmoid, 3–5 mm long, upper lip erect to reflexed, shorter than the lower lip 3–5 mm long, cucullate and enclosing the stamens; stamens with fused filaments. Fruit: achenes ovoid, 1 mm long, brown, spotted with reddish.

## Distribution

widely distributed in eastern and central Africa, and in Madagascar, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Haut Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

open disturbed areas, roadsides and river banks; in Gabon at 30–630 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1700 m. Uses: In the Republic of the Congo, the juice of the plant is used as a sedative to calm the heart in order to ensure peaceful sleep without nightmares. B: Burkill (1995), Morton (1963), Paton et al. (2009, 2013). Bibliography
