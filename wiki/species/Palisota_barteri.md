# *Palisota barteri* Hook.f. Planche 14

**Genus**: [[Palisota]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Mag. 88 : t. 5318 (1862)

## Description

, nom. nudum. P. pseudoambigua A.Chev. in Hutch. & Dalziel, Fl. W. trop. Afr. 2 : 315 (1936). Perennial herb, 30–60 cm tall; rhizome underground, horizontal, often branched. Leaves in rosette, sometimes compound; sheath ± 2 cm long; pseudpetiole slender, 14–21(–33) cm long, glabrous to strongly pubescent, with spreading hairs 3–4 mm, rusty; blade obovate-elliptic, 19–26(–36) × 6–10(–13) cm, base acute or obtuse, ± abruptly narrowed toward the pseudpetiole, apex distinctly acuminate over 1–3 cm. Inflorescences terminal, several at the center of the rosette, relatively short (at most 2.5 times longer than broad), erect; peduncle ± 8–10 cm, bearing at least one ovate-elliptic bract 3–5 × 1–1.5 cm; floral bracts narrowly triangular, scarcely visible. Flower white; pedicel puberulent; sepals elliptic, 4 × 1.5 mm, smooth; petals similar to sepals; stamens equal, filament 2 mm, very slender, white, anther basifixed, ovate, ± 1 × 0.75 mm, yellow; staminodes as long as stamens; ovary ovoid, 1.5 mm in diameter; style 1.5 mm, stigma globose. Fruit ovoid, ± 9 × 7 mm, with acute apex, bright red, glabrous, with spiraled pedicel, with ± 10 seeds. Seed conical, 1–1.5 mm in diameter; with 3 or 4 ribs with ± 20 irregular furrows, with dorsal face rounded in continuity with the furrows; testa smooth, dark brown; hilum raised and ± pointed; embryostega in a distinct depression, 1 mm in diameter.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Río Muni), Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon in Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Understory of humid forests; in Gabon from 10 to 700 m elevation, elsewhere from 220 to 1300 m. Flowering and fruiting nearly year-round. Bibliography: Bidault & van der Burg (2019), Brenan (1968b), Cabezas et al. (2014), Morton (1967), Sosef et al. (2006)
