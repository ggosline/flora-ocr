# *Chytranthus cauliflorus* (Hutchinson & Dalziel) Wickens

**Genus**: [[Chytranthus]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 23 : 345 (1969)

## Synonyms

- Laccodiscus cauliflorus Hutch. & Dalz., FWTA 1: 500 (1928); Bull. Misc. Inf. Kew 1929: 26 (1929).
- Chytranthus mangenotii N. HALLÉ & AKÉ ASSI, Adansonia, ser. 2, 2 : 295, tab. I (1962); TYPE : Aké Assi IA 6067, Côté d'Ivoire (holo-, P).

## Description

Monocaulous shrub 3 to 8 m tall; trunk exceeding 6 cm in diameter. Apex hirsute with tawny hairs. Leaves alternate loosely; rachis rust-coloured hirsute exceeding 1 m long, 12–45 cm below the first pair of leaflets; petiolules hirsute 4–7 mm long; 4–11 pairs of opposite or subopposite leaflets; upper leaflets the largest reaching 24–42 cm in length by 5–7.5 cm broad; upper side of blade glabrous with grooves on the midvein and on the secondary veins; the latter numbering 16–35 pairs in the largest leaflets.

Cauline inflorescences situated between 0.5 and 2 m above the ground, entirely brown hirsute, initially ± ascending; spikes very lax with rachis 10–70 cm long, slender, with unequal internodes of approximately 1–3 cm. Flowers sessile in pauciflorous glomerate clusters; bracts narrowly acute, hirsute, 2–6 mm long; calyx globoid 5–6 mm in diameter, very hirsute; 5 small sepals, weakly open at anthesis; 4 pale yellow petals puberulous, clawed spatulate 6–6.5 mm; disk fleshy, unilateral; 6–8 stamens 6 mm, with filament and anther pubescent; ovary hirsute, trilocular (floral characters according to material from Côte-d'Ivoire).

Fruit green at maturity, trigonous subhexagonal in cross-section, ± lanceolate, glabrescent becoming smooth and succulent, reaching 11–15 × 6 cm, with rounded base and persistent calyx. 3 seeds 2.5–6 × 1.6–2.1 × 0.8–1.4 cm, roseate then brown; hilum forming a scar almost as long as the seed. Hypogeal seedling; young stem villous; the first 4 leaves at least entire. PL. 19, p. 93.

## Type

Chipp 372, Ghana, (holo-, K)

## Discussion

This species grows in small stands in the ombrophilous primary forests of Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana and Gabon. It is surprising that its range is thus divided, no collection being known from Nigeria or Cameroon. The slight differences in the Gabonese material, plant size, length and number of leaflets, do not permit, for the present, recognition of a new variety.
