# *Vitex oxycuspis* Baker Planche 30(1)

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]

## Description

in Thiselton-Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 5(2) : 326 (1900). Shrub or tree of (1–)4–6(–12) m in height; branches practically glabrous, more rarely with soft spreading hairs. Leaf: petiole of 3–15 cm, slender, with very short papillose hairs, sometimes accompanied by long fine hairs; leaflets 3 to 5, with petiolules of (0–)5–18 mm, well distinct from the blade, blade 10–20 × 2.5–7.5(–9) cm, cuneate at the base, apex long-acuminate, crenate or serrate at least in the upper half, thin, translucent when dry, with veins often tinged dark red, upper surface glabrous or sometimes strigulose (hairs of 0.3 mm), except the veins shortly puberulent, lower surface glabrous except on the veins, entirely papillose or sometimes shortly pubescent; secondary veins 6 to 15 on each side, reticulum slightly raised above; domatia present. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia, 4–12(–20) cm long, of which 2–8(–15) cm for the peduncle, with 3 or 4 levels of ramification, lax, pauciflorous; axes entirely covered with papillose hairs and glands. Flower: calyx open bell-shaped, 2–3 mm long, with broadly triangular teeth, 0.5–1 mm long, puberulent, with very short hairs; corolla 7–9 mm long, whitish or yellowish-brown (?); stamens exsert; ovary glabrous, glandular at the apex. Fruit obovoid, ± 8 × 7 mm. Gynoecium. – 5. Fruit. Drawing by A. Aubréville (1-5), Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle, Paris (©), reproduced with permission from Aubréville (1956).

## Distribution

West tropical Africa, from Guinea to Angola; in Gabon, apparently rare: Haut-Ogooué and Moyen-Ogooué. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

Dense forests, secondary forests; at 200–450 m elevation

## Source note

This is the first record of this species for the flora of Gabon. Bibliography: Aubréville (1956), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Huber et al. (1963), Lisowski (2009), Meerts (2020a, 2020b)
