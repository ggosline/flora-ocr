# *Polygala acicularis* Oliv. Planche 23(1)

**Genus**: [[Polygala]]
**Family**: [[Polygalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr . 1 : 132 (1868)

## Description

Shrub, rarely perennial herb, 50–100 cm; stem woody at base, erect, densely leafy, striate, glabrous. Leaves alternate; petiole 0.5–1 mm; blade linear to acicular, (10–)20–60 × 1–3 mm, mucronate at apex, glabrous. Inflorescence: dense terminal racemes, 10–15(–30) cm long; rachis glabrous; bracts linear, 2–3 mm long, caducous; bracteoles similar, but up to 1 mm long. Flower whitish to violet or blue-purple; pedicel 1.25–1.5 mm, glabrous; upper sepal elliptic, 3 mm long, finely ciliate, winged sepals elliptic, 6–9 × 2.5–4 mm, glabrous, early caducous, lower outer sepals elliptic, 2.5 mm long, finely ciliate, connate; upper petals obovate, 6.5–8 × 3.5–4 mm, carina 5–7.5 × 3–4 mm, crest 2.5–3 mm long, fimbriate; fertile stamens 6, glabrous, 2 sterile and pubescent. Fruit flattened, ellipsoid, 5–7 × 4 mm, deeply emarginate at apex, very narrowly winged, glabrous. Seed ellipsoid, 4–6 × 2–2.5 mm, with silky white hairs; caruncle symmetric, 1.5–2 mm long, appendages absent.

## Distribution

Chad, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, Sudan, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Rwanda, and Angola; in Gabon known from Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga, and Haut-Ogooué.

## Ecology

Shrubby formations, open and dry shrubby savannas, and marshy grass formations; in Gabon at 100–600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m. Uses: The fragrant roots (as with the entire plant) are used as a scented unguent; reduced to powder, they are used against sore throat. The stem bark reduced to powder and the pulpy bark of the roots are used as a topical application for infected wounds.
