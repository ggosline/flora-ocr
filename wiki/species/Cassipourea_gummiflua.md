# *Cassipourea gummiflua* Tul. Planche 29.3, 32

**Genus**: [[Cassipourea]]
**Family**: [[Rhizophoraceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Sci. Nat., ser. 4, 6 : 123 (1856)

## Description

Tree or shrub (3–)5–20(–25) m tall; trunk reaching 40(–60) cm in diameter; bark dark brown to blackish, longitudinally striated; branches glabrous, with white lenticels, nodes slightly swollen, terminal bud very gummiferous. Stipules quickly caducous, ± triangular, 2–4 mm long, glabrous or glabrescent. Leaf: petiole (6–)8–15(–17) mm, glabrous; blade elliptic to oblong or ovate, 5–15(–21) × 3–4.5(–10) cm, (1.6–)2–2.6 times as long as wide, base rounded or truncate, apex acute to obtuse, glabrous, margins entire, slightly undulate or serrulate above the middle; lateral veins 8–11 pairs, reticulum of veinlets generally well visible on the lower surface with reddish midvein. Inflorescence axillary, fasciculate, multiflorous, with copious resinous exudate at the base of the flowers; peduncle ± 5 mm; bracts very small and quickly caducous. Flower (4–)5(–7)–merous, glabrous, fragrant; pedicel 1–1.5 mm, glabrous, stipe 0.5–1 mm; calyx tubular or cupuliform, 4–5.5 × 2.5–3.5 mm, including the erect or reflexed lobes, semi-elliptic to ± deltoid, 0.6–1.2 × 0.6–1.8 mm; petals narrowly spatulate, 4.5–6 × 1 mm, white or yellowish, divided at the apex into 7–10 filiform languets, 2–3(–4) mm long; stamens (8–)10(–12), inserted outside and at the base of the disk, filaments 3–7 mm, the epipetalous longer than the alternipetalous, anthers narrowly ovoid, 0.5–1.1(–1.5) mm long; disk approximately 1 mm tall, scarcely lobed, enveloping the ovary; ovary ± 1 mm in diameter, with 2(–5) locules and 2 ovules per locule, glabrous or hirsute above the middle; style rather stout, 0.7 × 2.5–4(–6) mm, truncate or slightly 2(–3)–lobed, glabrous. Fruit ellipsoid to obovoid, 7–11 × 4.5–7 mm, blackish, glabrous or puberulent above the middle, with 2(–3) locules. Seeds 1(–2) per locule, ovoid, 3–6.5 × 1.5 mm, dark red, partially covered by an orange aril.

## Distribution

Central African Republic, East and southern Africa to South Africa; in Gabon, known from Estuaire

Very rare species, endemic to Gabon, known from the Lastoursville region
