# *Strychnos camptoneura* Gilg & Busse

**Genus**: [[Strychnos]]
**Family**: [[Loganiaceae]]

## Description

in Engler Bot. Jahrb. 36 : 93 (1905); in Mildbraed, Wiss. Ergebn. zw. deutsch. Zentr.-Afr. Exped. 1910-11, 2 : 62 (1922). — LEEUWENBERG, Act. Bot. Neerl. 11 : 48, fig. 1 (1962). — FWTA ed. 2, 2 : 44. — LEEUWENBERG, Meded. Landbouwh. 69 (1) : 75, fig. 9, carte 8 (1969).

— Scyphostrychnos talbotii S. Moore in Cat. Talbot's Nig. Pl. : 71, pl. 10 (1913). — FWTA ed. 1, 2 : 24, non Strychnos talbotiae S. Moore, l. c. : 69.

— Scyphostrychnos psittaconyx DUVIGN., Bull. Class. Sci. ser. 5, 34 : 98, fig. 1 (1948).

Large liana reaching a height of 20–45 m and a length of 120 m, dark brown or black when dry; stem 2–25 cm in diameter; bark dark brown, with large lenticels, with leaf scars; wood pale yellow to dark ochraceus, with islands of pale brown bark; branches without or slightly lenticellate; small branches glabrous, non-lenticellate, ± cylindric, often somewhat furrowed when dry; central axis cylindric; tendrils in 1–3 pairs.

Leaves with petiole glabrous, 7–17 mm long; limb coriaceous or subcoriaceous, equally on the living plant, thinner in shade, elliptic, narrowly elliptic or often ovate (for leaves situated on the central axis), 6–31 × 3–12 cm, acute or shortly acuminate at the apex, often more longly acuminate in shade, cuneate or rounded at the base (and for leaves situated on the main axis rounded to cordate), the margin reflexed in leaves exposed to sunlight (flowering branches), glabrous on both faces; the basal pair of secondary veins larger than the other pairs, but generally not more distinct, with veins curved along the margin.

Inflorescences axillary or sometimes terminal, solitary, lax or contracted, 4.5–6.5 × 4–6 cm, pauciflorous; peduncle and branches usually long, glabrous, pale green like the pedicels and bracts. Flowers 5-merous. Sepals pale green, connate at the base, orbicular, 2–3.5 mm long, rounded, finely ciliate, glabrous on both faces. Corolla, in the mature bud, 3.3–4 times longer than the calyx, 8–11.5 mm long, white or yellow, glabrous on the outside, on the inside with a finely ciliate crown, undulate or ± distinctly 5-lobed and constricted at the throat; tube 0.9–1.2 times longer than the lobes, 4–6 mm long; lobes thick, triangular to ovate, 4–6 × 1.7–3 mm, acute, spreading or recurved. Stamens included in the crown, inserted at the throat of the corolla, with glabrous, very short filament; anther narrowly oblong, 2.2–3 × 0.6–0.7 mm, ciliate all around or only at the base. Pistil glabrous, 8–9 mm long; ovary ovoid 3–4 × 2–2.5 mm, with two locules; style 5–5.5 mm long; stigma capitate; ± 50 ovules per locule.

Fruits large, hard, often slightly obliquely pedicellate, variable in form and size, globose, ellipsoid, somewhat pyriform, or sometimes ovoid, pale glaucous to yellow, 6.5–20 × 6–13.5 cm, apiculate or rounded at the apex, with two locules, containing (2–) 10–112 seeds in horizontal layers; wall thick, hard, tuberculate on the inside, the thickness varying between (2–) 6 and 13 mm (even within a single fruit); pulp orange becoming black. Seeds flattened, convex especially on one side, obliquely suborbicular, ovate, triangular or oblong, non or slightly curved, 25–50 × 20–40 × 3–6 mm or sometimes smaller, glabrous, with a narrow, irregular wing, 1–6 mm in width. Pl. 21, p. 130.
