# *Dalbergia pachycarpa* (De Wild. & T.Durand) Uhlrich ex De Wild.

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Pl. Bequaert. 3 : 408 (1925)

## Synonyms

- Ecastaphyllum pachycarpum De Wild. & T.Durand, Bull. Herb. Boiss. sér. 2, 1 : 749 (1901).

## Description

, nom. inval., pro parte. Liane or shrub 2–10 m in height, reaching 10 cm in diameter, inodorous, without spines, but sometimes bearing prehensile hook-like branchlets; bark dark brown to blackish, with lenticels often poorly visible; young branchlets shortly pubescent to glabrescent. Stipules early caducous, elliptic to lanceolate ± falciform, 3–7.5 × 1–2 mm, obtuse, fairly thick, with short dense appressed pubescence. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.6–4.3 cm; rachis 1.5–11 cm, shortly pubescent to glabrescent; leaflets 5 to 10, alternate and distinctly unequal, with petiolule 2–7 mm, blade elliptic (or ± obovate in the terminal leaflet), symmetric, 3.7–14.5 × 1.5–7.3 cm in the upper leaflets and 2.4–8 × 1.5–4.3 cm in the lower ones, rounded or obtuse at the base, acuminate at the apex, fairly coriaceous, olive green to dark brownish when dry, upper surface glabrous, lower surface with short appressed hairs (0.1–0.4 mm) unidirectional, of highly variable density; lateral nerves 5 to 12, generally well visible, reaching almost to the leaf margin, veinlets in dense raised reticulum. Inflorescence in dense panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary and terminal on leafy branchlets, 0.8–5 cm in length, with short dense golden-brown hairs; bracts caducous after flowering, broadly ovate to elliptic, 0.7–1.5 × 0.3–0.8 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1.5–6.5 mm; bracteoles caducous after flowering, broadly elliptic, 0.5–1 × 0.3–0.6 mm, pubescent; calyx with dense golden-brown appressed hairs, tube 1.7–2.5 × 1.5–2.5 mm, truncate or with short ± equal valvate teeth, triangular, < 0.5 mm in length; corolla white or slightly pinkish, petals with claw exceeding the calyx, generally fairly long, standard obliquely bent, with claw 2–2.7 mm and suborbicular lamina 3–4 × 3–4.5 mm, with rounded base and bilobed apex, wings equalling or slightly exceeding the keel, with claw 2–3 mm and ± rectangular lamina 3–4 × 1.3–1.8 mm, smooth externally, with rounded or truncate base, keel distinctly curved, with claw 2–3 mm and lamina 3–4 × 1.7–2 mm, with truncate or slightly auriculate base; stamens in 2 bundles of 5, filaments 4–5 mm, fused for ⅔ to ⁴⁄₅ of their length, straight at the base and curved in their free part; ovary 1.2–1.5 × 0.5 mm, glabrous or with dense appressed hairs, with 1 or 2 ovules and straight stipe 2.5–3.7 mm; style linear, 1.3 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 2–5 mm and stipe 4–10 mm, woody, discoid or shortly ellipsoid, very thick at edge generally thinned and sometimes recurved, 2–4.8 × 2.2–4.3 × 0.7–1.7 cm, ± tuberculate, generally densely pubescent with felted brown hairs, more rarely glabrous, with 1 seed; pericarp 0.7–5 mm in thickness, with fairly hard outer layer formed of cork tissue reinforced with thick woody fibres, endocarp shortly pubescent on the inner surface. Seed reniform, very thick, ± 27 × 12 × 7 mm.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; widely distributed in Gabon, recorded from most provinces, except Haut-Ogooué and Moyen-Ogooué.

## Ecology

Secondary forests, forest galleries, river or swamp margins, regrowth, fallow land and savanna scrub re-colonisation areas; up to 600 m altitude in Gabon, elsewhere up to 1400 m. In Gabon, flowering from May to August (once in February) and fruiting from October to February.

## Source note

The pods of D. pachycarpa are fairly variable in form and thickness; they are generally pubescent, rarely glabrous in certain specimens from the Ivindo basin. The pubescence of the lower surface of the leaflets varies from fairly dense to very sparse. D. pachycarpa is very close to D. semiapplanata and D. xylocarpa, from which it is sometimes difficult to separate in the absence of fruits. It is even closer to D. heudelotii Stapf and could be only a subspecies of it. D. heudelotii is limited to West Africa (records from Central Africa are erroneous and refer for the most part to D. pachycarpa) and differs from D. pachycarpa by its smaller and more asymmetric fruits, with fairly soft cork pericarp. The description of D. monetaria var. oyemensis Pellegr. is not valid in the absence of a Latin diagnosis. The two specimens cited under this name by Pellegrin belong, one to D. pachycarpa, the other to D. semiapplanata (Lachenaud & Maesen 2016). b: Cronquist (1954), Pellegrin (1949). Bibliography
