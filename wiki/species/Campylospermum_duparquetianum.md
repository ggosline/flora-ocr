# *Campylospermum duparquetianum* (Baill.) Tiegh. Planche 7

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. Bot. (Morot) 16 : 43 (1902)

## Description

J. Bot. (Morot) 16 : 43 (1902). Ouratea duparquetiana (Baill.) Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 258 (1903). Ouratea corymbosa Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 17 : 80 (1893). Ouratea cameronii Hutch. & Dalziel, Fl. W. trop. Afr., ed. 1, 1 : 193 (1927). Tree reaching 8 m in height, monocaulous; bark dark brown. Stipules caducous, triangular, 3–5 mm long. Leaves in tufts at the apex of the trunk; petiole 0–10 mm; blade narrowly spatulate to narrowly elliptic-spatulate, 26–49(–58) × 5–12(–17) cm, ratio 3–5(–6.25), broadly cuneate to rounded or slightly cordate at the base, apex acute to slightly acuminate, coriaceous to papyraceous, plane to slightly bullate, with regularly serrate margin; midvein prominent on both surfaces, lateral nerves in 20 to 30 pairs, prominent but often situated in a depression above, prominent below, tertiary venation scalariform, perpendicular to the lateral nerves, indistinct to slightly distinct above, distinct below. Inflorescence axillary, corymbose, dense; peduncle cylindrical, woody, 2–5(–6) cm, with a large number of racemes reaching 1.5 cm and with (4–)20 to 35 solitary flowers, with 2 to 4 foliaceous bracts in the apical part; gemmate scales persistent, 4–8 mm long; bracts triangular, 1–2 mm long. Flower: pedicel 10–50 mm, articulate 4–8 mm from the base; sepals narrowly ovate, 7–11 × 3 mm in flower, 15–20 × 6–8 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 16–18 × 4–8 mm, shortly unguiculate, slightly emarginate at the apex; stamens: anthers 6–7 mm long; style approximately 7 mm. Fruit: receptacle globose, 5 × 6 mm; 2 to 4 well-developed drupelets per receptacle, obovoid, 9–10 × 5–7 mm; cotyledons incumbent, equal.

## Distribution

from Liberia to Ghana; Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon where it is fairly common in the western half of the country (Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime, Ngounié and Nyanga).

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest, sometimes in degraded forest or along forest trails, in dried swamps and in gallery forest, on clayey soil; in Gabon, at 10–380 m altitude, elsewhere up to 410 m. Use: This species is sometimes planted as an ornamental. Taxonomic note: very distinct species belonging to a restricted group of monocaulous species, with large leaves narrowly spatulate to narrowly elliptic-spatulate; C. duparquetianum is distinguished there by its short corymbose inflorescences. Bibliography: Bissiengou (2014), Farron (1963, 1968, 1985), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hutchinson, Dalziel & Keay (1954).
