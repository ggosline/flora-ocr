## Description

Stem 2–15 (30) cm, leafy without pseudobulbs. Leaves 1–5 × 0.3–1 cm, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, laterally compressed, acute, fleshy. Inflorescence 4–10 cm, 20–100-flowered. Flowers minute, not resupinate, translucent, yellow to orange. Dorsal sepal acute, translucent, ovate to elliptic-ovate; lateral sepals suboblique at the base, triangular-ovate. Petals linear to elliptic-oblong, obtuse. Lip distinctly trilobed just above the base, thin, fragile, translucent, lower part cochleate with a thick transverse crest at the base; median lobe transversely elliptic to cordate, entire or margins irregularly denticulate, apex truncate to subacute; lateral lobes oblong-ovate, apex obtuse to rounded, margins entire. – Pl. 63, p. 131.

## Distribution

Guinea, Ghana, Sao Tomé, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo (Kinshasa), Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, South Africa, Comores, Madagascar, Réunion, Mauritius

## Ecology

alt. 430–1250 m. Epiphytic on the trunk of a large forest tree in a humid and shaded ravine in rainforest, but also observed on the trunk of a Rinorea sp.

V. BULBOPHYLLINAE Schltr.

Orchideen: 319 (1915).

Pseudobulbs with a single internode bearing 1 (–2) leaf (leaves). Flowers generally small, resupinate. Stelidium prominent very often present. Rostellum truncate, fleshy. Viscidium semi-liquid or cellular, unique, sometimes very large. Small digitate hamulus present in a few species. Pollinia generally 4, very unequal in many species, but sometimes only 2.

Important subtribe of some thirty genera, the majority Oceanic, of which only 1 genus occurs in Gabon: Bulbophyllum Thouars. Two conceptions of this genus based on totally different generic definitions have been published recently by VERMEULEN (1987) and GARAY & al. (1994).
