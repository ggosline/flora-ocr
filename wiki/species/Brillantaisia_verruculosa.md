# *Brillantaisia verruculosa* Lindau

**Genus**: [[Brillantaisia]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lindau, Bot. Jahrb. 22: 113 (1895) et in Engler et Prantl, nat. Pflanzenfam. IV, 3B: 296 (1895)

## Description

and in Engler et Prantl, nat. Pflanzenfam. IV, 3B: 296 (1895). — Burkill, F.T.A. 5: 43 (1899). — Benoist, l.c. 334 (1913). — Robyns, Fl. Spermatophyt. Parc Nat. Albert 2: 269 (1917). — Brillantaisia madagascariensis auct.: R. Benoist, l.c. (1913); A. Chevalier, Expl. Bot. Afr. Occ. Franç. 493 (1920). — Hutchinson et Dalz. F.W.T.A., ed. 1, 2: 25 (1931), pro parte: specimens from West Africa only. — Heine, F.W.T.A., ed. 2, 2: 406 (1963), pro parte: specimens from West Africa and syn. Br. verruculosa Lindau only, not Brillantaisia madagascariensis T. Anders. ex Lindau, Bot. Jahrb. 17: 103 (1893).

Suffrutescent plant, robust, ± glabrescescent, 1.5–2 m in height, with quadrangular stems. Petioles 3–9 cm, winged. Leaf blade ovate, acuminate, 10–14 × 4–7 cm, rounded or acute at the base, subentire and slightly undulate at the margins, decurrent into the winged petiole; indumentum rather variable; approximately 13 pairs of lateral veins.

Inflorescences 10–15 cm long, weakly hairy, in interrupted or uninterrupted spikes toward the base; bracts rhomboidal, 11 × 7 mm, slightly ciliate; bracteoles elliptic. Flowers 1–2 in rather contracted axillary fascicles, subsessile. Calyx 1.1–2.5 cm, accrescent after flowering, ciliate: hairs of rather variable length, glandular; the posterior segment 1–3 mm longer than the others, and wider. Corollas white; corolla tube 10 mm. Anterior lip 13 × 8 mm, its three lobes approximately 2 × 2 mm, with a few multicellular hairs on the outside; posterior lip 14–16 × 6–7 mm, with two teeth 2 × 1.5 mm. Stamens inserted slightly below the throat; a line of pubescence on the lower surface of the filament descends on the salient nerve which extends into the corolla tube: this line is formed of subulate hairs, erect-ascending and arranged in the form of a long brush; it presents at its upper extremity a small salient tooth (absent in the Malagasy and East African species). Filaments 12 mm long; anther sacs 3.5 mm long, glabrous, slightly curved and somewhat separated at the base of the anther; staminodes erect, with a very small enlargement in place of the anther, 3–5 mm long (distinctly longer and with rudimentary anthers in the Malagasy and East African taxa). Disk 0.7 mm; ovary 3 mm high; style 25 mm long, subpubescent.

Fruit glabrous, 2 cm long, with approximately 10 seeds. Seed 2 × 2 mm, with dark brown testa, with dense and fine pubescence.

## Source note

This species was described from Cameroon based on a single collection (Zenker and Staudt 166). R. Benoist, in his "Contribution to the flora of the Acanthaceae of French Africa", also reports it from Cameroon, but without citing a specimen (therefore, evidently, based on the literature). In the same publication, he assigns a plant from Guinea (Chevalier 20607, Timbikounda) to the Malagasy species B. madagascariensis. This latter identification was repeated in 1920 by A. Chevalier and then by Hutchinson and Dalziel in F.W.T.A. (ed. 1, 1931). Benoist, in 1913, had not seen this authentic specimen of B. verruculosa, and he distinguishes this species after Burkill (l.c., 1899). In reality the specimen Chevalier 20607, as well as other material collected since in West Africa, turn out to be conspecific with B. verruculosa; the identification of these plants as B. madagascariensis obscured the specific separation of the two taxa; and, for this reason, the first binomial was given as a synonym of the second in F.W.T.A. (ed. 2, l.c., 1963). These are in reality two well-distinct species. Besides the corolla color (white in B. verruculosa, purple in B. madagascariensis), there are characters of pubescence (very strong in B. madagascariensis, weak or almost absent in the vegetative parts in B. verruculosa), of dimensions of the reproductive apparatus and of the whole inflorescence (distinctly smaller in B. verruculosa than in B. madagascariensis) as well as the characters indicated above which make it possible at first glance to distinguish these two species. It is highly probable that the taxon called until now B. madagascariensis in East Africa (originally described under the binomial B. spicata Lindau, from the Usambara, assigned by Burkill in 1899 to B. madagascariensis) represents, in truth, a third taxon. This question will need to be studied by a future monographer, all the more so since the differences between these two taxa might merit only a separation at an infraspecific rank. Lindau had nevertheless distinguished (Natürliche Pflanzenfamilien IV 3B: 296, 1895) the three species B. madagascariensis, B. spicata, and B. verruculosa, as constituting his sect. II. Stenanthium of the genus Brillantaisia; all the other species of the genus form his sect. I. Euryanthium, which would become, according to the current rules of nomenclature, sect. Brillantaisia.
