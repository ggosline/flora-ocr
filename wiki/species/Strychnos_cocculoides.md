# *Strychnos cocculoides* Baker

**Genus**: [[Strychnos]]
**Family**: [[Loganiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. : 98 (1895)

## Description

— FTA 4 (1) : 533. — DUVIGNEAUD, Lejeunia 13 : 114 (1949); Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg. 85 : 20, fig. 6 A (1952). — E. A. Bruce, Kew Bull. : 38 (1955). — FTEA, Loganiaceæ : 16, fig. 3, 1-5. — VERDOORN in FSA 26 : 149 (1963). — LEEUWENBERG, l.c. : 86, fig. 12, carte 10.

— S. suberosa DE WILD., Ann. Mus. Congo, ser. 5, 1 : 177 (1904). — FTA 4 (1) : 624.

Shrub or small tree with deciduous leaves, 0.30-8 m in height; trunk 4-25 cm in diameter; bark pale grey to pale brown, thick, subereous, with longitudinal fissures, neither scaly nor lenticellate; wood hard, whitish, without bark islands; branchlets pale to dark brown when dry, ± fissured and subereous and thus irregularly thickened, not lenticellate, with spines often recurved or sometimes straight, sometimes terminated by a straight spine; branchlets pubescent or sometimes glabrous, often furrowed when desiccated.

Leaves with short petiole, pubescent or less often glabrous, 2-8 mm in length; blade coriaceous, in young leaves thinner and papyraceous when dry, very variable in shape and size, orbicular, ovate, elliptic or sometimes narrowly elliptic, generally wider on fertile branchlets, smaller (as in S. spinosa) on young very spiny shoots after passage of bush fires, 2-10 × 1-9 cm, emarginate, rounded to acute and apiculate or sometimes acuminate at apex, rounded, subcordate or cuneate at base, often somewhat corrugated or subpapillose, often with pilose domatia at the axils of main veins below, pubescent or less often glabrous on both surfaces; 1-3 pairs of secondary veins curved along the margin; venation impressed above, prominent and distinct below.

Inflorescences terminal, resembling an umbel, contracted, 2.5-4 × 2-5 cm, generally multiflorous; peduncle, branches and pedicels sometimes with scattered pubescence. Flowers 5-merous. Sepals pale green, connate at base, narrowly triangular, 2-5 × 0.7-1.3 mm, elongated during flowering, acuminate or subulate, indistinctly ciliate, pubescent without, with uniform indumentum of generally erect hairs, within pubescent at apex and glabrous at base, or sometimes entirely pubescent. Corolla, when expanded 1-2 times longer than calyx and (3-4)-4-5 mm in length, pale green, greenish, white or yellowish-green, with scattered pubescence or glabrous without, within with a narrow entire corona, white-penicillate at throat; tube 1-2 times longer than lobes, 2-2.5-3 × 2-3 mm, urceolate or campanulate; lobes triangular, 1.3-2.5 × 1-1.5 mm, acute, erect or nearly so. Stamens included, inserted 0.4-1 mm from base of corolla, with glabrous filament, 0.4-1 times longer than anther; anther oblong or elliptic, ciliate with villous hairs all around. Pistil pubescent, 1.8-2.8 mm in length; ovary ovoid or globose, 1-1.6 × 1-1.4 mm, two-celled; style generally very short; stigma oblong, large, up to 0.9 mm in length; 30-50 ovules per locule.

Fruits large, hard, resembling an orange, globose, yellow or orange, often spotted with green, 6-11 cm in diameter and containing approximately 10-100 seeds; wall rather thick, 2-5 mm in thickness; pulp edible. Seeds flattened, ± plano-convex, obliquely ovate or elliptic, ordinarily irregularly curved, 12-22 × 8-15 × 2.5-4 mm, somewhat rugose, with very short pubescence. Pl. 24, p. 133.
