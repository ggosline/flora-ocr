# *Vigna subterranea* (L.) Verdc. var. subterranea Planche 128

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 35(3) : 474 (1980)

## Description

Kew Bull. 35(3) : 474 (1980). Voandzeia subterranea (L.) Thouars, Gen. nov. madagasc. : 23 (1806). Annual herb, sparsely pubescent; stems trailing. Stipules ovate, ± 3 mm long, prolonged below the insertion and cordate at the base. Leaf: petiole 8–30 cm; rachis 10–25 mm; stipels ovate, 1 mm long; leaflets with petiolule of 3 mm, lamina obovate to oblanceolate or lanceolate, 3–8 × 1–4 cm, acute at the base, emarginate at the apex, papyraceous. Inflorescence: peduncle 0.5–2 cm, tomentose, with flowers at soil level; after fertilization, the peduncle elongates by pushing the flowers into the soil where the pods develop; rachis with one or two nodes; bracts ovate-lanceolate, ± 1 mm long. Flower: bracteoles ± 1 mm long; calyx glabrous, with tube ± 1 mm long, lobes triangular, ± 0.5 mm long; corolla 8–12 mm long, yellow, standard 9 × 13 mm, without callosi on the inner face, wings obovate, 5–11 mm long, keel curved, 5–11 mm long, with a short rostrum; ovary with 1 to 4 ovules; style prolonged beyond the stigma by a short beak. Underground pod, 1–3.5 cm × 1–1.8 mm, with 1(–4) seeds. Seed more or less circular, 5–15 mm in diameter; hilum central, without strophiole.

## Distribution

of African origin, cultivated somewhat in all tropical zones and rarely naturalized; its presence in Gabon remains to be confirmed.

## Ecology

cultivated fields, fallows; cultivated up to 2000 m. Use: The roasted or boiled seeds are eaten as a delicacy. The flatulence associated with its consumption, as well as modest yields, given the work required, doubtless reduce the popularity of this seed legume. In West Africa, this species constitutes an important traditional crop.

## Source note

The wild forms (var. spontanea (Harms) Pasquet) grow from northeastern Nigeria to Sudan. They have long stems extending over several meters and their pods and seeds are smaller than those of cultivated Bambara groundnut. The pod wall is thinner. Bi: Brink et al.
