# *Adhatoda le-* Testui (R. Benoist) Heine, comb. nov.

**Genus**: [[Adhatoda]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]

## Description

≡ Rungia Le-Testui R. Benoist, in Pellegrin, Mém. Soc. Linn. Norm. N. S., Bot., 1 (3): 48 (1928).

Shrublet erect, with young branches covered with a reddish-brown pubescence; internodes short, 12-15 mm. Petioles 7-15 mm. Leaf blade glabrous, 10-30 × 5.5-11 cm, obovate, long-attenuate but not decurrent toward the distinctly rounded base; acumen obtuse or rounded. 10 pairs of secondary veins covered with brown pubescence beneath.

Inflorescences in the form of spikes, axillary and terminal, 9-17 cm long, tetragonous and distichous, with peduncles 12-15 cm long; sterile bracts sessile, imbricate broadly ovate-acuminate, 12.5 × 10.5 mm, quintupleinerved, obtuse; floriferous bracts ovate-lanceolate, 12.5 × 6.5 mm, subtriplinerved obtuse at the apex; these bracts are all covered with very fine and regular pubescence, very pale brown with dark and well-marked veins. Bracteoles 1 mm long or less. Sepals 5, subequal, 7 × 1.9 mm, lanceolate acute very finely and regularly pubescent on both faces and slightly ciliate. Corolla 15 mm long, white, slightly pubescent on the exterior and along the principal veins on the interior; glabrous otherwise on the interior; the tube is subcylindric, scarcely enlarged in its upper part; posterior lip bilobed, approximately 6 × 6 mm; anterior lip tripartite to the middle, somewhat longer than the posterior lip; filaments of stamens pubescent, 9 mm long; anther sacs glabrous, inserted at different levels, 2 mm long; the lower sac is furnished with a spur 0.7 mm long, the upper sac with a much smaller spur 0.3 mm in length. Ovary densely pubescent, 2.5 mm high; disk little developed, approximately 0.7 mm high. Style pubescent, slender 10 mm long.

Fruit finely pubescent, with indehiscent septum, 2.5 cm long, stipitate, 4 mm in width and thickness. Seeds ellipsoid, 4 × 3.5 × 2 mm, with seed coat finely verruculate-granulate.

## Source note

— The case of Adhatoda Le-Testui is a good example of the difficulties in circumscribing genera of Acanthaceae in the sense of C.B. Clarke: the species in question possesses at the same time characters of Justicia, of Rungia and of Adhatoda. R. Benoist had described this species in the absence of fruit (these have perfectly indehiscent septa) and had considered it justly, by the majority of its other morphological characters, as a species of the genus Rungia Nees. It is nevertheless very clearly, in the circumscription of C.B. Clarke accepted here, a species of the genus Adhatoda. The most closely related species is Adhatoda Engleriana (Lindau) C.B. Clarke (≡ Justicia Engleriana Lindau), from East Africa; very close to it are also Adhatoda Vasica Nees (≡ Justicia Adhatoda L., type species of the genus Adhatoda Mill.), from East India, Adhatoda Schimperiana Nees (≡ Justicia Schimperiana (Nees) T. Anders., East, central Africa, Ethiopia, etc., as well as Justicia Pseudorungia Lindau, also from East and central Africa.
