# *Annona reticulata* L. Coeur de boeuf

**Genus**: [[Annona]]
**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp.Pl.,ed.1 : 536 (1753);R.E.Fries,Act.Hort.Berg.10 :278 (1931).— Walker et Sillans,Enc.Biol. 56 :63 (1961)

## Description

;R.E.Fries,Act.Hort.Berg.10 :278 (1931).— Walker et Sillans,Enc.Biol. 56 :63 (1961).

Shrub reaching 8 m in height with greyish pubescent branchlets, becoming glabrous. Leaves with petiole 8–12 mm long: limb lanceolate to lanceolate-oblong, long-attenuate-acuminate at apex, acute at base, 10–20 cm long, 2–5 cm wide. Lateral veins 10–15 pairs, plane above, prominent below.

Inflorescences extra-axillary, 2–5-flowered, with peduncle approximately 5 mm long; pedicel 1.5–3 cm long. Sepals broadly triangular-ovate, acuminate at apex, 2–3 mm diameter. External petals linear-oblong, obtuse at apex, dilated and concave at base, 1.5–2.5 cm long, puberulent; internal petals rudimentary, oblong, 2 mm long, glabrous. Stamens 1.5 mm long. Carpels with hairy ovaries, elongate stigmas.

Fruits subglobose-ovoid, reddish at maturity, 9–10 cm diameter, nearly smooth, with subplane areoles.

## Discussion

Plant native to the Antilles. Small tree rarely cultivated. The fruit is edible but only moderately appreciated; the whitish pulp is rather bland, thick and creamy.

Species native to the north of the Andes. Fruit edible, little cultivated.

27. ANONIDIUM Engler et Diels

Notizbl.Bot.Gart.Berlin 3:56 (1900);in Engler,Monogr.Afr.Pflanzenf.6: 35(1901).—R.E.Fries,Acta Hort.Berg.10:76–81 (1931);in Engler et Prantl,Nat.Pflanzenf.,ed.2,17a,2 : 156 (1959).

Trees or shrubs with indumentum of simple hairs. Inflorescences arising on the trunk or on leafless branchlets, in unipparous cymes, pluriflorous, very elongate, or extra-axillary uniflorous, or grouped by 2–3 at the apex of small branchlets. Flowers ♂ or ♀, large. Sepals 3, valvate, fused at base, smaller than the petals. Petals 6, in two valvate whorls. Receptacle conical or convex. ♂ Flowers with very numerous stamens, sessile to subsessile, oblong, linear thecae extrorse, connectives dilated, truncate above the anthers and puberulent. ♀ Flowers bearing 3–5 (or a single) rows of stamens of same form as in ♂ flowers, but shorter; carpels very numerous covering the rest of the receptacle, free, pressed against one another, ovaries cylindric immersed in the receptacle, styles thick enlarged into ± capitate stigmas. Ovule 1, basal, erect.

Fruits syncarpous, large, ovoid, reticulate, fleshy, formed of very numerous united mericarps. Seeds numerous, one per mericarp, oblong-subreniform, flattened.

Type species: A. Mannii (Oliver) Engler et Diels (= Annona Mannii).

Since Engler et Diels the genus Anonidium has always been recognized as part of the Uvarieae, on account of the internal petals "slightly imbricate". Only R.E.Fries expresses some doubt as to the imbrication of these petals (l.c. 1931) and classifies the genus, in any case, within the Unoneae (l.c. 1959). The dissection of several young buds actually supports Fries' conception, because all the floral parts of Anonidium are valvate by cycles of three. In a general classification of the family, based as heretofore on the prefloration of the floral parts, the genus Anonidium should therefore be attached to the Unoneae with syncarpous fruits such as Ararocarpus (Java), Raimondia, Rollinia (South America), Annona (America, Africa) which constitute for Hutchinson the subtribe Annonineae, and for Sinclair the tribe Annonineae.

In the description of the species of this genus, the length of the pedicel is measured between the base of the calyx and the first lower branching (carrier axis or borne axis); thus the lengths are not morphologically comparable from one species to another, depending on whether the lower bracteole (or prophyll) is floriferous or not.

Genus of tropical Africa comprising 4 species of dense humid forest.

Tree 10 to 30 m in height, 40–80 cm diameter; bole short, slightly twisted and fluted; bark dark green, smooth, thick (2.5 cm), and spongy; wood yellow oxidizing very rapidly, becoming wine-red in surface color, fibrous, aromatic. Crown dense with spreading branches. Branchlets glabrous, ramilli puberulent to glabrous. Leaves with petiole 3–7 mm long, thick, puberulent to glabrous; limb papyraceous to subcoriaceous, elliptic-oblong to obovate-oblong, 20–45 cm long, 7–18 cm wide, attenuate towards the rounded and subcordate base, rounded and abruptly acuminate at apex, or attenuate-acuminate, the acumen 2–3 cm long; lower face puberulent when juvenile, becoming glabrous. Midvein impressed above, very prominent below; lateral veins 10–20 pairs, prominent on the lower face.

Inflorescences arising on the trunk or on leafless branchlets, in elongate unipparous cymes, sometimes reaching 3–4 m in length, puberulent to ferruginous pubescent. Bracts coriaceous, sessile, orbicular, amplexicaul, 7–12 mm long, 7–10 mm wide, rusty pubescent. Flowers with thick pedicel, 1–7 cm long, furnished in its lower half with a simple or bilobed, embracing, orbicular bracteole, 12–25 mm diameter, tomentellose on the exterior. Sepals broadly triangular-ovate, 1.5–2 cm long, 1.7–2.2 cm wide, tomentellose on the exterior. Petals thick, coriaceous, yellowish-green, broadly elliptic, ovate, or obovate, the external ones 2.5–5 cm long, 1.5–4 cm wide, the internal ones sub-equal and narrower, 1.3–2 cm wide, tomentellose on the exterior. ♂ Flowers with conical receptacle, entirely covered by the very numerous oblong stamens, 4–4.5 mm long, sessile, linear extrorse thecae, connectives enlarged, truncate above the anthers and puberulent. ♀ Flowers with conical receptacle bearing at the base 4 rows of stamens 2–2.5 mm long, connectives more dilated and thicker than in ♂ flowers. Carpels very numerous, covering the rest of the receptacle, free, pressed against one another, oblong, 3–4 mm long, ovaries included in the receptacle, styles free, approximately 2 mm long, stigmas enlarged, ± capitate. Ovule 1 erect.

Fruits syncarpous, large, pendulous on the trunk, cylindric-ovoid, 25–50 cm long, 10–30 cm wide, broadly reticulate-areolate, reaching 10 kilos. Very numerous oblong-subreniform seeds, 3–5 cm long, 1.7–3 cm wide, flattened, immersed in abundant pulp.

Species of dense humid forest and forest galleries or preforest savannas on clayey soil with humus, widespread from Nigeria to the Central African Republic and Congo-Kinshasa.

The very large fruit often falls due to wind action, it is then immediately attacked by animals, the pulp being edible. The bark is used in maceration in African medicine.

Very widespread in dense humid forest in the region of the Gabonese, Congolese and Cabinda Mayombe, as well as in the Crystal Mountains and the south of former British Cameroon.

Bull. Soc.Bot. Fr.94 : 258 (1947), pro parte, excl. Le Testu 9184.

Petals coriaceous, subequal, lanceolate, acute, 4–5 cm long, 0.9–1.5 cm wide, appressed pubescent on both faces. ♂ Flowers with conical receptacle covered with very numerous stamens, approximately 5 mm long, oblong, subsessile, linear thecae, connectives dilated above the anthers and puberulent. ♀ Flowers with conical receptacle bearing at the base two or three rows of stamens shorter than in the ♂ flower, approximately 3 mm long. Carpels very numerous covering the entire rest of the receptacle, free, closely pressed against one another, oblong, 3–4 mm long, ovaries included in the receptacle.

Fruits unknown.

This forest species, known only from Gabon, is close to A. Mannii var. Brieyi by the form of its lanceolate petals, but differs from it by the form of the bracts, the form and position of the bracteole.

Among the syntypes cited by Pellegrin following the diagnosis, we have noted the number 9184 of Le Testu. Cited probably by the author owing to remarks by the collector who compared this specimen to the other syntypes, it is in reality a very characteristic specimen of A. Mannii, which cannot be included in the description of A. floribundum.
