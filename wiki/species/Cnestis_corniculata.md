Ordinarily a small-sized liane, but sometimes reaching 20 m in length. Branches cylindrical, young branches often somewhat angular with brown or yellow pubescence, or ± glabrous.

Leaves 2-13(-18)-jugate; petiole 0.2-10(-18) cm long, rachis 5-40 cm, with grey, brown or yellow pubescence, subsequently ± glabrous, except often at the base of the petiole. Leaflets (rigidly) papyraceous, the lateral ones opposite or not, ovate to narrowly oblong, (0.5)-1-15 (-30) × 0.5-7.5(-13) cm, with rounded or (sub)cordate base, generally unequal, the terminal leaflet (narrowly) ovate or obovate to (narrowly) elliptic, 4-15(-30) × 1.5-7.5(-15) cm, rounded or slightly cuneate at the base ; all leaflets acuminate or sometimes obtuse or rounded, glabrous or ± pubescent above, glabrous to densely pubescent below ; midvein impressed above, prominent below ; lateral veins 5-15(-20) pairs ; tertiary veins reticulate, ± distinct ; petiolules 0-5 mm long, pilose or rarely glabrous.

Inflorescences, racemes or rarely panicles, 1-20 per axil, on the stem, young or old branches, 1-15(-25) cm long, 5-40-flowered, with dense brown-yellowish hairs, rarely also with brown-reddish glandular hairs. Bracts ovate to subulate, 1-2 mm long, curved. Pedicels articulate 0-2 mm below the calyx, with dense pubescence.

Flowers heterodistylous. Sepals generally clearly reflexed (sometimes not), in bud valvate or narrowly imbricate, ovate to subulate or (narrowly) oblong, (2-2).5-6(-8.5) × 0.5-1(-2.5) mm, acute, sometimes obtuse at the apex, with dense pilose hairs sometimes with glandular hairs on the external side, glabrous on the internal side, often with ≤ 5(-10) faintly visible veins. Petals in bud valvate or narrowly imbricate, (narrowly) oblong to elliptic, sometimes sublinear, 3-9.5 × 0.5-1(-2) mm, rounded to cuneate at the base, obtuse, rounded or retuse and often inflexed at the apex, glabrous, with ≤ 3(-7) distinct veins. Stamens ± united at the base, 0.5-5(-6) mm long, generally with clear difference in length between the two cycles ; anthers 0.2-0.3 × 0.1-0.2 mm. Pistil : 1-5 (but rarely 2-3) mm long ; ovaries ≈ 0.5 mm long, brown-golden pubescence ; styles most often straight, pubescent at base ; stigmas generally oblique, ± clearly 2-lobed.

Fruits: mature follicles 1-2(-4), (narrowly) ellipsoid, ± oblique, 2.5-4.5(-5) × 0.5-1.2 cm, cuneate, most often with a stipe 5-10 mm long, thin beak, straight or curved, 5-20(-30) mm long ; pericarp with very short red spiny hairs and long urticating hairs easily caducous on the external face while the internal face bears long slender hairs.

Seeds ovoid, 10-15(-17) × 5-10 mm, sarcotesta 5-8 mm long, ruminate.

Epigeal germination. - Pl. 13, p. 47.

## Type

Smeathman s.n., Sierra Leone

## Distribution

Tropical Africa from South Senegal to East Tanzania and, to the South, as far as North Angola ; common species in Gabon

## Ecology

Ombrophilous forests, semi-deciduous forests, thickets. The most frequent flowering in Gabon occurs between August and November

## Source note

C. corniculata is the species which displays the greatest variation within the genus. An overall consideration of this variation and the modalities of its distribution does not allow C. corniculata to be divided into distinct taxa. Despite the existence of various populations showing a certain homogeneity, the variation of characters proves to be in fact quite gradual and independent and does not allow for clear taxonomic divisions.

The most extreme forms present in Gabon can be summarized as follows:

- "C. congolana": large glabrous and few leaflets; long inflorescences; sepals broad approximately as long as the petals.

- "C. grisea": differs from the preceding by pubescent leaflets, narrower sepals shorter than the petals.

- "C. grandiflora": differs from the preceding by its small and numerous leaflets and flowers generally somewhat smaller.

- "C. aurantiaca": numerous, small and pubescent leaflets; petals approximately as long as the sepals.

- "C. iomalla": leaflets pubescent on the upper face; young branches and leaf rachis bearing numerous long and yellowish hairs; small flowers; petals somewhat longer than the sepals.

However, many specimens cannot be classified into any of these forms as they are intermediate between two or more of them: for example, the specimen Breteler, Lemmens & Nzabi 8154 which shows at once leaves with numerous (29) and small leaflets (1.5-6.5 × 1-2 cm), and others with few leaflets (11) and much larger (5-13 × 3-4.5 cm); thus, intra-individual variation can be considerable.

In conclusion, this extremely variable species is very distinct through the combination of characters: follicles with beak and urticating hairs and inflorescences in racemes.
