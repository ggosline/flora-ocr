## Description

**Habit**: shrub or small shrub 0.3–3(–8) m tall. Branches green and tortuous,
often spreading. Branchlets angular, 4-winged, striate and dotted with whitish
points.

**Leaves**: petiole glabrous, 0–2 mm long, canaliculate above, wrinkled
beneath. Blade glabrous on both faces, greenish dark brown above, brown-green
beneath, elliptic or lanceolate, 4.5–12 × 1.5–5 cm; base cuneate or rounded,
margins sometimes undulate, apex apiculate or acuminate, acute or narrowed.
Midrib concave above, prominent and striate beneath. Secondary nerves in 6–7
pairs, ascending, arcuate, anastomosing 2–6 mm from the margin, concave above,
prominent beneath; the two basal nerves sometimes more developed than the
others. Nervules forming a loose reticulum, visible beneath.

**Inflorescence**: a many-flowered axillary raceme. Rachis flattened,
4-angled; the flowers borne on the **narrow** faces of the rachis. Bracts oval.
Flower bud ovoid with valvate petals. Pedicel 1.5 mm long, striate.

**Flowers**: female, pentamerous, 4 × 1.3 mm, yellow, yellowish or
yellow-green. Calyx of sepals fused into a glabrous cup, the upper margin
slightly undulate. Petals glabrous on both faces, free from one another except
at the base, linear, 3.5 × 0.6 mm, weakly keeled on the median line, the apex
reflexed, acute. Stamens 3, alternipetalous, 2.5 mm long; filament broad,
flattened, fused to the lateral margins of the petals, 1.7 mm long; anthers
2-locular with longitudinal dehiscence slits, 0.75 mm long. Staminodes 5,
epipetalous, 3 mm long; filament ± fused to the petals, 2.25 mm long, each
terminated by 2 blades ± fused at the base, 0.75 mm long, the outer margin
undulate, the apex widened. Disc cupuliform, surrounding the base of the ovary.
Ovary glabrous, ovoid or conical, ribbed at the base, with 3 locules each
containing one ovule pendulous from an erect central placenta, 1 × 0.6 mm.
Style slender, 1.6 mm long, ending in a capitate lobed stigma.

**Fruit**: drupe globose, apiculate, red at maturity, **not** enclosed by the
fruiting calyx, which persists at the base of the fruit. Endocarp thin,
crustaceous.

**Seed**: globose, solitary. Embryo apical.

## Types

HOLOTYPE: *Heudelot 715*, Fouta Djalon, Guinea (P!).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Guinea to Yangambi in Zaire (Democratic Republic of the Congo)
and to Angola.

## Habitat and ecology

A shrub of dense humid forest, growing chiefly in damp places but also on terra
firma.

## Vernacular names

- *eko*, *ekobe* (Fang)

## Properties and uses

The wood is yellowish-white. The Fang use the bark of young plants to make
boxes for *Byéri* and granaries for storing groundnut and maize harvests. The
bark and seeds give off a strong smell of garlic and are used as condiments.

Villiers notes that the species varies greatly in leaf shape — which accounts
for the unusually long synonymy, including two names originally placed in
[[Ptychopetalum]].
