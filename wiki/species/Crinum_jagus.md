# *Crinum jagus* (Thompson) Dandy

**Family**: [[Amaryllidaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Crinum]]

Journ. Bot. 77: 664 (1939).

## Synonymy

- *Amaryllis jagus* Thompson, Bot. Displ.: tab. 6 (1798).
- *Crinum giganteum* Andrews, Bot. Rep. 3: tab. 169 (1801).
- *Crinum vanillodorum* Welwitsch ex Baker, Journ. Bot.: 196 (1878).
- *Crinum podophyllum* Baker, Curt. Bot. Mag. 106: tab. 6483 (1880).
- *Crinum laurentii* Th. Dur. & De Wild., Rev. Hort. Belg. 23: 97 (1897).

## Description

**Bulb**: globose to elongated, 3–8 (–14) cm in diameter, with a distinct neck 5–25 cm long, often with an elongated rhizomatous section (up to 20 cm), and often stoloniferous.

**Leaves**: present at the same time as flowers, in basal rosette, bright to dark green, of rather fine texture, more or less petiolate, embracing or not, lanceolate, of very variable length, 15–75 (–130) cm, 2.5–8 (–12) cm wide, all generally having an intact, acute apex, prominent midvein, and up to 30 discrete longitudinal nerves connected by oblique nerves; margin glabrous to slightly scabrous, often undulate.

**Inflorescence**: pale green or tinged with reddish floral scape, thick, 10–75 (–90) cm long. Solid, green bracts erect, subtending 1–7 (–15) sessile, pleasantly fragrant flowers. Perianth tube greenish, slightly recurved, 10–22 (–13) cm long; white segments, often green at the tip and with a greenish dorsal stripe, lanceolate, 5–10 cm long, 2–4.5 cm wide, the inner segments slightly wider than the outer ones, spreading during anthesis, with the apical part reflexed, forming a fairly open bell. White filaments bent in a zygomorphic manner, reaching about two-thirds the length of the segments. Black anthers, curved, 0.7–1 cm long. Style greenish at the base, exceeding the anthers.

**Fruit**: green or tinged with red, subglobose, 4–5 cm long, 3–4 cm wide, with a distinct beak 5–9 (–13) cm long. 

**Seeds**: greenish, of irregular shape, approximately 1 cm at their greatest diameter, 15–30 per fruit.

## Distribution

**Range**: West Africa from Guinea to Sudan and western Uganda in the east, and to Angola in the south.

**Gabon**: Adouma region (Ogooué drainage), Ndjolé area, Mékambo area, Libreville, Mondah, Djoua.

## Habitat and ecology

Distributed in fairly humid regions; especially frequent in riparian vegetation and able to tolerate flooding. Found also as a troublesome weed in maize, cocoa, oil palm, and banana plantations due to vegetative multiplication. Occurs from sea level to approximately 1900 m altitude, in various vegetation types: "littoral forest," "Biafran forest," "deciduous forest," "periforest savannas," and "Adamaoua savannas." In savannas, *C. jagus* is always limited to riparian vegetation.
