# *Utricularia spiralis* Sm. Planche 42, Figure 15

**Genus**: [[Utricularia]]
**Family**: [[Lentibulariaceae]]
**Protologue**: U. baumii Kamieński., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 102 (1902)

## Description

in Rees, Cycl. 37: No. 5 (1819). U. baumii Kamieński., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33: 102 (1902). Terrestrial herb; rhizoids and capillary stolons numerous at the base of the flowering scape. Leaves few in number, disappearing at anthesis, opposite on the stolons, linear, reaching 5 × 0.25 cm, 1- to 3-nerved. Utricles numerous, globose, shortly stipitate, 0.6–1 mm in length, glandular; basilar orifice, with upper lip bearing 2 simple, subulate appendices. Inflorescence erect or usually voluble, 5–50 cm in length, with 1 to 15 distant flowers; pedicel filiform, glabrous; bracts basifixed, broadly oval, ± 2 mm in length, at least 4 times as wide as the bracteoles. Flower: bracteoles very narrowly elliptic-oval, usually shorter than the bracts; pedicel erect or ascending, 3–6 mm at anthesis, reaching 15 mm at maturity, flattened or ± narrowly winged, always longer than the fruiting calyx; calyx with subequal lobes, oval to narrowly oval, the upper one acute, the lower one slightly 2-dentate at the apex; corolla 12–30 mm in length, violet to throat marked with a dark blue, greenish, yellowish or white spot, rarely entirely yellow or white, with upper lip longer and 2 to 3 times as wide as the upper calyx lobe, upper and lower lips orbicular, palate prominent, spur generally curved, acute. Fruit narrowly ovoid, dehiscent by an abaxial longitudinal slit, with non-thickened margins, with numerous seeds. Seed spherical, 0.2–0.3 mm in diameter; testa thin, with distinct, ± isdiametral cells.

## Distribution

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Chad, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, Tanzania, Zambia, Malawi and Angola; in Gabon, observed in the Estuaire, the Moyen-Ogooué, the Nyanga and the Ogooué-Maritime.

## Ecology

Seasonally flooded marshy savannas; in Gabon at 5–100 m altitude, in East Africa chiefly between 0 and 1860 m. B: Taylor (1964, 1972, 1989), Vandewe­ghe et al. (2016). Bibliography
