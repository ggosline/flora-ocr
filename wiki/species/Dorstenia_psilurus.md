Tall herbs (15-) 25-80 cm, with creeping, cylindrical rhizomes and sometimes tuberous, 5-7 cm in diameter. Stems erect or ascending, sometimes branched; base woody, leafy part with short, densely white uncinate hairs; internodes (± swollen) (0.5-) 1.5-4.5 cm long and 1.5 mm thick. Leaves spiral, sometimes tending to be distichous (especially toward the stem apex). Blade lanceolate to elliptic, widest in the middle, below or above, (4-) 9-15 (-20) × (2-) 3-7 (-8) cm, chartaceous; apex acute to acuminate, sometimes mucronate; base narrowly to broadly cuneate, rarely subcordate; margin entire to sinuate, denticulate or coarsely dentate to almost lobed with fairly large, obtuse or acute to mucronate, triangular teeth, 0.25-1 (-1.5) × 0.25-1.75 (-2) cm; upper surface glabrous or with small white, dome-shaped to conical hairs, sometimes also hairs reaching 0.8 mm, larger, yellowish-white (curved); lower surface with small white uncinate hairs, denser especially on the main veins, sometimes mixed with larger yellowish-white (curved) hairs; veins almost flat above, prominent below; 5-8 (-10) pairs of secondary veins. Petiole 1-4 cm long and 1-2 mm thick, with short white, ± uncinate hairs, sparse to dense. Stipules (sub)persistent, subulate, stiff, 0.5-2 (-4) mm long, 0.25 mm wide, with an acute apex, sparsely puberulent with short, white, ± uncinate hairs.

Inflorescences solitary or in pairs, erect, greenish. Peduncle (6-) 15-30 (-60) mm long and 0.15-0.75 mm in diameter with small dense white uncinate hairs (sometimes scales at the base of the peduncle). Receptacle elongate, narrowly navicular, 3-6 cm long, 2-5 mm high and c. 2.5 mm wide with small white uncinate hairs, extended at both ends by a long linear appendage; upper appendage directed upward, (2-) 3-6 cm long and 0.35-1.5 mm in diameter; lower appendage directed downward, (1.2-) 3-10 (-12) mm long and 0.25-0.5 mm in diameter; both appendages covered with small white ± uncinate hairs. Male flowers numerous; perianth 1-, 2-, or 3-lobed; stamens 1, 2, or 3, with filaments c. 0.5 mm long and c. 0.12 mm thick, inflexed even at anthesis, with anther 0.15-0.35 × 0.1-0.25 mm and connective as wide as or wider than the anther sacs; pistillode sometimes present. Female flowers 5-10 (-15), most of them located along the midline of the receptacle; style 0.15-0.35 mm long and 0.07-0.13 mm thick; stigmas 2, linear to spatulate, 0.15-0.5 × 0.05-0.13 mm, sometimes with a 3-4-lobed apex.

Pyrene oval to elliptic, 2.5-4 × 2-4 mm, smooth or sometimes slightly tuberculate containing albumen remnants; cotyledons unequal, the outer fairly fine, enveloping a smaller, thicker one; radicle fairly long. – Plate 23, p. 79.

Ctenocladus mildbraedii ENGL., based on a specimen with an extremely branched and apparently abnormal inflorescence, belongs very probably to this species.

The type of D. tenuifolia has slightly tuberculate pyrenes. In other characters it appears similar to other D. psilurus specimens. The slightly tuberculate pyrene as well as other characters, such as those of the inflorescence, suggest that D. psilurus is a link between the group of non-succulent Dorstenia with smooth pyrenes and a group of East African species including D. soerensenii, D. brownii RENDLE and D. afromontana (cf. FRIIS, 1974), and the group of succulent species in which D. buchananii ENGL. (a species apparently very close to D. benguellensis WELW.) shows many resemblances to D. psilurus.

The species D. psilurus ranges from Nigeria to Uganda and Zambia. It is an understory species of humid forests, riverine forests and swamp forests, up to 800 m altitude.
