# *Antrocaryon klaineanum* Pierre Planche 1

**Genus**: [[Antrocaryon]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Mens. Soc. Linn. Paris., n.s. 2 : 24 (1898)

## Synonyms

- Spondias klaineana (Pierre) Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 36 : 215 (1905).

## Description

Tree reaching 50 m in height; trunk up to 150(–180) cm in diameter; exudate translucent, slightly yellowish. Leaves in tufts at the end of the branchlets; petiole and rachis together 20–40 cm, puberulent; leaflets 11 to 17, with channeled petiolule, 1–3(–5) mm, puberulent, with unequal, ± asymmetric limb, oblong-lanceolate, 6–16 × 2.5–4.5 cm, rounded to cuneate at the base, acuminate over 5–10 mm at the apex, papyraceous, with 10 to 14 pairs of lateral veins, glabrous above, puberulent on the veins to glabrous below. Inflorescence up to 30 × 13 cm, tomentellose; bracts and bracteoles caducous, lanceolate, 1–2 mm in length. Male flower: pedicel 1–3 mm, articulate at ⅓ from the base, tomentellose; calyx pubescent, with lobes spreading, ± 1 mm in length; petals spreading to reflexed, oblong-ovate, 1.5–2 × 1–1.2 mm; stamens 1.5 mm in length, with anthers 0.5 mm in length; disk 2 mm in diameter, lobulose-undulate, thick; pistillode minute. Female flower: pedicel, calyx and disk similar to those of the male flower; ovary subglobose, 1–2 mm in diameter, glabrous; styles terminal-lateral, up to 0.5 mm. Fruit yellow at maturity, flattened, 25–30(–40) mm in diameter, 15–20 mm in thickness, glabrous; stone 5- or 6-lobulose, 10–15 mm in thickness, 22–26 mm in diameter. Seeds 3 or 4 per stone, flattened and curved, 1 cm in length. Seedling with hypocotyl 7–8 cm, glabrous; cotyledons appressed, narrowly oblong, 1.5–2 cm in length, glabrous; epicotyl 2–4 cm; the two first leaves opposite, 3-foliolate, sparsely hairy; following leaves pinnate with 5 leaflets.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda) and very probably also in the Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from all provinces except Moyen-Ogooué

## Ecology

primary and secondary forest; in Gabon up to ± 400 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Uses: The fruit has an edible pulp, with slightly acidic taste. The reddish-white wood, sometimes exported, can be used for carpentry, construction and plywood. The bark powder is used against liver diseases
