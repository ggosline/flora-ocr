# *Ceratophyllum submersum* L.

**Family**: [[Ceratophyllaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Ceratophyllum]]

*Species plantarum*, ed. 2: 1409 (1763).

## Description

Aquatic herb to about 3 m long. Main stem to 2 mm in diameter, delicate and finely longitudinally furrowed. Leaves 7–11 per whorl; blade (2–)3 or 4(–5) times dichotomous, 2–4 cm long and 0.1–0.3 mm wide, light green; lower segments sometimes to 1 mm thick; marginal teeth few or absent on the two lower segments and few on the ultimate segments, 0.1–0.2 mm long.

Male inflorescences with 1–3 flowers per node, often several per branch; female inflorescences with one flower per node and few flowers. Male flower to 2.5(–3.5) mm in diameter; perianth broadly cup-shaped, lobes 0.5–1.3 × 0.2–0.4 mm with a glandular projection to 0.2 mm; stamens often fewer than 10; anther subsessile, 0.6–1 × 0.4–0.7 mm; pistillode about 0.6 mm. Female perianth persistent around the ovary, lobes (1.5–)1.8–2 × 0.1–0.3 mm with a glandular projection to 0.7 mm; ovary to 1 × 0.6 mm, smooth; style generally over 2 mm.

Fruit ovoid or ellipsoid, rarely obovoid, more or less laterally flattened and developing a marginal ridge or a variably developed, entire, crenate or spinulose membranous wing. Basal and apical spines present or absent and very variable in size. Surface rough, with numerous more or less prominent, small or elongated, papillose or warty appendages.

## Distribution

Cosmopolitan and known throughout Africa. It has not yet been reported from Gabon but is considered very likely to occur there. The treatment records two candidate taxa from Cameroon: subsp. *submersum* var. *submersum* and subsp. *muricatum* var. *echinatum*.

## Habitat and ecology

Fresh standing or fast-flowing water, near the surface or at depth, in lakes, rivers and marshes. It tolerates seasonal alkaline pools but not highly saline estuarine conditions; 3–1500 m altitude.

## Notes

The possible varieties are separated by the presence and dimensions of apical and basal fruit spines, the warts and papillae on the fruit surface, and the degree of lateral flattening. In contrast with *C. demersum*, the fruits are never wholly smooth.
