# *Campylospermum auriculatum* Biss.

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Blumea 58 : 2 (2013)

## Description

Shrub reaching 6 m in height; branches with pale brown bark. Stipules caducous, 3–5 mm long. Leaf: petiole 0–2 mm; lamina elliptic-obovate to oblanceolate, (9–)14–24(–31) × (4–)6–8(–11) cm, ratio 2–4(–5), base auriculate to deeply cordate, often amplexicaul, apex acute, coriaceous, margin serrate; primary vein plane above, prominent below, lateral veins in 16 to 19 pairs, tertiary venation very prominent on both surfaces. Inflorescence terminal or rarely axillary, branched, rather dense, main axis 12–15(–18) cm, flattened; racemes 1 to 6, 3–9(–11) cm; gemmate scales absent; bracts persistent, triangular, 3–5 mm long; cymules distant by 5–9 mm, 2- to 4(–6)-flowered. Flower: pedicel 5–7 mm, articulate 2–3 mm from base; sepals oval, 7–8 × 2–3 mm in flower, 9–10 × 3–4 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 7–12 × 3–4 mm, cuneate at base, rounded at apex; stamens: anthers 3–4 mm long; style approximately 4 mm. Fruit: receptacle approximately 5 mm in diameter; 1 to 3 well-developed drupels per receptacle, reniform, approximately 8 × 5 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni) and Gabon (Woleu-Ntem)

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forest, on brown silvery soil; at 475–600 m altitude. Taxonomic note: the lamina with base auriculate to deeply cordate is strongly reminiscent of C. schoenleinianum (Klotzsch) Farron, a species restricted to West tropical Africa (from Guinea to Ghana) which is distinguished by more numerous lateral veins (20 to 30 pairs), sepals of only 6–7 mm long in fruit and equal cotyledons. b: Bissiengou (2014), Bissiengou et al.
