D. adamii was previously recorded from Senegal to Liberia; the West African plants, which constitute the nominate subspecies, have short bracteoles (0.5–0.75 mm), relatively long petals (standard 7–7.5 mm) and leaves with 6 to 10 leaflets, with appressed hairs on the lower surface. Central African specimens are globally very similar, which prompts us to unite them in a single species; but they have longer bracteoles, somewhat shorter petals, and leaflets often less numerous. They represent two new taxa, subsp. erectipilis and subsp. gabonica, which differ from each other in the pubescence of the leaflets. D. adamii is very close to D. setifera and D. stenopetala (see these for differences) as well as to D. oblongifolia G.Don, whose range is limited to West Africa, and which differs from D. adamii by its slightly woody pods with hard walls and very faint reticulation, and its distinctly asymmetric leaflets. In D. oblongifolia the pubescence of the leaflets is always ± erect on the lower surface, as in D. adamii subsp. erectipilis, but unlike the two other subspecies. The type of D. adamii subsp. erectipilis was cited in the Flora of Equatorial Guinea (Velayos et al. 2010) under D. bakeri, a species well differentiated by its distinctly asymmetric leaflets with appressed short pubescence on both surfaces, its much broader bracteoles, and its glabrous pods. Several specimens related to D. adamii, but showing slight differences, have been collected in Gabon; additional collections are necessary to clarify their identity. These are: Breteler 6519 (BR, WAG) and Texier & Akouangou 133 (BRLU), both in fruit, from the Lastourville region, with almost concolorous glabrescent leaflets; Floret et al. 1897 (P, WAG) and Walters et al. 455 (BR, WAG), in fruit, from the middle Ogooué valley, with glabrous pods and leaflets with more prominent reticulation. ## Discussion

bracteoles persistent long after flowering, narrowly elliptic to lanceolate, 1–1.5 × 0.3–0.5 mm, pubescent; calyx green or brown, with fairly dense and ± appressed golden-brown hairs, tube 1.7–2 × 1.8–2.5 mm, teeth ± equal, valvate, triangular, acute, 0.5–1 mm long; corolla white, petals with claw equalling or exceeding the calyx, standard sharply bent very obliquely, with claw 1.5–2.3 mm and orbicular limb 3.2–4.5 × 3–4.5 mm, rounded at base and apex scarcely emarginate, wings ± equalling the keel, with claw 2–2.3 mm and ± rectangular limb 3.6–4.5 × 1.5–2 mm, smooth externally, with truncate or shortly auriculate base, keel distinctly curved, with claw 2–2.3 mm and limb 3–4.5 × 1.5–2 mm, with shortly auriculate base; stamens in 2 bundles of 5 (or exceptionally all fused), filaments 5.5–6 mm, fused at ⅔ or at ⅘ of their length, straight at the base and curved in their free part; ovary 2–2.7 × 0.5–0.6 mm, pubescent at least on the upper border, with 2 ovules, with straight stipe 2–3 mm; style linear, 1–1.5 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 2 mm and stipe 3–4 mm, papyraceous, flattened and ellipsoid, 4.7–7.2 × 1.5–2.6 cm, rounded at both ends, 1-seeded, slightly reticulate over the whole surface, with very dense appressed short hairs when young then fairly sparse at maturity. Seed ellipsoid and flattened, 10 × 6 mm.
