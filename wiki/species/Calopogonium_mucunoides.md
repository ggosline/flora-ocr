# *Calopogonium mucunoides* Desv. Planche 21

**Genus**: [[Calopogonium]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Sci. Nat., Bot. , sér. 2, 9 : 423 (1826)

## Description

Trailing or twining herb; stem rooting at the nodes, one to several meters in length, with spreading brown hairs. Stipules oval-attenuate, ± 5 mm in length, striate, hairy. Leaf: petiole 5–10(–17) cm; rachis (0.8–)1–2 cm; stipels linear-subulate, 3–4 mm; terminal leaflet with oval or oval-rhomboidal blade, 4–10 × 2–7 cm, base almost truncate, apex obtuse to acute, the lateral ones oblique. Inflorescence up to 10 cm in length, on a peduncle of 0.2–13(–17) cm and bearing fascicles of ± 6 well-separated flowers or sometimes composed of a subsessile fascicle, hairy-rusty; bracts and bracteoles lanceolate, 4–5 mm in length. Flower: calyx 6–8 mm in length, hairy, tube ± 3 mm in length, with subulate teeth of 3–4 mm; corolla blue to rose-lilac or sometimes white, standard 5–8 × 4–5 mm; ovary hairy. Pod linear, 2–4 cm × 4 mm, hairy-bristly, constricted and chambered between the (3–)5 to 8 seeds. Seed quadrangular, ± 4 × 3 × 2 mm, brown or pale brown.

## Distribution

now pantropical; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

open stations, oil palm plantations etc., roadsides, dry savanna; in Gabon, at 5–350 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m and cultivated up to 2000 m. Uses: One of the best cover plants in plantations; very widespread due to human introduction. Its use as fodder is preferable only in mixture with other grasses

## Vernacular names

pois bleu, pois violet (fr.) ; calopo, wild groundnut (angl.)

## Source note

Probably introduced to Central Africa after the Second World War, because it is not treated by Pellegrin (1949), nor in the Flora of the Belgian Congo. Bibliography: Botton & Hallé (1958), Chen & Aminah (1997), Lewis et al. (2005), Mackinder et al. (2001), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961)
