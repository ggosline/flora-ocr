# *Sherbournia streptocaulon* (K. Schum.) Hepper

**Genus**: [[Sherbournia]]
**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Protologue**: F. N. Hepper, Kew Bull. 16 : 459 (1963)

## Synonyms

- Randia streptocaulon K. Schum., Bot. Jahrb. 23 : 440 (1897). — Werham, J. Bot. 55 : 8 (1917).
- Amaralia streptocaulon (K. Schum.) Keay, Bull. J. B. Brux. 28 : 59 (1958).
- Randia amaraloides K. Schum. nomen (msc. Zenker 1017 et 2123, P!) non ex Hua (qui cita ce nom sans le valider et proposa au contraire la comb. ci-dessous); non ex Hutch. et Dalz. mixt.
- Sherbournia amaraloides (K. Schum.) Hua, Bull. Soc. Hist. Nat. Autun, 14:401 (= 15 de l'extrait) (1901) (Zenker 1017 BM! et 2123 BM!).

## Description

a. — var. streptocaulon (not from Gabon, described briefly for the record).

Stipules finely pubescent, elliptic, 5–10 mm long. Petiole 8–23 mm, pubescent. Blade grey olivaceous and entirely glabrous on both faces, rhombic, (8) 9–14 × (3) 4–6 cm, acute and attenuate at both extremities with a rostrate acumen. Midvein moderately pilose beneath with appressed hairs; (5) 6 pairs of secondary veins very sparsely pilose with appressed hairs or glabrescent beneath; margin clearly furnished with appressed hairs.

Inflorescence with (1) 2–4 flowers; pedicels 2–4 mm long; calycine cupule 6 × 6 mm with ochraceus silky pubescence. Corolla with tube 15–20 × 7 mm. Ovary longitudinally wrinkled when dry.

Fruit briefly elliptic, not ridged, exceeding 12 mm in diameter.

## Type

Staudt 12 (P!, isotype K!, BM!), Lolodorf, Cameroun 1896

## Discussion

The rare other collections are from Bipindi (Annet 166) and Bitye (Bates 1267 BM!).

The specimen Bates 976 (BM!) with leaves rounded at the base and inflorescences 7–12-flowered, appears different.

b. — var. situtunga N. Hallé, var. nov.

It differs from the typical variety in the reddish-brown elliptic leaves, 6–9 pairs of secondary nerves, inflorescences 5–9-flowered, and pedicels 3–10 mm long.

Liane with internodes quickly glabrescent. Stipules moderately pubescent, elliptic, 6–10 × 4 mm, quickly caducous. Petiole pubescent then glabrescent, 5–20 mm. Blade brownish-red and entirely glabrous on both faces, elliptic, 9–15 × 3.5–7 cm, not attenuate at the extremities; base subacute to obtuse; apex obtuse, rarely subacuminate. Midvein clothed with small appressed hairs on the lower face; 6–9 pairs of secondary veins moderately pilose with appressed hairs beneath; margin ± pilose.

Inflorescences pubescent with (3) 5–9 flowers; peduncle 2–3 mm; pedicels 3–10 mm; bracts embracing, ± obtuse, 3–4 mm long. Flowers pinkish-violet. Calycine cupule 7–8 mm long, 6–7 mm in diameter, with dense ochraceus pubescence on both faces, with 5-apiculate margin, sometimes broken by tears (from drying?). Corolla with tube 20–25 × 8–10 mm, reddish-ochraceus pubescent, with suborbicular apiculate lobes approximately 6 mm. Anthers 9 × 1.8 mm, inserted at the lower third, with the marginal lines of the connective pubescent (about thirty hairs or more per side); the apex of the anther is triangular; the base does not appear split. Style glabrous with column 17 mm, with spindle-shaped stigmatic club 7 × 2 mm. Disc with fleshy margin 2 mm in height, sinuously wrinkled. Calyx densely pubescent at the level of the ovary and appearing furrowed: in fact the ribs appear in section unrelated to the vascularization and are probably wrinkles due to drying; 2 locules. Fruit unknown.

Type of the variety: Le Testu 9285 (P), Oyem, Gabon (fl. 9-IX-1933).

"Liane-au-Guib."

OTHER MATERIAL STUDIED:
