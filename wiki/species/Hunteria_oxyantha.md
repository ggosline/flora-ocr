# *Hunteria oxyantha* Omino Planche 42 ; Figure 6(D, E)

**Genus**: [[Hunteria]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Wageningen Agric. Univ. Papers 96(1) : 112 (1996)

## Description

Wageningen Agric. Univ. Papers 96(1) : 112 (1996). Tree or shrub reaching 6 m tall, rarely climbing, branches slender, finely striated. Leaf: petiole 6–8 mm, blade elliptic to oblong, 2–4 times as long as wide, 3.7–12.8 × 1.3–4.6 cm, acute to obtuse at the base, acuminate at the apex; secondary veins straight, slightly prominent below, in 15 to 25 pairs, forming a submarginal vein and an angle of 75–80° with the median. Inflorescence terminal or axillary, of 15–25 × 8–12 cm, once branched, with 6 flowers, peduncle 10–15 mm, primary axes 2–10 mm. Flower: pedicel 6–8 mm, calyx with lobes 2–4 times as long as wide, 1–1.3 × 0.6–1.1 mm, with rows of colleters inside up to ¾ of their length; corolla whitish, red in bud, 15–15.5 mm long in the mature bud and with ovoid head, relatively small, 0.8–1 times the length of the bud, acute at the apex, with a pubescent band 1.5–2 mm wide inside just below the insertion of the stamens, with tube 9.5–12 mm long, widened at the level of the anthers, 1.3–1.5 mm, with lobes 2–3 times as long as the tube, 1.8–2.2 × 1.2–1.5 mm, stamens at the apex at 1 mm below the throat, inserted at 0.73 of the length of the tube, anther 1.8–2 mm long; pistil 7.3–8.5 mm long, at the apex halfway up the anthers, with 6 or 7 ovules in each carpel. Fruit: mericarps ellipsoid, 28–35 × 20–26 × 15–20 mm, rounded to acuminate at the apex, yellow to orange, smooth, with 3 to 7 seeds, with wall c. 1 mm thick; pulp slightly fibrous. Seed: oblong to ellipsoid, 22–26 × 16–19 × 12–15 mm, testa smooth, orange. Distribution: Gabon and the Republic of the Congo in Gabon, Haut-Ogooué, Nyanga, l'Ogooué-Ivindo, l'Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: under forest canopy in Gabon, up to 500 m altitude.

## Source note

In Omino's (1996) revision, the considerably larger size and the description of the trunk and wood appear to be based entirely on a single specimen geographically isolated from the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This specimen in fruit, Michelson 85, is today identified as H. umbellata. B: Beentje (2021), Omino (1996). BIBLIOGRAPHY
