# *Eriocaulon nadjae* S.M.Phillips

**Genus**: [[Eriocaulon]]
**Family**: [[Eriocaulaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 55 : 199 (2000)

## Description

Slender herb. Leaves numerous, in rosette, linear to almost filiform, the short ones acicular, up to 13 cm × 0.5–2 mm, distinctly septate, attenuate to a setaceous apex, soft, pale greyish-green. Inflorescence: up to about 15 scapes, erect or the immersed ones, filiform and soft, far exceeding the leaves, 5–40 cm × 0.3–0.7 mm, 5-sulcate, with sheath shorter than the longest leaves; capitulum subglobose, with a cratériform involucre, 2.5–5.5 mm in diameter, straw-coloured or dirty white with small black spots (corresponding to the glands of the petals and to the anthers), glabrous, bracts loosely erect; the involucral ones, oblong, 2–2.5 mm long, with obtuse apex, scarious, pale; the floral ones comparable to the involucral ones in colour and texture, about 2 mm long, concave, obtuse or the inner ones acute; receptacle glabrous. Flowers sessile, 1.6–2.5 mm long; ovary 2- or 3-merous. Male flowers: sepals 2, free, linear; petals 2 or 3, minute, surmounted by a black gland; stamens 4 or 6, with black anthers. Female flowers: sepals usually 2, keeled, generally broad and overlapping in the upper part, but sometimes narrower, keel with spongy wing, almost as broad as the hyaline sepal and with margin generally coarsely dentate, the apex generally broad, but cuspidate-acuminate on the narrow sepals, the median suppressed or rudimentary, rarely as long as the lateral ones, but narrower; petals 1–3, only the median often present and one or both lateral ones suppressed, linear, surmounted or not by a small black gland; ovary 2- or 3-merous, black. Seeds plump, ellipsoid, 0.35 mm long, medium brown, at first indistinctly striate, then becoming finely papillose with T-shaped hyaline projections.

## Distribution

Equatorial Guinea and Gabon, where it is known from Estuaire, Ngounié and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

coastal savannas on white sands, swampy soil or periodically flooded, with leaves immersed and capitula emergent; up to about 500 m altitude

Taxonomic notes: The plant is apparently short-lived, with a small tuft of roots and without a clearly thickened stem at the base. Nevertheless, several specimens show a flush of young green leaves inside the rosette of withered leaves, perhaps in response to a return of rains. The habit depends strongly on water level. On swampy soil, the plant develops small rosettes of acicular leaves. More frequently, the rosette is submerged and only the capitula emerge above the water surface. The leaves then elongate and are soft and the scapes elongate until reaching the water surface. The leaves are always fine and translucent, clearly septate, with a long setaceous apex. This species exhibits unusual floral variability. The flowers generally have a 3-merous ovary, even though the other floral parts are reduced or absent. In female flowers, there are always two well-developed sepals and the third generally absent, but it can also be present in a rudimentary state or rarely fully developed, and then linear. The broader median petal is always present, but one or two lateral petals are sometimes rudimentary or absent. Entirely 2- and 3-merous flowers are even present in the same capitulum.
