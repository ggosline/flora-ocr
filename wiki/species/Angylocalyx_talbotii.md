# *Angylocalyx talbotii* Baker f. ex Hutch. & Dalziel Planche 11.10–12

**Genus**: [[Angylocalyx]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. W. trop. Afr., éd. 1, 1 : 371 (1928)

## Description

Small tree or shrub. Leaf: petiole 1.5–7 cm; rachis 7–15 cm; leaflets 5 to 7, with petiolule 8–9 mm, limb oblong to elliptic, 12–23 × 5–9 cm, base cuneate or rounded, apex shortly to fairly acuminate, glabrous above, sparsely pubescent below on the nerves; lateral nerves prominent, in 3 to 6 pairs, alternating with finer nerves, finely reticulate. Inflorescence cauliflorous, ± 2 cm long, pubescent, with 8 to 16 flowers; peduncle 5–10 mm. Flower: pedicel ± 4 mm; receptacle (hypanthium) 3–6 mm long; calyx with cup-shaped portion distinct from receptacle and forming an angle of approximately 90°, 5–8 mm long, margin truncate and somewhat oblique, a minute notch on the standard; petals up to 2 cm long, yellow-orange, standard obovate, emarginate, reflexed, long-clawed, keel angular, as long as the wings; ovary stipitate, oblong-linear, ribbed, with grey pubescence. Pod winged when dry, slightly or distinctly moniliform, 8–15(–25) × 1.2–1.5 cm, orange or yellow, with dry pericarp at maturity, apex mucronate, with 5 to 7(–13) seeds.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon and Gabon: in Gabon, known from the Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

degraded forest, clearings; at 0–740 m altitude. b: Baker (1929), Povydysh et al. (2010), Yakovlev (1968). Bibliography
