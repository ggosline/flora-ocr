# *Heckeldora trifoliolata* J.J.de Wilde Planche 9

**Genus**: [[Heckeldora]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Blumea 52(1) : 193 (2007)

## Description

Blumea 52(1): 193 (2007). Shrub up to 1.50 m tall. Leaf: petiole 5.5–13.5 cm; rachis 3–7.5 cm; petiolules 0.2–0.5 cm, puberulent; leaflets 3, opposite to subopposite or alternate, ovate to obovate, 9–20 × 5–10 cm, with cuneate to subrounded base, with acuminate or sometimes acute or obtuse apex, glabrous above except the groove of the midvein with short and stiff indumentum, below with surface slightly scattered with minute brownish trichomes and prominent and puberulent midvein; lateral veins 3–5 pairs, arched, puberulent. Inflorescence axillary, solitary, paniculate, pendulous, narrow, sparsely branched, up to 33 cm long; lateral axes reaching 1 cm long. Functionally male flower: pedicel articulate, 1–4 mm, puberulent and scattered with small glandular trichomes; calyx cupuliform, 4-toothed, 1–1.5 × 2–3 mm, with indumentum like the pedicel; petals 4, narrowly rectangular to obovate, 7.5–8.5 × 2.5 mm, glabrous; staminal tube cylindric, 7–7.5 mm long, with margin weakly crenulate or subentire, glabrous, with 8 anthers not exceeding the level of the crenulations and 1 mm long; gynoecium with stipe 1–2.5 mm long, swollen in the middle; ovary ellipsoid to ovoid, 1.5–2.5 × 1–1.5 mm, glabrous; style 2–3 mm, stigma discoid. Functionally female flower unknown. Infructescence: probably only one per plant, short, 1–4 cm long, bearing 1–3 fruits. Fruit with stipe 0.5–1 cm, ovoid to obovoid or cylindric, often of irregular shape, 3.5 × 2.5 cm, with beak up to 1.5 cm long, yellowish, with dense and very short indumentum.

## Distribution

endemic to the coastal region of Gabon; known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga and Ogooué-Maritime. Wilde et al. 11594; B, G: van Valkenburg et al. 2715). Drawing by Hans de Vries, Naturalis Biodiversity Center, Leiden (©)

## Ecology

understory of dense humid forest, primary and secondary, on sand or on rocky outcrops; up to 350 m altitude. B: de Wilde (2007). Bibliography
