# *Vitex madiensis* Oliv. subsp. madiensis Planche 29 ; Figure 6(F, G)

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]
**Protologue**: Trans. Linn. Soc. London 29 : 134 (1875)

## Description

, nom. inval. Shrub, small shrub or small tree 0.5–8 m tall; branchlets densely pubescent, with fine hairs, partly crispate. Leaf: petiole 2–8 cm, pubescent, with fine hairs, partly crispate; leaflets 1 to 3, the median distinctly larger and more prominently petiolulate (petiolule 5–25 mm) than the lateral ones (petiolule 1–5 mm), with lamina (5–)8–12(–15) × (2.5–)4–9 cm, in the one-leaflet forms: lamina broadly ovate, obtuse-rounded at base, acute at apex, entire or undulate, in the three-leaflet forms, lamina of the median leaflet obovate-elliptic to obovate-spatulate, cuneate at base, obtuse-rounded to subtruncate at apex, coarsely crenate in the upper half, more rarely entire, coriaceous, upper surface generally lustrous, sparsely pubescent, with tomentose nerves, lower surface shortly pubescent mainly on the nerves or beige tomentose (one-leaflet forms), with fine crispate hairs; secondary nerves 5 to 12 on each side. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia, 5–20(–27) cm long, 3–15 cm for the peduncle, with 2 to 4 levels of branching; axes pubescent, with fine soft hairs, partly crispate. Flower: calyx campanulate, somewhat bilabiate, 2.5–3.5(–4) mm long at anthesis, densely pubescent-tomentose, with triangular teeth 0.5(–1) mm long, with obtuse apex; corolla 6–8 mm long, whitish outside, blue-purple inside, lobes margined with white; stamens barely exsert, filament generally bearded; ovary glabrous. Fruit ovoid, ± 25–30 × 15–20 mm, subtended by the spreading calyx.

## Distribution

a large part of tropical Africa with a dry season, from Senegal to Angola and Mozambique; in Gabon, seems to be present mainly in the south of the country: Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Maritime. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

savannas, shrubby and wooded savannas, thickets; at 0–1000 m altitude

## Vernacular names

disógu-di-bagwandji (bavarama, bapunu, bavungu, éshira) ; diyaga-di-bagwandji (balumbu) ; gépobwè (mitsogo) ; gépoko-poko (apindji)

## Source note

A remarkable morphotype has been collected in Gabon, with all unifoliolate leaves, with ovate lamina, entirely beige tomentose on the lower surface. This form, widespread in the Sudanian region, was formerly treated at the rank of species (V. simplicifolia Oliv.) (e.g.: Huber et al. 1963). Given the existence of intermediates with the three-leaflet forms, recent authors place the two taxa in synonymy (Sales 2005; Sosef et al. 2006; Verdcourt 1992). The value of this taxon warrants further study. Bibliography: Arbonnier (2019), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Huber et al. (1963), Lisowski (2009), Meerts (2020a, 2020b), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Sales (2005), Vandeweghe et al. (2016), Verdcourt (1992)
