# *Acioa chevalieri* De Wildeman

**Genus**: [[Acioa]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: BJBB 7:213 (1920)

## Synonyms

- Acioa icondere auct. non BaILL. : A. Chev., Veg. Ut. Afr. Tr. Fr. 9, For. Bois Gabon : 203, tab. 18 (1917).

## Description

A shrub of a few meters in height sometimes, most often a medium-sized tree, reaching 30-35 m total height of which 20 m below branches and 80-90 cm in diameter above very prominent buttresses. Bark dark gray, washed with reddish, very rugose, excessively thin (1-2 mm), peeling off in small scales. Sapwood yellow-reddish, little thick; heartwood dark red, with interlocking fibers, hard and very dense. Young twigs sparsely lenticellate, covered, as well as the stipules, the petioles and the lower surface of the blades with a pink-blond arachnoid tomentum then grayish-blond, persistent, but eventually caducous, in patches, on the glabrescent blades. Stipules lanceolate, 2 × 8 mm, sometimes somewhat falcate, midvein alone prominent. Petiole 3-8 mm, tomentellose then glabrescent. Blade elliptic, 3-8.5 × 6-18 cm, sometimes elliptic-oblong, sometimes slightly ovate; base rounded to subcordate, apex rounded with small acute acumen; midvein with trace of arachnoid tomentum above, tomentellose then glabrescent below; lateral veins 6-8(-10) pairs, generally somewhat arched and ascending toward the apex, at first with arachnoid tomentum then glabrescent below; blade rarely somewhat scabrous above, with arachnoid tomentum below as indicated above; 2 large circular glands at the base of the blade below near the midvein and sometimes a few glands higher up; 4-5 circular glands below the apex of the acumen and sometimes a few small glands lower down below the apex of the blade.

Inflorescences cymose of ± dichotomous type, terminal and axillary subternminal, reaching 8-12 cm in height, ± hemispherical with peduncle of 1-2 cm and lower ramifications of 8 cm. Peduncle, axes, bracts, bracteoles and receptacles tomentose. Bracts on the peduncle slightly tricuspid; bracts on the primary axis broadly ovate pointed, 4-5 mm, sparsely pubescent within; axes 1-2-3 times branched with alternate bracts, of type progressively intermediate between primary bracts and bracteoles; portion of pedicels below the articulation ± 10 mm (20 mm for flowers at the apex of the inflorescence) with alternate bracteoles broadly ovate, about 3 mm, sparsely pubescent within, furnished on their margin with 7-9 stipitate glands reaching about 0.5 mm in height; articulate terminal portion of pedicel shorter, equal to or longer than the upper bracteole. Pedicel of the disarticulate flower (2)-4-6 mm. Receptacle (6)-8(-12) mm, tubulate or narrowly infundibuliform; sepals 4-5 mm, tomentellose externally, furnished, on the margins exposed externally in the bud, with 7-9 stipitate glands reaching about 0.5 mm in height, the inner border of the sepal being then glabrescent; petals obovate with cuneate or rounded base, 3-4 mm in height, yellow (?); androecium 30 mm, 20-25 filaments.

Infructescences formed most often of the peduncle and the primary axis thickened, furnished with its reflexed bracts, the latter bearing a few ± zigzag branches reaching 10 cm in length; properly speaking fertile peduncle 10-15 mm. Drupe very shortly stipitate, subovoïd-conical compressed with blunt apex, 20 × 25 × 40 mm (in sicco), with irregularly but densely colliculate surface, of golden yellow-brown tint, tomentose. — PL. 4, p. 21.

## Discussion

TAXONOMIC NOTES:

Confusions, at first sight, between Acioa chevalieri De Wild. and A. pierrei De Wild. seem possible; the second species presents ovate stipules, a blade glabrous below, bracteoles and sepals without marginal stipitate glands.

PHYTOGEOGRAPHIC NOTES:

Acioa chevalieri De Wild. is essentially ripicole and known only from the shores of the lakes of Bas-Ogooué in Gabon.
