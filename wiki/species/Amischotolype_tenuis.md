# *Amischotolype tenuis* (C.B.Clarke) R.S.Rao

**Genus**: [[Amischotolype]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Maharashtra Vidnyan Mandir Patrika 6(2) : 53 (1971)

## Synonyms

- Buforrestia tenuis C.B.Clarke in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phan. 3 : 233, t. 234 (1881).
- Forrestia tenuis (C.B.Clarke) Benth., Gen. pl. 3 : 851 (1883).
- Forrestia preussii K.Schum., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 24(3) : 344 (1897).
- Forrestia africana K.Schum. ex C.B.Clarke, Fl. trop. Afr. 8 : 77 (1901).
- Forrestia lescrauwaetii De Wild., Ann. Mus. Congo Belge, Bot. sér. 5, 3(1) : 53 (1909).

## Description

Herb decumbent or ascending, 0.6–1 m tall, basal part rhizomatous and rooting at the nodes, internodes straight, 1.5–4(–6.5) cm, longer toward the base, shorter toward the apex. Leaves: ± 4 leaves clustered at the stem apex; sheath reaching 20 mm long, glabrous or slightly hairy, with a few hairs on the margin and suture; pseudpetiole 1–2 cm, with 2 lines of short hairs; blade widely elliptic to elliptic, 6–12 × 4–5 cm, base cuneate to obtuse, apex shortly acuminate, upper surface smooth and glabrous, dark green, lower surface with a line of hairs toward the margin, pale greenish-violaceous. Inflorescence composed of 2 (or 3) cincinni coiled at first, finally almost straight, ± 4–5 cm long, with ± 24 rather closely crowded flowers per cincinnus, rarely few-flowered toward the plant apex; peduncle reaching 3 mm; floral bracts ovate, 8–9 × 3–3.5 mm, sometimes with a few hairs on the basal margin, green or purple. Flower: sepals oblong elliptic-ovate, weakly keeled, quasi conduplicate, 5–5.5 × 2 mm, with truncate apex, cucullate with a few hairs, green; petals oblong elliptic-ovate, 5 × 1.5 mm, glabrous, white; stamens with filament longer than the petals, 7–8 mm, sinuous, anther with oblong thecae, pale yellow; ovary obovoid, 1 mm long; style ± 4–5 mm, stigma slightly 3-lobed. Ripe fruit exposing 3 to 6 seeds. Ripe seed not seen.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Río Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, in Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

humid understory; in Gabon from 85 to 750 m altitude, elsewhere from 40 to 910 m

## Vernacular names

ako-nkok (fang)

## Source note

In the past, several African species were described—most in the genera Forrestia and Buforrestia—but they have all been placed in synonymy with A. tenuis (C.B.Clarke) R.S.Rao. Clarke (1901) mentions in particular a subglobose inflorescence and a stem with square section for F. tenuis, whereas F. africana would present a stem with round section and a linear-oblong inflorescence. It was subsequently proven that the form of the inflorescence depends on the stage of maturation, and that the section of the stem is a drying artifact. Nevertheless, even separated from A. scandens, A. tenuis still presents considerable morphological variability, and we think that this name probably covers several taxa. A thorough study, based on a larger number of specimens, ideally associated with photographs and material preserved in alcohol, would be necessary in order to resolve this complex. Bibliography: Brenan (1968b), Cabezas et al. (2014), Clarke (1901), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Sosef et al. (2006).
