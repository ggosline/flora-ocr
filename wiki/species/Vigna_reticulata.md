# *Vigna reticulata* Hook.f.

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]

## Description

in Hook., Niger Fl. : 310 (1849). Trailing to climbing herb, densely pubescent to hirsute. Stipules ovate-lanceolate, 5–15 × 2–4 mm, extended below the insertion point and cordate at the base, pubescent. Leaf: petiole 1.5–8.5 cm; rachis 5–25 mm; stipels ± 2 mm long; leaflets with petiolule 2–3 mm, blade oblong-lanceolate to ovate, 3–22 × 0.3–6 cm, truncate to rounded at the base, acute to rounded at the apex, pubescent on both surfaces; tertiary veins parallel and perpendicular to the main vein. Inflorescence: peduncle 4–25 cm, pubescent; rachis 0–2 mm, with only 1 or 2 nodes; bracts lanceolate, 8–12 × 1 mm. Flower: bracteoles 5–12 × 1.5 mm; calyx pubescent, with tube 3–6 mm long, lobes linear-lanceolate, 6–20 mm long; corolla 13–25 mm long, pink to purple or rarely white, standard 12–22 mm wide, with apex emarginate, bearing on the inner surface two parallel appendages, wings obovate, 12–23 mm long, keel curved, 12–20 mm long, practically without a beak; ovary with 8 to 14 ovules; style extended beyond the stigma by a short beak. Pod erect to spreading, 4–7 cm × 3–4.5 mm, pubescent, terminated by a curved beak. Seed 2.5–5 × 2–3.5 mm; hilum and strophiole eccentric, 2–3 mm long.

## Distribution

from Senegal to Ethiopia, to the South, as far as Angola, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Mozambique, also in Madagascar; in Gabon, known only from the Nyanga

## Ecology

wooded savanas, sandy and rocky places, humid soils, ruderal stations, fallows; in Gabon at 160 m altitude, elsewhere up to 2500 m. Flowering in April

## Vernacular names

haricot sauvage (fr) ; wild vigna (angl.)

## Source note

Not cited by the Flora of Equatorial Guinea (Velayos et al. 2010), nor by Pellegrin (1949). Not to be confused with V. vexillata which also has long calyx lobes. The tertiary veins parallel and perpendicular to the main vein are very characteristic of V. reticulata; the leaflets are sometimes variegated with white. B: Burkill (1995), Hepper (1958), Pasquet (2001), Verdcourt (1971c), Wilczek (1954). Bibliography
