# *Octoknema chailluensis* Malécot & Gosline

**Family**: [[Octoknemaceae]] *sensu lato* · **Current placement**: Erythropalaceae · **Genus**: [[Octoknema]]

Kew Bull. **66**: 388 (2011). Species 6 of 14 in the monograph.

IPNI: `urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:77114308-1`

Described as new in 2011 from the **Massif du Chaillu**, spanning the
Gabon–Congo border.

## Diagnosis

*Latin diagnosis*: similar to *O. aruwimiensis* but differing in its shrubby
habit (not a medium-sized tree); petioles with an apical pulvinus (not lacking
one); leaves caudate at the apex (not gradually acuminate); male inflorescences
sparsely (not densely) pubescent, with **trimerous** (not pentamerous) flowers
bearing distinct calyx lobes (not at most tufts of hairs); and petals nearly
glabrous, drying clear pink (not pubescent and dull brown).

With [[Octoknema_ogoouensis|O. ogoouensis]] it shares petals that are rather
thin and translucent, concave, and barely opening at anthesis — the character
pair separating these two from the remaining species, whose petals are patent
at anthesis with thread-like hairs arising from the centre of the inner face in
male flowers.

## Description

**Habit**: shrub or small tree to 4 m tall; bark grey. Young twigs dark brown,
pubescent. Hairs stellate, 0.25 × 0.1 mm, semi-erect, **pink**; semidendritic
hairs absent.

**Leaves**: obovate, 10–18 × 5–7 cm; apex **caudate**, the acumen spathulate,
to 3 × 0.5 cm; base cuneate to decurrent. Lamina glossy very dark green above,
olive green below, glabrous except at the base. Nerves contrasting **red**
below, sparsely pubescent below. Secondary nerves in 6–7 pairs, ascending at
30° from the midrib, anastomosing c. 5 mm from the margin, glabrous. Tertiary
nerves apparent on both faces.

**Petiole**: 0.8–3 cm long, canaliculate, with a distinct apical pulvinus,
sparsely pubescent.

**Flowers**: **trimerous** — unique in the genus as treated here.

**Male inflorescence**: 2.5–6 cm long, densely to sparsely pubescent; bracts
ovate, 0.25 × 0.2 mm; flowers towards the end of the rachis in groups of 1–3,
c. 3 mm apart.

**Male flowers**: buds 1–1.5 mm diam.; pedicel 2–3 × 0.4 mm, sparsely
pubescent. Flowers globular, c. 1.5 mm diam., possibly never opening fully.
Sepals 3, oval, c. 0.4 mm diam., pubescent. Petals 3, circular, c. 1.2 mm
diam., glabrous or sparsely pubescent outside, glabrous within. Stamens 3;
anthers c. 1 mm wide; filament strongly flattened, 1 × 0.8 mm; receptacle
cushion-like. Pistillode minimal.

**Female inflorescence**: 1–1.5 cm long, densely to sparsely pubescent, with
4–12 flowers; bracts 0.25 mm diam.; peduncle 3–5 mm long.

**Female flowers**: 2.5–3 × 2 mm. Sepals ovate, 0.8 mm diam. Petals 3,
triangular, 2.5 × 2 mm, glabrous or sparsely pubescent outside, glabrous
within. Staminodes lanceolate, 1 mm long. Stigmas 0.5 mm long, laciniate.

**Fruit**: infructescence to 3 cm long with 1–5 fruits, each sessile on the
rachis. Fruit ellipsoid, 1.5–2 × 1.2–1.5 cm, the stipe 2 × 1 mm; orange when
ripe, drying reddish-brown; glabrous or with a few scattered hairs.

## Types

**Holotype**: Gabon, Massif du Chaillu, near Guévédé village, about 40 km N of
Lébamba, 30 Nov. 1983, *Louis A.M., Breteler F.J. & de Bruijn J. 1044* (WAG);
**isotypes** B, K, MO, P, WAG.

## Distribution

The **Massif du Chaillu**, in Gabon (Ngounié, near Guévédé) and in the Republic
of the Congo (Lékoumou). Map 4 in the monograph.

## Habitat and ecology

Primary and secondary forest; 500–600 m elevation.

## Conservation

Not formally assessed to a category in the extract read. The authors note the
area is very under-collected and further localities are expected, but that the
species is far from common: six localities are known, in areas subject to
forest clearance and a possible open-cast iron mine.

## Notes

The **trimerous** flowers are the striking feature: every other *Octoknema* in
the monograph is tetra- or pentamerous. Taken with the pink stellate hairs, the
red-contrasting venation and the caudate leaf apex, the species is
straightforward to recognise even sterile.

The two Congolese gatherings are both from 2009, well after the Gabonese
material, and extend the species across the Massif du Chaillu into the
Ogooué-Léketi area.
