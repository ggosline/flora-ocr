# *Solanum giganteum* Jacq. Planche 40

**Genus**: [[Solanum]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Collectanea 4 : 125 (1791)

## Description

Shrub or small tree, up to 4 m tall, prickly; branches erect to spreading, densely pubescent, with multangulate stipitate stellate hairs, armed with straight or sometimes curved prickles. Leaf: petiole 1.5–5.5 cm, sparsely to densely pubescent, unarmed; blade elliptic, 12–40 × 3–13 cm, cuneate at base, acute to acuminate at apex, margin entire, unarmed, strongly discolorous, glabrescent above, densely pubescent with multangulate stipitate stellate hairs and whitish-grey pubescent below. Inflorescence lateral or terminal, 5–13(–20) cm long, branched 1 or 2 times, with 30 to 150 bisexual flowers; peduncle 2–6 cm; axes sparsely to densely pubescent, unarmed. Flower 4- or 5-merous; pedicel 0.4–1.5 cm, moderately to densely pubescent, unarmed; calyx 2.5–7 mm long, moderately to densely pubescent, unarmed, with triangular lobes, 0.5–2.5(–4) mm long, obtuse to acute at apex; corolla stellate, 0.8–1.5 cm diameter, mauve, sometimes white, divided to three-quarters, with spreading, triangular lobes, 3.5–6 × 1.5–2.5 mm, moderately pubescent below; stamens equal, filament glabrous, anther 2.5–3 mm long; ovary glabrous; style 4.5–6.5 mm, glabrous. Fruit spherical, 6–8 mm diameter, bright red at maturity, with 15 to 30 seeds. Seed reniform, 2.3–2.9 × 1.5–2.5 mm, dull yellow to orange-brown.

## Distribution

Present in sub-Saharan Africa, but also in southern India and Sri Lanka; in Africa, in Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and northeastward to Ethiopia and southward to South Africa; in Gabon, a single collection from Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

mainly in open and disturbed habitats, forest margins, clearings, sometimes in rocky areas or savannas; in Gabon at ± 700 m altitude, elsewhere in Africa, generally between 1000 and 1700 m altitude, with some populations at sea level (South Africa). Use: The fruits are edible, but very bitter and are used to treat throat ulcers and to curdle milk. In East Africa, the leaves are used to clean and dress wounds.
