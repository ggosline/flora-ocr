# *Boehmeria nivea* (L.) Gaudich.

**Genus**: [[Boehmeria]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Voy. Uranie : 499 (1830)

## Description

Shrub or small tree, rarely robust herb, generally dioecious, more rarely monoecious, 1–4(–7) m tall; ultimate branches robust, 3–4 mm in diameter, with short and finely appressed hairs or fine hairs mixed with coarsely spreading hairs. Stipules linear-lanceolate, free or fused toward the base, 5–12 × 1–2 mm. Leaves alternate, not or scarcely unequal in size; petiole (3–)6–11 cm, pubescent; blade broadly subcircular or oval to elliptic, highly variable, 7–20 × 4–18 cm, base cordate, truncate or broadly rounded, apex abruptly attenuate into a long acumen, margin coarsely dentate, dentate-serrate or crenate, with 14 to 20(–25) teeth on each side, membranous or rather finely chartaceous, upper surface glabrous, with large cystoliths giving a rough texture, or also with scattered hairs, lower surface with or without brilliant white tomentum, highly variable in density; venation almost symmetrically triplinerved, the basal pair of lateral veins extending to the middle or upper two-thirds, upper veins (1–)2 or 3(–4) on each side, tertiary venation scalariform. Inflorescences: the female ones toward the apex of the stems, the male ones toward the base, some axes with male and female groups and then the male ones toward the base; glomerules rarely axillary, generally arranged on one side and along specialized slender branches and axes of 3–10 cm, a pair at each axil, the basal branches often partially dichotomous; bracts minute, broadly triangular, 0.5–1 × 0.3–0.5 mm; glomerules 2–4 mm in diameter, the male ones with up to 10 flowers, the female ones with 10 to 30 flowers. Flower sessile; bracteoles insignificant, up to 0.3 mm in length. Male flower pink, 4-merous; mature bud depressed-globose, 1.5 mm in diameter; tepals without dorsal appendage, with scattered spreading hairs. Female flower yellowish green, narrowly ovoid, 0.5–0.8 × ± 0.3 mm, with abundant stiff and hooked hairs; stigma often minute, 0.3–1 mm in length. Fruit: fructiferous perianth ovoid or ellipsoid, 1–1.5 × ± 0.6 mm, without beak, ± flattened, with a marginal crest, tapering into a pedicel-like zone 0.3 mm long, with long clearly spreading hairs.

## Distribution

Apparently indigenous to China, Indochina and Japan, but the natural range remains difficult to ascertain, as this species, cultivated for more than 3,000 years in China, is now widely cultivated and also naturalized in the Old World. Information on its presence in countries neighboring Gabon is unreliable, but it has been reported as cultivated in Cameroon. In Gabon, it is known from Estuaire, probably escaped from cultivation or naturalized, and has been reported from Ngounié.

## Ecology

Disturbed habitats; indigenous in evergreen forest, in particular along waterways. When cultivated, it readily naturalizes at forest margins, along roads and in other disturbed stations; altitudinal range unknown in Gabon, elsewhere at 70–1700 m. Use: The stem provides fibers for the manufacture of textiles, and sometimes for cords, twine, fishing lines, etc. It has been used since antiquity, in particular in Asia.
