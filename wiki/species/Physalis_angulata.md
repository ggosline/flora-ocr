# *Physalis angulata* L. Planche 32(10-16), Figure 7(A, B)

**Genus**: [[Physalis]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 1 : 183 (1753)

## Description

Annual herb, erect, up to 60(–100) cm tall; stems erect, glabrous to sparsely pubescent with simple hairs. Leaf: petiole 1.5–5 cm, glabrous; blade elliptic to ovate, 5–8.5 × 2–5.5 cm, cuneate and often oblique at base, acute at apex, with margin irregularly and often deeply dentate, rarely entire, concolorous, glabrous to sparsely pubescent with simple hairs, more densely pubescent on the nerves and beneath. Inflorescence with solitary flowers. Flower: pedicel erect, (4–)9–12 mm; calyx cupuliform, 3–4.5 mm long, weakly pilose, enlarged and persistent in fruit, with lobes narrowly triangular, 1–2.5 mm long, in fruit up to 6.5 mm; corolla campanulate, 0.5–1.2 cm in diameter, yellow, inner face ± strongly spotted with pale green or brown towards the centre, with lobes broadly triangular, 0.5–2 × 3 mm; stamens glabrous, filament 2.5–3.5 mm, anther 1–2 mm long, purple or yellow; style 4–5 mm. Fruit spherical, distinctly angular, 0.7–1 cm in diameter, smooth, pale green to yellow at maturity, enclosed in the enlarged calyx 2–3 cm long, glabrescent, pale green. Seed ovoid, 1–2 × 1–1.5 mm, yellow-orange.

## Distribution

native to tropical America, naturalized throughout tropical regions and several temperate zones, present in almost all of Africa; in Gabon, collected in the Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

common weed in dumps, wetlands, marsh margins or in cultivations; in Gabon, from sea level to 800 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m. Uses: In tropical Africa, the fruits are consumed and the leaves are prepared as salad. They are analgesic and used to treat skin problems and muscular pain (itching, smallpox, whitlow, rheumatism), alleviate certain symptoms (fever, stomach ache, vomiting and diarrhea) and treat infectious and parasitic diseases (malaria, sleeping sickness, Guinea worm disease)

## Vernacular names

coqueret, coqueret anguleux (fr.) ; angular winter cherry, balloon cherry, cut-leaved ground cherry, gooseberry, hogweed (angl.) ; binyum’élóc (fang) ; bogo-bogo, mbowolo (nkomi) ; ebola (mpongwè) ; nganboukoumb (bapunu)

## Source note

Physalis has long been recognized as a particularly complex genus from a taxonomic and nomenclatural point of view (Pretz & Deanna 2020). The names P. angulata, P. lagascae Roem. & Schult., P. micrantha Link and P. minima have often been used interchangeably in herbaria and Floras; furthermore the delimitation of species remains still ambiguous. Pending a general revision of the genus Physalis, we follow here the latest taxonomic work in considering that the type materials of P. angulata and P. minima are conspecific (Waterfall 1967).
