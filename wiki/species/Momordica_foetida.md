# *Momordica foetida* Schumach.

**Genus**: [[Momordica]]
**Family**: [[Cucurbitaceae]]
**Protologue**: Beskr. guin. pl.: 426 (1827)

## Description

Plate 29; Figure 5(D-F)

Perennial herb, dioecious or sometimes monoecious, climbing; stem up to 5 m, pale green with dark green stripes, glabrous to ±white-tomentose, ±woody and up to 6 mm in diameter with age; roots tuberized, ±woody. Tendrils simple or 2-fid. Leaf: petiole 1–17 cm, glabrous; lamina broadly ovate-cordate to triangular-cordate in outline, 8–16 × 5–17 cm, entire, narrowly decurrent at the base, acute and apiculate at the apex, membranous, with margin subentire bearing minute teeth to distinctly sinuate-dentate, subglabrous above, subglabrous to grey tomentose below, especially on the nerves, strongly fetid when crushed. Inflorescence: the male in a fascicle or umbel of 1 to 9 flowers, with a peduncle 2–23 cm, subtended by a bract elliptic to obovate-spatulate or broadly ovate-cordate, 3–22 × 1–26 mm; the female often on different branches of the same individual, with a bract

4–16 mm long. Male flower: pedicel 0.5–6 cm, glabrous to densely tomentose; receptacle patelliform, 2–5 mm long; sepals ovate to triangular, 5–9 mm long, obtuse or rounded at the apex, convex, often with a few short soft spines in the lower part on the outside, shortly ciliate near the apex, brown to dark violet with green margins; petals obovate, 17–35 × 9–27 mm, caducous, 3 of them with incurved basal scales, orange on the inner surface, white, cream, yellow or orange, marked with dark green or brown or black at the base; stamens 3, of which 2 are bithecal and 1 is monothecal, anthers coherent, with reversed-S theca. Female flower: pedicel 1.4–13 cm; receptacle broad and shallow, 2–2.5 mm long; sepals ribbon-like triangular, 6–11 mm long, acute to obtuse or rounded or somewhat dilated and green at the apex, black at the base; petals 2–3.5 × 2–2.5 cm; ovary ovoid, 1–2.5 × 0.5–1 cm, densely papillose-spiny. Fruit ellipsoidal, 3.5–7 × 3–5 cm, fleshy, densely and slightly spiny, the spines fleshy, pointed, ±curved, approximately 1 cm long, orange at maturity, dehiscent into 3 valves, with numerous seeds in scarlet pulp. Seed obloid, compressed, 7–12 × 5–7.5 × 2.5–3 mm; testa brown, sculptured.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical and subtropical Africa, from Senegal to Ethiopia and south to South Africa; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ngounié and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

evergreen forest, clearings, riverine forest and lake shores, grasslands, on disturbed soils and abandoned cultivation; in Gabon up to 480 m altitude, elsewhere 0–2400 m. Flowering and fruiting throughout the year

Uses: The plant is considered aphrodisiac, emetic, emmenagogue and parturient. It is given to women in cases of difficult childbirth. Young leaves are consumed to treat stomach ailments and are also used in dropsy. The sap from the leaves is drunk to treat intestinal disorders, nosebleeds and severe headaches. Leaf decoction is used against smallpox; leaves are rubbed on parts affected by spitting cobra to prevent inflammation. Juice from crushed leaves is applied to treat earaches. The tuberized root is purgative and used against intestinal worms and constipation; it is also used to wash small children and their mother's breasts as a tonic against malaria. Leaves are rarely eaten cooked (bad taste and odour); the pulp of ripe fruits is sometimes consumed raw.

## Vernacular names

concombre sauvage (fr.); débumbulu, nkolé-mbè-mbosi (bakélé); dibumbulu (balumbu, bapunu, bavarama, bavungu, éshira, ngowé); éyélé-nzôm (fang); ibumbulu (benga, galoa, mpongwè, nkomi, orungu); libumbulu (bavili); modjungé (mitsogo); modjungé-djungé (ivéa); nyangé-lé-kodyè (béséki)
