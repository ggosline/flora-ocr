# *Mapania afro-orientalis* Lye Planche 108, Figure 14a

**Genus**: [[Mapania]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lidia 7 : 86 (2011)

## Description

, non Mapania testui Cherm. (1930). Robust herb, with a short rhizome; stems solitary, central, 50–200 cm × 2–7 mm, triangular, scabrous below the inflorescence, green to brownish. Leaves numerous, mostly basal with 1–3 cauline; sheath 6–12 × 2 cm, with the margin finer and pale brown to medium brown; blade flat or slightly folded, fairly thick, trinerved, the largest 50–150 × 1.5–3 cm, with scabrous margin at least near the apex gradually acute. Inflorescence in a terminal panicle, lax to fairly dense, 3–15 × 3–15 cm, light to dark brown, with 6–15 primary branches 0.5–5 cm long; involucral bracts foliaceous, linear, often spreading, the lower longer than the inflorescence; mature spikes obovoid to almost globose, 5–8 × 2.5–4 mm, brown, apex obtuse; spicoid bracts oblong to obovate, 2.5–3 × 1.5–1.8 mm, brown, glabrous, apex obtuse. Flower: floral bracts 2, oblong, 2–2.5 mm long, glabrous or with a few hairs, keel not winged, apex obtuse; style with 2 branches. Achene ellipsoid and slightly flattened at the base, 2–2.5 × 1–1.5 mm, sometimes with the lower and upper portions separated by a constriction, lower portion striated or longitudinally ribbed, pale to brownish, upper portion almost smooth, brownish.

## Distribution

Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Tanzania; rare in Gabon, found in Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem (5 collections)

## Ecology

humid forests, sometimes along rivers and watercourses; in Gabon, at 0–600 m altitude, up to 1300 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: M. afro-orientalis is easily identifiable only at full maturity. It is then recognized by its achene slightly constricted above the middle and not wrinkled in the upper portion. It is very close to M. purpurascens, but differs by its constricted achenes and perhaps by the absence of stolons. It is also close to M. chevalieri which has larger spikes (9–18 mm long versus 5–8 mm) and achenes entirely wrinkled and without constriction. Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Nelmes (1955a)
