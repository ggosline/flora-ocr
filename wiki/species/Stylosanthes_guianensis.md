# *Stylosanthes guianensis* (Aubl.) Sw.

**Genus**: [[Stylosanthes]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kongl. Svenska Vetensk. Acad. Handl. 10 : 301 (1789)

## Description

Suffrutex or perennial herb with often lignified base, up to 2 m tall; stems semi-erect to erect, well branched, almost glabrous to densely puberulent, often sticky. Stipules with sheath 2–15 mm long, glabrous to pilose or setulous, extended into teeth 2–10 mm long. Leaf: petiole 1–10 mm; rachis 0.5–1.5 mm; leaflets with petiolule of 1 mm, blade elliptic-ovate to narrowly elliptic-ovate, 5–45 × 2–20 mm, up to eight times as long as wide, with cuneate base, acute to obtuse apex, glabrous to puberulent. Inflorescence loosely capitate, 1–1.5 cm long; primary bract 1–2.2 cm long, of which 3–7 mm for the sheath; secondary bracts 2, 2–5.5 mm long. Flower: bracteole 1, 2–3 mm long; calyx with tube 4–8 mm long, lobes 3–5 mm long; standard 4–8 × 3–5 mm, yellow to orange, often streaked with black, wings and keel 3.5–5 mm long. Pod generally with 1 ovoid article, 2–3 × 1.5–2.5 mm, glabrous or with very short pubescence, venation not prominent; beak 0.1–0.8 mm, strongly curved. Seed pale brown to purple.

## Distribution

native from Mexico to Argentina and disseminated by man throughout all tropics; in Gabon, known from Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

pastures and savannas, roadsides, riverbanks; in Gabon at 275–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1600 m. Flowers and fruits in October, December to February and April. Use: The plant provides good fodder and green manure. It is also used as a cover plant

## Vernacular names

luzerne du Brésil, luzerne tropicale (fr.) ; stylo, Brazilian lucerne (angl.)

## Source note

Not reported in Check-list of vascular plants of Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006). This is a highly variable species in which 4 to 6 varieties have been distinguished (respectively Ferreira & Costa Sousa 1979 and 't Mannetje 1977). However, this variation is continuous and discussion on the utility of this subdivision continues (Gillies & Abott 1996, Santos Garcia et al. 2012).
