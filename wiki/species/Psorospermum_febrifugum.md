Subshrub to small deciduous tree, up to 5(–10) m tall; bark subereous, longitudinally fissured; branchlets tomentose to glabrous; indumentum of simple or stellate hairs. Leaves opposite, subopposite or sometimes alternate (on shoots); petiole 0–2 (–4) mm, pubescent to glabrous; blade elliptic to ovate, obovate or suborbicular, 2–14 × 1–9 cm, cuneate to subcordate at base, apex rounded to weakly acuminate, coriaceous, upper surface glabrous to glabrescent and glossy, lower surface tomentose to glabrous and with scattered black glandular punctations; lateral nerves 4–7 pairs, tertiary network dense and very prominent. Inflorescence terminal, corymbiform cymes, few- to many-flowered; peduncle 0.5–5 cm, tomentose to glabrous. Flower: pedicel 3–12 mm, tomentose to glabrous; sepals ovate, 3–4 × 1.5–2 mm, tomentose to glabrous externally; petals obovate, 4–6 × 2–3 mm, whitish with red or purple glandular lines; stamen fascicles with 5–6 stamens 3–4 mm long, pubescent on dorsal surface, anthers 0.3 mm in diameter; ovary ovoid, 3–4 mm long, glabrous; styles 2 mm long, stigmas capitate. Fruit globose, 4–10 mm in diameter, red. Seed ellipsoid, 4–5 mm long; cotyledons rolled.

## Distribution

Species widespread in tropical Africa, from Guinea to Ethiopia in the north and Angola and Mozambique in the south; in Gabon fairly common, recorded in the provinces of Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Nyanga, probably present also elsewhere.

## Ecology

Open or wooded savanna, sometimes in forest margins or secondary formations, on sandy soil; in Gabon at 100–400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1950 m. Use: The bark and root have been used against several skin diseases, from itching to wounds, ulcers and leprosy.

## Vernacular names

ontcholè (Téké)

## Discussion

Taxonomic note: In the past, plants with tomentose indumentum on the lower leaf surface, peduncles, pedicels and sepals were recognized as a distinct variety: P. febrifugum var. ferrugineum (Hook.f.) Keay & Milne-Redh. As already noted by Pellegrin (1959), we have also observed that among different collections these structures range from glabrous to hairy by small transitions and that, for example, young leaves may be tomentose below, while older ones on the same branch are glabrous; sometimes floral buds are tomentose, whereas on the same collection, persistent sepals under the fruits are glabrous. This is why we have not recognized this variety. B: Bamps (1966, 1970), Milne-Redhead (1953), Pellegrin (1959), Robson (1961).
