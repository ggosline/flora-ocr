# *Isolona pilosa* Diels

**Genus**: [[Isolona]]
**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Protologue**: Engler,Bot.Jahrb.4I :328(1908)

## Synonyms

- Isolona theobromina Exell, Journ.Bot.64, Suppl. Polypet.:Io (1926).- Exell et Mendonga,Consp.Fl. Angol. I,I :31 (1937).- Pellegrin, Bull.Soc.Bot.Fr. 94 : 387 (1947).- Paiva,Mem. Soc.Brot. I9 : 117 (1966),syn. nov.

## Description

Shrub with young branches densely hirsute, brown. Leaves with petiole 4–6 mm long, hirsute; blade papyraceous, oblong to obovate-oblong, 9–24 cm long, 4–9 cm wide, rounded at the base, long-acuminate at the apex, glabrous on the upper surface except on the midvein densely hirsute, sparsely to densely pubescent on the lower surface. Midvein prominent on both surfaces; lateral veins 16–20 pairs, obliquely ascending, united in arches at 5 mm from the margin, prominent on the lower surface; reticulum loose, scarcely visible above, prominent below.

Flowers solitary, axillary, with pedicel very short, 2–3 mm long, bearing at the base 4 lanceolate bracteoles, 2–6 mm long, 1–1.5 mm wide, densely pubescent on both surfaces. Sepals triangular-ovate, 3–5 mm long, 3–4 mm wide, united at the base, acute at the apex, pubescent outside and on the margins. Corolla yellow with tube 6–7 mm long; lobes oblong, triangular-acute at the apex, 11–13 mm long, 5–6 mm wide, pubescent outside and on the inner margins. Stamens 1.5 mm long, filaments very short, thecae linear extrorse, connectives dilated above the anthers and very slightly prolonged toward the center of the flower. Ovary ovoid, 2 mm long, stigmas lobulate.

Fruits (not seen), with short pedicel; syncarp ovoid-ellipsoid, 6–9 cm long, 3.5–6 cm wide, apiculate, longitudinally striate, pubescent. Seeds numerous, ovoid to ellipsoid, 14–20 mm long, 8–10 mm wide, glabrous, red.

## Type

Ledermann II,Lualaba,Congo-Kinshasa,fl. (holotype B!)

## Discussion

Species of humid dense forest at low altitude, widespread in Gabon, Congo-Brazzaville and Congo-Kinshasa and Angola (Cabinda).

The type specimen still existing in the Berlin herbarium, we were able to examine it and confirm that it was indeed the same species as *I. theobromina* which Exell had brought close to it invoking only differences of a quantitative nature in the length of the petals, stamens and carpels. *I. pilosa* is thus found in Congo-Kinshasa in the presence of another very closely related species: *I. congolana* (De Wild. et Th.Dur.) Engl. et Diels, from which it differs especially by its wider leaves, with midvein pubescent on the upper surface, the corolla lobes wider, long-pubescent outside and on the inner margins.
