# *Stenandriopsis thomensis* (Milne-Redhead) Heine, comb. nov.

**Genus**: [[Stenandriopsis]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]

## Description

≡ Crossandra thomensis Milne-Redhead, Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1935 : 280 (1935), (S. Thomé, Cameroon).

To date, eight Malagasy species and six species from tropical Africa (all cited here) of this genus are known, which is related to the genus Crossandra Salisb.; it differs from it by the calyces which have an entire posterior sepal (bifid in Crossandra), corollas white, pale lilac or violet (vermilion-red, orange, or yellow in Crossandra), the corolla limb spreading in a star shape, the lobes subequal and well-separated to the throat of the corolla, the two posterior lobes slightly smaller than the others, erect and slightly fused to each other at the base; the limb is unilabiate, with a long slit between the two posterior lobes; the five lobes are fused longitudinally between each other over a third of their length, and form a kind of lower lip in Crossandra; the inflorescences finally are absolutely glabrous in all their parts (pubescent in all species of the genus Crossandra). S. Moore, the author of the genus, clearly recognized the somewhat aberrant position of the Malagasy and African species described in the genus Crossandra and transferred in 1943 by R. Benoist to the genus Stenandriopsis; but S. Moore had not the slightest intention of attaching these species to his genus Stenandriopsis. A surprising fact in this regard is that the following discussion by S. Moore (Journ. Bot. 67 : 271, 1929) was not cited in the works of R. Benoist: "It is usual for the calyx of Crossandra to have a posticous calyx (sic! he doubtless means the posterior segment of the calyx) traversed by 2 nerves and ending in 2 teeth. Several exceptions to this are known; in these cases the segment is similar to the others and like them is entire. The plant under notice (namely Crossandra Gossweileri S. Moore = Stenandriopsis gabonensis (R. Benoist) Heine) makes the eighth exception to this rule, the others being Crossandra Boivini and longipes (Madagascar), Warneckii (E. Tropical Africa) pinguior (S. Central Africa), Buntingii, elatior, and Talbotii (W. Africa). It is most surprising that S. Moore does not cite here the Crossandra guineensis Nees, which J. D. Hooker had already noted (Bot. Mag. 104, t. 6346, 1878) with "sepals quite entire".

Previously, S. Moore after the descriptions of two new species Crossandra Warneckei and Cr. pinguior, had made the following remarks: "By an obvious oversight this (C. Warneckei; specimen Warneckei 230, from Amani) has been distributed as C. guineensis Nees. The small bracts are peculiar and remind one of the Madagascar genus Stenandriopsis, which, however, has different pollen, that of C. Warneckei being quite normal for Crossandra. Like the species just described (= Cr. pinguior S. Moore), this has an 1-nerved hinder segment of the calyx, a rare occurrence in the genus." (Journ. Bot. 51 : 215, 1913). Crossandra longipes S. Moore is a typical Crossandra; it was included in the note by S. Moore cited above doubtless because of an error. The posterior segment of the calyx, slightly bifid at the apex, is provided with 2 main nerves. We were unable to revise the type specimens of Crossandra Warneckei and pinguior S. Moore, species passed over in silence in the study by R. Benoist, and we refrain here

from a taxonomic judgment on them. Crossandra Boivini Lindau was transferred by R. Benoist to the genus Stenandriopsis (St. Boivini (Lind.) R. Benoist, Bull. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat., 2nd ser., 15 : 235, 1943); Cr. Buntingii S. Moore and Cr. Talbotii S. Moore are transferred in the present work to the same genus; Cr. elatior S. Moore belongs to the synonymy of Stenandriopsis guineensis (Nees) R. Benoist (q. v.).

1. Leaves acuminate with more or less rounded acumen, with 6–10 (13) pairs of lateral nerves; bracts entire or weakly toothed towards the apex, with rounded or acicular acumen, never emarginate :

2. Leaves long-acute at the base and acuminate at the apex, with approximately 10 (13) pairs of lateral nerves; bracts oval-oblong, entire, very obtuse or rounded, 15–21 × 7–11 mm.

2'. Leaves rounded, sometimes very weakly subcordate at the base, acuminate at the apex, with rounded acumen, with 5–7 pairs of lateral nerves; bracts lanceolate, entire or weakly denticulate towards the apex, very acuminate, with an acicular point, 13–19 × 2.5–4 mm..... 2. S. guineensis.

1'. Leaves rounded, without acumen, subcordate at the base, with 3–5 pairs of lateral nerves; bracts lanceolate, toothed in the upper part, emarginate at the apex..... 3. S. Talbotii.
