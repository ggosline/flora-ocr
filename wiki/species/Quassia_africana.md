# *Quassia africana* (Baill.) Baill.

**Family**: [[Simaroubaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Quassia]]

## Synonymy

- *Simaba africana* Baill. (1867)

## Description

Shrub or subshrub. Leaves glabrous, imparipinnate, with 2–4 pairs of leaflets. Rachis swollen at opposite leaflet insertions and slightly winged below them. Leaflets elliptic-oblong, unequal and cuneate at the base, caudate-acuminate, 10–15 × 4–6 cm; blade decurrent to the rachis so the leaflets appear subsessile, membranous, matt above and pale glossy green beneath; about nine pairs of secondary veins joined in arches.

Flowers white and fragrant, in terminal racemes sometimes shortly branched and shorter than the leaves. Pedicels 3–5 mm. Calyx with five ciliate lobes about 1 mm. Petals 5, narrowly oblong, contorted in bud, about 9 mm, glabrous except for the pubescent lower third inside. Stamens 10, alternipetalous five slightly longer; filaments 6.5–7 mm and the longest 8 mm, sometimes to 1.5 cm, with hairy basal ligules; anthers 2–2.5 mm. Disc cushion-shaped, lobed and pubescent. Ovary of five pubescent carpels; style glabrous, 7 mm. Fruits obovate-elliptic, about 2.5 cm, with two prominent ribs and slight reticulation.

## Distribution

An equatorial African understorey species from Cameroon through the central Congo Basin to Angola; present in Gabon.

## Habitat and ecology

The bitter root and leaves are infused as a reputed febrifuge.
