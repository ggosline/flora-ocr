# *Garcinia quadrifaria* (Oliv.) Pierre var. quadrifaria

**Genus**: [[Garcinia]]
**Family**: [[Clusiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. forest. Cochinch. 5 : 4 (1883)

## Description

Shrub or small dioecious tree, up to 25 m tall; trunk up to 20 cm in diameter; latex white; branchlets tetragonous to slightly winged; branching often 3–4-whorled. Leaves opposite or 3–4-whorled, glabrous; petiole 6–15 mm, deeply channelled above, transversely wrinkled, foveola 1–4 mm long; blade elliptic to broadly elliptic, sometimes narrowly elliptic, 6–19 × 2–9.5 cm, cuneate to rounded at base, generally shortly acuminate at apex, coriaceous, often slightly bullate; venation prominent on both surfaces, midvein acute beneath, lateral veins 5–10 pairs, black secretory canals (if visible) at first subparallel to the midvein, but toward the margin subparallel to the lateral veins. Inflorescence: solitary terminal raceme, up to 35 flowers; rachis tetragonous, 1.5–3 cm long; bracts about 1 × 1 mm, acute, glabrous, caducous. Flowers unisexual, pentamerous, glabrous; pedicel 6–15 mm; sepals subequal, suborbicular, 1–1.5 × 1–1.5 mm; petals suborbicular, 4–7 × 4–7 mm, white to pale yellowish-green. Male flower: interstaminal disc glands 1 × 1 mm, spongy; stamen bundles 3–4 mm long, filaments free at apex, anthers (3–)6–10 per bundle, ovoid. Female flower: reduced stamen bundles present; interstaminal disc glands 1.5 × 1.5 mm; ovary 2(–3)-locular, ellipsoid; style 0.5 mm, stigmas 2 or more rarely 3, flabellate. Fruit on pedicel 8–20 mm, broadly ellipsoid to transversely ellipsoid, 2–2.5 × 1.5–3 cm, verrucose, glabrous, yellowish to orange; sepals and interstaminal glands persistent. Seeds 1–3, broadly ellipsoid, 14–16 × 10–14 × 8–9 mm.

## Distribution

from Sierra Leone to Ghana and Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Republic of the Congo; fairly common in Gabon, especially in the coastal zone, recorded from Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Maritime, Ngounié, Woleu-Ntem, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Ivindo

Ivory Coast, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, fairly rare, known from Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

primary or secondary forest, thickets or forest edge, riparian forest, inselbergs; in Gabon, up to 830 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m.
