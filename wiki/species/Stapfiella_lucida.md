# *Stapfiella lucida* Robyns Planche 27

**Genus**: [[Stapfiella]]
**Family**: [[Turneraceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 28 : 320 (1978)

## Description

Shrub 0.4–3 m tall; young branches puberulent. Leaf without stipules; petiole 1–1.5(–2.5) cm; blade elliptic, 4–17 × 1.7–5 cm, bearing numerous small black glands, base cuneate to attenuate, apex long acute-acuminate, margin crenate-serrate, upper surface glabrous or pilose but glabrescent, pubescent below; lateral nerves 6–12 on each side, plane above, prominent below. Inflorescence in diffuse panicles reaching 10 × 6–9 cm. Flower glabrescent; pedicel 3–6 mm; receptacle approximately 0.5 mm long; sepals elliptic, 2–4 mm long, outer surface dotted with a few black glands, nectary approximately 1 mm long; petals obovate, 4–6 × 2–3 mm, white to whitish-yellow, membranous; stamens 3–5.5 mm long, anthers approximately 0.5 mm long; ovary ovoid, 1 mm long, finely puberulent and with black glands; styles slender, 3.5–4 mm. Fruit with arched pedicel, 4–5.5 × 2–2.5 mm, puberulent, whitish to olivaceous, with black glands. Seed ellipsoid-obovoid, 4 mm long, chestnut-coloured; aril 1–1.25 mm long.

## Distribution

disjunct; the species was formerly known only from Rwanda and Burundi, but has recently been discovered on Mont Pélé, in the south-west of Gabon (Nyanga province).

## Ecology

undergrowth of montane or submontane primary forest, on slopes; in Gabon at 800 m altitude, elsewhere at 1650–2400 m. Taxonomic note: As the branches and leaves below are pubescent, the Gabonese population may belong to var. pubescens Verdc. The typical variety has glabrous branches and leaves. However, the Gabonese material also shows some characters of Stapfiella muricata Staner from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, notably narrow leaves with few lateral nerves and attenuate base, and flowers with small sepals. Moreover, this material is distinguished from these two species by the presence of hairs on the upper surface of young leaves, its distribution, and its occurrence at a much lower altitude. It seems to us that a more thorough taxonomic study is desirable to clarify this situation. B: Robyns (1964, 1967), Verdcourt (1973). Bibliography
