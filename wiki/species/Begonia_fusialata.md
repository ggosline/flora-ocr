# *Begonia fusialata* Warb. var. fusialata Planche 41A, 41B (1–14)

**Genus**: [[Begonia]]
**Family**: [[Begoniaceae]]
**Protologue**: B. alepensis A.Chev. in Bull. Soc. Bot. France 58 ( Mém. 8) : 174 (1912)

## Description

in Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 22(1) : 37 (1895). B. alepensis A.Chev. in Bull. Soc. Bot. France 58 ( Mém. 8) : 174 (1912). Terrestrial or subepiphytic herb; stem erect, sometimes prostrate, up to 3 m long. Stipules persistent, narrowly triangular, 8–15 × 3–7 mm. Leaves: petiole (0.8–)2.5–9 cm; blade strongly asymmetric, ovate to obovate, 3–14 × 1.5–6.5 cm, base obliquely cordate to truncate, apex acute to acuminate, margins dentate. Inflorescence unisexual, the ♂ 7(–15)-flowered, peduncle 2–6 mm, the ♀ (1–)2(–3)-flowered, with peduncle of ± 1 mm. ♂ Flower: pedicel 6–11 mm; outer tepals 6–14 × 4–8 mm, inner ones 5–11 × 2–5 mm; androecium flattened, zygomorphic, stamens 10–35, fasciculate, column 1–2 mm, free parts of filaments 0.5–1.5 mm long; anthers with longitudinal dehiscence. ♀ Flower sessile; tepals similar to those of ♂ flowers; ovary 8–26 mm long, of which a basal sterile and narrowed part of 2–10 mm resembles a pedicel, triangular section 2–4.5 mm; styles (2–)3, free parts bifid at the apex, stigmatic band horseshoe-shaped, the extremities spiralled and contracted at the branch tips. Infructescence generally bearing 2 fruits. Fruit 15–30(–45) × 5–7 mm (without sterile part of 6–15 mm), trigonous, not winged, whitish, sometimes pale pink or red, dehiscent by 3 valves, placental tissue yellow.

## Distribution

Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (including Bioko), Gabon, Congo (Brazzaville), Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola

## Ecology

hygrophyte; preferring ombrophilous forests or old secondary forests, also in swamp forests or riparian forest strips; up to 1100 m altitude. Uses: eaten as a spinach; as a vegetable with fish or meat. Used to treat vaginitis or metritis
