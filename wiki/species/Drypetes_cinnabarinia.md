# *Drypetes cinnabarinia* Pax & K. Hoffm.

**Genus**: [[Drypetes]]
**Family**: [[Putranjivaceae]]

## Description

Plate 43(4-6); Figure 8(E)

in Engler, Pflanzenr. IV, 147, XV (Heft 81): 257, fig. 21 A, 263 (1922).

Tree up to ±15 m tall; trunk up to ±15 cm in diameter; bark smooth, with numerous small horizontal wrinkles, grey or cream-coloured, sometimes pinkish, with mustard oil odour; branchlets deeply furrowed and angular, glabrous or with very scattered hairs; buds densely puberulent, glabrescent. Stipules caducous, narrowly triangular, (8.5–10–12–(13) mm long, glabrous, with scattered lateral expansions. Leaf sometimes subsessile and clasping the stem at the base of young branches; petiole (1.7–2.5–4–(5) mm, glabrous or shortly hairy; lamina ovate to oblong, often slightly ligulate or even oblong-obovate, (10–11.5–15–(17) × (3.5–)4–5–(7) cm, coriaceous, with oblique to asymmetric base, obtuse-truncate and cordate, sides of the lamina meeting the petiole at the same point or separately, abruptly acuminate to caudate at the apex, with serrulate margin, plane to slightly recurved, shiny above, dull and glabrous below; venation weakly reticulate, midvein glabrous, secondary veins 5 to 7(or 8), slightly marked above, ascending, looped and almost anastomosing well before the margin, prominent below. Male inflorescence in the leaf axils, with (1 or)2 to 4 flowers; bracts suborbicular, 1–1.5 mm long, puberulent, glabrescent. Male flower: pedicel slender, (2.2–)2.3–4–(5) mm, puberulent; sepals 5, imbricate, suborbicular to oblong, (3.9–)4.5–4.9 × (4–)5–6–(7.1) mm, obtuse at the apex, greenish or yellowish to orange-yellow, densely furnished with golden hairs on the outside, with short hairs on the inside, with ciliate margin; stamens 16 to 25, arranged in an obscure manner, most surrounded by the radial folds of the disc, filament (2.2–)2.3–3.2–(3.8) mm, anther 1.2–1.5–(2.1) mm long; disc slightly convex, (4.9–)5.2–7.3–(8.8) mm in diameter, rugose-tuberculate, densely hairy, with irregular margin, projecting between the filaments. Female inflorescence like the male, with solitary flower; bracts 0.7–1.9 mm long, similar to the male ones. Female flower: pedicel 0.7–1.6–(2.4) mm, puberulent; sepals 5, 6.2–6.7–(7) × (5.4–)6–7–(7.3) mm, greenish, similar to those of the males; disc (2.8–)4.5–8–(9.5) mm in diameter, rugose-tuberculate, encircling the base of the ovary, puberulent; ovary subglobose, 2-locular, hairy; style (1.9–)2.5–3.2–(3.3) mm, with 2 free branches, with fan-shaped stigma, 2.2–2.7–(3.2) × (4.1–)4.7–5.5 mm, with finely and irregularly dentate margin. Fruit subglobose, depressed at the apex, (17–)19–25–(35) × (18–)21–29.5 mm, orange to reddish, with persistent sepals and stigmas, densely hairy, with yellowish hairs, glabrescent, with (1 or)2 seeds; pedicel (1.1–)1.2–2–(3) mm, hairy. Seed (10–)13–13.5–(16.5) mm long.

## Distribution

Cameroon, continental Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

Habitat: primary or secondary evergreen forests, riparian forests, swamps, sometimes on rocky soils; in Gabon at 150–980 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1080 m.

## Source note

D. cinnabarinas is widely distributed in Gabon, but appears to be much rarer in the coastal plain. The tree has coriaceous caudate leaves with a serrulate margin and obtuse truncate base. Another species having leaves with distinctly asymmetric and truncate base is D. chevalieri (with 4 sepals), but its leaves are not as thick and the margin is less densely serrulate, and sometimes subentire in the basal half.
