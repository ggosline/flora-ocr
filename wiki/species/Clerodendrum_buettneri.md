# *Clerodendrum buettneri* Gürke

**Genus**: [[Clerodendrum]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 18 : 174 (1893)

## Description

Shrub often sarmentose or climbing or liane of several metres in length, ± densely hairy, with brownish hairs reaching 4 mm; stem subcylindric, hollow, not reported as myrmecophile, weakly striate, pale brown, with long hirsute and dense hairs, red or brown, 2–5 mm, the older branches generally with pseudospines 4–10 mm. Leaves opposite or subopposite; petiole 0.3–3.5 cm, hirsute; blade ovate to elliptic or oblong, often asymmetric, (4–)6–10(–14) × (2–)2.5–6(–7.5) cm, acute to rounded or subcordate (sometimes asymmetrically) at base, acuminate over 0.4–0.9 cm at apex, with entire or sometimes obscurely sinuate margin, brown, hispid, finely membranous, 3-nerved. Inflorescence terminal, paniculate, branched, lax, ± 30 × 18 cm overall, with villous axes (hairs brown-reddish 2–5 mm long); cymes with 3 to 6 flowers, distant (1.5–)3.5–5 cm apart; peduncle 2.5–5.5 cm; bracts foliaceous, (1.5–)3–6.5 × (0.5–)2–3 cm, hirsute; bracteoles ± linear, 2–3 mm long. Flower: pedicel 2–20 mm; calyx hirsute, tube ca. 2–3 mm long, pale green or greenish-white, fleshy, with triangular lobes, 10–16 × 4–7 mm long, connate over 1–3 mm, apiculate at apex, rose-mauve, tinged with white; corolla pale green at base, then white, rarely yellowish, sometimes with centre tinged pink, red or purple, with tube 13–19 × 1 mm, with oblong-elliptic lobes, 6–8(–10) × 2–3(–3.5) mm; stamens and style exsert 3–4.5 cm.

Fruit ± 1 × 1.2 cm, glossy dark green, becoming black.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime

Conservation status: Preliminarily assigned to Least Concern (LC) by Pollard in Cheek et al. (2004).

## Ecology

Forest, forest edge, roadside, near mangrove swamps, littoral forest on sand bordering lagoon, savanna edge; in Gabon, at 2–400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m

## Source note

The species is closely related to C. atlanticum. Bi: Cheek et al. (2004), Huber et al. (1963), Jongkind (2006). Bibliography
