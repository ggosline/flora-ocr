# *Acanthonema strigosum* Hooker f.

**Genus**: [[Acanthonema]]
**Family**: [[Bignoniaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Mag.: tab. 5339 (1862); CLARKE C.B., in DE CANDOLLE A. & C., Mon. Phan. 5 (1): 156 (1883); BAKER J.G. & CLARKE C.B., FTA 4 (2): 502 (1906); HUTCHINSON & DALZIEL, FWTA 2 (1): 237 (1931); BURTT, ed. 2, 2: 362 (1963); Fl. Cam. 27: 18, tab. 5 (1984)

## Description

CLARKE C.B., in DE CANDOLLE A. & C., Mon. Phan. 5 (1): 156 (1883); BAKER J.G. & CLARKE C.B., FTA 4 (2): 502 (1906); HUTCHINSON & DALZIEL, FWTA 2 (1): 237 (1931); BURTT, ed. 2, 2: 362 (1963); Fl. Cam. 27: 18, tab. 5 (1984).

One-leafed herb; hypocotyl ("stem") 2 to 6 cm long. Leaf (accrescent cotyledon) highly variable in size and shape, 13 × 2.5 cm to 30 × 15 cm, often 15–22 × 9–13 cm, showing continued growth near the base and desiccating towards the apex, cordate at the base, shortly pilose on the upper surface, more densely near the meristematic zone of the base, more finely also below; lateral veins widely spreading from the midvein, curved upward near the margins, approximately 1 cm apart at the middle of the blade.

Inflorescences developing in acropetal series at the apex of the hypocotyl and at the base of the midvein; peduncle approximately 2 to 3 cm long, with one or two small pilose bracts at the base; cyme dichasially branched (at least at the first fork) or more often monochasially. Flowers in pairs; pedicels 1 to 2 cm long, pilose, hirsute below, often with long scattered hairs at the apex glandular or subglabrous. Calyx divided nearly to the base into five lanceolate to elliptic segments approximately 6 mm long, pilose externally, with some glandular hairs. Corolla approximately 2 cm long (lobes included) shortly pilose externally with some glandular hairs, tubular very weakly ventricose in the upper half and weakly reflexed upward at the opening; lobes short almost semi-orbicular ± 3 × 6 mm. Disk incomplete, surrounding the ovary on one side only, oblique or truncate, rather variable. Stamens attached to the middle of the tube, only the anterior pair normally fertile; filament ± 5 to 7 mm long, flattened, twisted near the base, widened approximately at the middle and then narrowed toward the apex, glabrous in its lower part, with above it shortly pedicellate glands, furnished at the apex, beyond the anther, with a prominent downward-pointing tooth; anthers coherent face to face, thecae widely divergent, turned at right angles to the filament, the back of the connective with a short downward-reflexed tooth. Staminodes 2, often large, flattened, 3 mm long. Ovary glabrous, conical, ± 2 to 3 mm long, narrowing into a thin style ± 10 mm long; style glabrous or with scattered glandular hairs, thickened and shortly puberulent at the apex; stigma shortly bifid.

Fruit ovoid, conical, approximately 6 mm long, with a short beak 1 mm long, surrounded by the persistent calyx, glabrous; apparently dehiscent only by possible disintegration. Seeds very dark brown, numerous, ellipsoid, ± 0.5 mm long, weakly and abruptly striate. - Pl. 2, p. 13.

## Type

## Discussion

The floral color of Acanthonema strigosum is variable. The lower part of the tube appears to be invariably pale, the upper part, the interior and exterior of the lobes, can be uniformly wine-red (Raynal J. & A. 10472, Cameroon); or the upper part of the tube and the back of the lobes are only colored pale purple, the interior being pale yellow around the opening, with 2 to 4 pale purple lines on the lower part of the lobes and a pale purple patch beneath the apices of the lower lobes (Raynal J. & A. 10478, Cameroon). There is an indication on the field drawing (Raynal J. & A. 10478, Cameroon) showing that the stamens are located on one side of the central line of the flower: if this is so, the style is probably located on the other side. This form of enantiostyly is not singular among the Gesneriaceae.

The most frequently recognized habitats are moist, mossy rocks in forest and patches on rocky walls of shaded ravines, but the species is also encountered, as an epiphyte, on the lower part of mossy trunks.
