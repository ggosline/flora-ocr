## Description

— Bombax sp. "with white flowers" Chev., Forêt Bois Gabon: 77, tab. 1 (1917).

Tree 25–30 m tall, deciduous; trunk straight and cylindrical, reaching a circumference of 5 m or more. Small aliform buttresses at the base of the trunk; crown rounded and loose. Bark greyish, rugose when mature, fibrous, 2–2.5 cm thick, wine-red in section; rhytidome exfoliating in rare thick plates of irregular shape, leaving marked depressions with fine parallel vertical grooves when detaching; lenticels slightly prominent. Buds and young shoots pubescent. Leaves 5–7-foliolate; stipules triangular, densely pubescent externally; petiole 2–7.5 cm long, densely pubescent, hirsute on young leaves, flat above. Leaflets sessile or subsessile, pubescent in juvenile stages and glabrous in older stages, dark green above and pale green below when fresh, matt, dark brown above and lighter brown below when dry, quite coriaceous, lanceolate, 4–14 × 2–4 cm, base cuneate and apex rounded, shortly apiculate, obtuse or emarginate. Midvein glabrous when mature, prominent on both faces; secondary veins 10–15 pairs, slightly ascending, slightly curved, anastomosing 3–5 mm from the leaf margin, prominent on both faces. Tertiary veins in very loose reticulate pattern with elongated meshes, weakly prominent and distinct on both faces, quaternary veins indistinct.

Inflorescences in short few-flowered racemes on short pubescent branchlets. Flowers ♀, white; pedicels pubescent to sparsely puberulent (appressed stellate hairs and erect laciniate hairs) 0.5–1 cm long. Receptacle 0.2 cm long, with 5 blackish glands, sparsely puberulent (appressed stellate hairs). Calyx cupuliform with 5 ± distinct lobes, 0.6–0.9 × 0.8–1 cm, ± strongly pubescent with appressed stellate hairs externally and densely pubescent with short dense hairs at the base, becoming ± sparse at the apex internally. Petals 5, contorted, pubescent, tomentose externally and glabrous internally (but margins tomentose), oblanceolate, elliptic or oblong, 3.5 × 0.8–1 cm, fused by the base to the staminal tube, apex sometimes asymmetric. Stamens 220, bright red when fresh, 4 cm long, united into a glabrous staminal tube, 0.5–0.6 cm long, dividing into 10 fascicles; anthers medifix, ellipsoid, 2 mm long, extrorse. Ovary glabrous, 5-locular; disc with 5 ± distinct lobes. Style equal to the length of the petals, pentagonal in section.

Capsule loculicidally dehiscent with 5 valves, obovoid to oblong, 5–8 × 3–4 cm, base attenuate and apex apiculate. Calyx persistent at the base of the fruit. Valves glabrous externally, smooth, brown or yellowish when dry. Seeds pyriform, smooth, marked with a strong depression on one of the broad faces, dark brown, 0.9–1.2 × 0.7 cm. Kapok reddish and abundant. PL. 9, 2, 6, p. 35; PL. 11, p. 43.

Seedling with hypocotyl approximately 6 cm; cotyledons petiolate, weakly undulate, with palmate venation (5 primary veins). First leaves trifoliolate.

## Type

## Discussion

Rhodognaphalon breviscuspe is a Guinean species whose range extends from Sierra Leone to Gabon. It is found in primary or secondary ombrophilous forests.

## Uses

The sapwood is white when fresh and browses in air. The heartwood, red, can be used in joinery. The kapok and bark have the same uses as in the kapok tree. The bark is used against sore throats or as an emetic or to prevent abortion. This bark, mixed with that of Pycnanthus angolensis or Xylopia aethiopica and Anthocleista nobilis, is said to combat infertility when taken as a potion or enema.

It is also used in the composition of ritual baths intended to drive away evil spirits. The tree is planted near dwellings, it is a protective fetish; it also indicates habitations where twins have been born.
