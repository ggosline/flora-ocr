# *Strophanthus preussii* Engl. & Pax Planche 107 ; Figure 12(A, B)

**Genus**: [[Strophanthus]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 15(3) : 369 (1892)

## Description

Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 15(3) : 369 (1892). Sarmentose shrub or more often a liane reaching 12 m in height; exudate clear or white; branchlets glabrous or rarely scabrous. Leaf: petiole 2–9(–14) mm; blade oval to obovate-elliptic, 4–14 × 1.5–8 cm, cuneate to rounded at base, acuminate at apex, glabrous. Inflorescence finely puberulent or rarely glabrous, with 3–7 flowers, bracts 2–7 × 1–11 mm. Flower: pedicel 4–25 mm; calyx with unequal lobes, the outer broader than the inner, 4–25 × 1–14 mm; corolla with tube 12–26 mm long, white becoming yellow then red-orange, expanding over 40–66% of its length into an upper cupuliform part 8–18 mm wide at the throat, finely puberulent, crowned with ligulate lobes 0.8–2.3 mm long, fleshy, yellow becoming orange with pink or purple bands, corolla lobes oval, 6–12 × 2–5 mm, white to yellow to orange, abruptly narrowed into pendulous tails 11–18 cm × 0.3–1 mm; stamens shortly exserted or included, anther 5–7.5 mm long; style 7–14 mm. Fruit: follicles divergent, 6–12 × 1–2 cm, with apex elongate-acuminate ending in an obtuse point or a button, exocarp thick and hard, sulcate, glabrous, densely provided with elongate lenticels. Seed 12–20 × 2–3.5 mm, puberulent or pubescent; beak glabrous for 3–8 mm and bearing a coma for 30–50 mm of its length; the latter 57–100 mm long. Distribution from Guinea to Uganda and Angola, in Gabon: Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest, gallery forest, forest margins and clearings, in Gabon up to 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Use: the latex or the seeds serve in the manufacture of arrow poison, but they are less toxic than those of S. gratus or S. hispidus. The latex also has medicinal applications and young cooked leaves would be consumed as a vegetable. S. preussii is an ornamental species for its superb flowers with a corolla equipped with very long tails. Notes: photographs and notes on herbaria suggest that in Gabon the corolla is generally less coloured than in West Africa. B: Beentje (1982, 2008c, 2021), Omino (2002). BIBLIOGRAPHY
