# *Cyperus amabilis* Vahl Planche 20

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. pl . 2 : 318 (1805)

## Description

Annual herb, slender or fairly robust; stems solitary or densely packed, 5–30 cm × 0.3–1.5 mm, trigonous, glabrous, leafy only towards the base. Leaf: sheath reddish-brown to purplish, all (except the 1 or 2 lowermost) bearing a well-developed blade; blade 2–15 cm × 0.5–3 mm, flat or rolled, fairly thick, scabrous on the margins. Inflorescence rather open, formed by a sessile digitate fascicle of spikelets and 1–10 fascicles borne on primary rays 0.5–9 cm long, sometimes some borne on secondary rays; inflorescence rarely reduced to a single group of sessile spikelets; involucral bracts 1–6, foliaceous, spreading, the largest 1–10 cm × 1–2.5 mm, generally shorter than the inflorescence; prophyll tubular at the base of each ray, greenish or light brown. Spikelet linear, 3–18 × 1–1.6 mm (2.5–3.5 mm with glumes spread), reddish-brown to golden, with 10–35 flowers; glumes elliptic, 1–1.8 mm long, reddish-brown to golden brown with the central nerve greenish and usually prolonged by a slightly incurved mucro. Flower: stamen 1; style with 3 long branches. Achene obovoid to ellipsoid, 0.5–0.9 × 0.3–0.5 mm, trigonous, brownish, surface with fine papillae in longitudinal rows.

## Distribution

widely distributed, almost pantropical; in Gabon, uncommon or poorly known, found in Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

in seasonally humid habitats, on sandy soils in grasslands, savannas, wooded areas, on the margins of ponds and marshes; in Gabon, at 0–400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1700 m.

## Vernacular names

foothill flatsedge (angl.). Note taxonomique : se reconnaît à son port annuel, ses feuilles assez épaisses et son anthèle de fascicules digités orange-brun. Elle ressemble un peu à C. cuspidatus, qui a des glumes avec un mucron recourbé beaucoup plus long. C. amabilis n’est probablement pas distincte de l’espèce asiatique C. castaneus Willd. (1797). B : Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), ibliographie Lye (1997)
