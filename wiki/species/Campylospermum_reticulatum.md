Tree or shrub reaching 20 m tall; trunk up to 35 cm in diameter; branchlets with whitish-brown bark. Stipules caducous, triangular, 2–13 mm long. Leaf: petiole 2–10 mm; blade narrowly elliptic or sometimes narrowly elliptic-obovate, (5–)10–17(–23) × (1.5–)3–5(–8) cm, ratio (2.5–)3.0–6.8, cuneate at the base, acute to slightly acuminate at the apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, rarely slightly bullate, margin serrulate, often indistinctly so, sometimes entire towards the base; primary vein prominent on both faces, lateral veins in 7 to 17 pairs, prominent on the upper face, slightly prominent on the lower face, tertiary veins scalariform along the margin, reticulate towards the primary vein, generally well-defined on both faces and darker than the surrounding parenchyma. Inflorescence terminal, rarely with 3 or 4 panicles grouped together, branched or rarely unbranched, lax, main axis (4–)11–17(–28) cm; gemmate scales absent or rarely persistent; racemes (0 or)1 to 5, (1.5–)4–13(–21) cm, ascending; cymules distant 0.5–3 cm apart, 1- to 8-flowered; bracts caducous, triangular, reaching 4 mm long. Flower: pedicel (3–)6–10(–18) mm, articulated 1–3 mm from the base; sepals narrowly ovate, (4–)6–8 × 2–4 mm in flower, (5–)6–9 × 2–5 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 5–13 × 3–10 mm, cuneate at the base, rounded to slightly emarginate at the apex; stamens: anthers 3–6 mm long; style 3–5 mm. Fruit: receptacle 3–5 × 3–5 mm; drupeoles 3 or 4 well-developed per receptacle, ellipsoid, 6–9 × 5–6 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

### Distribution

From Senegal to south-western Ghana and southern Nigeria, Cameroon, southern Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda and to the north) and southern Uganda; in Gabon, common throughout the country

### Ecology

Humid primary and secondary forest, forest galleries, along streams and swamp forest; on sandy, lateritic, clayey or clay-granitic soil; in Gabon, up to 1000 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1750 m. Use: The trunk is used as firewood and for planks (Bamps & Farron 1967). Taxonomic note: O. reticulata is a very widespread and highly variable species. According to information on the labels of herbarium material, this species can form a shrub or a large tree whose trunk can reach 35 cm in diameter. The leaves vary from narrowly elliptic to elliptic (ratio (2.5–)3.0–6.8) and the inflorescences can be robust or slender. O. reticulata is close to O. laxiflorum; see under the description of O. laxiflorum for the characters that distinguish them. Bibliography: Bamps & Farron (1967), Cheek et al. (2004), Farron (1963, 1968, 1985), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hutchinson, Dalziel & Keay (1954), Verdcourt (2005)
