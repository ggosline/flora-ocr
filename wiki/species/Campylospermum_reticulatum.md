## Also treated in vol 48

Tree or shrub attaining 20 m in height; trunk up to 35 cm in diameter; branches with whitish-brown bark. Stipules caducous, triangular, 2–13 mm in length. Leaf: petiole 2–10 mm; lamina narrowly elliptic or sometimes narrowly elliptic-obovate, (5–)10–17(–23) × (1.5–)3–5(–8) cm, ratio (2.5–)3.0–6.8, cuneate at base, acute to slightly acuminate at apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, rarely slightly bullate, margin serrulate, often indistinctly, sometimes entire towards base; main vein prominent on both surfaces, lateral veins in 7 to 17 pairs, prominent on upper surface, slightly prominent on lower surface, tertiary veins scalariform along the margin, reticulate towards the main vein, generally well distinct on both surfaces and darker than the surrounding parenchyma. Inflorescence terminal, rarely with 3 or 4 panicles grouped together, branched or rarely unbranched, lax, main axis (4–)11–17(–28) cm; gemmal scales absent or rarely persistent; racemes (0 or)1 to 5, (1.5–)4–13(–21) cm, ascending; cymules distant 0.5–3 cm, 1- to 8-flowered; bracts caducous, triangular, attaining 4 mm in length. Flower: pedicel (3–)6–10(–18) mm, articulate 1–3 mm from base; sepals narrowly ovate, (4–)6–8 × 2–4 mm in flower, (5–)6–9 × 2–5 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 5–13 × 3–10 mm, cuneate at base, rounded to slightly emarginate at apex; stamens: anthers 3–6 mm in length; style 3–5 mm. Fruit: receptacle 3–5 × 3–5 mm; drupels 3 or 4 well developed per receptacle, ellipsoid, 6–9 × 5–6 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

### Distribution

from Senegal to south-west Ghana and southern Nigeria, Cameroon, southern Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola (Cabinda and to the north) and southern Uganda; in Gabon, common throughout the country

### Ecology

humid primary and secondary forest, forest galleries, along streams and swamp forest; on sandy, lateritic, clayey or clayey-granitic soil; in Gabon, up to 1000 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1750 m. Use: The trunk is used as firewood and for planks (Bamps & Farron 1967). Taxonomic note: C. reticulatum is a very widespread and highly variable species. According to information on herbarium specimen labels, this species can form a shrub or a large tree whose trunk can reach 35 cm in diameter. Leaves vary from narrowly elliptic to elliptic (ratio (2.5–)3.0–6.8) and inflorescences can be robust or slender. C. reticulatum is close to C. laxiflorum; see under the description of C. laxiflorum for the characters that distinguish them. Bibliography: Bamps & Farron (1967), Cheek et al. (2004), Farron (1963, 1968, 1985), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hutchinson, Dalziel & Keay (1954), Verdcourt (2005)
