# *Maranthes chrysophylla* (Oliver) Prance

**Genus**: [[Maranthes]]
**Family**: [[Chrysobalanaceae]]

## Description

in WHITE, BJBB 46 : 295 (1976).

— Parinari chrysophylla OLIV., FTA 2: 369 (1871); ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1): 306 (1915); A.CHEV., Vég. Ut. Afr. Trop. Fr. 9, For. Bois Gabon: 204 (1917); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2): 428 (1958); AUBR., FFCI, ed. 2, 1: 184, tab. 57 (1959); KEAY, ONOCHIE & STANFIELD, Nig. Trees 1: 316 (1960); WALKER & SILLANS, Pl. Ut. Gabon: 359 (1961); DE SAINT-AUBIN, Forêt Gabon; 168, phot. (1963).

— P. iodocalyx MILDBR., Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Berlin 8 : 57 (1921), e descr.

Tree reaching up to 30–40 m in total height and 80–120 cm in diameter; bole fairly straight and regular, somewhat flared or slightly buttressed at the base, without contreforts; bark fairly smooth, mossy and mottled with reddish towards the base, greyish towards the top, exfoliating in small thin scales revealing lighter greyish patches; sapwood thin (5 cm) passing into dark red heartwood, hard, heavy; crown globose or hemispherical, with stout radiating branches sometimes spotted with reddish, with dense foliage showing characteristic tawny reflections. Mature branchlets brown furnished with white lenticels. Young branchlets and petioles with long ascending hispid pilosity and, like the underside of the leaves, covered with a thin but compact tawny-golden woolly tomentum, ± caducous on older leaves, in patches or totally, the underside of the lamina becoming then ± ashy. Innovations enclosed in caducous scales, concave semi-orbicular towards the base of the shoot, elongated (18 mm) in channel form, obtuse, retuse or emarginate at the apex towards the top of the shoot, densely silvery-silky externally, with margin fringed-pubescent, glabrous internally. Petiole glabrous, transversely cracked, 10–12 mm, bearing 2 elliptic or circular glands, deeply crater-shaped, at its upper part in contact with the margin of the lamina. Lamina polymorphous with intermediates and overlapping of characters between the 2 following extreme types; for mature trees: 1) in very humid coastal zone with short dry season (cf. Mann 978): parchment-like texture, tomentum on underside permanent, elliptic-rhombohedric form, reaching approximately 10 × 18 cm, base broadly acute to obtuse, apex broadly acute with small acumen, (6–)8–10 pairs of lateral veins inclined at 45°; 2) in humid interior zone with fairly marked dry season (cf. Le Testu 5969): coriaceous texture, tomentum on underside caducous, elliptic-ovate form, reaching approximately 7 × 11 cm, base obtuse to subcordate, apex obtuse-rounded with abrupt small acumen, (6–)8–10 pairs of lateral veins, very open towards the base, inclined at 45° towards the middle and upper part of the lamina. Young shoots, in one and the other zone, with longly elliptic leaves, acute at base and apex, papyraceous texture, reaching 16 × 22 cm and, in the first zone considered, frequently broadly lanceolate (up to 16 × 22 cm) on older trees.

Inflorescences, with greyish tomentose axes, in terminal corymbiform panicles, with bracts and bracteoles early caducous, open (height 10 cm, width 15 cm) and pauciflorous with large flowers (short pedicel + receptacle 16–18 mm, ensemble obconic) in zone 1 above, compact (height 5 cm, width 12 cm) and multiflorous with small flowers (long pedicel + receptacle 10–12 mm, ensemble ± funnel-shaped) in zone 2 above, but all intermediates possible. Receptacle tawny pink-orange externally; sepals: 1) 8–10 and 2) 4–6 mm, greyish tomentellose externally, cream tomentellose internally; petals concave obovate: 1) 8 × 16 and 2) 5 × 10 mm, pale yellow; ca. 25 stamens with filament, 1) ca. 30, and 2) ca. 20–25 mm, white and cream anther; 3 hirsute carpels with line of ascending silvery hairs below, on the inner wall of the receptacle or line absent.

Drupe obovoid, 20–25 × 45–50 mm, with surface ± furnished with fine whitish-pinkish punctations in relief, often with sepals and stamens persistent at the base. — PL. 30, p. 103.

## Discussion

As mentioned above (leaves, flowers), it may seem possible to split Maranthes chrysophylla (Oliv.) Prance into two subspecies that could be considered parapatric: subsp. chrysophylla in coastal zone and subsp. coriacea in interior zone; however this morphological and ecological distinction is valid only for specimens with extreme characters and is greatly attenuated by the existence of intermediate or overlapping-character specimens.

This species is known from Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire (Tabou), Ghana, Nigeria (Southeast), Cameroon, Gabon and Zaïre (Kinshasa). In Cameroon and Gabon it occurs throughout the dense humid forest massif, in scattered state in the form of trees generally of medium dimensions, but is especially present in Atlantic forest with Caesalpiniaceae; in semi-deciduous forest with Sterculiaceae and Ulmaceae it is encountered isolated, in forest nuclei of evergreen type, either with Caesalpiniaceae, or with Gilbertiodendron dewevrei.
