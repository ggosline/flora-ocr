Tree 6 to 36 m tall with diameter reaching approximately 50 cm (rarely 1.20 m). Sometimes with small aliform buttresses concave at the base. Bark smooth to rugose, grey to brown, very thin, cut face reddish with strong odour. Young branchlets weakly pubescent to glabrous. Older branchlets glabrous, black, ± flattened toward their apex. Leaves at the extremity of branchlets opposite to subopposite, otherwise alternate.

Petiole finely pubescent especially at its distal extremity, (3.5–) 4.3–13.5 cm long, flat above toward the base, canaliculate toward the apex. Rachis ± pubescent, (5.7–) 7.4–45 cm long, canaliculate above. Pinnae 5–13 pairs opposite (sometimes alternate). Pinna axis pubescent, (3.6–) 5–10.5 (–14.5) cm long, with prominent crest above and lateral margins ± winged. Leaflets alternate, 18–24. Petiolule pubescent, 0.5–1 mm long. Blade glabrous above and ± pubescent below (often tuft of denser hairs appressed to ± hirsute at the base of the primary vein, proximal side of the blade), dark green to black above and green-brown to dark brown below when dry, oblong to oblong elliptic, sometimes oblong lanceolate, (0.3–) 0.7–1.8 (–2.2) × (0.2–) 0.4–0.9 (–1.4) cm, base very asymmetric: proximal margin attenuate and distal margin very rounded; apex rounded, frequently retuse with small reflexed mucro toward the lower surface. Midvein weakly pubescent at the base, concave to weakly prominent above and prominent pubescent below. Secondary veins 4–6 pairs, ascending, slightly or not arched, often scarcely visible, weakly prominent or not on both surfaces. Tertiary veins not visible.

Inflorescences in solitary or geminate axillary racemes, or in short terminal panicle. Axis pubescent. Pedicel pubescent, 1.5–2.5 mm long. Floral bud ellipsoid fusiform, whitish-green to pink in life; with corolla glabrous or shortly pubescent at apex. ♂ Flowers, pentamerous, solitary or grouped by 2–3, yellow to salmon-pink in life. Sepals united, 0.7–1.5 mm long, pubescent externally; free lobes triangular, 0.25–0.5 mm long, apex acute. Petals 5, free, glabrous on both surfaces, elliptic, 3.5–4 × 0.75–1 mm, base attenuate into a claw; apex acute thickened. Stamens 10, free; filament slender, 5–6 mm long; anther ellipsoid, 0.5 mm long, with connective terminating in a caducous gland. Pistil with very short stipe 0.25 mm long; ovary glabrous, 2.5–3 mm long, with 11 ovules; style slender, 3.5 mm long, terminated by a very short tubulous stigma.

Pod persistent on the tree, pendulous, indehiscent, cruciform in section, glabrous, dark purple-brown to shiny black, oblong, most often arched, 15–25 × 4.5–6 cm; base attenuate to rounded on short stipe; lateral wings well developed; lateral margins straight to ± lobed; apex attenuate to rounded. Lateral wings hard; vertical wings filled with fleshy mesocarp. Seeds hard, ovate, 9 × 7 × 4 mm.

Seedling glabrescent. Cotyledons epigeal. First four leaves whorled, pinnately compound with 8–9 pairs of leaflets. Fifth leaf solitary as previously. Leaves 6–7 bipinnately compound. – Pl. 16, p. 65.

## Discussion

The range of this species is very extensive as it extends from Sénégal (Casamance) to Tanganyika toward the East and to Angola toward the South. It is a lowland to montane forest species. Flowering occurs from December to June; fruiting likewise.

We note some specimens with particularly small leaflets (Zenker 773, Cameroun; Le Testu 8666). This form may also be encountered in Congo and Centrafrique. It does not seem appropriate to establish a new taxon given the variability of the leaves.

## Uses

The pale yellow soft wood is used in joinery. It also serves to make paddles. Infusions are obtained from the very fragrant fruits with added chili pepper; these are used as an enema or fumigation as a febrifuge. The decoction of bark is an effective emetic. The bark and crushed fruits serve as a fishing poison. Moreover, for the interior tribes of the country, pods scattered in food crop plantations would protect harvests against harmful animals.
