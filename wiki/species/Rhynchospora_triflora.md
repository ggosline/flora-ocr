# *Rhynchospora triflora* Vahl Figure 23b

**Genus**: [[Rhynchospora]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Enum. pl. 2 : 232 (1805)

## Description

Robust herb, perennial, in tufts, with solitary stems at the apex of thick scaly stolons 1–2 mm in diameter; stem 80–150 cm × 4–5 mm, scabrous below the inflorescence. Leaves numerous, throughout the stem; sheath closed, the upper one greenish with a pale brownish-red translucent zone opposite the blade, basal sheaths softer and brownish-red; blade 50–90 cm × 2–5 mm. Inflorescence formed of a terminal corymb and sometimes 1–3 lateral ones; primary rays 1–10 cm, ± trigonous or compressed, scabrous; leaves subtending the primary branches 10–20 cm × 1–2 mm; corymbs with main axis 1–2 cm, scabrous and terminated by erect branches; branches with filiform bracts and tubular prophylls at the base, the terminal ones bearing 1–3 spikelets. Spikelet lanceolate, 6–10 × 2–3 mm, brown; glumes brownish-red, glabrous with a fine central nerve ending in a mucro, the lower ones 2–4 mm long, sterile, the two upper ones 6–10 mm long. Flower: perianth with 6 subequal, scabrous bristles, 4–6 mm long; stamens 3; style long, entire. Achene obovoid to oblanceolate, approximately 4 × 2.5 mm, brownish-red, finely ridged transversely with spiny hairs on the upper surface; persistent base of the style longer than the achene, whitish.

## Distribution

very widespread in tropical Africa, Asia, Australia and America; very rare in Gabon, found only in Ogooué-Ivindo (1 collection)

## Ecology

savanna, swampy habitat; in Africa up to 1100 m altitude. Taxonomic note: very close to R. corymbosa, but differs by its more slender habit, its leaves with narrower blade and its spikelets fewer and more acute on longer and more slender peduncles. b: Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1997), Raynal (1971), Robinson (1961a).
