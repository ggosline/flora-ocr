# *Beischmiedia minutiflora* Benth. et Hook. f.

**Genus**: [[Beischmiedia]]
**Family**: [[Lauraceae]]

## Synonyms

- Oreodaphne (?) minutiflora Meissn. Prodr. D.C. 15, 1 : 130 (1864).
- Afrographne minutiflora Stapf, Journ. Linn. Soc. 37 : 111 (1905).
- Tylostemon minutiflorus Stapf, F.T.A. 6, 1:176 (1909).
- Beilschmiedia fruticosa Engl. Bot. Jahrb. 26 : 386 (1899).
- Afrodaphne fruticosa Stapf, 1. c. (1905).
- Tylostemon fruticosus Stapf, 1. c. (1909).

## Description

(pl. XII et II).

— Bentham et Hooker f., Gén. Pl. 3 : 153 (1880).

Shrub or small tree 1 to 8 m tall; young branches glabrous, greenish-brown; buds acuminate, tawny. Leaves oblong, acuminate (acumen linear reaching 13 mm), concolorous, coriaceous, furnished with 8–10 pairs of arched lateral nerves and displaying a network of tertiary and quaternary nerves very visible on both sides. Flowers red (Klaine).

Meissner's type had small flowers 1 mm in diameter, whereas the reference specimen cited by Stapf (Zenker 1695, Bipinde, Cameroon) has flowers almost twice as large, deserving less the name minutiflora. The calyx is glabrous or finely puberulent on the outside, sometimes ciliate at the margin; the filaments of the outer stamens are variable: either twice the length of the anther (Klaine 2776), or equal in length to the anther and as wide as it (Klaine 1581, Zenker 1695), or with short filaments constricted beneath the anther (Le Testu 8833).

These plants were classified into two species whose differences noted in the descriptions and in the examination of specimens Zenker 1695 and 1063 would be as follows:

The leaves, branches and buds are by contrast described without notable differences; Engler notes that B. fruticosa is close to B. minutiflora but with leaves wider in the middle. Specimen Le Testu 8833 shows a calyx finely puberulent and ciliate at the margin, revealing a disjunction of the characters placed in opposition above. In short, the only notable differences reside in the wide or constricted filaments and in the length of the tepals; but the presence of wide and long filaments on plants with very typical leaves such as Klaine 2776 shows that filament shape is not a criterion allowing the separation, into two groups, of plants so similar.
