# *Xyris decipiens* N.E.Br.

**Genus**: [[Xyris]]
**Family**: [[Xyridaceae]]

## Description

in Dyer, Fl. cap. 7 : 3 (1897). Annual or short-lived perennial herb, tufted. Leaf: sheath brown, smooth, glabrous, with membranous margins; blade linear, 12–25(–45) cm × 3–5 mm, flattened, surface and margin covered with minute papillae or glabrous, apex narrowly acute, (sub)symmetric. Inflorescence: peduncle 40–75 cm × 1–1.8 mm, terete with 6−7 longitudinal ridges bearing minute papillae; spike initially ovoid, 9–14 × 10–14 mm, elongating and becoming conical at maturity, up to 25 × 15 mm; the two lowermost sterile bracts broadly ovate, 2 × 2 mm, the second pair about 2.5 × 2 mm, the upper ones about 4 × 2 mm, all brown, the upper ones with a dorsal mark, sometimes with a tuft of hairs at the apex; fertile bracts very broadly ovate, about 5 × 4 mm, brown to reddish-brown, slightly lustrous, with a pale matt narrowly elliptic dorsal mark, with a strong median nerve, membranous margins, slightly fimbriate. Flower: lateral sepals narrowly triangular, 4–5 × 1 mm, irregularly dentate along the upper margin and often with a bifid apex, the other margin dentate; corolla yellow, the other floral details not visible on dry material. Fruit oblong to obovoid, 4.5–6 mm long, smooth, brown. Seeds ellipsoid, 0.8 × 0.3−0.4 mm, surface extensively covered with reticulate ridges.

## Distribution

very widespread in West Africa, from Senegal to Nigeria, Cameroon, and southward to Angola, always in the coastal zone; in Gabon in the Lopé Reserve and Estuaire, Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

sandy marshes close to the sea, but sometimes in upland savanna or on inselbergs; in Gabon at 0–500 m altitude, elsewhere mainly at low altitude. Taxonomic notes: X. angularis and X. decipiens are very close, particularly in the shape of the lateral sepals and the shape and outline of the seeds. These two characters are not found in the other African taxa. Further studies need to be conducted to determine whether they are distinct and at what rank to place them

Lewis (1981) included X. angularis in X. decipiens. Lisowski (2009) suggested that X. decipiens could be synonymous with X. macrocephala from South America. But additional studies, particularly molecular ones, remain to be done to support this interesting hypothesis. B: Hepper (1968a, 1968b), Lewis (1981). Bibliography
