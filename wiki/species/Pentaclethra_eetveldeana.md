Large tree reaching 30 m. Crown dense ± spreading in a dome. Trunk most often sinuous. Bark light grey, fissured, light brown in cross-section. Young branchlets puberulent to pubescent appressed. Stipules caducous, linear-lanceolate. Petiole glabrous, 4.3-7 cm long ± distinctly canaliculate above. Rachis pubescent above, 15.3-35.5 cm long, ridged crest on the upper face. Pinnae 9-16 pairs oblong elliptic. Axis of the pinna pubescent above, 4.1-12.1 cm long. Leaflets 15-30 pairs (basal leaflet often solitary), sessile, glabrous on both faces, dark green above, paler below when dry, rhomboid, 8-13 × 2-3.5 mm, base very asymmetric: proximal margin strongly auriculate and distal margin rounded, lower margin of the blade rather pubescent sometimes only on the auricle, apex asymmetric very acute. Main vein substantially close to the upper margin, weakly prominent above and prominent below, glabrous. Secondary venation not visible above and scarcely visible below. Veinlets not visible.

Spikes solitary or terminal panicles. Peduncle pubescent, 1.5-2 cm long. Flowers sessile, white when fresh. Calyx glabrous, 1.5-2 mm long, with sepals fused; free lobes imbricate, broadly triangular, apex obtuse. Petals 5, glabrous, 5 mm long, fused into a narrow tube 2 mm long, free lobes 2.5-3 mm long, ± linear, acute at the apex weakly apiculate towards the inner face. Stamens 5 and staminodes 5, fused to each other and to the corolla tube. Stamens with free filament 4-4.5 mm long, swollen in the median part, then tapering towards the apex; anther ellipsoid 1 mm long, glandular at the apex. Staminodes with free filament ± 8.5-9 mm long; aborted terminal anther in the form of a weakly echinulate thickening 0.3 mm long prolonged by a yellow pedicellate gland. Pistil with a short stipe 0.3 mm long; ovary densely pubescent, 1.6-2 mm long with 8 ovules; style short 4 mm long, with claviform stigma.

Seedling at the 1st pair of leaves with 4 pairs of pinnae of 14-20 pairs of leaflets – Pl. 3, Fig. 6-9, p. 23, Pl. 4, p. 25.

## Discussion

The range of this species extends from southern Cameroon to Zaïre. It appears to prefer reworked forest groupings.

## Uses

The wood, white very slightly yellowish or pinkish, is a good construction wood. The bark is used to make enemas. The seed contains abundantly solid fatty substances.
