# *Xyris capensis* Thunb.

**Genus**: [[Xyris]]
**Family**: [[Xyridaceae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. pl. cap. 1 : 12 (1794)

## Description

Annual or sometimes perennial herb (see note), forming spreading tufts. Leaves flabellate; sheath gradually widening at the base, glabrous, smooth, green, brown at the base, hyaline margin; ligule absent; blade linear, reaching 40 cm × 2 mm, glabrous, apex thick, paler, acute, slightly incurved. Inflorescence: peduncle 30–50(–75) cm × 2 mm, section ± rounded, longitudinal ridges weakly marked; spike subspherical, becoming depressed-spherical when mature, 5–8 mm in diameter, dark brown; sterile bracts 4, broadly ellipsoidal to broadly obovate, rounded, the outer ones split at the apex, brown, dark toward the middle and apex; fertile bracts as the sterile, but paler and thicker, strongly concave, principal nerve carinate toward the retuse and apiculate apex. Flower: lateral sepals naviculate, arched, glabrous, pale brown, dark brown toward the acute or acuminate apex, keel entire; corolla yellow, tube approximately 4.5 mm long, lobes broadly obovate, approximately 3.5 × 2.5 mm; anthers approximately 1 mm long, filament approximately 0.5 mm; staminodes bifurcate, highly branched; ovary obovoid, 2.5 × 1.5 mm; style 2 mm, branches approximately 1 mm, highly branched, stigmas aggregated into a mass. Fruit: ellipsoid to obovoid, 3-lobed, 4–6 mm long, smooth, brown. Seeds ellipsoid, 0.3 × 0.2 mm, with 12–16 longitudinal ridges, the transverse ones also distinct.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa, also known from India and South-East Asia, but it remains doubtful that these populations belong to the same species; in Gabon known only from Ngounié

## Ecology

marshes sometimes or always inundated, often in elevated regions; in Gabon at 500−700 m altitude, elsewhere in Africa up to 3000 m. Taxonomic note: species highly variable in size and other characters; the variation is discussed by Lock (1999). B: Lewis (1981), Lock (1999, 2010). Bibliography
