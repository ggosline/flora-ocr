# *Craterosiphon soyauxii* H.H.W. Pears.

**Genus**: [[Craterosiphon]]
**Family**: [[Thymeleaceae]]
**Protologue**: Pearson, F.T.A. 6 (1): 248 (1909)

## Description

Pearson, F.T.A. 6 (1): 248 (1909). — Domke, Not. Bot. Gart. Berlin, 11 (105): 274 (1934).

Shrub with lianescent branches, with brown-red bark, densely covered with pustulous grey lenticels, glabrous.

Leaves opposite; petiole 5–8 mm, thick, rugose, channelled. Blade coriaceous, with thick cuticle, shining on upper surface, paler and matt on lower surface, very broadly ovate, 5–9 cm long, 3–4 cm wide, with greatest width situated towards the middle, or more often towards the proximal third (Soyaux 263, Le Testu 9626), sometimes subcordate at the base, gradually attenuated into a short and broadly triangular appendage (10 × 6 mm) at the apex; margins thick, strongly revolute. Midvein well-marked, very prominent on lower surface; secondary veins 12–25 pairs, approximately perpendicular to the midvein, forked and finally anastomosing near the blade margin.

Flowers (Le Testu 9626) in bi- or triflorous fascicles; peduncle 2 mm, bracteoles ovate, less than 1 mm. Perianth tube 10–15 mm, rather broadly cup-shaped, 1–2 mm in diameter at the base, 2–4 mm in diameter at the throat. Calyx lobes rather broadly oblong-lanceolate, 5–7 mm by 2–3 mm, rounded at the apex. Stamens all exserted from the tube at anthesis, the episepalous ones with filaments 4–5 mm, the alternisepalous ones shorter; anthers small, less than 1 mm, with globose-spherical thecae. Ovary glabrous; style 15–18 mm; stigma spherical or discoid, 1 mm in diameter, generally exceeding the stamens at anthesis.

Fruit (Soyaux 263) fusiform, rather attenuated at both extremities, rugose, 2–3 cm long and 1.2 cm wide; pericarp (hypertrophied perianth) fibrous; endocarp sclerified very hard, whitish-yellowish.

We have made the connection between the material of Soyaux bearing only fruits and that of Le Testu bearing only flowers on account of the rather clear resemblance of the leaves. Pearson having considered the fundamental character of rounded leaves to distinguish the plant of Soyaux from Craterosiphon scandens Engl. et Gilg, we have followed this way of seeing; if there is truly identity between the specimens from the two localities (which will only be verifiable when flowers have been able to be collected in the region of Sibang), it is a species that is easily distinguished from C. scandens by its smaller flowers, with stamens long-exserted, with broadly flared tube; the plant is also distinguished from the Congolese species of the C. Louissii Wilczek ex A. Robyns group, for this latter plant has different leaves and moreover, the petals are almost as long as the tube, which is not the case for the plant from Gabon. C. Soyauxii differs also clearly from C. Quarrei Staner by its much larger leaves, as well as by its bark not thickened and fissured.

## Specimens examined

Le Testu : 9626, Evorombil, région d'Oyem (f., fl., juil.). Soyaux : 263, Sibange farm (f., fr.), holotype (K).
