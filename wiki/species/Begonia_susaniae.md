# *Begonia susaniae* Sosef

**Genus**: [[Begonia]]
**Family**: [[Begoniaceae]]
**Protologue**: Wageningen Agric. Univ. Papers 91-4 : 105 (1992)

## Description

Plant reaching 15(–20) cm in height. Leaf peltate; petiole 1.5–15(–22) cm, hirsute; blade spreading when fresh, very diversely bullate, nearly symmetric to distinctly asymmetric, subcircular or broadly elliptic-ovate to elliptic-ovate or ovate, 3.5–12.5(–15.5) × 2.5–7(–8) cm, apex gradually attenuate or sometimes slightly acuminate, margin generally denticulte or sometimes coarsely dentate, sinuate or nearly entire, upper surface with one hair on each protuberance. Inflorescence: peduncle 1–6 cm, with 2(–3) ♂ flowers and (0–)1(–2) ♀ flowers with yellow tepals; bracts (1–)2–5 mm in length. ♂ Flower: tepals (6–)7–19 × (6.5–)7–17 mm; androecium with (15–)19–33 stamens. ♀ Flower: pedicel elongating in fruit to 8(–10.5) mm; tepals (6–)8–20 × (7–)8.5–20 mm; ovary (wings included) obtriangular depressed to obtriangular very depressed, (2.5–)3–7.5(–9) × (6.5–)7–15(–17.5) mm, without long hairs, with 3(–4) obtriangular very depressed or sometimes obtriangular depressed wings, (1–)1.5–6(–8) mm wide; styles 3(–4). Fruit as the ovary or sometimes obtriangular-obovoid depressed, (4–)5–7.5(–9) × (6.5–)9–15(–17.5) mm; rostrum distinct, up to 4.5 mm.

## Distribution

south-west Cameroon and north-west Gabon (Monts de Cristal)

## Ecology

locally common, on soil or on mossy rocks, preferring slopes or embankments, in primary forest or old secondary forest, often along watercourses, rapids or waterfalls; 250–900 m altitude

## Source note

The characters of the leaves are very variable: the shape, width, colors and number of protuberances can vary greatly. On one hand, there are plants with nearly circular leaves, uniformly colored, furnished with small protuberances. On the other hand, one finds plants with elliptic-ovate leaves, purplish-brown along the margin and at the center, furnished with large protuberances. However, all intermediates can be found. B: Sosef (1992a), Sosef (1994). IBLIOGRAPHY
