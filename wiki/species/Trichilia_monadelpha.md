# *Trichilia monadelpha* (Thonn.) J.J.de Wilde Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Trichilia]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Acta Bot. Neerl. 14 : 453 (1965)

## Description

Small tree 4–16 m tall; trunk cylindrical, up to 40 cm in diameter; cross-section pink, rapidly fading to rust colour, fragrant, sometimes exuding a little cream-coloured latex. Leaf: petiole 4–13 cm; rachis (4–)8–20(–23) cm; petiolules 2–7(–8) mm, the terminal ones (4–)5–11(–13) mm; leaflets (7–)9–13(–15), opposite to subopposite, narrowly obovate to ovate, 4–26 × 1.5–9 cm, cuneate or obtuse at the base, acuminate at the apex, sometimes faintly punctate, with scattered hairs on the lower surface. Inflorescence axillary to supra-axillary, lax to fairly compact, 1.5–12(–21) cm long, with very fragrant yellowish-green flowers; bracts rapidly caducous. Functionally male flower: pedicel up to 2 mm; bracteoles caducous; receptacle 0.8–2.5 mm long; calyx 1.5–2.7 × 3–5 mm, pubescent outside; petals narrowly rectangular, 6.5–11 × 1.5–3 mm, puberulent outside; staminal crown 5.5–7.5 mm long, including the anthers, free part of the filaments almost truncate at the apex by the absence of small points and densely hairy inside, the rest slightly pubescent, anthers narrowly rectangular, 1–1.7 × 0.4–0.6 mm; pistillode filiform, 4.5–6.5 mm long, pubescent. Functionally female flower similar; ovary well developed, globose, 2–3 mm in diameter, 3(–4)-locular, pubescent-strigulose; style 3–4 mm, pubescent, stigma capitate. Fruit obovoid-subglobose, often mucronate, 3(–4)-lobed, 1.5–2.5 cm in diameter, with a stipe 1–5 mm, puberulent, dehiscent by 3(–4) valves. Seed 15–17 × 8–11 mm, with blackish lustrous testa, the ⅔ modified into scarlet sarcotesta.

## Distribution

Republic of Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (including Bioko), Gabon, Angola (Cabinda) and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, fairly rare and known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

understory of humid and deciduous or semi-deciduous forest, often of secondary formations or riparian forests; in Gabon, 10–250 m altitude, elsewhere up to 650 m

## Uses

The wood is used in the construction of dwellings, particularly for piles, and is locally appreciated for carving. The bark is commonly used in traditional medicine. B: Aubréville (1959), Burkill (1997), Lemmens (2008c), Staner & Gilbert (1958), de Wilde (1968)
