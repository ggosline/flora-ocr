- H. venulosa Hutch. & Moss, FWTA 1: 449 (1928); Kew Bull.: 19 (1929), descr. TYPE : Thomas 10550, Sierra Leone (K!).

Very large liane reaching 40 to 100 m in length; trunk exceeding 20 cm in diameter with smooth or rough bark, ochraceous or greyish, fissured lengthwise, somewhat cracked transversely; latex rare in the cortical parenchyma, absent elsewhere. Old wood as in the preceding species. Internodes more slender and less lenticellate. Stipules reduced, most often spaced on a transverse callus. Petiole (5)7–15 mm. Blade moderately but fairly regularly crenulate-dentate, elliptic, 5–15 × 2.5–8 cm, chartaceous rather thin, olive or brown when dry; base variable acute or rounded; apex with acumen 6–15 mm. Secondary veins 6–8(–10) pairs; the apparent veinlets are finer than in the preceding species.

Inflorescence very finely pubescent (small multicellular hairs with oblong elements). Panicles and cymes multiflorous very lax and divaricate; peduncle glabrescent; the largest cymes 10–12 cm long. Pedicels of lower flowers up to 30 mm, those of upper flowers 5–18 mm. Bud narrowly ellipsoid to almost oblong approximately 6–10 mm, finely puberulent. Flower pale green becoming

## Type

Zenker 2483, Cameroun (iso-, BR ! K !, L !, P !)

## Discussion

Widespread distribution in Africa: Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte-d'Ivoire, Ghana (Lock 43939), Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Muni (Tessmann 765 and 865), Central African Republic, Zaire, Cabinda, Angola, Uganda and Malawi.

The closely related West African species, L. ectypetala Hallé, was recognized with doubt in fruiting material from Ondo, South Nigeria. To be sought in Cameroon; distinctive characters in the key page 234.

In dense forests less humid than for the preceding species, and even in semi-deciduous forests. Recorded altitudes 500 to 800 m.

## Uses

plant used together with other species in the manufacture of vine bridges
