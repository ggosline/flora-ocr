# *Pseudarthria fagifolia* Baker

**Genus**: [[Pseudarthria]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 2 : 167 (1871)

## Description

Subshrub 1 to 1.5 m in height. Stipules linear to lanceolate, 6–8 mm long. Leaf: terminal leaflets with rhomboidal blade, 7–13 × 5–8 cm, apex mucronate, subcoriaceous, finely scabrous above, densely silky and grey below, with undulate margins and fairly pronounced venation, lateral leaflets elliptic-rhomboidal, 5–10 × 2–4 cm. Inflorescence in an open, much-branched panicle, 6–35 cm long; flowers in groups of 3 or 4, subtended by 3 or 4 silky bracts, the primary ones lanceolate, ± 5 × 1 mm, the secondary ones ± 2 × 0.5 mm. Flower: pedicel 3–5 mm; bracteoles subulate, 1–2 mm long; calyx 3–5 mm long, pubescent; corolla 2 times longer than the calyx, pink or red; ovary usually stipitate. Pod 8–10 × 3–4 mm, with 1 to 5, most often 2 or 3, seeds, puberulent. Seed ovoid, ± 2 × 1 mm.

## Distribution

Tropical West Africa; has not yet been collected in Gabon, but is very widespread elsewhere

## Ecology

Shrubby savannas, forest galleries, Raphia or bamboo vegetation; at low altitude. Uses: Used in Côte-d'Ivoire in the treatment of bronchial affections, sore throat, febrile pain and dysentery

## Source note

Very similar to P. hookeri var. hookeri and may be just a form of it. B: Burkill (1995), Pellegrin (1949), Verdcourt (1971d). Bibliography
