# *Triplochiton scleroxylon* K. Schum.

**Genus**: [[Triplochiton]]
**Family**: [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Malvaceae]] s.l. — *Sterculiaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Sterculiaceae]]

## Synonyms

- T. johnsoni C. H. Wright.
- T. nigericum Sprague.

## Description

Obéché or Samba (commercial names).

Very large tree capable of exceeding 50 m in height and 2 m trunk diameter, with raised winged buttresses. Bark whitish becoming very scaly. Branchlets angular at the nodes, glabrous to the extremities. Ribbon-like acute stipules 15–30 mm long, rapidly caducous, leaving a scar that encircles the node. Petiole 2–10 (15) cm long, glabrous, enlarged at the upper extremity. Blade glabrous 7–20 (25) cm wide, palmatilobate, with 5–7 lobes of highly variable length between 1.5 and 15 cm. 5–7 primary basal nerves; secondary nerves numerous and slightly ascending.

Inflorescences axillary in small pauciflorous panicles with moderately pubescent axes. Bracteoles broadly enveloping, 3–5 mm long, with 1–3 apices, puberulent on both faces and caducous. Pedicels 8–15 mm long, articulated near the upper third. Bud subspheric 6–7 mm wide.

Calyx with 5 valvate persistent lobes 5–6 mm long, connate. Calyx puberulent externally and densely pubescent internally. 5 spreading petals rapidly caducous, with dense woolly pubescence on both faces, white with purple and black claws, approximately 1 cm long. Androgynophore greenish, with 5 pubescent ridges, reaching 3–4 mm long. 15–23 stamens with bifid filament: each branch is slender and bears a single anther lobe. 5 petaloid staminodes appressed on the ovary. 5 pubescent carpels with subulate coalescent styles. 6–8 ovules per locule.

Fruit composed of 5 winged glabrescent carpels 5–6 cm long, with punctiform insertion, ornamented with a projection beneath the base of the wing rib.

## Type

Zenker et Staudt 595, de Yaoundé (P.)

## Discussion

Widespread from former French Guinea to former Belgian Congo, this beautiful and very large deciduous species is well characterized by its maple-like leaves. Typical of semi-deciduous forests, it is, together with Kotibé (Nesogordonia papaverifera), an indicator, in northern Gabon, of a less humid sector whose climate recalls that of the Yaoundé region. It is a light-demanding species; small stands of Obéché tend generally to increase by establishing themselves in abandoned cultivated land.

Uses. Soft and light wood, cream-white, with normally indistinct sapwood, homogeneous texture and coarse grain. Irregularity of log outline causes significant sawing losses. Its use is recommended for light joinery, box-making, etc. After peeling into thick veneer, it is extensively used in the interior of plywood. For roofing it produces good wooden shingles.

## Vernacular names

Ayos, Ayous (Fang) : Obéché et Samba, noms commerciaux, sont originaires respectivement du Nigéria et de la Côte-d'Ivoire
