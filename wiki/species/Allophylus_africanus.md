# *Allophylus africanus* Palisot de Beauvois

**Genus**: [[Allophylus]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Oware et Benin 2 : 75, tab. 107 (1807); BLUME, Rumphia 3 : 130 (1847); RADLK., in ENGL. & PRANTL, Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 1, 3 (5) : 313 (1895)

## Description

RADLK., in ENGL. & PRANTL, Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 1, 3 (5) : 313 (1895).

— Schmidelia africana (P.B.) DC., Prodr. 1 : 610 (1824); BAK., in OLIV., FTA 1 : 421 (1868).

— Ornitrophe tristachyos SCHUM. & THONN., Danske Vidensk. Selsk. Skrift. 3 : 208 (1828).

Branching shrub (3–4 m), small tree (10–12 m) or large tree (20 m) reaching 30 cm in diameter; branchlets rounded, 2–3 mm in diameter, at first puberulent then glabrous or with persistent pubescence (fa. subvelutinus); bark brownish with lenticels. Leaves with 3 apiculate but not acuminate leaflets whose size defines the forms of RADLKOFER; fairly coriaceous, distinctly discolorous, reddish-brown above, brownish-green below, matte.

Inflorescence rarely presenting simple racemes mixed with branched racemes (fa. timboensis); most often 2 or more branches; length equal to that of the leaf (fa. africanus) or shorter (fa. senegalensis, timboensis); rachis puberulent; cymules subsessile, dense (6–8 per cm), with 4–5 flowers. Flowers white, fragrant. Fruit red, subspheric 5–7 mm in diameter.

To distinguish certain forms, RADLKOFER gives the following dimensions:

fa. africanus fa. senegalensis fa. timboensis

petiole petiolule mid-leaflet inflorescence domatia

4–6 cm 4–10 mm 8–18 × 3.5–8 cm (7)–10–15–(22) cm absent 2–3 cm 3–4 mm 6–8 × 2.5 cm 4–6 cm present 2 cm 2–5 mm 4–6 × 2–3 cm 4–6 cm present

fa. africanus

in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 538 (1932).

Leaflets acuminate (RADLKOFER). However, following the example of HAUMAN (Fl. Congo), only plants with leaflets having an acute apex (with 11–12 pairs of veins) or an obtuse apex (with 8–9 pairs of veins) are classified here.

Inflorescences 7–15 cm long (of which 3–5 cm for the peduncle) with (1–) 2–3 (–5) densely floriferous branches. PL. 7, 1–6, p. 41.

## Discussion

Form widely distributed in dry forests and savannas throughout tropical Africa up to 1800 m altitude: Liberia, Guinea, Côté d'Ivoire, Sierra Leone, Dahomey, Central African Republic, Congo, Zaïre, Tanzania.

Bounougou 40, Moutouroua hills, 45 km SSW Maroua (tree 2–4 m, fl., August).

Breteler 359, 7 km S Ngaoundéré (fr., Sept.); 1804, Bertoua, alt. 650 m (tree 4 m, August).

De Wilde 2491, Bangou (fl., May); 2520, Bamougoum (fr., May) (WAG).

Jacques-Félix 4550, Bétaré Oya (fl., July; deviates from type in somewhat acuminate elongate leaflets, 3 carpels, presence of a gall: cymules in balls of 2 cm with hypertrophied flowers of 1 cm).

Letouzey 612 (= SRFCam 2727), Belel (fl., March); 2167, 2224, Yoko (3 m, fl., June).

Meurillon CNAD 1592, Kékem, Ht Nkam, alt. 800 m (shrub 5–8 m, fl., July).

Mpom Benoit 389, road Bafia–Yaoundé (fl., August).

Nana Pierre 95 (= SRFCam 2737), Deng Deng–Bertoua (pole 5 m).

Preuss 1340, s.l., primary forest.

Raynal J. & A. 11980, Foulasi near Obola (2.5 m, red fr.).

Zenker 2485, Bipindi.

Its range extends to the East: Guinea (Jacques-Félix s.n.), Togo (Büttner 18), Côte d'Ivoire (Pobéguin 262) and even Angola (Mechow 94). Plants from Senegal have leaf teeth reaching 1–2 mm; those from Cameroon have teeth not exceeding 1 mm; the leaflets are rounded at the apex. The Cameroonian collections can be separated into 2 groups:

1. plants from waterside: Letouzey 7054, Biholong 167.

2. plants from granite summits or slopes up to 1400 m: Letouzey 6510, 6934, 6874, 7302.

Certain specimens of this second group then have smaller leaves and approach, in aspect, fa. timboensis (Hua) Radlkofer of which the type (Miquel 69, P) collected in June 1897 at Timbo in Fouta-Djalon, at approximately 1000 m altitude, bears 2 inflorescences at each leaf axil, one simple, the other branched. Specimens Letouzey 6874 and 6934 do have conforming leaves, but have only one branched inflorescence per axil.

Biholong 167, Makary, 100 km Fort Foureau (fl., waterside, October).

Letouzey 6510, Messengel needle hill near Moutouroua, 45 km SSW Maroua, abundant in granite scree (fl., fr., August); 7054, bank of the Serbewel near Maltam, 25 km WNW Fort Foureau (shrub 3–4 m, compact globose crown, fl., September); 7302, Bourha, 60 km SSW Mokolo, granite sand (shrub 1.5 m, red fr., October).

PLANTS RELATED TO fa. timboensis:

Letouzey 6874, Mts Mandara, 15 km NNW Mokolo, alt. 1250 m (shrub 2.5 m, fl., September); 6934, eod. loc., summit of Monts Mandara, 1442 m (fl., September).

fa. mawambensis (Gilg) Hauman

Fl. Congo 9:292 (1960).

— Allophylus mawambensis GILG, Wiss. Ergeb. Afr. Exped. 2 : 475 (1912).

Distinguished from fa. africanus by less dense inflorescences (5 cymules per cm) and longer stipitate cymules (1.5 mm). PL. 9, 1–6, p. 51.

This form is recognized fairly easily by the dark reddish-brown color of the upper surface of the limb, contrasted with the brown tint of the lower surface; the dentition is very weak (1 mm or less); the leaves are glabrous except for the petiole channel and midvein puberulent above. Subspheric to obovate fruit, 5–7 mm. It appears widely distributed in Congo and Zaïre, in rainforests, flooded or plateau forests, on riverbanks.

Form known from Senegal, Guinea, Côté d'Ivoire, Nigeria and Ghana.
