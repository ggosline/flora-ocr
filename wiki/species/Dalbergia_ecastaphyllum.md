# *Dalbergia ecastaphyllum* (L.) Taub. Planche 37

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Hedysarum ecastaphyllum L., Syst. Nat., éd. 10, 2 : 1169 (1759)

## Description

in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. 3(3): 335 (1894). Hedysarum ecastaphyllum L., Syst. Nat., ed. 10, 2: 1169 (1759). Ecastaphyllum brownei (Jacq.) Pers., Syn. pl. 2: 277 (1807).

Liane or sarmentose shrub, reaching 6 m in height and 10 m in length, inodorous, unarmed, but sometimes bearing hooked prehensile branchlets; bark blackish, with pale lenticels sometimes scarcely distinct; young branches angular, with short appressed pubescence fairly dense. Stipules early caducous, lanceolate ± falciform, 4–9.5 × 1–2.3 mm, acute, with dense appressed pubescence. Leaf unifoliolate; petiole 0.3–0.6 cm, with short blond ± appressed hairs; leaflet with petiolule 0.3–0.75 cm, blade elliptic, symmetric, 4.8–16 × 1.4–8.5 cm, rounded or very slightly cordate at base, shortly acuminate or rarely obtuse at apex, coriaceous, green or brownish when dry, clearly glaucous on lower face, with very short unidirectional appressed hairs, at first fairly dense on both faces then more sparse, even entirely caducous on upper face; lateral veins 7 to 9(–11), very marked and fairly distinctly ascending (except the lower ones), veinlets in dense reticulate pattern, slightly prominent on lower face. Inflorescence in short panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary or on old branchlets, sometimes also terminal, 1–2.5 cm long, with dense golden-brown pubescence; bracts caducous after flowering, ovate to narrowly elliptic, 0.7–1.7 × 0.4–0.5 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1.5–3 mm; bracteoles persistent or caducous after flowering, elliptic to lanceolate, 0.5–1 × 0.3–0.4 mm, pubescent; calyx brown to pale green, with fairly dense appressed pubescence, tube 1.5–2 × 1.8–2.5 mm, teeth valvate ± equal and triangular, 0.3–0.8 mm long, acute or the upper ones obtuse; corolla white, petals with claw equalling or exceeding the calyx, standard obliquely bent, with claw 2–3 mm and suborbicular limb 4.5–5 × 5–5.5 mm, with rounded base and slightly bilobed apex, wings exceeding the keel, with claw 2.5–3 mm and ± rectangular limb 5.5 × 2–2.5 mm, smooth externally, with obliquely truncate base, keel distinctly curved, with claw 2.8–3 mm and limb 3.7–4.3 × 1.7–2 mm, with shortly auriculate base; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments 5–6 mm, united over 4/5 of their length, straight at base and curved in their free part; ovary 2–2.5 × 0.5–1 mm, with dense appressed hairs or sometimes glabrescent, with 2 or 3 ovules and straight stipe 3–3.5 mm; style linear, 1–1.3 mm, with punctiform stigma. – Keel petal. – Gynecium. – Seed. Drawing by Bobbi Angell (©), reproduced with permission from Acevedo-Rodriguez (2005).

Pod with pedicel 2–3 mm and stipe 2.5–4 mm, woody, strongly compressed, discoid or shortly ellipsoid, (1.6–)2–4 × (1.5–)1.8–2.8 × 0.25–0.4 cm, reticulate and with very sparse appressed hairs, appearing glabrous to the naked eye, with 1(–2) seeds; pericarp 0.5–1 mm thick, with fairly hard outer layer formed of spongy tissue reinforced with thick woody fibers. Seed reniform, flattened, ± 13 × 8 × 2 mm.

## Distribution

Tropical America, Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni and Bioko), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola and São Tomé and Príncipe; abundant along the entire Gabonese coast, absent from the interior of the country

## Ecology

margins of mangroves and backshore thickets. Flowers and fruits almost year-round. Fruits are dispersed by water, and are often washed up on beaches. Uses: The Benga of Cap Esterias and Corisco Island would use the leaves as a fishing poison. The Nkomis of Fernan-Vaz rub their bodies with the leaves of this plant before taking their first sea bath. In Senegal, a decoction of the leaves is given by fumigations and baths, as a complement to treatment of asthenia and anemia (Berhaut 1976)

## Vernacular names

coin vine (angl.) ; ihavina (benga) ; ntehaba (fang de la Côte) ; ntehava (mpongwè, orungu, nkomi) ; tehava (ngowé) ; tsapa (béséki)

## Source note

The var. cuprea Pellegr., not validly described, should rather be linked to D. louisii. The two species are very close, and sometimes difficult to separate in the absence of fruits, but D. ecastaphyllum is strictly coastal, whereas D. louisii is found mainly in the interior. D. ecastaphyllum is the only amphi-Atlantic species of the genus. b: Berhaut (1976), Cronquist (1954), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hepper (1958), Pellegrin (1949), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961)
