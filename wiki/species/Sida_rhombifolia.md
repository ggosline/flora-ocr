# *Sida rhombifolia* L. Planche 26

**Genus**: [[Sida]]
**Family**: [[Malvaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 684 (1753)

## Description

Perennial herb or suffrutex, 0.5–1 m tall; main stem erect; branches non-distichous, covered with very fine pubescence. Stipules filiform, 5–8 mm, caducous. Leaves spiral; petiole ca. 5 mm; blade distinctly rhombic to oblong-lanceolate, 1–4.5 × 0.6–2 cm, finely tomentose below and covering the fine venation between the main veins, base cuneate, margin serrate in the upper half. Flower solitary or several grouped on a short axillary or terminal branch forming a pseudo-fascicle with many flowers ca. 4 cm long; pedicel "geniculate", composed of a peduncle ca. 3 mm and a pedicel 5 mm; sepals connate for ca. ⅔ of their length, 8 mm long, foliaceous, tomentose, becoming brown in fruit, lobes acuminate, main vein of sepals thickened toward the base; petals 12 mm long, salmon, pale yellow or white, with orange base; staminal tube 1–2 mm long, stamens free for more than half their length, anthers very numerous; ovary ± spheric with 8–10 carpels; style divided into 8–10 branches 3 mm long. Fruit with trigonous indehiscent mericarps, 2–2.5 mm long, with reticulate faces, with 1 or 2 awns 0.5–2.0 mm long.

## Distribution

Pantropical species, in Africa, from Senegal to Ethiopia, southward to Angola and Mozambique; in Gabon known from Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga, and Ogooué-Ivindo.

## Ecology

Degraded gallery forest, savannas; in Gabon at 100–700 m altitude, elsewhere at 0–1850 m. Flowers and fruits in May. Uses: The Fang use the leaves in enemas. The plant yields very strong textile fibers. With the stems, brooms are made or teeth are cleaned.

## Vernacular names
