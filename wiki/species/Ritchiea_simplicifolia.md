## Description

GILG, in ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1): 231 (1915); HUTCH. & DALZ., FWTA, ed. 1, 1 (1): 86 (1927); PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17 b: 170 (1936); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (1): 91 (1954); DE WOLF, Kirkia 1: 98, p.p. (1961); KERS, FCAM 29: 99 (1986).

– R. caloneura GILG, Bot. Jahrb. 33: 210 (1903); GILG & BENEDICT, Bot. Jahrb. 53: 181 (1915); GILG, in ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1): 232 (1915); PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17 b: 169 (1936).

– R. brachypoda auct. non GILG : DE WOLF, Kirkia 1 : 98 (1961).

– R. tessmannii GILG ex ENGLER, in ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1): 231 (1915), nom. nud.

Small shrub reaching a height of 3 m, evergreen, sparsely branched, entirely glabrous or slightly puberulent on the peduncle and floral parts. Stems slender but not flexuous, diameter at the base of 1 cm at most, wood yellowish-white and rather hard, pith white and narrow. Young branches glabrous, pale green glossy or brown, slightly striated, epidermis smooth but sometimes with elevations corresponding to lenticelle primordia. Older branches greyish-brown matte bearing numerous, clearly visible, narrowly elongate, greyish, farinose lenticels, often confluent in longitudinal rows with age and forming short cork-like crests on old wood.

Axillary buds isolated or arranged in series of 2–3, mostly dormant except at the upper leaf axils of isolated shoots.

Leaves simple or compound (1–3(–4)-foliolate, the former shortly petiolate, the latter long-petiolate; unequally distributed along the branches, rare or absent on the lower half, increasingly crowded near the apex of shoots. Texture, colour and venation similar in compound and simple leaves. Blade papyraceous to subcoriaceous, dark olive green glossy above, pale greyish-green matte below, wrinkled at least on the upper face (at high magnification!) when dry. Simple leaves elliptic oblanceolate or rarely obovate (8–13.5–20(–30) × (3.5–4.5–9.5(–10) cm, acuminate or caudate, base narrowly cuneate to attenuate decurrent on the petiole, subsessile or shortly petiolate; petiole 2–9(–12) mm, robust, grooved on the upper face, often finely rugulose, weakly articulate or not at all at the base, not decurrent on the branch. Midvein prominent below, generally straw-coloured wrinkled longitudinally, fine and depressed above. Lateral veins (9–10–13(–15) prominent below and depressed above, regularly arranged on the midvein, anastomosing in neat loops; veinlets in loose reticulum and very inconspicuous. Unifoliolate leaves rare, rather shortly petiolate, corresponding to an occasional transitional form between simple and other forms. Three-foliolate long-petiolate leaves, leaflets with equally and long-stipitate base; middle leaflet symmetric, elliptic or more often oblanceolate and obovate, (12–15.5–30 × (3.5–4.5–11.5 cm, acuminate or shortly caudate, base narrowly attenuate or cuneate; lateral leaflets slightly smaller, symmetric or slightly asymmetric at the base, of similar shape to the middle leaflet. Four-foliolate leaves rare, sometimes present simultaneously with 3-foliolate leaves. Petiolules 2–4 mm long, pale green to brown, grooved on the upper face, strongly articulate at the base. Petioles of compound leaves fine and gracile, (3.5–4.5–14.5(–15) cm long, pale green glossy, glabrous, vaguely grooved at the apex of the upper face, cylindric towards the base, not or weakly articulate and not decurrent. Stipules 1 mm long more free, narrowed to a setaceous point from a broad base, scarious, long-persistent and marcescent.

Inflorescence in a rigidly erect corymbiform raceme, reaching a length of 2.7 cm–3–10-flowered, terminal and isolated or a few racemes appearing at the leaf axils of upper leaves, rarely lower; flowers rarely isolated at the upper leaf axil. Flowers conspicuous with petals and stamens long-exserted. Rachis glabrous or very finely puberulent, generally somewhat grooved due to the decurrence of stipules, pale green, with limited growth, aphyllous but often showing a few rare scaly prophylls near the base. Floral bracts simple, sessile, narrowly elliptic or lanceolate sometimes obovate, $ 1.5 \, \text{cm} \times 3 \, \text{mm} $ for the lowermost flowers, becoming progressively smaller higher on the rachis, finely puberulent on the margins, mostly caducous before anthesis. Stipules of floral bracts very reduced, $ 1 \, \text{mm} $ at most, subulate to filiform incurved at the apex, persistent hard and coriaceous like spines at fruiting. Pedicels slender, $ 1 \, \text{mm} $ long, glabrous or finely puberulent, pale yellowish-green, mostly striated below the receptacle, cylindric and clearly articulate at the base. Receptacle broad and conical in the bud, vaguely hemispherical at anthesis, $ 0.5–1 \, \text{mm} $ high, $ 1.5 \, \text{mm} $ wide, glabrous or very weakly puberulent, persistent, subcoriaceous and almost flat at fruiting. Sepals 4, equal or nearly so, broad, ovate or obovate $ 17–25 \times 6–12 \, \text{mm} $, acute or acuminate, mucronate, papyraceous, olive green, glabrous or with very weak and sparse pubescence, ciliolate on the margin; divaricate at anthesis, they later spread horizontally and are caducous. Petals 12–30, entire, white, glabrous, $ 4–7–6.5 \, \text{cm} \times 1–3 \, \text{mm} $, narrow and lanceolate to sublinear, long-cuspidate, often twisted with non-undulate margins, fine and fragile, early caducous; petals sometimes arise in a continuous series but most often present in groups of $ 3 \, \text{to} \, 6 $ opposite $ 2 \, \text{to} \, 2 $, alternating with the sepals.

Androgynophore 0.5–1.5 mm long at fruiting, rarely exceeding the receptacle at anthesis, glabrous. Stamens ca. 50–110, conspicuous at anthesis; filaments 2.5–3.5 cm long, the outer slightly longer than the inner, white and fine with yellow base initially, turning blue towards the apex at anthesis, brown when dry; anthers oblong 1.75–3 mm, fine dorsbasifixed, early caducous. Gynophore slender glabrous, up to 2.5 cm long, slightly shorter than the filaments at anthesis, whitish turning purple after anthesis then brown on drying; at fruiting thicker than the pedicel, lignified, dark brown, somewhat verruculose, aligned with the pedicel or slightly drooping. Ovary cylindric to narrow and ellipsoid, 3.5–5.5 mm long, glabrous smooth green, vaguely 4-angulate in cross-section, unilocular with 2 parietal placentas; ovules numerous. Stigma sessile or subsessile, swollen-capitate, dark brown to blackish, margin entire or vaguely lobed generally somewhat recurved; after anthesis it broadens laterally and often ends up incorporated into the apex of the ripe fruit where it forms a grey, circular and flat spot.

Fruit oblong or fusiform sometimes ovoid when containing few seeds, often slightly curved, reaching $ 3.5 \times 1.5 $ cm, obtuse at the apex, cuneate at the base; pericarp subcoriaceous, olive green glossy, finely verruculose without ridges or grooves, without swelling at the level of the seeds. These, 1–5 per fruit, are subspherical about 2 cm in diameter, slightly compressed laterally, separated from each other by fine and fleshy dissepiments arising from the internal wall of the fruit.

## Discussion

For this species, we have followed here the broad description appearing in the Flora of Cameroon. One can likewise distinguish 2 varieties: var. simplicifolia and var. caloneura (Gilg) Kers. which differ by vegetative characters especially at the level of the leaves. The following key allows one to separate these 2 entities; extracted from the Flora of Cameroon, it has been adapted by modifying it slightly. Only var. simplicifolia is known from Gabon.

KEY TO THE VARIETIES

1. Leaves generally simple, rarely simple and compound leaves on the same branch, 1–3-foliolate. Rachis pedicels and sepals glabrous or bearing extremely short and sparse pubescence ..... var. simplicifolia.

1'. Leaves generally compound 3-foliolate, sometimes simple and compound leaves on the same branch. Rachis, pedicel and sepals glabrous ..... var. caloneura.
