# *Stanfieldiella oligantha* (Mildbr.) Brenan

**Genus**: [[Stanfieldiella]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 14 : 285 (1960)

## Description

Creeping-ascending herb, ± 30–50 cm tall; stem erect, often purplish, internodes glabrous. Leaves scattered on the stem and concentrated at the apex in an apical whorl, or sometimes the terminal leaves very reduced; sheath 1–2 cm long, with a line of dense pubescence on the suture, and generally the apex densely or sparsely covered with long pubescence; pseudpetiole short or absent; blade ovate-elliptic, fairly broad, 10–16 cm long, base cuneate, apex acuminate to attenuate, glabrous or sometimes with a few long hairs at the base, lower surface purplish or reddish. Inflorescence in a terminal compound thyrse, subsessile, fairly dense, globose, ± 2–3 cm in diameter; bracts narrowly elliptic-ovate; cincinni with bisexual flowers. Flower: pedicel ± 10 mm, strongly declined during fruiting, with glandular pubescence; sepals narrowly elliptic-ovate, 5 × 2.5 mm, with red glandular pubescence; petals elliptic to narrowly elliptic-obovate, 4–5(–6.5) × 1.5–1.8 mm, glabrous, white tinged with pink; stamens with filament 3–6 mm, white, anther discoid, 0.7–1 mm in diameter, yellow; ovary oblong, ± 2 × 1 mm, pubescent at the apex; style ± 5 mm, as long as the petals or clearly emerging from the bud before anthesis. Fruit reflexed to curved, cylindric, 2.5–3 mm wide, up to 2 times longer than the calyx, glabrous, smooth. Seed verrucose, pink.

## Distribution

Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko) and Gabon; in Gabon in Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Primary forest on rocks, fallows, cacao plantations, granitic soil, inselbergs, often in colonies; in Gabon from 360 to 760 m altitude, elsewhere from 70 to 1350 m. Flowers in February, March, June and October; flowers opening at 3 p.m.

## Source note

The blade and petiole are less densely ciliate than in S. imperforata, and the flowers open in the afternoon at 3 p.m. (instead of in the morning). See also the note under S. imperforata. B: Brenan (1968b), Cabezas et al. (2014), Morton (1967), Sosef et al. (2006). Bibliography
