# *Napoleonaea vogelii* Hook. & Planch. Planche 14

**Genus**: [[Napoleonaea]]
**Family**: [[Lecythidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Icon. Pl. 8 : tab. 799-800 (1848)

## Description

Shrub or small tree 3–11 m tall. Leaf: petiole 1–5 mm, canaliculate, black or sometimes brown; blade ovate to elliptic-oblong or ± obovate, (5–)7.5–15(–18) × (2–)2.5–7 cm, cuneate to obtuse at the base, apex abruptly terminating in an acumen 7–22 mm long, papyraceous to coriaceous, margin entire, weakly undulate or denticulate, abaxial surface with conspicuous leaf glands, variable in number at the base or along the marginal vein; lateral veins 5–7(–9) pairs, free in the lower half and joined in arches in the upper half of the blade; tertiary veins scalariform. Inflorescences cauliflorous, ramiflorous or axillary; bracts orbicular, up to 2 × 4 mm in diameter, glabrous. Flowers solitary or in fascicles; calyx 12–22 mm in diameter, puberulent to glabrous, smooth to strongly verrucose, persistent, with lobes 4–6 × 4–6 mm bearing 2 glands at the apex; first corona 25–40 mm in diameter, white tinged with red; third corona erect, white. Fruit globose to transversely ellipsoid, 2.2–5 × 3.5–7 cm, smooth, orange or greenish, marked with white spots. Seeds reaching 25 × 15 mm.

## Distribution

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon common and already observed in almost all provinces

## Ecology

Dense humid forests, seaside or swamp forests; in Gabon up to 500 m elevation, elsewhere up to 1100 m

## Uses

The species would be sought for its fruits and also to treat stomach ailments. B: Aubréville (1959), Hutchinson & Dalziel (1954), Liben (1971), Akah et al. (2007)
