# *Polyspatha paniculata* Benth. Figure 6(C)

**Genus**: [[Polyspatha]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]
**Protologue**: P. glaucescens (C.B.Clarke) Hutch., Fl. W. trop. Afr. 2 : 320 (1936)

## Description

in Hook., Niger Fl. : 543 (1849). P. glaucescens (C.B.Clarke) Hutch., Fl. W. trop. Afr. 2 : 320 (1936). Herb 15–30(–60) cm tall; stolons slender, up to several tens of cm long; stem with internodes covered only with hooked hairs or mixed with short uniseriate hairs. Leaves scattered on the stem and some concentrated at the apex; sheath (1–)1.5–2(–2.3) cm long, puberulent, ciliolate at the apex; pseudopetiole ± 1–2 cm; blade narrowly elliptic to ovate or obovate, (3.5–)7–17(–19) × (2.5–)3–5.5(–6.5) cm, base cuneate, apex acuminate to acute, margins glabrous, upper surface glabrous or scabrous, lower surface sparsely puberulent. Inflorescence strictly terminal or terminal and accompanied by a few others at the upper axils, the most apical sometimes piercing the sheath, 5–15 × 0.5–4 cm, bearing up to 7 simple thyrses, each with a short peduncle and an elongate axis clearly zigzag, with reflexed hairs, with 7 to 16 sessile distichous spathes, at first erect, then deflexed, covering the rachis, falciform, 8–11 × 3–5 mm (folded), puberulent, reddish-brown, with acuminate to acute apex, with ciliolate margins. Flower: pedicel 1.5–2 mm, sparsely puberulent; sepals 4–5(–8) × 1–2(–4.5) mm at anthesis, white translucent, petaloid, up to 6.5 × 2.5 mm in fruit, glabrous or with ciliolate margins and dorsal nerve; upper petals broadly spathulate, ± 10 × 5 mm (including 4–6 mm for the claw), the lower one almost linear, 4 × 1 mm; stamens subequal, with filament 6–9 mm, yellow; staminodes with filament 3–5 mm, white; ovary white. Fruit oblong-ellipsoidal, 3.5–4 × 2–3 mm, with rounded to truncate apex. Seed ellipsoidal, 2.5–2.8 × 1.4–1.7 mm; testa weakly or distinctly wrinkled with (12–)14 to 18 continuous ridges, smooth, light brown, sometimes (partially) covered by a pale brown layer.

## Distribution

Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko and Río Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Uganda, Tanzania, Sudan and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon in all nine provinces

## Ecology

wet soils, humid dense forest, disturbed areas; in Gabon up to 890 m altitude, from 1150 to 1400 m in East Africa, elsewhere up to 1300 m. Flowers and fruits all year in central Africa (in February and from September to December in West Africa). Flowering in the afternoon. B: Brenan (1968b), van der Burg (2006), Cabezas et al. (2014), Faden (2012), Morton (1967), Sosef et al. (2006), Vandeweg he et al. (2016)
