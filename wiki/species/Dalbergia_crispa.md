# *Dalbergia crispa* Hepper

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 1956 : 132 (1956)

## Description

Lianescent shrub 2–5 m tall, inodorous, unarmed, and apparently without prehensile branches; bark dark brown, generally with lighter lenticels; young branches with spreading ± crisp, short and dense pubescence. Stipules early caducous, elliptic, falciform, 2.5–10 × 1–4 mm, acute or obtuse, with short and dense appressed pubescence. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.8–2 cm, rachis 1.5–7.7 cm, with short and dense hairs similar to those of the branches; leaflets 4 to 7, alternate and unequal, petiolules 2.5–5 mm, blade elliptic or

slightly obovate, symmetric or nearly so, 4.5–11 × 2–6.3 cm in the upper leaflets and 1.8–5 × 1.1–3.2 cm in the lower ones, rounded to acute at the base, acuminate or acute at the apex, coriaceous, browning when dry and generally glaucescent beneath, upper surface glabrous or with very scattered crisp hairs, lower surface with brown crisp ± erect hairs and fairly long (0.25–0.5 mm); lateral veins 6 to 12, very prominent and strongly ascending, much more salient than the veinlets, reaching nearly the margin of the blade, veinlets in fairly dense raised reticulation. Inflorescence in short and dense panicle of unilateral racemes, axillary and terminal on the leafy branches, 1–3 cm in length, with dense crisp hairs similar to those of the branches; bracts caducous, ovate to broadly elliptic, ± 1 × 0.6 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1.5–3 mm; bracteoles caducous, broadly elliptic, ± 1 × 0.6 mm, pubescent; calyx pale green, with dense and golden-brown ± crisp hairs, tube 1.7–2.2 × 2.2–3 mm, teeth valvate, ± equal and broadly triangular, 0.3–1 mm in length; corolla white, petals with claw exceeding the calyx, standard obliquely bent, with claw 2.2–2.7 mm and suborbicular blade 3.3–3.5 × 3.5 mm, rounded at the base and bilobed at the apex, wings equalling or slightly exceeding the keel, with claw 2.5 mm and blade 3.7 × 1.5–1.8 mm, smooth externally, truncate at base or shortly auriculate, keel slightly curved, with claw 2.5 mm and blade 3–3.5 × 1.5 mm, auriculate at base; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5 (or exceptionally all fused), filaments 5–5.5 mm, fused for ¾ to 4/5 of their length, straight at the base and curved in their free part; ovary 1.5–2 × 0.7–0.8 mm, glabrous or with dense appressed hairs, with 1 or 2 ovules, with straight stipe 3–3.5 mm; style linear, 1–1.3 mm, with punctiform stigma. Legume with pedicel 3–5 mm and stipe 3–4 mm, woody, discoid, with thick central part and bosselated, margin progressively thinned, 1.5–3 × 1.5–2.6 × 0.6–0.8 cm, with very dense brown felted hairs, 1-seeded; pericarp ± 0.7 mm thick, hard and entirely woody, with glabrous endocarp.

## Distribution

Sierra Leone, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, fairly rare, collected in Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

secondary forests and river edges; 30–480 m altitude in Gabon. In Gabon, flowers in August and September and fruits in October

## Source note

Our description is based on specimens from Central Africa. These differ slightly from material from Sierra Leone (where the species was described) by their thicker legumes and their leaflets with persistent brown hairs (not whitish and fairly rapidly caducous). However, the West African material we have seen is incomplete, and we therefore do not know if these differences are truly significant. D. crispa is very close to D. pachycarpa, but differs by its leaflets with ± erect crisp hairs, and its generally smaller legumes with glabrous endocarp. D. crispa can also be confused with D. nervosa; for the differences, see that species. Another close species, D. rugosa, which differs mainly by its fruits (Hepper 1956, 1958) exists in Sierra Leone and Liberia. It has been reported from Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006) by confusion with D. crispa. D. crispa is reported here for the first time from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (according to specimens Dacremont 295 and Vanderyst 7788 (BR) both from Matadi). Bibliography: Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Hepper (1956, 1958).
