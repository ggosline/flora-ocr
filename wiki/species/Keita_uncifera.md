# *Keita uncifera* (J.Louis & Boutique) Cheek

**Family**: Aptandraceae (in [[Olacaceae]] s.l.) · **Genus**: [[Keita]]

Kew Bull. **79**: 317–332 (2024).

**Basionym**: *Anacolosa uncifera* J.Louis & Boutique, Bull. Jard. Bot. Brux.
**18**: 256 (1947). — J.Louis & J.Léonard, Fl. Congo b. et R.U. **1**: 264,
tab. 25 (1948).

Treated in Flore du Gabon Vol 20 (1973) as *Anacolosa uncifera*; the
description below is translated from that treatment.

## Description

**Habit**: liana 8–10 m long. Old stem reddish or grey, strongly ribbed. Young
branchlets russet with a short deciduous pubescence. The base of each branchlet
subtended by a **circinate hook**, the base of the hook often fused with that
of the branchlet; the hook sometimes bearing a leaf at its apex.

**Leaves**: petiole articulate near its base, 0.5–1 cm long, canaliculate
above, ± pubescent and wrinkled-pubescent beneath. Blade glabrous on both
faces, very dark brown or black above, greenish, brownish-green or brown
beneath, elliptic to broadly elliptic, 3–7 × 1.8–3 cm; base rounded or
attenuate, almost cuneate; apex attenuate, shortly acuminate, obtuse. Midrib
concave above, prominent and weakly wrinkled beneath. Secondary nerves in 3–6
pairs, ascending, arcuate, anastomosing 1–2 mm from the margin, concave above,
prominent beneath. Nervules forming a fairly dense reticulum, obscure above,
prominent beneath.

**Inflorescence**: very short axillary panicled racemes, solitary,
many-flowered (20–30 flowers). Rachis 2–3 mm long. Flower bud conical.

**Flowers**: female, hexamerous, greenish. Calyx with alternipetalous sepals
fused for almost their whole length, the upper margin undulate to almost
toothed, puberulent outside, 0.5–1 mm long. Petals puberulent outside and
inside on the upper half, valvate, erect, very weakly reflexed outwards, 3 mm
long, fleshy. Stamens 6, fused by the filament to the petals, reflexed towards
the interior of the flower; filament ribbon-like, 0.4–0.6 mm long; anther
subterminal, introrse, with arcuate dehiscence slits. Disc adnate to the ovary,
cupuliform, with 6 fleshy lobes. Ovary superior, with 1–2 uniovulate locules.
Placentation central. Style short, stout, weakly conical, ending in a weakly
capitate stigma.

**Fruit**: drupes solitary or in groups of 2–3, ellipsoid, about 2 cm long.
Disc accrescent, forming a greenish envelope, puberulent outside, finely
longitudinally striate, around the fruit. Endocarp coriaceous, 0.5 mm thick.

**Seed**: ellipsoid, longitudinally furrowed. Embryo apical.

## Types

SYNTYPES: *Lebrun 5908*, Lumuma, Maniema, District forestier central, Zaire
(BR); *Louis 13598*, Yangole near Yangambi, District forestier central, Zaire,
Feb. 1939 (BR).

## Distribution

**Range**: from Gabon to Zaire (Democratic Republic of the Congo).
**Gabon**: Ogooué-Ivindo (Bélinga); also collected on the Ogooué.

## Habitat and ecology

Primary swamp forest and periodically inundated forest. Collected at Bélinga at
about 1000 m altitude.

## Notes

**Generic transfer (2024).** Cheek, Molmou, Gosline & Magassouba removed the two
continental African species from *Anacolosa* — an otherwise Asian genus — and
placed them in the new genus [[Keita]], tentatively in Aptandraceae. The
African species are unique within Olacaceae s.l. in being **climbers**, using
perennial hook-like structures built from five separate traits found nowhere
else in the group. Its sister species, *K. deniseae* Cheek, is endemic to
Simandou in the Republic of Guinea and is assessed as Endangered.

Villiers had already made the observation that underpins the split without
drawing the generic conclusion: his key separates this plant at the first
couplet as the only liana of the family bearing circinate hooks, and his
description records the hook at the base of each branchlet in detail.

With this transfer, [[Anacolosa]] is no longer represented in Gabon.

The Vol 20 treatment gives a Gabonese list only — no *MATÉRIEL CAMEROUNAIS
ÉTUDIÉ* section exists for this taxon, and Villiers gives the range as Gabon to
Zaire. The absence of Cameroonian material here is real, not an ingest gap.
