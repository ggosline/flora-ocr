# *Lavigeria macrocarpa* (Oliver) Pierre

**Genus**: [[Lavigeria]]
**Family**: [[Icacinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Forest. Cochinch. 4, tab. 267, in obs. (1892)

## Description

— L. salutaris PIERRE, 1.c.

— Icacina macrocarpa OLIV., FTA 1 : 357 (1868). — Hooker, Ic. Pl. 24 : tab. 2338 (1894).

Liana with stem reaching up to 4 cm in diameter, with subquadrangular section. Branches tomentose, yellow to ferruginous. Petiole 1–2 cm long, tomentose, longitudinally striated. Blade glabrous on both surfaces, rather pale green above, very pale brown or light brown below, oblong or narrowly obovate, 16–33 × 7–11 cm, rounded or narrowed at the base, acute, obtuse or retuse at the apex. Midvein concave on the upper surface, prominent pubescent longitudinally striated on the lower surface. Secondary veins 6–11 pairs, ascending, arched, anastomosing 2–5 mm from the blade margin, prominent on both surfaces, pubescent below. Veinlets in dense network prominent on both surfaces.

Inflorescence a many-flowered panicle on old wood. Rachis tomentose. Floral bud ♀ 3 × 2–2.5 mm, ellipsoid. Flower ♀ pentamerous, 6 × 4.5 mm. Bracteole pubescent externally and glabrous internally, linear, obtuse at the apex. Sepals connate, pubescent externally, glabrous internally, broadly elliptic, 4 × 2 mm; apex acute. Petals free, glabrous, erect, 4 × 2 mm, lanceolate; apex acute. Stamens free, erect, 5 mm long; filament arched 4 mm long, widened at the base, connate with the connective at the upper 1/3 of the latter; anthers with 2 ellipsoid locules. Pistil 2.5 mm long; ovary pubescent to hirsute, ovoid; style short and glabrous terminating in a capitate stigma.

Drupe ellipsoid or oblong 8 cm long, red in color at maturity. Endocarp woody, ± pubescent on the internal surface. Seed oblong with embryo equalling the albumen. PL. 15, p. 67.

## Type

Jolly 153, Gabon (P!)

## Discussion

This species is found from Nigeria to Zaire, in forest groupings or forest galleries.

## Vernacular names

agoukoum-obvimondsamin-ovan (fang)

## Uses

The fruit is edible and the sarcocarp is used as an aphrodisiac.
