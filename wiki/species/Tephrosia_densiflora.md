# *Tephrosia densiflora* Hook.f.

**Genus**: [[Tephrosia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 114 (1871)

## Description

Erect shrub ± 2 m tall; stems with dense yellow or ferruginous pubescence, hairs short and long. Stipules lanceolate, 0.8–1 cm long, striate. Leaf: petiole 1–2 cm; rachis 5–8(–10) cm, brown hairy, not prolonged beyond the lateral leaflets; leaflets 9 to 19, the terminal one with elliptic-oblanceolate blade, reaching 3.5–5 × 1.5 cm, apex emarginate, appressed tomentose above, somewhat denser below. Inflorescence in pseudoracemes up to 15 cm long, brown tomentose. Flower: pedicel 10–12 mm, up to 30 mm in fruit; calyx brown, tomentose, tube ± 2 mm long, lobes oblong, the upper ones broadly united for 1–2 mm, the lower one lanceolate, 5–6 mm long; standard suborbicular, ± 20 × 15 mm, violet to mauve within, silky and whitish-green without, wings obovate, ± 18 × 5 mm, glabrous, keel falcate, ± 14 × 5 mm, the lower margins pubescent; stamens with tube ± 19 mm, free part 4–8 mm; ovary with dense pubescence; style incurved at apex, dorsally slightly pubescent. Pod straight or falcate, reaching 11 × 1.1 × 0.4 cm, apex straight or slightly curved, with 10 to 20 seeds, with very dense cream pubescence, sutures brown, tomentose. Seed transversely oblique, ± 6 × 4.5 × 3 mm, brown, smooth; hilum eccentric, near the narrow side.

## Distribution

West Africa, from Guinea to Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon, known only from the Estuary

## Ecology

among rocks near rivers, also cultivated; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Flowers in July. Uses: Used as an ichthyotoxin. The plant has some medicinal properties

## Source note

Collected only once in Gabon. B: Burkill (1995), Chevalier (1937a), Watt & Breyer-Brandwijk (1962). Bibliography
