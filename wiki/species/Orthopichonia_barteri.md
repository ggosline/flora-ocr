# *Orthopichonia barteri* (Stapf) H.Huber Planche 84

**Genus**: [[Orthopichonia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kew Bull. 15 : 437 (1962)

## Description

Liane, reaching 30 m in length. Leaf: petiole 7–14 mm; lamina elliptic to elliptic-oblong, rarely ovate, 1.5–4.5 times as long as wide, 2.5–9 × 1–3 cm, cuneate at the base, with acuminate apex over 5–15 mm, glabrous on both faces; secondary nerves 30 to 50 pairs, alternating with less visible nerves. Inflorescence axillary, often apparently terminal, solitary or up to together, sometimes rather lax, more often condensed into dense fascicles of 4–15 mm with 1–4 flowers; pedicel 0.5–3 mm. Flower: sepal 1.2–2 mm; calyx with lobes 2–4 times as long as wide, long of 0.7–1.3 mm; corolla white, sometimes with green tube (sometimes tinged with pink), long of 7.5–13 mm in the mature bud, with oblong and rounded head, of 0.35–0.5 of the length of the bud, glabrous on the outside, with tube long of 4.5–7 mm, widest at 1.9–2.9 mm above the base and above, wide of 0.7–1.4 mm, then constricted just above the anthers; on the inside with a pilose band at 1–2 mm above the base up to the throat; with oblong to narrowly oblong lobes, 1.5–3 times as long as the tube, 1.5–2.5 times as long as wide, of 1.7–5.5 × 0.8–2.5 mm, rounded at the apex, glabrous, ± ciliate or sometimes sparsely hairy at the base inside; stamens inserted at 1.2–2.3 mm from the base of the tube, anther long of 0.5–0.9 mm; pistil long of 1.4–2.3 mm, ovary glabrous, apex of the pistil situated at 0.6–1.8 mm below the apex of the anthers. Fruit ± globular, often with a stipiform base, sometimes velvety, rugulose, 1.5–2.5 mm, yellow or orange. Seed irregularly ellipsoid or reniform, long of 1–1.5 mm. Distribution: Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Central African Republic; in Gabon, Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: primary and secondary forest in Gabon, up to 800 m altitude; elsewhere up to 1,200 m. References: Beentje (2021), Vonk (1989).
