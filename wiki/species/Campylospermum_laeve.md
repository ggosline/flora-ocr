# *Campylospermum laeve* (De Wild. & T.Durand) Farron Planche 15

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 35 : 399 (1965)

## Description

Ouratea laevis De Wild. & T.Durand, Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belgique 38(2) : 34 (1899). Ouratea buchneri Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 270 (1902). Ouratea lundensis Cavaco, Bull. Mus. Natl. Hist. Nat. sér. 2, 26 : 641 (1954). Tree reaching 15 m in height; trunk up to 16 cm in diameter; branches with brownish-whitish bark. Stipules caducous, triangular to narrowly triangular, 6–9 mm long. Leaf: petiole (6–)10–20(–25) mm, stout; lamina elliptic to narrowly elliptic or narrowly elliptic-oblong, (6–)14–29(–36) × (2.5–)4–8(–10) cm, ratio (2.0–)2.5–6.0(–8.5), attenuate to narrowly cuneate or sometimes broadly cuneate at base, acuminate at apex, often abruptly so, or rarely obtuse, coriaceous, margin distinctly thickened and often revolute, serrulate; primary vein plane to slightly prominent on upper surface, prominent on lower surface, lateral veins in 9 to 12(–15) pairs, generally prominent above, prominent to slightly prominent below, tertiary venation scalariform, perpendicular to primary vein, distinct above and below. Inflorescence terminal, branched, lax, main axis (5–)15–30(–35) cm, more or less angular; gemmate scales absent; racemes 1 to 5(–7), (2–)5–9(–14) cm, spreading to erect-spreading; bracts caducous, 1–2 mm long; cymules distant by 0.5–1.5 cm, 1- to 7-flowered. Flower: pedicel 10–12 mm, articulate 1–2 mm from base; sepals ovate, (6–)9–11 × 3–5 mm in flower, 8–12 × 4–6 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 6–12 × 3–5 mm, base cuneate, apex rounded; stamens: anthers 4–6 mm long; style 5–6 mm. Fruit: receptacle approximately 5 × 5 mm; drupels 3 or 4 well developed per receptacle, ellipsoid, 9 × 5–6 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda, Lunda Norte); in Gabon, not common, known from Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime.

## Ecology

Primary and riparian forest; in Gabon, at 30–400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 750 m. Use: The wood is used for construction and the leaves can be used against headaches and tooth pain. The dried leaves are smoked as tobacco (Bamps & Farron, 1967). Taxonomic note: This species is easily distinguished by its serrulate and revolute margin. The variation in the lamina ratio is remarkable: 2.0 to 8.5. It resembles C. engama by the long petiole, but differs from it by the lamina which is not decurrent on the petiole and by its shorter racemes. Furthermore, it was represented in 1986 on a postage stamp of Gabon. Bibliography: Bamps & Farron (1967), Bissiengou (2014), Farron (1963, 1968, 1985).
