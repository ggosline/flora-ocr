# *Pseudospondias longifolia* Engl.

**Genus**: [[Pseudospondias]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 36 : 218 (1905)

## Description

P. microcarpa (A.Rich.) Engl. var. longifolia (Engl.) Keay, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 26 : 203 (1956). Haematostaphis pierreana Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 36 : 219 (1905). Medium-sized tree, 8–30(–50) m tall; trunk ± straight, up to 25(–55) cm in diameter, sometimes with buttresses or counterforts at the base; branchlets with small erect hairs. Leaves opposite or alternate; petiole and rachis 15–50(–60) cm, puberulent to glabrous; petiole semicylindric, swollen at the base; leaflets (5–)13 to 21, petiolules channeled, flattened, 0.5–1.5 cm, with blade variable, asymmetric or not, oblong-elliptic, 5–17 × 2.5–6(–10) cm, obliquely cuneate to rounded at the base, acuminate over (0.5–)1–1.5 cm at the apex, ± glabrous, with 6 to 10 pairs of lateral veins. Inflorescence terminal and/or axillary, 20–60 × 15–40 cm, puberulent to glabrous. Flower 3-merous, glabrous. Male flower: calyx slightly gamosepalous, with 3 ± elliptic lobes 0.6–0.8 mm in length; petals broadly elliptic, 1.3–1.8 mm in length, white to yellow; stamens 6, free, shorter than the petals; pistillode distinct. Female flower: slightly larger than the male; staminodes 6; ovary subglobose, ± 1 mm in diameter, with 3 locules (one of which fertile); styles 3. Fruit ellipsoid, (2–)2.5–3.5 × 1.5–2 cm, with edible pulp, red to dark red at maturity; stone cylindric, 1.8–2 × 0.6–1 cm, with 3 subapical opercula, with 1 seed. Seed oblong, compressed.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

Secondary or secondarized forest; in Gabon up to 350 m altitude, elsewhere up to 600 m. Uses: The fruits can be used to make jams or alcohol. The bark has been used in traditional medicine against toothache and ulcerations

## Source note

The name Haematostaphis pierreana was treated as a synonym of P. microcarpa by Keay (1958), but because of its 3-merous flowers, it was correctly placed as a synonym of P. longifolia by Van der Veken (1960a). B: Keay (1958), Meunier et al. (2015), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Van der Veken (1960a), Wilks & Issembé (2000)
