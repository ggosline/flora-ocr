Liane or shrub. Branches cylindric, young branches often somewhat angled with dense rust-brown pubescence.

Leaves 4-16(-19)-jugate; petiole 2-10 cm long, rachis 12-27 cm long, dense rust-brown pubescence. Leaflets papyraceous, the lateral ones ± alternate or sometimes opposite, ovate to narrowly oblong, 2-13 × 1-5 cm, with rounded, truncate or subcordate base, equal or slightly unequal; the terminal leaflet elliptic or narrowly (ob)ovate, 4-10 × 1.5-5 cm, cuneate at base; all leaflets acuminate or obtuse, glabrous or rarely somewhat pubescent above, densely pubescent below; midvein impressed above, prominent below; lateral veins 5-12 pairs; tertiary veins reticulate, generally indistinct; petiolules 1.5-3 mm long, with dense brown hairs.

Inflorescences in panicles or (pseudo)racemes, 1-10 per axil, on young branches (often from reduction of axillary leaves results a (pseudo)terminal compound inflorescence), 5-20 cm long, ≤ 100-flowered, with dense rust-brown hairs and with short glandular hairs. Bracts ovate to subulate, 1-1.5 mm long. Pedicels articulate 1-2.5 mm below the calyx, dense brown pubescence.

Flowers nearly homostylous. Sepals never reflexed, in imbricate prefloration, ovate to narrowly elliptic, 2-4 × 0.8-1.3 mm, acute at apex, dense brown indumentum, generally accompanied by short glandular hairs, inner surface pubescent. Petals in imbricate prefloration, orbicular to slightly obovate, 1-2 × 1-1.5(-2) mm, rounded or truncate at base and apex, glabrous (rarely with hairs on outer side), veins generally indistinct. Stamens ± united at base, subequal, 0.7-1.2 mm long, anthers ≈ 0.2 × ≈ 0.3 mm, bearing a basal appendage. Pistil: carpels 0.5-1.5 mm long; ovaries ≈ 0.5 mm long, brown pubescence; styles most often strongly curved towards the anthers, pubescent; stigmas oblique.

Fruits: mature follicles 1-5 per flower, often united at base, ovoid, ± oblique, 2-4.5(-5) × 1-2.5 cm, rounded or truncate at base; beak blunt and broad, 5-20 mm long, often indistinctly marked; pericarp with short red hairs on outer surface while inner surface bears long brownish hairs.

Seeds ovoid, 12-20 × 5-10 mm, sarcotesta 3-7 mm long.

Epigeal germination. - Pl. 14, p. 51.

## Type

Smeathman s.n., s.l., Sierra Leone

## Distribution

Tropical Africa from Gambia to East Zaire and South Sudan and, to the south, to North Angola; species common in Gabon

## Ecology

Ombrophilous forests, in savanna thickets, often in secondary growth of pastures, in strips of vegetation bordering lagoons and near beaches. Flowering is found at any time of the year

## Uses

In different parts of Africa, C. ferruginea is reported as a medicinal plant. Its roots are a remedy for dysentery, syphilis, gonorrhoea, migraine and snake bites; they are also said to be purgative. A decoction constitutes an aphrodisiac and also a preventive against abortion and ovarian troubles. Its bark reduced to powder mixed with gums is applied to gum swelling in cases of pyorrhea (AINSLIE 1937). Its leaves are laxative and febrifugal. Frictions with its boiled leaves relieve puerperal pains as well as other pains (BERHAUT 1975, IRVINE 1961). A decoction of leaves is used as an abortifacient as well as to combat colds and the juice of leaves is administered in drops in the eyes against ophthalmias. The fruits, mentioned as bitter, juicy and perhaps toxic (KERHARO 1971) are boiled in water and used against bronchitis, coughing fits, tuberculosis and wounds. Ground in rum or palm wine, they have been noted as a remedy against snake bites (AINSLIE 1937, TROUPIN 1952)
