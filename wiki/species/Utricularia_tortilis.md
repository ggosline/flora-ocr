# *Utricularia tortilis* Welw. ex Oliv. Planche 46, Figure 17(E, F)

**Genus**: [[Utricularia]]
**Family**: [[Lentibulariaceae]]
**Protologue**: J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 9 : 150 (1865)

## Description

U. spiralis var. tortilis (Welw. ex Oliv.) P.Taylor, Taxon 12 : 294 (1963). Terrestrial herb; rhizoids and capillary stolons numerous at the base of the flowering scape. Leaves few, disappeared at anthesis, opposite on the stolons, linear, reaching 5 × 0.25 cm, 1- to 3-nerved. Utricles numerous, globose, shortly stipitate, 0.6–1 mm in length, glandular; basal orifice, with upper lip furnished with 2 simple, subulate appendices. Inflorescence erect or usually voluble, 5–50 cm in length, with 1 to 15 distant flowers; pedicel filiform, glabrous; bracts basifixed, broadly ovate, ± 2 mm in length, at least 4 times as wide as the bracteoles. Flower: bracteoles very narrowly elliptic-ovate, shorter than the bracts; pedicel erect or ascending, 3–6 mm at anthesis, reaching 15 mm at maturity, flattened or ± narrowly winged, always longer than the fruiting calyx; calyx with subequal lobes, ovate to narrowly ovate, the upper acute, the lower slightly 2-dentate at the apex; corolla 5–10 mm in length, violet with a throat marked with a dark blue, greenish, yellowish or white spot, rarely entirely yellow or white, upper lip narrowly oblong, not or scarcely longer than the upper lobe of the calyx, lower lip orbicular, palate prominent, spur ordinarily curved, acute at the apex. Fruit narrowly ovoid, dehiscent by an abaxial longitudinal fissure with non-thickened edges, with numerous seeds. Seed spherical, 0.2–0.3 mm in diameter; testa thin, with distinct cells, ± isodiametric.

## Distribution

Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Mali, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Nigeria, Central African Republic, Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Botswana and Angola; in Gabon, observed in the Haut-Ogooué

## Ecology

swampy savannas on Kalahari sands; in Gabon, at 400 m altitude, in East Africa, between 0 and 1860 m. B : Taylor (1964, 1972, 1989). Bibliography
