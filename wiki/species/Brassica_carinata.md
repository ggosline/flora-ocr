## Description

Annual plant glabrous or somewhat hispid at the base of the stems and on the petioles. Root slender, taproot. Stems erect with long oblique branches in the upper part, most often 30–150 cm tall. Basal leaves with obovate blade reaching 20 × 10 cm, margins dentate or crenate, narrowing into a long petiole, base not auriculate, a pair of small ± triangular lateral segments, present or absent. Cauline leaves obovate and obtuse, the upper ones oblanceolate and acute, attenuated into a ± distinct petiole, not auriculate, ordinarily 5–10 cm long, margins entire or crenate.

Corymb-like racemes not condensed, flowers numerous. Fruit-bearing inflorescences reaching up to 50 cm in length. Fruiting pedicels oblique, 5–12 mm long. Sepals green, oblong, 4–6 mm. Petals pale yellow with obovate unguiculate blade, 6–10 mm long. Stamens with anthers 1.5–2.5 mm long. Ovary containing 10–20 ovules. Siliques oblique, linear, often somewhat torulose sometimes inflated, 25–65 × 1–3.5 mm, attenuated into a conical beak, aspermatous, 2–8 mm long. Valves with distinct midrib, ± carinate. Seeds dark brown, spherical, distinctly reticulate, 1–1.5 mm wide. – Pl. 00, p. 00.

## Discussion

This species is probably indigenous to Éthiopie where it is frequently cultivated and highly polymorphous. Very widespread as an adventive plant in certain regions of tropical Africa (and also cultivated), it has often been confused with Brassica juncea (L.) Czern. from the region of the steppes of Russia.
