# *Alvesia rosmarinifolia* Welw. Planche 3

**Genus**: [[Alvesia]]
**Protologue**: Trans. Linn. Soc. 27 : 55, t. 19 (1869)

## Synonyms

- Plectranthastrum rosmarinifolium (Welw.) B.Mathew, Kew Bull. 25 : 408 (1971).

## Description

Shrub or herb with woody base, 1–1.5 m tall, reported as slightly fetid; stem often with fascicles of young leaves or young shoots in the axils, pubescent or densely pubescent with hairs directed upward, often violaceous distally, with scattered sessile glands scarcely visible. Leaf: petiole 0–2 mm; blade narrowly elliptic to linear, 2–6 × 0.5–0.9 cm, cuneate to attenuate at base, acute at apex, entire and strongly revolute, ± glabrous above, paler below, tomentose, with scattered sessile glands. Inflorescence violaceous; cymes sessile, with (3 to) 5 to 7 flowers; bracts sessile, 4–5 mm long, elliptic, with a gland-like structure on the external face clearly visible. Flower: pedicel 7–15 mm; calyx 2–3 mm long, pinkish, tomentose, persistent after anthesis, ovoid, vesiculous, 25–30 mm long, distinctly veined, attached to the pedicel at the middle of the truncate base, 3-lobed, all lobes pressed together to close the throat; corolla 17–22 mm long, violet, with tube 10 mm long, bent at right angles at the base, with a dorsal spur, upper lip shorter than the lower, reflected against the tube, lower lip 7–12 mm long. Fruit: achenes broadly ovoid, 5 mm long, slightly flattened, dark brown, with numerous red sessile glands.

## Distribution

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola and Zambia; in Gabon reported from Ogooué-Maritime, only one specimen seen, collected in the vicinity of Cape Lopez in 1913; could be more frequent in Gabon, particularly to be sought on the Batéké Plateaus. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

periodically inundated zones in dry forest; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 1400 m. Bi: Paton et al. (2013). Bibliography
