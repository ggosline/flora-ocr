# *Phaulopsis barteri* (T. Anders.) Lindau

**Genus**: [[Phaulopsis]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Lindau, in Engler-Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam., Nachtr. zu II-IV, 1:305 (1897)

## Description

— Heine, F.W.T.A., ed. 2,2: 399 (1963).

≡ Phaylopsis Barteri T. Anders, Journ. Linn. Soc., Bot. 7 : 27 (1863); C. B. Clarke, F.T.A. 5 : 86 (1899).

≡ Micranthus Barteri (T. Anders.) O. Kuntze, Rev. Gen. 2 : 493 (1891).

Plants very much resembling those of the following species, about 1 m high, ± glabrous or very finely pubescent. Petioles up to 3 cm long. Leaf blade elliptic-lanceolate, elongate, 14 × 4 cm, with entire margins, acuminate, attenuate at the base and long-decurrent on the petiole; base of blade symmetric, never notably oblique.

Inflorescences terminal and axillary, strobiliform, pubescent, up to 2.5–8 cm long and 2.5 cm in diameter (without the expanded corollas); bracts oval-orbicular, 10 × 6 mm, symmetric; bracteoles elliptic 9 × 4 mm, obtuse; posterior calyx segment resembling the bracteoles and of the same dimensions; lateral segments filiform-aciculate 5 mm long; anterior segments obovate-spatulate 8–10 mm long and 2.5 mm wide in the upper third, rounded and obtuse at the apex, ciliate, very slightly asymmetric-oblique. Corolla up to 17 mm long, ± funnel-shaped, white; the other characters of the reproductive apparatus approach those of the following species, except in their dimensions.

## Source note

The presence of the preceding species in Gabon does not seem entirely certain, but nevertheless probable. The specimen cited is the only known record for Gabon. The species is distributed in West Africa, from Mali to Cameroon; it also occurs in Central Africa, in the Congo (Brazzaville and Léopoldville), and in East Africa. It is a species chiefly widespread in mountainous regions. The preceding description could only be established from specimens from countries bordering Gabon.

Some samples of this species are not easily separable from the following species and from Ph. falcisepala C. B. Clarke; the dimensions of the corollas and the form of the anterior sepals, the most important distinctive characters of these taxa, are not entirely constant. As has already been said after the description of the genus, there is much uncertainty concerning the delimitation and geographical distribution of the species of the genus Phaulopsis and of its taxa as they were accepted and treated by C. B. Clarke in the F.T.A. There is an almost inextricable mixture in the group of taxa that were assembled by C. B. Clarke under the names Phaylopsis parviflora Willd., Phaylopsis longifolia T. Thoms. (Speke, Journ. Append. 643, not of Sims), and Phaylopsis falcisepala C. B. Clarke, which required much clarification. The following species undoubtedly also belongs to this group. It is not impossible that it has very close taxonomic relationships with Phaylopsis longifolia T. Thoms. non Sims, of C. B. Clarke (= Phaulopsis inaequalis Pichi-Sermolli, Webbia 7 : 339, 1950), at least with part of the specimens cited by C. B. Clarke under this illegitimate binomial. The lectotype of Pichi-Sermolli, Schimper 367, from Ethiopia, is quite distinct from the taxon called here Phaulopsis Poggei (Lindau) Lindau. In any case, it is not possible for me to consider the Gabonese specimens cited under the following species as belonging to Phaulopsis imbricata (Forsk.) Sweet, even in the broad sense. See in this regard also the notes of Milne-Redhead (Mem. N. Y. Bot. Gard. 9 : 22, 1954), and of Heine (F.W.T.A., ed. 2,2 : 400, 1963).
