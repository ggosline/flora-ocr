## Description

Tubers ellipsoid to ovoid. Stem glabrous 20–64 cm, leaves glabrous, 5.5 × 1.5 cm, ovate-lanceolate to lanceolate, acute. Inflorescence 4.5–16 cm, dense, (8)12–30-flowered. Flowers rather small, purple or violet, often with darker spots or whitish portions. Dorsal sepal elliptic, convex, obtuse, cucullate at apex; lateral sepals obliquely oblong-ovate, subobtuse. Petals oblong, sigmoid, obtuse. Lip thick, fleshy, with very small papillae, three-lobed for nearly half its length; median lobe oblong, obtuse, with two decurrent keels terminating abruptly anteriorly; lateral lobes semi-ovate in diamond shape, obtuse to subacute. Spur cylindric, apex obtuse, curved downward. – Pl. 7, p. 25.

II. HABENARIINAE Benth.

J. Linn. Soc. Bot. 18 : 288 (1881).

Underground storage organs of diverse nature. Leaves basal to subradicant, or cauline. Inflorescence with flowers rare to numerous, resupinate or not, large and showy to small and inconspicuous. Lip generally three-lobed, with spur of variable size. Stigma bilobed, generally stipitate with an apical region mostly fertile. Rostellum three-lobed, the two lateral lobes elongate into rostellophores, projecting in front of the gynostemium. Anther erect, retroverse to arcuate, lower portion elongate into antherophores in connection with the rostellophores.

Group of 15 genera, the majority paleotropical and subtropical. Several of these genera require taxonomic revision, such as Habenaria Willd. with its 700–800 species, Arnottia A. Rich.; Cynorkis Thouars and the Physoceras Schltr. complex.
