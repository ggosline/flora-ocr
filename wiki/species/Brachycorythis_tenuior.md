## Description

– Habenaria tenuior (Rchb. f.) N. E. Br., Gard. Chron., ser. 2, 24 : 307 (1885).

– Platanthera tenuior (Rchb. f.) Schltr., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 20, Beibl. 50 : 12 (1895).

– Brachycorythis engleriana Kraenzl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 22 : 20 (1896).

– Platanthera engleriana (Kraenzl.) Rolfe, FTA 7 : 204 (1898).

– Diplacorchis engleriana (Kraenzl.) Schltr., Beih. Bot. Centralbl. 38(2) : 129 (1921).

– Diplacorchis tenuior (Rchb. f.) Schltr., Beih. Bot. Centralbl. 38(2) : 128 (1921).

– Brachycorythis crassicornis Kraenzl., Vierteljahrsschr. Naturf. Ges. Zurich 74: 103 (1929).

Tubers ellipsoid to ovoid. Stem glabrous 20–64 cm, leaves glabrous, 5.5 × 1.5 cm, ovate-lanceolate to lanceolate, acute. Inflorescence 4.5–16 cm, dense, (8)12–30-flowered. Flowers rather small, purple or violet, often with darker spots or whitish portions. Dorsal sepal elliptic, convex, obtuse, cucullate at apex; lateral sepals obliquely oblong-ovate, subobtuse. Petals oblong, sigmoid, obtuse. Lip thick, fleshy, with very small papillae, trilobed for nearly half its length; median lobe oblong, obtuse, with two decurrent keels terminating abruptly at the front; lateral lobes semi-ovate in lozenges, obtuse to subacute. Spur cylindric, apex obtuse, curved downwards. - Pl. 7, p. 25.

II. HABENARIINAE Benth.

J. Linn. Soc. Bot. 18 : 288 (1881).

Underground storage organs of diverse nature. Leaves basal to subradicant, or cauline. Inflorescence with flowers rare to numerous, resupinate or not, large and showy to small and inconspicuous. Lip generally trilobed, with spur of variable size. Stigma bilobed, generally stipitate with an apical region largely fertile. Rostellum with 3 lobes, the 2 lateral ones elongate into rostellophores, projecting before the gynostemium. Anther erect, retroverted to arched, lower part elongate into antherophores connecting with the rostellophores.

Group of 15 genera mostly palaeotropical and subtropical. Several of these genera require taxonomic revision, such as Habenaria Willd. with its 700–800 species, Arnottia A. Rich.; Cynorkis Thouars and the Physoceras Schltr. complex.
