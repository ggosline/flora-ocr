# *Ludwigia abyssinica* A.Rich.

**Family**: [[Onagraceae]] · **Genus**: [[Ludwigia]]

*Tentamen florae abyssinicae* **1**: 274 (1848).

## Description

Bushy herb, sometimes woody at base, 0.60–3 m high; wholly glabrous except for very short hairs on the margin and midrib of young leaves. Stems often reddish; branches spreading or prostrate at base, then erect and branched. Petiole 5–20 mm, reddish-brown; blade narrowly elliptic-ovate to elliptic, 2.5–13.5 × 0.7–3.5 cm, cuneate, acute, with a slightly thickened pubescent violet margin; lateral veins 7–15 pairs. Short axillary racemes bear variable bract leaves, sometimes reduced to triangular pieces 1–2 mm long.

Pedicel 1–3 mm with two minute bracteoles; sepals 4, triangular, 1.5–3 × 0.4–2.5 mm; petals 4, rapidly deciduous, 1–3.5 × 1–2.5 mm, yellow; stamens 4; ovary about 7 × 0.5 mm; style 0.5–0.8 mm. Fruit linear, slightly curved, round or nearly four-angled, 12–25 × 2–3 mm, glabrous, with one horizontal seed row per locule. Seed ellipsoid, 0.6–0.8 × 0.4–0.5 mm, enclosed by a roughly horseshoe-shaped endocarp fragment.

## Distribution

From Senegal and Guinea to Sudan and Ethiopia, south to Angola, Zambia, Mozambique and South Africa, also Madagascar. In Gabon it is recorded from Estuaire, Ngounié and Woleu-Ntem.

## Habitat and ecology

Marshes, stream and pond margins, rocky riverbeds, drains and forest regrowth; 200–250 m in Gabon and to 2300 m elsewhere.

## Notes

Reported as new for Gabon in this treatment. The bract-bearing branches are more or less perpendicular to the main axes. Leaves are eaten and used medicinally and for dye in parts of Africa.
