# *Urera flamigniana* Lambinon Planche 31

**Genus**: [[Urera]]
**Family**: [[Urticaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belgique 91 : 199 (1959)

## Description

U. hypselodendron (Hochst.) Wedd. var. flamigniana Hauman, Fl. Congo Belge, Rwanda, Urundi 1 : 182 (1948), nom. inval. Robust woody liana, dioecious, or very rarely monoecious, climbing up to 8 m in height; young stems, stipules, petioles and both surfaces of the leaf blade bearing a remarkable golden tomentum or dense pubescence, this pilosity is particularly velvety and golden on the upper surface of the young blade; woody stem, hanging in long curves, with circular or weakly pentagonal section, the oldest up to 3 cm in diameter, with a porous, orange pith, surrounded by five peripheral ridges of pale ligneous conducting tissue; bark smooth, brownish, 1–2 mm thick, with leaf scars, and sometimes with conical protuberances; cystoliths punctiform or somewhat elongated. Stipules ± laterally connate, triangular-lanceolate, (1–)1.5–2.5 × ± 1 cm, bifid at the apex, dark brown and smooth internally, ciliate and pubescent, particularly on the two outer longitudinal ribs. Leaf: petiole up to 15 cm; blade ovate to suborbicular, up to 30 × 22 cm, base deeply cordate, apex acute and mucronulate at the tip, more often with a narrow and entire acumen, margin with numerous straight triangular teeth reaching up to 2–3 × ± 2 mm, densely pubescent on the upper surface, hairs on the lower surface dense and tangled, more whitish than on the upper surface, except on the veins where they have a more golden tint; basal pair of lateral veins scarcely exceeding half the blade, forming a smaller angle with the midvein (± 45°) than in related species (U. batesii, U. cordifolia and U. gabonensis), with 4 or 5 pairs of upper lateral veins arched towards the midvein before dissolving into the tertiary network. Inflorescence: the male generally in defoliate axils, paniculate to corymbiform, up to 10–12 cm wide; peduncle 4–5 cm, slightly fleshy, pinkish, pubescent; glomerules comprising up to 10 flowers; the female in defoliate axils, in a dense panicle, ± 2–3 × 4–5 cm, covered with dense stinging hairs longer than the flowers. Male flower 5-merous (rarely 4-merous): floral bud globose or flattened, ± 1.5 mm in diameter; pedicel slender, reaching 2.5 mm; tepals covered with short stinging or stiff and spreading hairs, ending in a tiny pectinate structure. Female flower surrounded by a dense tuft of stinging hairs, subsessile or with short pedicel; perianth tubular, shorter than the ovary and with (3–)4(–5) irregular and indistinct marginal teeth; ovary ovoid, ± 1 mm long. Fruit ± 1.5 mm long, almost entirely covered by the red perianth.

## Distribution

from Nigeria, Cameroon and the Mayumbe zone of the Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, the species is known from Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

In Gabon, almost always in secondary and sterile forest; elsewhere with a broader ecological spectrum, found in dense forest, open forest and wooded savanna; in Gabon at 300–500 m, elsewhere marginally more extensive than in Gabon

## Source note

The species appears close to U. batesii, U. cordifolia and U. gabonensis, but has more pronounced margin teeth on the blade, is more pubescent and appears generally devoid of dark brown protuberances on young stems, whereas these are prominent in U. cordifolia and U. gabonensis. In Gabon, it is known only from two localities, represented by four collections, generally sterile and the identity of three of them is somewhat doubtful.
