# *Turraeanthus longipes* Baill.

**Genus**: [[Turraeanthus]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Adansonia 11 : 262 (1874)

## Description

Shrub or small tree, 1–7(–9) m tall. Leaf: petiole 9–21(–30) cm; rachis 19–39(–52) cm; petiolules 3–15(–20) mm, those of the terminal leaflets up to 25 mm; leaflets (5–)7–9(–13), alternate or subopposite, narrowly elliptic to obovate or ovate, (7–)14–26 × (3.5–)5–9 cm, with cuneiform to obtuse base, with acuminate apex, often with a more or less elongate acumen reaching 17 mm, glabrous above, below, especially on the venation, shortly puberulent. Inflorescence axillary or supra-axillary, 5–60 cm long, puberulent-ferruginous; bracts and bracteoles persistent, subulate, curved, 6 × 0.6 mm. Functionally male flower: pedicel 3–5 mm, with receptacle 1–1.5 mm long; calyx with entire or weakly 4(–5)-toothed margin, 2–2.5 × 3–4 mm, tomentose; petals (4–)5, fused into a narrow tube 16–18 mm long, puberulent outside, spreading at the apex into (4–)5 ovate spreading lobes, 5–7 × 3–4 mm; staminal tube 19–23 mm long, fused to the corolla on the lower ¾, glabrous inside, with crenulate margin, anthers 10(–11); pistillode filiform, 20 mm long, glabrous in the upper quarter, the rest puberulent; stigma discoid. Functionally female flower similar; anthers sterile; ovary clearly developed, broadly ovate, deeply 4-ribbed, 1–1.5 mm in diameter, 4-locular, densely puberulent; style filiform, 13–16 mm. Fruit obovoid-subglobose, 2–2.5 × 3–4 cm, flattened and depressed at the apex, weakly 2–4-lobed, yellow. Seed convex or plano-convex, 20 × 13–18 mm, completely surrounded by a thin, pulpy, whitish sarcotesta, 1 mm thick.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Dense humid forest; in Gabon, up to 700 m elevation, elsewhere at low elevation. Taxonomic note: Lacking a Latin diagnosis, the names of the varieties "komoensis" and "albiflora" of Pellegrin (1940) are not validly published. Moreover, we have not found consistent characters to recognize infraspecific entities. Bibliography: Pellegrin (1911, 1940).
