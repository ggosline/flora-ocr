# *Thunbergia affinis* S. Moore

**Genus**: [[Thunbergia]]
**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: S. Moore, Journ. Bot. 18 : 5, 194, 196 (1880)

## Synonyms

- Thunbergia Holstii Lindau 1. c., 95 (1893); Beibl. 41 : 37, 39 (1893).

## Description

Woody-suffrutescent plant, at first erect then often sarmentose or even slightly twining, reaching 2.5 m in height, with stem more or less quadrangular, especially in the new parts, slightly winged, glabrous. Axillary buds covered with brownish-red hairs. Petioles ± 2 cm, with spines at the base, resembling stipules. Leaf blades ovate-acuminate, with cuneiform base, 4.5-10 × 1-2.5 cm; acumen with a small mucro; margins glabrous; 4-5 pairs of secondary veins.

Flowers solitary, axillary, with pedicels 1.5-2.5 cm long; bracteoles oval, membranous, acuminate, reaching 3 × 1.2 cm, glabrous. Calyx lobes 12 in number, unequal, attenuate, elongate, covered with white scales, measuring at anthesis about 4-5 mm. Corolla tube yellow, 4.5 cm, nearly straight, glabrous or with a few hairs on the outside; lobes rounded, violet, 15 mm. Long stamens, 8-11 mm; short stamens, 5-7 mm; anthers 4 mm, oblique at the base, acuminate at the apex with a setulose crest prolonged by a series of soft hairs. Style 3.5 cm; stigma bilobed, with lower lobe 2.5 mm and upper lobe 4 mm. Capsule 2.5 cm long. Seed ± reniform, with verrucose-furrowed testa, 3.5 × 5.5 mm.

## Type

Hildebrandt 2004, Mombassa, Afrique orientale (BM, syntype); Monteiro s. n., Angola (NM, syntype)

## Discussion

Gabon, Angola, Congo, Ethiopia, East Africa, Mozambique.

## Source note

The specific value of this taxon is doubtful (see note after the key p. 59). The description above relates exclusively to var. affinis. Var. pulvinata S. Moore (J. Bot. 18 : 6, 1888) differs considerably from it and appears to be distributed exclusively in East Africa.

The indigenousness of this taxon to Gabon is incontestable, but nevertheless sometimes somewhat uncertain: it is an ornamental plant that may have been cultivated, escaped from cultivation and become established in the same manner as the preceding species.
