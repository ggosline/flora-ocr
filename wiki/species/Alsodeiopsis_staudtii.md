# *Alsodeiopsis staudtii* Engler

**Genus**: [[Alsodeiopsis]]
**Family**: [[Icacinaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 24 : 479 (1898)

## Description

— A. weissenborniana auct. non J. BRAUN & K. SCHUM. : A. CHEV., Expl. Bot. Afr. occ. : 128 (1920).

Shrub 2–5 m tall, diffusely spreading. Old branches wrinkled longitudinally and weakly pubescent with appressed yellow hairs. Petiole 1.5–4 mm long, very strongly pubescent. Blade glabrous on upper surface and pubescent with appressed or ± erect hairs on lower surface, elliptic to oblanceolate, 5–12 × 2–4.5 cm; base ± strongly inequilateral (one side rounded, the other attenuate) and apex acuminate obtuse or retuse, mucronate. Midvein concave pubescent on upper surface and prominent pubescent on lower surface. Secondary veins 6–7 pairs, ascending, arched, anastomosing 0.5–1 mm from the blade margin, weakly prominent pubescent on upper surface and prominent pubescent on lower surface. Veinlets in fairly dense network prominent on both surfaces. Axils of secondary veins sometimes bearing pocket domatia.

Inflorescences in corymbiform panicles, axillary, solitary or fasciculate, multiflorous (3–8 flowers). Peduncle pubescent, 3–6 mm long. Pedicel pubescent, 2–4 mm long. Floral bud conical. Flower yellow, 4.5 × 2.5 mm. Sepals fused at base, pubescent externally and glabrous internally, ± erect, narrowly lanceolate, 1.5 mm long, apex acute. Petals erect pubescent on external surface and sparsely pubescent on internal surface, narrowly lanceolate, 4 × 1 mm, apex acute reflexed. Stamens free, gynobasic, 2.5 mm long; filament 0.8 mm long; anthers basifixed with 2 ellipsoid locules, 1.25 mm long; connective appendiculate at apex, appendicule acute triangular, narrow, 0.5 mm long. Pistil 4 mm long; ovary ellipsoid 1 mm long, pubescent; style slender 3 mm long, glabrous.

Drupe ellipsoid, sparsely pubescent, red at maturity, 1–1.3 × 0.5–0.7 cm. PL. 6, 6–10, p. 25.

## Type

Staudt 142, Cameroun

## Discussion

This species is found in forests from Côte d'Ivoire to Gabon.

## Uses

The roots are known as aphrodisiacs.
