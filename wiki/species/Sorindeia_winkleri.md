Trichoscypha 27 Shrub to tree reaching 15 m tall; branchlets puberulent to glabrous. Leaf: petiole 3–16 cm, like the rachis puberulent to glabrous; leaflets (1–)7 to 11(–13), alternate or opposite, with petiolule 3–17 mm, oblong-elliptic lamina, (5–)10–18(–30) × (2–)3–6(–10) cm, rounded to cuneate at base, with a narrow acumen up to 2 cm at apex, papyraceous to coriaceous, ± glabrous, with (9–)11 to 18(–20) pairs of lateral nerves, oblique nerve very distinct below, midvein canaliculate above. Inflorescence axillary and/or terminal, pendulous, up to 60 cm long, reddish to purple, puberulent. Male flower ± sessile; calyx ± 2 mm long, with lobes 1–1.5 mm long, ciliate; petals valvate, ovate-elliptic, 2.5–3.5 mm long; stamens 10 to 16, 2–3 mm long. Female flower: pedicel 2–3 mm, lower portion 1–2 mm, puberulent; calyx and corolla similar to those of male flower; staminodes 5, 2 mm long; pistil 2.5–3 mm long, glabrous. Fruit ovoid-ellipsoid, often slightly oblique, slightly compressed laterally, up to 3.5 × 2 cm, orange-red, glabrous; immature fruit 3-angled, often whitish.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda and Tanzania; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Primary and secondarized forest; in Gabon at 100–500 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m

## Source note

This species shows a bipolar distribution, one area from Nigeria to Gabon, the other east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda and western Tanzania. B : Breteler (2003), Van der Veken (1960a). Bibliography
