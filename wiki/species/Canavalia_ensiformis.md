# *Canavalia ensiformis* (L.) DC. Planche 23.1–5

**Genus**: [[Canavalia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. 2 : 404 (1825)

## Description

Climbing herb, triennial. Leaf: leaflets with oval to elliptic blade, 5–20 × 3–12 cm, apex acute or acuminate, base broadly cuneate, glabrous, with 6 or 7 pairs of lateral veins. Inflorescence: peduncle 10–20(–25) cm; rachis 10–20 cm. Flower: calyx 10–12 mm long, slightly pubescent; corolla dark purple or white with pink base, standard obovate, reaching 25 × 15 mm, wings narrowly oval and keel obovate, smaller than the standard. Pod pendulous, flattened, 11–30 × 3–3.5 cm, each valve with 2 crests, one close to the suture, distant from the second by 4–5 mm, with 8 to 15 seeds. Seed ovoid-oblong, approximately 21 × 15 × 10 mm, white or pink; hilum brown.

## Distribution

domesticated in South and Central America, cultivated around the tropics, and often naturalized; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Nyanga, and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

fields and fallows; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere cultivated up to 1800 m. Use: Seed vegetable but requiring long cooking. Fetish plant against plantation marauders

## Vernacular names

haricot sabre, pois sabre (fr.) ; jack bean, sword bean (angl.) ; gisima (éshira, bavarama) ; musémana (baduma) ; musimana (bavil) ; musimina (bavungu, bapunu) ; musimuna (banzabi)

## Source note

Sometimes confused with C. gladiata (Jacq.) DC. (also called sword pea) with straighter pods and brown seeds, native to Southeast Asia. b: Bosch (2004a), Chee et al. (1992), Kay (1979), Mackinder et al. (2001), Pellegrin (1949), Bibliography Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Sauer (1964)
