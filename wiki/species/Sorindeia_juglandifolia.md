# *Sorindeia juglandifolia* (A.Rich.) Planch. ex Oliv. Planche 9, 11(D), 12(D)

**Genus**: [[Sorindeia]]
**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Afr. 1 : 440 (1868)

## Description

S. simplicifolia (G.Don) Exell, Cat. vasc. pl. S. Tomé : 145 (1944). S. lundensis Exell & Mendonça, Bol. Soc. Brot., sér. 2, 26 : 277 (1952). S. ngouniensis Pellegr., Bull. Soc. Bot. France 102 : 330 (1956). S. collina Keay, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 26 : 208 (1959). S. katangensis Van der Veken, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 29 : 245 (1959). S. mayumbensis Van der Veken, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 29 : 246 (1959). S. submontana Van der Veken, Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 29 : 250 (1959). S. rhodesica R.Fern. & A.Fern., Bol. Soc. Brot., sér. 2, 33 : 147, t. 5-7 (1965). S. undulata R.Fern. & A.Fern., Bol. Soc. Brot., sér. 2, 33 : 149, t. 8 (1965). S. ripicola Champl., Syst. Geogr.Pl. 69 : 42 (1999). Shrub, liane or medium tree, reaching 23 m in height; trunk up to 40 cm in diameter; branchlets, leaves and inflorescences subglabrous to sparsely puberulent or, less frequently, pubescent. Leaf: petiole 4–12 cm; leaflets (1–)5 to 11(–15), with petiolule 4–15 mm, with blade elliptic to oblong or ovate to obovate, often narrow, (2–)10–18(–28) × (1–)3–6(–12) cm, cuneate to rounded and often asymmetric at the base, acuminate for 5–15(–20) mm at the apex, papyraceous to coriaceous; midrib prominent above, lateral nerves in

Sorindeia 23 (7–)10 to 15(–17) pairs, oblique nerve often very distinct, especially below. Inflorescence axillary or terminal, more rarely cauliflorous, ± pendulous, up to 60 cm in length, with indument, if present, generally not exceeding the articulation of the pedicel, rarely also pubescent above. Male flower: pedicel 1–2 mm, the upper part 0–0.5 mm; calyx 1 mm in length, glabrous, with deltoid lobes; petals valvate, ovate-elliptic, 2–3 mm in length, glabrous; stamens (10)13 to 15(–18), slightly shorter than the petals, anthers 1.2–1.5 mm in length. Female flower: pedicel, calyx and petals as in male flowers; staminodes 5, 1–1.5 mm in length; pistil 2.5 mm in length, glabrous. Fruit ± broadly ellipsoid, 12–20 × 9–10 mm, smooth, glabrous, orange at maturity (immature fruits sometimes dark red to purple).

## Distribution

in West Africa, from Senegal and Mali eastward to Nigeria, then in Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Angola, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Tanzania, Zambia and Mozambique; in Gabon, known from all provinces

## Ecology

primary and secondarized forest, gallery forest; in Gabon at 5–630 m elevation, elsewhere up to 2000 m. Use: The fruits are edible. The leaves are used in traditional medicine. B: Burkill (1985), Keay (1958), Breteler (2003), Van der Veken (1960a). Bibliography
