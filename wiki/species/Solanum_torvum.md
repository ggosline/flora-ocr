# *Solanum torvum* Sw. Planche 43, Figure 7(M)

**Genus**: [[Solanum]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Prodr. [O.P. Swartz] : 47 (1788)

## Description

Shrub, often much-branched at the base, up to 3 m tall, unarmed or prickly; branches erect to spreading, cylindrical, pubescent to densely pubescent, with stellate, patent stipitate hairs, armed with curved prickles. Leaf: petiole 1.5–4 cm, densely pubescent, armed with a few curved or straight prickles; blade elliptic to ovate, (5.5–)9–17 × 4–12 cm, truncate or oblique at base, acute to acuminate at apex, margin entire or superficially lobed, with 2 to 4 broadly triangular lobes per side, acute at apex, bearing a few prickles on both surfaces, slightly discolorous, sparsely to densely pubescent with sessile stellate hairs above, densely pubescent with stipitate stellate hairs below. Inflorescence lateral, occasionally oppositifoliate, 2–6 cm long, branched 1 to 4 times, with more than 50 flowers generally all bisexual, but sometimes with male flowers at the apex; peduncle 0.5–2 cm; axes densely pubescent. Flower 5-merous; pedicel 1–1.2 cm, sparsely to densely pubescent with stellate and simple hairs, the latter unicellular and crowned with a gland; calyx 4–6 mm long, densely pubescent, unarmed or bearing small prickles, with triangular lobes, 3–4 mm long, caudate at apex; corolla stellate, 1.5–2 cm diameter, white, divided to two-thirds, with spreading, triangular lobes, 7–9 × 4–5 mm, densely pubescent on exterior; stamens equal, filament glabrous, anther 6–8 mm long; ovary puberulent to small glandular hairs; style 1–1.2 cm, glabrous. Fruit spherical, 1–1.3 cm diameter, grey-green at maturity, with more than 100 seeds. Seed reniform, 2.5–3 × 2–2.5 mm, pale yellow.

## Distribution

introduced and naturalized throughout West Africa; probably indigenous to the Caribbean and Central America, today often the most common adventive Solanum species in tropical and subtropical regions; in Gabon, present in all provinces

## Ecology

very common adventive in open and disturbed habitats; in Gabon from sea level to 800 m elevation, elsewhere up to 2500 m, but more frequent at low altitude. Uses: The fruits, bitter but edible, are used in soups and sauces. In therapeutic use, S. torvum is used for its antimicrobial properties. In Gabon, the leaves are used to dress wounds; the fruits are used in decoction to treat sore throat and stomach ailments.
