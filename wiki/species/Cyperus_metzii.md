# *Cyperus metzii* (Steud.) Mattf. & Kük. Planche 58

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Kyllinga metzii Steud., Syn. pl. glum . 2 : 70 (1855)

## Description

in Engl. & Diels, Pflanzenr. IV. 20(101): 612 (1936). Kyllinga metzii Steud., Syn. pl. glum. 2: 70 (1855). Kyllinga squamulata Vahl, Enum. pl. 2: 381 (1805), non Cyperus squamulatus Steud. (1855). Annual herb, growing in small tufts with a weak root system; stems crowded, 2–30 cm × 0.5–0.8 mm, trigonous or cylindrical near the base, striate, glabrous, greenish with sometimes small dark spots, with at the base a tubular prophyll and one to several grey-pink sheaths lacking a blade. Leaf: sheath translucent with dark longitudinal veins; blade 2–25 cm × 0.5–2 mm, usually exceeding the stems, greenish with or without dark spots, flat, with small spiny teeth on the margin and the central vein at least near the apex. Inflorescence in a brownish globose head, with a single spike (or rarely in an irregular head with 1–2 additional smaller spikelets) 3–8 × 3–8 mm; involucral bracts 3–4(–5), foliaceous, spreading, the primary one 3–20 cm long. Spikelet 2.5–4 mm long, formed of one flower and 2 glumes, borne on a short peduncle with 2 small bracts; glumes 2–3 mm long, brownish, but green in the upper part of the central vein, keel winged and with very large teeth, each tooth triangular in outline, flat and antrorse at the apex. Flower with 2 stamens; style with 2 branches. Achene lenticular, flattened, 1.3–1.6 × 1.1–1.3 mm, black-brown to blackish.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa and Madagascar, also in India and America; rare in Gabon (but perhaps overlooked), found only in Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Weed of lawns, cultivated areas and other disturbed ground; in Gabon, at 0–150 m altitude, up to 1400 m elsewhere. Taxonomic note: easily identified by its small size and winged glumes with large teeth. Bibliography: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1997)
