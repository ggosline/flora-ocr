# *Buchholzia tholloniana* Hua

**Genus**: [[Buchholzia]]
**Family**: [[Brassicaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Philom. Paris, sér. 8, 7 : 78 (1895) ; RISSEEUW, Acta Bot. Neerland. 13 : 169 (1964) ; KERS, FCAM 29 : 18-19 (1987)

## Description

– Buchholzia macrothyrsa GILG & BENEDICT, Bot. Jahrb. 53: 214-215 (1915); SILLANS, Bull. Inst. Études Centrafr. 6: 200 (1953); TISSERANT & SILLANS, Not. Syst. 15: 204-205 (1955).

– Buchholzia polyantha GILG & BENEDICT, Bot. Jahrb. 53: 215 (1915).

– Buchholzia coriacea auct. non ENGL.: DE WILD., Miss. Laurent: 236 (1906); DE WILD., Ann. Mus. Congo Belge, Bot., sér. 5: 395 (1912); T. & H. DURAND, Syll. Fl. Congo: 31 (1909); DE WILD., Bull. Jard. Bot. Etat Brux. 4: 78 (1914) & 5: 240 (1916); WALKER, Bull. Inst. Études Centrafr. 5: 26 (1953).

– Buchholzia macrophylla auct. non PAX: HAUM. & WILCZ., Fl. Congo 2: 504-506 (1961); WALKER & SILLANS, Les plantes utiles du Gabon: 117 (1961).

Large shrub or tree reaching 10–15 m and sometimes even 20 m in height, with a dense and rounded crown. Trunk reaching 0.5 m in diameter and 17 m in height, fluted and without buttresses. Wood whitish-yellow of medium hardness. Bark thick, about 1 cm, non-fibrous, smooth, dark green or grey sometimes streaked with pink, brownish-red on the cut surface with a bitter taste. Young branches thick with angular section, greenish, becoming brown to almost black on drying. Lenticels not observed but probably scattered and scarcely visible. Leaves narrowly elliptic sometimes narrowly obovate, coriaceous, 10–25(–36) × 3–7(–9) cm; apex acute to acuminate rarely emarginate, base cuneate to attenuate decurrent on the petiole, margin slightly revolute on mature leaves. Upper surface dark olive, glossy, lower surface ordinarily paler, greyish-green dull often tinged with reddish, surface wrinkled when dry. Midvein strong and prominent below, longitudinally ridged when dry, slightly prominent above. Lateral veins 11–17, prominent on both surfaces, at most 3 cm apart, equally spaced along the midvein, slightly curved at the origin and anastomosing in a scarcely marked loop near the margin; micro-venation in loose reticulum, scarcely marked. Petiole 2–3 mm long, brown-black, darker at each end, apically thickened to 0.5–1 mm below the limb, upper surface deeply grooved near the apex and weakly channelled towards the thick non-articulate base. Stipules broadly triangular, about 1 mm long, free, becoming coriaceous with age, marcescent.

Inflorescences often paniculate and terminal, rarely simple axillary and subterminal, reaching 20(–35) cm in length at the end of growth, 10–20 cm wide when paniculate; the lateral branches sometimes surpassing the central axis, the lowest ones are axilled by foliaceous bracts whose size decreases rapidly towards the apex; the bracts axilling the apical branches are about 2 mm long, they are subulate and connate at their base to the stipules. The axis of the inflorescence is longitudinally grooved. The floral bracts, subulate, about 2 mm long, persistent, are connate in their lower half to the broadly triangular stipules. Pedicels rather short, reaching 2 cm long under the flower and fruit, not articulate at the base. Sepals imbricate in the bud, 4 sometimes 5–6 (after early splitting of the primordia), obovate to oblong, rounded at the apex, rarely acute, 2.5–4(–5) × 1–4(–5) mm, yellowish-green, concave within, coriaceous with thin margins, strongly reflexed from the base at anthesis and fruiting, marcescent. Disk campanulate at the end of anthesis, 2–3 mm high, entire or more rarely interrupted into 2 or 3 sections, striated or longitudinal with age, margin often irregularly lobed, crenate to dentate, sometimes even fimbriate at the level of the sections, persistent and coriaceous at fruiting. Androgynophore reaching 2 mm long at the end of flowering, slightly longitudinally grooved due to the basal decurrence of the filaments. Stamens 40–45; filaments rapidly elongating and reaching 1.5–2(–2.5) cm at the end of anthesis, yellowish-green at first, white at maturity, reaching a maximum length of 4 cm, straight at the moment of their opening; anthers oblong about 1 mm. Gynophore little exceeding the stamens during their elongation, about 3 times wider than the filaments at anthesis, green with whitish base, 6 mm wide and lignified at fruiting, oriented exactly in the extension of the pedicel. Ovary narrowly ellipsoid, 5–7 mm long, smooth, unilocular, containing about 16 ovules. Stigma sessile, scarcely visible, truncate, weakly lobed on its margin.

Fruit usually spherical or subspheric, 4–7 cm in diameter, sometimes oblong, often bearing an off-centre beak; pericarp 0.5–1 cm thick, inner part becoming brown and granular with age, surface smooth or finely verruculose, yellowish-green, becoming dark brown and hard at maturity. Seeds globular or showing obtuse and irregular angles in section, diameter 2.5–4 cm; testa papyraceous, brown, often partially connate to the inner wall of the fruit, cotyledons large fleshy, of irregular shape. Pl. 1, p. 11 & map C, p. 7.

## Discussion

This species is closely allied to B. coriacea; it differs from it by the characters mentioned in the key. Contrary to B. coriacea, it is not strictly restricted to regions near the coast. As RISSEUW believes, there remains nevertheless some doubt: is it a distinct species or not?

Its general habit and foliage resemble those of the mango tree; its large seeds recall Cola nuts, so some vernacular names reflect these resemblances. The cut fruit is said to have an agreeable or disagreeable fragrance depending on the person. By rubbing, the leaves and bark give off a mustard-like odour. The seeds are edible, but they have a purgative effect; because of their peppery taste, they are called "onion" or "garlic" of "gorilla" or "panther". On the same inflorescence, fruit formation is rare: a compound inflorescence, which can have more than 50 flowers, will rarely produce more than a single fruit.

Buchholzia tholloniana has been collected in Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, Zaïre, Congo and Cabinda (Angola). It belongs to the dominant layer of the ombrophilous forest; it is also encountered frequently in gallery forests or along rivers. It is widely distributed and apparently quite common in most regions of Gabon, except in the North-West and along the coast.
