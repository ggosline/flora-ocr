# *Baphia leptostemma* Baill. Planche 16

**Genus**: [[Baphia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Adansonia 6 : 214 (1866)

## Description

Shrub to small tree reaching 12 m in height; trunk up to 20 cm in diameter; branchlets glabrous or glabrescents. Stipules narrowly elliptic, ± 3–5 × 1 mm, with ciliate margin. Leaf: petiole with contiguous pulvini, (2–)4–8(–9) cm, ± glabrous; leaflet with ± elliptic blade, (2–)5–12(–15) × (1–)2.5–5(–7) cm, ± rounded at base, apex acuminate for 0.5–1(–2) cm, coriaceous to papyraceous, glabrous; lateral veins in 5 to 7 pairs. Inflorescence axillary or (sub)terminal, with solitary, fascicled or in simple or compound raceme flowers, glabrous to ± densely brown-pubescent; bracts and bracteoles transversely elliptic, 1–1.5 × 2 mm, ciliate. Flower: bracteoles situated at apex of pedicel against the calyx; pedicel ± filiform, 9–20 mm, sparsely pubescent to glabrous; calyx spathate, 9–15 × 9–20 mm, glabrous except for a few hairs at apex; standard ± circular, 10–18 × 11–20 mm, scarcely unguiculate, bilobed at apex, wings ± falcate, 11–18 × 4–10 mm, with claw of 1–2 mm, keel petals ± falcate, 13–18 × 4–6 mm, with claw of ± 1 mm; stamens 7–15 mm, anthers 2–3 mm in length; pistil 7–15 mm in length, ovary glabrous or partially velvety, with 5 to 8 ovules. Pod oblong, 7–14 × 2–2.5 cm, glabrous or with a few hairs on the sutures, with 1 or 2(–4?) seeds. Mature seed 12–14 × 10–12 mm, brown.

## Distribution

South-East Nigeria, Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon, collected in all provinces except Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié and Nyanga

Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known only from Nyanga

## Source note

The species recently described by Cheek et al. (2014) as B. vili Cheek, is morphologically very close to B. le-testui and occupies roughly the same region. The only collection of B. vili cited from Gabon by this author, Van Valkenburg 2819, bears pods and represents well B. le-testui. The pods of this collection are not winged as in B. vili. This makes B. vili endemic to the Republic of the Congo. b: Cheek et al. (2014), Pellegrin (1949), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Soladoye (1985). Bibliography
