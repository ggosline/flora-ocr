# *Salacia staudtiana* Loesener

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 44 : 187 (1910) ; HUTCH. & MOSS, FWTA 1 : 453 (1928) ; HALLÉ, Monogr. Hippocr. Afr. occid., Thèse : 167 (1958) ; Bull. IFAN, sér. A, 2 (3) : tab. 85 (1959) ; Pollen & Spores 2 (1) : 12 (1960) ; Mém. IFAN 64 : 170 (1962)

## Description

Bull. IFAN, ser. A, 2 (3): tab. 85 (1959); Pollen & Spores 2 (1): 12 (1960); Mem. IFAN 64: 170 (1962).

- Salacia staudtiana LOES. in FRITSCH, Bot. Centrbl. Beibh. 11: 355 (1902), nom. nud.

This complex species exhibits remarkable variation in various characters often not correlated with each other; this results in a certain difficulty in delimiting infraspecific taxa. To clarify the confusing situation found, we distinguish 6 varieties unequally distributed and unequally distinct, especially if the material is incomplete.

PL. 16. — Salacia staudtiana, Loes. var staudtiana, var. leonensis, var. tshopoensis: 1-3, buds; 4, calyx seen from below, diam. 2.2 mm; 5, flower, diam. 4.6 mm; 6, petal, 2 × 1.5 mm; 7, section of disc, diam. 1.6 mm; 8, disc seen from above; 9, 10, discs of buds; 11, juvenile anther; 12, stamen 1.2 mm; 13, pistil and biovulate locule opened; 14, 15, fruits, diam. 17 and 29 mm; 16, seed of 17 mm and its sections (var. staudtiana: 8, Farron 7339; 10, Hallé N. & Villiers J.-F. 4478; 9, Le Testu 7090; 2,4-7, 11-13, Staudt 631; 3, Zenker 1413 - var. leonensis: 1, 14, Hallé N. 488, Côte-d'Ivoire - var. tshopoensis: 15, 16, Hallé N. 3308). — S. staudtiana var. scaphodisca Hallé: 17, bud; 18, calyx seen from below, diam. 1.6 mm; 19, sepal spread out; 20, flower, diam. 3.3 mm; 21, section of disc, diam. 1.3 mm; 22, petal, 1.5 × 1 mm; 23, juvenile disc; 24, disc in oblique view (17-19, 21, 22, Le Testu 9582; 20, 23, 24, Klaine 3485).

1. Disc as an annular or subpentagonal cushion spread out, sometimes only slightly distinctly cupuliform with entire margin.

2. Internodes cylindrical; generally no persistent perular scales; fruit attenuate into a basal neck, more rarely without.

3. Inflorescence peduncle short, of (1)2–10(15) mm only; petals often somewhat oblong and somewhat angular.

4. Very young floral bud sometimes or often with a longer and more acute sepal whose tip dominates the others, this apicule is sometimes arched; pericarp thin in the dried fruit.

5. Fruit rather large 25–30 mm in diameter; corolla rotate; pedicels of 10–30 mm ..... 7a. var. staudtiana.

5. Fruit rather small, 10–25 mm in diameter; corolla somewhat urceolate or rotate (see also 6) ..... 7b. var. leonensis.

4'. Very young bud with several slightly acute sepals, very slightly unequal, or equal and all obtuse.

6. Pericarp thin; corolla sometimes urceolate; fruit with or without a narrow basal neck (see also 5') ..... 7b. var. leonensis.

6. Pericarp thick; corolla rather rotate; fruit with a narrow basal neck 7c. var. tshopoensis.

3'. Inflorescence peduncle long and slender, 20–25 mm; petals suborbicular; sepals somewhat carinate, with keel briefly acute 7d. var. longuria.

2'. Internodes with 4 ridges; petals suborbicular, entire or finely denticulate; presence of persistent perular scales; blade sometimes finely denticulate; fruit globose without basal neck, rather small, 20 mm in diameter or less, with thin pericarp ..... 7e. var. cerasiocarpa.

1'. Disc as a deep erect cupule with margin ordinarily 1–3 erose; fruit small, with thin wall and very short neck; pedicels short; internodes sometimes with 4 ridges ..... 7f. var. scaphodisca.

7a. var. staudtiana

High liane exceeding 8 m, entirely glabrous, devoid of rubber, with cylindrical wood marked in cross-section by radiant parenchymatous grooves 3–10 mm deep (diam. 2 cm, 15–20 grooves, Leeuwenberg 2322; diam. 3 cm, 24 grooves, Hallé 3308). Internodes smooth, olivaceous when dry. Leaves opposite or subopposite with petiole 4–10 mm with straight margins of the canal. Blade olivaceous above ± yellow-green below, with rounded or acute base, acuminate apex, rarely sublanceolate, 6.5–15 × (1.6)3–8.5 cm, with entire or very slightly angular margins. Secondary nerves 6–8 pairs arched; intersecondary nerves often subparallel and subtransverse. Midvein in slight rounded relief above.

Axillary inflorescences pluri- to multiflorous with peduncle 2–6 mm bearing a bracteoled glomerule sometimes divided into several short multibracteolate branches; bracts and bracteoles acute often less than 1 mm. Floral pedicels slender, 10–30 mm. Very young floral bud often presenting one dominant apiculate or acuminate sepal. Bud globose. Flower rotate yellow or yellow-green 4–4.7 mm in diameter. Sepals 5 subdeltoid equal with somewhat fleshy keel; the external ones more acute and less glandular; the internal ones with slightly blunt apex and more glandular margins. Petals 5 oblong or ovate, sometimes somewhat angular, with about 7 main nerves. Disc as an annular cushion 1.3–1.7 mm in diameter and 0.25 mm thick, sometimes subpentagonal. Perigynous furrow distinct where 3 stamens insert deeply, 0.8–1.2 mm long, with narrow filament even at the base, with anther having a single transversely dehiscent locule, 0.4–0.5 mm wide. Pistil about 0.5 mm with punctiform stigma. 3 half-sunken locules each containing 2 superposed ovules (and not collateral as according to LOESENER).

Globose fruit attenuate into a basal neck at the base, smooth or nearly so, 2–3 cm in diameter. Seeds 1–4 of about 1–2 cm; the cotyledons are separate or slightly adherent in the middle; embryonic radicle much reduced. - Pl. 16, fig. 2–3, p. 59.

Note. – Floral buds and flowers of all varieties are rich in yellow soluble coloring matter during rehydration for analysis.

7b. var. leonensis Loesener

Bot. Jahrb. 44: 188 (1910); HALLÉ, MEM. IFAN 64: 171 (1962). - S. caillei A. CHEV. ex HUTCH. & MOSS, KEW BULL.: 22 (1929).

This variety differs from the preceding one by a somewhat more reduced number of secondary nerves namely 4–7 pairs, by the floral pedicel often short 2.5–9 mm, by its flowers often somewhat urceolate, by its globose fruits small with short basal neck, more rarely without neck; in these two varieties the pericarp remains thin. Flowers yellow-green or more rarely orange. - Pl. 16, fig. 1, 14, p. 59.

West Africa up to Guinea with interruption between Ghana and Nigeria. The limit to the East is not precise but it reaches Gabon.

From Cameroon (fide Wilczek, material not seen) to Zaïre.

7d. var. longuria Hallé

Notul. System. 16: 133, fig. 6 (1960).

The only true peculiarity of this variety is the length of inflorescence peduncles, (4)10–65 mm, very slender; fruit 25 mm in diameter, with very reduced basal neck, with very thin pericarp. - Pl. 17.

7e. var. cerasiocarpa (Wilczek) Hallé, comb. nov.

– Salacia tshopoensis DE WILD. var. cerasiocarpa WILCZEK, Bull. Jard. Bot. Etat Brux. 27: 693 (1957).

The peculiarities of this variety are as follows: shrubby habit, height 2–3 m; flower yellow, pink or salmon; young internodes quadrangular subalate; young branches often bearing at the base brownish persistent perular scales; foliar blade finely and more or less distinctly denticulate. The fruits are small, 10–20 mm in diameter, globose without basal neck, with thin pericarp.

To be searched for in Cameroon, Gabon and Congo. Abundant in Zaïre and Central Africa (Tisserant 134 and 419, Boukoko; 2271, Bambari; Le Testu 4140, Yalinga) where this species favors forest galleries.

7f. var. scaphodisca Hallé, var. nov.

Differs markedly from var. typica by the disc deeply cupulate pistil-high, margin erect 1–3-fissate, fissures opposite the stamens.

This variety is remarkable for its disc as a tubular cupule, as high as the pistil, namely 0.6–0.9 mm for a diameter of 1.3 mm. Only the anthers project beyond the disc; the latter is sometimes erose with 1–3 fissures opposite the stamens. The sepals have a narrowly obtuse apex but one of the 5 may be apiculate. The flower diameter is 3.3–3.5 mm. The fruit is not substantially different from that of var. leonensis. The young internodes are with 4 ridges as in var. cerasiocarpa but the plant is well here lianevine. - Pl. 16, fig. 17–24, p. 59.

Gabon and Congo (Sita 1324, Pointe Noire). Dense humid forests sometimes littoral.
