# *Landolphia angustisepala* Pichon Planche 45(1-6)

**Genus**: [[Landolphia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Mém. Inst. Franç. Afrique Noire 35 : 108 (1953)

## Description

Large liana; branchlets tomentose to villous. Leaf: petiole 3–6 mm, pilose; blade ovate, 2.1–2.2 times as long as wide, 6.5–12 × 3–6 cm, acute to rounded at base and decurrent on the petiole, acuminate or sometimes rounded or obtuse at apex, subglabrous or with some hairs on the upper surface slightly more densely packed with curled hairs on the midrib, less dense beneath; secondary veins 11 to 16 pairs, straight or sometimes slightly curved, tertiary venation reticulate, clearly visible on both surfaces. Inflorescence terminal, long of 5–12 cm, with 4 flowers, pedunculate of 8–12 mm, woolly or densely villous. Flower: pedicel 1–2.2 mm, pilose; bracteole 1.7–3.2 mm long, partially pilose on the exterior; calyx with unequal lobes, distinctly recurved at the apex, 1.8–3.6 times as long as wide, 2.2–4.8 × 0.9–1.4 mm, partially pilose on the exterior, sometimes glabrous, with ciliolulate margin; corolla cream, tube long 4–5.5 mm, at the level of the anthers 2–2.3 mm and at the throat 0.9–1.9 mm wide, pilose near the base on the exterior, villous on the interior at 1–1.2 mm below the anthers as far as the throat, lobes narrowly ovate to ovate or elliptic, 1.1–1.3 times as long as the tube, 2.1–2.5 times as long as wide, 2.6–7.4 × 0.9–2.6 mm, partially pilose to the apex and partially glabrous; stamens long 1–1.4 mm, inserted at mid-height of the tube, included 0.4–0.8 mm, anther long 0.5–1 mm; pistil long 2.8–3.9 mm; ovary mainly pilose or villous, with longer and denser hairs towards the apex; style 1–1.7 mm, glabrous. Fruit unknown. Distribution known only from a few localities in Gabon and one in the northeast of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, in Gabon, Haut-Ogooué and Ngounié. Ecology secondary forests, forest edges in Gabon, 240–580 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m.

B: Beentje (2021), Persoon et al. (1992), Pichon (1953).
