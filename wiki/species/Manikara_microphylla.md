# *Manikara microphylla* Aubr. et Pellegr.

**Genus**: [[Manilkara]]
**Family**: [[Sapotaceae]]
**Protologue**: Notulae Systematicae. Vol. XVI, fasc. 3-4 : 233 (1960)

## Description

Shrub. Buds glandular. Branchlets glabrous, marked with very conspicuous lenticels. Small leaves glabrous, very coriaceous, lanceolate or obovate-oblong or oblong, obtuse, attenuate or distinctly acuminate at the apex, cuneiform at the base; blade 5–7 cm long, 15–28 mm wide; petiole ca. 12 mm; very numerous lateral nerves parallel and drawn to the margin, deeply impressed in the thick blade, which gives it a very distinctive appearance.

Small flowers in axillary fascicles. Pedicel 5–7 mm, scaly-tomentose. Sepals 6, ca. 5 mm, pubescent on the outside, glabrous on the inside or finely tomentose on the margins. Corolla ca. 5–6 mm with very short tube, ca. 1 mm. Lobes 6, each provided with 2 appendages as long as the lobe. Lobe oblong, ca. 1.5 mm wide, but with margins folded inward. Stamens 6; filament short, 2 mm, anther 2 mm. Staminodes 2.5 mm, bi-dentate. Stamens and staminodes are fused at the base into a thick ring, ca. 2 mm high, itself adherent to the base of the corolla for ca. 1 mm.

Ovary pubescent prolonged by a long style, 10–12 locules. Fruit unknown.

Type of the species: Le Testu 8408, Poupa, region of Lastourville.

This species is remarkable at first sight by its small very thick leaves in which the numerous and fine lateral nerves and nervilles are impressed. There exist in equatorial Africa 3 other species of Manilkara with small leaves. M. angolensis (Engl.) H. Lec. ex Pellegr. discovered in the littoral thickets of the mouth of the Congo, is to me, after Engler's drawings, a form of M. lacera (Bak.) Dub. shrub of the beaches whose broadly obovate leaves are sometimes small and distinctly emarginate at the apex. The oldest small-leaved species described (1877) is M. cuneifolia (Bak.) Dub. of which I do not know the type and which according to the description in Oliver could likewise be a form of M. lacera. Dubard had placed M. cuneifolia and M. angolensis in synonymy.

The third species, M. Welwitschii (Engl.) Dubard, the closest to M. microphylla, is distinguished from it by the absence of this strongly impressed network of lateral venation, by the more distinctly obovate leaves, and by the stamens and staminodes directly fused on the corolla tube, and not fused at the base into a distinct ring.

M. microphylla is a poorly known species, reported only so far on the edge of a pond at Brazzaville, and in the interior of Gabon in the region of Lastoursville.

## Specimens examined

Gabon. — Le Testu : 8408 (holotype), Poupa (fl. oct.); 8377, Lindémé (fl. sept.).

Rép. Congo. — Koechlin : 6043, Brazzaville (fl. sept.).
