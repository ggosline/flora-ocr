# *Cassipourea ruwensorensis* (Engl.) Alston Planche 29.4, 36

**Genus**: [[Cassipourea]]
**Family**: [[Rhizophoraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1925 : 263 (1925)

## Description

C. mawambensis (Engl.) Alston, Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1925: 262 (1925). C. mildbreadii (Engl.) Alston, Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1925: 262 (1925). Shrub of 3–4 m to tree of 20–25 m tall; branchlets pubescent with long tawny hairs, then glabrescent; lenticels elongate, inconspicuous. Stipules caducous, long ovate, 5–7 mm long, pubescent on outer surface. Leaves distinctly decussate; petiole 5–7(–10) mm; blade elliptic, 7–10 × 2.5–3.5 cm, ± 2.8 times longer than wide, base attenuate to obtuse, apex shortly acuminate to acumen of 5–7 mm, subcoriaceous, with weakly serrate margins, glabrous on both surfaces except for hairy midvein beneath; lateral veins 5–6(–8) pairs, joined in arches before the margin. Inflorescence axillary, fasciculate, dense, with 7–16 flowers; bracts 1–2 per flower, concave, semicircular, 1.5 mm in diameter, pubescent on outside. Flower 4(–5)-merous; pedicel 1–3 mm, stipe up to 0.5 mm, with appressed hairs; calyx campanulate, 4.5 mm long, persistent in fruit, with 4(–5) spreading lobes, narrowly ovate, 4–6.5 mm long, densely hairy on outer surface, glabrescent to glabrous on inner surface; petals spatulate, 4.5–6 mm long, smooth, white, fringed-laciniate in the upper 1/3 into 5–7 filiform lobes; stamens 16–20, glabrous, fused at base into a disc of 0.5 mm tall, slightly or distinctly laciniate, filaments 2.5–4 mm, anthers oblong, ± 1.4 mm long, glabrous; ovary subglobose, with 3(–4) locules, tomentose; style 2(–4) mm, glabrous, persistent in fruit, stigma trilobed. Fruit ovoid, 13 × 7 mm, with 3(–4) locules, finely verrucose, velvety and silky. Seeds (1–)2(–4), ellipsoid, 4–4.5 × 3 mm, reddish-brown, surmounted by a pink-orange aril.

## Distribution

Central African Republic, Gabon, Democratic Republic of Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania; rare in Gabon, single collection from the iron mines of Bélinga (Ogooué-Ivindo).

## Ecology

Dry forests, gallery forests, savannas, dense humid and swampy forests; at 640–860 m altitude. Flower buds and ripe fruits in July. Uses: Elsewhere, the wood is used in carpentry. Bibliography: Breteler (2008), Lewis (1956), Liben (1987).
