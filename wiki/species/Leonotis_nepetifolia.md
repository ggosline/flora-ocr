# *Leonotis nepetifolia* (L.) R.Br. var. nepetifolia

**Genus**: [[Leonotis]]

## Description

in Aiton, Hort. Kew., ed. 2, 3: 409 (1811). Annual or short-lived perennial herb, (0.3–)1–3(–4.5) m tall; stem generally single, arising from a taproot, more or less branched at the nodes below the inflorescence, with short hairs directed downward or upward, with sessile glands; nodes with a tuft of white or yellowish hairs in the upper half. Leaf: petiole (0.5–)3–8(–12.5) cm; blade ovate, (2.5–)5–20(–23) × (2–)4–15 cm, cuneate-attenuate to cordate at the base, acute to acuminate at the apex, deeply crenate, pubescent. Inflorescence terminal; verticillasters (1 or) 2 to 5 (to 7) per branch, separated by long internodes, spherical to cylindrical, 3–6.7 cm wide; bracts narrowly elliptic-ovate or absent; bracteoles linear, (3–)7–15(–20) × 0.5–1.5 mm, acuminate. Flower: pedicel 0.5–2 mm; calyx 14–25 mm long, slightly accrescent, pubescent, with oblique throat, tube slightly curved dorsally, with 8 to 10 deltoid to narrowly triangular teeth, apiculate, the upper tooth the longest, the 3 lower teeth united at the base forming the reflexed lower lip; corolla 19–38 mm long, with orange hairs, tube 9–20 mm long, widening at the throat, with 3 rings of hairs inside, upper lip 10–20 mm long, with well-developed woolly hairs, white or orange and enclosing the anthers and stigma, lower lip 3-lobed, (3–)5–7 mm long, deflexed on the tube, but shriveled and twisted. Fruit: achenes 3–4 mm long, marbled.

## Distribution

very widespread in Africa, Madagascar, India, and widely naturalized in all tropical regions; present in Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko, Annobon), São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

cultivated fields, roadsides, mixed forests, riverbanks, grasslands and near settlements; in Gabon at 0–500 m altitude, elsewhere in Africa at 0–2670 m. Use: The Bakélé consume the leaves with kola nuts and place the leaves on circumcision wounds. They are also used to seal "pacts" between spouses or lovers.
