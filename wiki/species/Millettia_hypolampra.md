# *Millettia hypolampra* Harms

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 168 (1902)

## Description

Liana; branchlets velutinous yellowish-brown, lenticels whitish to brown; exudate reddish-brown. Stipules caducous. Leaf: petiole 3–7 cm, channeled above, yellowish-brown silky; pulvinus 0.8–1 cm, silky; rachis as petiole, 5–10 cm; stipels subulate, 1–1.5 mm, densely pubescent; leaflets 7 to 11, petiolule 4–6 mm, silky, blade elliptic to obovate or narrowly obovate, 5–13 × 1.8–4.3 cm, blade of basal leaflets often wider than that of terminal leaflet, base cuneate to obtuse, apex with acumen 1–2 cm, glabrous above, yellowish-brown silky below; lateral nerves in 8 to 14(–16) pairs, prominent below. Inflorescence terminal or subterminal, paniculate 14–23 cm long; peduncle 1–2.5 cm, silky to tomentose yellowish-brown; bracts caducous. Flower: pedicel 3–4 mm, silky; bracteoles near base of calyx, ovate, ± 1.5 × 1 mm, margins ciliate, with 9 or 10 nerves; calyx silky yellowish-brown, tube 4–5 mm long, upper lobes ± 2 × 1.5 mm, lower lobes 3–4 × 1.5 mm; corolla cream-white, standard with claw ± 2 mm, limb obovate to suborbicular, 9–11 × 6–8 mm, apex rounded, auricles inflexed, basal callositites present, wings with claw 2–3 mm, limb elliptic, 10–11 × 2–3 mm, auricle ± 1 mm, lateral pocket present, keel with claw 3–3.5 mm, limb elliptic, 10–12 × 4–5 mm, apex rounded, auricle ± 1.5 mm, lateral pocket present; stamens: tube 8–9 mm, free portion 4–5 mm; disk absent; ovary ± 4 mm long, densely pubescent, stipe ± 0.5 mm, ovules 4 or 5; style flattened, 10–12 mm, straight portion pubescent, curved portion glabrous, stigma capitate. Pod narrowly oblong, 13–13.3 × 2–2.5 cm, base cuneate to attenuate, apex with straight beak, ± 5 seeds, brown, pubescent or velutinous. Seed not seen.

## Distribution

Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon, known from Haut-Ogooué and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

forest edge, degraded forest; at 10–250 m altitude. Flowering from February to August; fruits from July to December. b: Hepper (1958), Sosef et al. (2006). Bibliography
