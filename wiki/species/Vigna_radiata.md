# *Vigna radiata* (L.) Wilczek var. radiata

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Congo Belge 6 : 386 (1954)

## Synonyms

- Phaseolus radiatus L., Sp. pl. 2 : 725 (1753).

## Description

Erect herb; stems generally covered with long (1–3 mm) erect russet hairs. Stipules oval to elliptic, 8–18 × 3–10 mm, prolonged below the point of insertion and spurred at the base, hirsute, ciliate. Leaf: petiole 5–17 cm; rachis 15–40 mm; stipels 4–10 mm long; leaflets with petiolule 3–6 mm, blade oval, 5–16 × 3–13 cm, rounded to subacute at the base, acuminate at the apex, sparingly pubescent on both surfaces. Inflorescence: peduncle 4–18 cm; rachis 0.5–1.5 cm; bracts oval-lanceolate, 2.5–4.5 mm long. Flower: bracteoles 2.5–5 mm long; calyx glabrous, with tube 3–4 mm long, lobes triangular-lanceolate, 1.5–2.5 mm long; corolla 8–12 mm long, pale yellow, standard asymmetric, ± 10 × 13 mm, with emarginate apex, bearing on the inner face a single callosity, wings obovate, 6–11 mm long, keel falcate, incurved, 7–11 mm long, with a long beak curved to the left, leading to a keel incurved through 280–360°, left petal bearing on the side a spur 1–2 mm supporting the left wing in horizontal position; ovary with 10 to 16 ovules; style prolonged beyond the stigma by a short beak, less than 1 mm. Pod spreading to subpendent, subtorulous, 5–9 cm × 4–6 mm, dark brown to blackish, pubescent-setulose, terminated by a curved beak. Seed 3–4 × 3 mm, green, with smooth testa; central hilum, without strophiole.

## Distribution

of Indian origin, today cultivated under all tropics and sometimes naturalized. The presence in Gabon is probable, but remains to be verified.

## Ecology

near cultivations, fallows, roadsides, humid savanna; cultivated up to 2000 m altitude. Use: The mature seeds of mung bean or the flour derived from them are ingredients in all kinds of protein-rich dishes; often eaten as sprouts/seed germs in Chinese cuisine. Immature pods and young leaves are consumed as a vegetable.

## Vernacular names

haricot doré, haricot mungo (fr.) ; mungbean, green gram, golden gram (angl.)

## Source note

This is the true mung bean. Caution: Vigna mungo (L.) Hepper is the urad bean or black gram to be distinguished by its black dull seeds. B: Lewis et al. (2005), Mogotsi (2006), Pasquet (2001), Tateishi (1985), Verdcourt (1971c), Bibliography Wilczek (1954)
