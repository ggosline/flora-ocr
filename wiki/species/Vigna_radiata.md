# *Vigna radiata* (L.) Wilczek var. radiata

**Genus**: [[Vigna]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Congo Belge 6 : 386 (1954)

## Description

Erect herb; stems generally covered with long (1–3 mm) reddish erect hairs. Stipules ovate to elliptic, 8–18 × 3–10 mm, extended below the insertion and spurred at the base, hirsute, ciliate. Leaf: petiole 5–17 cm; rachis 15–40 mm; stipels 4–10 mm in length; leaflets with petiolule 3–6 mm, blade ovate, 5–16 × 3–13 cm, rounded to subacute at the base, acuminate at the apex, sparsely pubescent on both surfaces. Inflorescence: peduncle 4–18 cm; rachis 0.5–1.5 cm; bracts ovate-lanceolate, 2.5–4.5 mm in length. Flower: bracteoles 2.5–5 mm in length; calyx glabrous, with tube 3–4 mm in length, lobes triangular-lanceolate, 1.5–2.5 mm in length; corolla 8–12 mm in length, pale yellow, standard asymmetric, ± 10 × 13 mm, with emarginate apex, provided on the inner face with a single callosity, wings obovate, 6–11 mm in length, keel falcate, incurved, 7–11 mm in length, with a long rostrum curved to the left, leading to a keel incurved over 280–360°, left petal provided on the side with a spur 1–2 mm supporting the left wing in horizontal position; ovary with 10 to 16 ovules; style extended beyond the stigma by a short beak, less than 1 mm. Pod spreading to subpendant, subtorulous, 5–9 cm × 4–6 mm, dark brown to blackish, pubescent-setulose, terminated by a curved beak. Seed 3–4 × 3 mm, green, with smooth testa; central hilum, without strophiole.

## Distribution

of Indian origin, nowadays cultivated throughout all the tropics and sometimes naturalized. The presence in Gabon is probable, but remains to be verified.

## Ecology

near crops, fallows, roadsides, humid savanna; cultivated up to 2000 m altitude. Use: Ripe seeds of mung bean or the flour derived from them are ingredients in all sorts of protein-rich dishes; often eaten as sprouts/seed germs in Chinese cuisine. Immature pods and young leaves are consumed as vegetables.

## Vernacular names

haricot doré, haricot mungo (fr.) ; mungbean, green gram, golden gram (angl.)

## Source note

This is the true mung bean. Caution: Vigna mungo (L.) Hepper is the urad bean or black gram to be distinguished by its dull black seeds. B: Lewis et al. (2005), Mogotsi (2006), Pasquet (2001), Tateishi (1985), Verdcourt (1971c), Bibliography Wilczek (1954)
