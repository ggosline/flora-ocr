# *Nauclea diderrichii* (De Wild.) Merr.

**Genus**: [[Nauclea]]
**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Protologue**: Merrill, Journ. Wash. Ac. Sc. 5 : 535 (1915)

## Synonyms

- S. Trillesii Pierre in A. Chev., Bois du Gabon, Vég. Ut. Afr. Trop. Fr. 9 : 229-213 (1917), nom. inval. — H. Heitz, Forêt du Gabon : 273 (1943). Walker et Sillans, Pl. Ut. du Gabon : 373 (1961).

## Description

A first-class tree capable of reaching 0.90–1.50 m stem diameter and 30–40 m in height. Base of trunk without buttresses or only small thick ridges not exceeding 1 m above ground. Cylindrical, clear bole 20–30 m beneath the first branches; these latter horizontal, sometimes ± drooping, forming a fairly regular crown. Bark yellowish-grey, longitudinally fissured, with yellowish-red, very fibrous inner surface, reaching 1.5–2 cm in thickness. Large foliaceous stipules, oval or obovate, broadly rounded at apex, 10–25 (70) × 4–12 (68) mm, adorned with a thin and very prominent axial keel. Petiole glabrous, 8–13 (18) mm on the fruiting branches. Limb elliptic or oblong, (4)7–15 (18) × (2.5) 4–9.5 (12) cm and up to 45 × 21 cm on young sterile trees, glabrous, dark green ± glossy above, reddish-brown when dry; base rounded or briefly acute at apex of petiole; apex of limb obtuse or very indistinctly acuminate. Secondary veins (4) 6–8 (13) pairs. Intersecondary veins ± irregularly arranged in chevron pattern.

Inflorescences terminal, solitary, sometimes arising from fairly short lateral branches; subcapitate peduncle enlarged at apex, 11–18 mm long, with two scar-bearing levels, one near the base, the other towards the middle. Floral clusters spherical, ca. 3 cm diameter at anthesis. Calices very shortly lobed and pubescent, with sepals unequally fused together in small groups. Corolla 7–8 mm long, 3–4 lobes, white, whitish-green or yellowish; tube pubescent inside at the level of the stamens and on the base of the lobes. Anthers acute, 1–1.5 mm long, exceeding the notch of the lobes. Style 11–13 mm; stigma enlarged, acuminate. Ovary 2-celled, multiovulate.

Infructescence ± spherical, reaching 4 cm diameter, fleshy, fragrant and greyish-brown with whitish flesh at maturity; the subspiniculate surface is adorned with a discontinuous network of calycinal lobes unequally fused together around the floral pits. Numerous ovoid seeds, brown, 1 mm long, not margined, adorned with a fossulate network.

In light of these facts, it is preferable to accept De Wildeman's name, which is earlier and already implicitly accepted by Pellegrin in Périquet in 1916.

Bilinga is a light-demanding timber species widespread in secondary forests; it adapts to various edaphic conditions, dry or fresh alluvial soils, and sometimes forms fairly significant stands. The species is distributed from Sierra Leone to the central forest district of the Congo.

Bilinga, also called golden wood or yellow mahogany of Gabon, is an excellent timber tree. It is exported in often substantial quantities after Okoumé. The beautiful yellow-orange colour and the physical qualities of its semi-hard, fine-grained wood make it sought after in cabinet-making and decorative carpentry. It is resistant to wear, weathering and numerous parasites. Its mechanical qualities sometimes also lead to its use as railway sleepers.

The fruit is edible. The base of the trunk is often mutilated by bark-stripping for medicinal uses: bark decoction in intestinal enemas (Heitz); infusions of young leaves or bark as febrifuges (Walker).
