# *Alstonia boonei* De Wild. Planche 9 ; Figure 1(D-G)

**Genus**: [[Alstonia]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg. 13 : 382 (1914)

## Description

Tree up to 25 m high, up to 1 m in diameter, with fluted base or steep buttresses rising to 8 m in height, white latex in the bark; branches, leaves, flowers and fruits white-waxy. Leaves whorled 4 to 9, with numerous large axillary colleters; petiole (0.2–)0.6–2.6(–2.9) cm; blade obovate to narrowly obovate, or sometimes elliptic-oblong, 1.7–4.3 times as long as wide, (8–)10–19(–24) × 3.5–7.5 cm, apex acuminate or sometimes obtuse or retuse, coriaceous; secondary veins 24 to 50(60) pairs, almost perpendicular to the midrib, connected to each other by a submarginal vein. Inflorescences composed of quasi-umbels or up to sets of –; 4–17.5 cm; last ramifications always densely pubescent, the first 2 to 4 ramifications umbellate, bracteate up to mm in length. Flower: pedicel 0.2–0.6 cm; calyx with lobes 1–2.2 mm long, puberulent on both faces; corolla yellow-green or pale green, lobes rarely white, pubescent on the outside and on the lobes on the inside, on the inside with a ring of hirsute hairs at the throat and with stiff curved hairs lower down, tube 1.5–3.5 times as long as the lobes, 6.5–14 mm long, constricted above the ovary and wide ± mm; lobes deformed at the apex, obliquely ovate to obliquely obovate, 2.5–6.5 × 1.3–4 mm; stamens inserted in the upper part of the tube, anther 1–1.6 mm long; pistil 4.5–11.5 mm long, with style 3.1–8.5 mm, glabrous. Fruit: follicles linear, connate at the base, 22–57 cm long, pubescent, with numerous biseriate seeds. Seed flattened, 4–6 mm long, with thickened margin all around, with long stiff hairs at the ends and much shorter hairs on the sides. Distribution from Senegal to Ethiopia, Tanzania and Angola; Cabinda, in Gabon, Estuary, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: mangrove swamps, primary and secondary forests in Gabon, up to m in altitude; elsewhere up to m. Uses: The wood is used for sculptures and utensils, for making plywood and in interior joinery. The bark and latex are used in traditional medicine.

## Vernacular names

émien (nom pilote). B et al. (2015), Omino (2002), Sidiyasa (1998). IBLIOGRAPHIE
