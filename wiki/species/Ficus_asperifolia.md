Shrubs, often sarmentose, up to 2 m tall, sometimes 4 m, or lianes reaching 8 m, sometimes with twining branches; latex often aqueous. Leafy twigs, 1–5 mm thick, brown to white, shortly strigose, strigose or hispidulous, with brown or white hairs, glabrescent to almost glabrous, yellowish to reddish-brown when dry, periderm somewhat detaching. Leaves distichous. Blade elliptic to oblong, ovate, narrowly lanceolate, subobovate, occasionally linear, usually ± asymmetric, 3–23 cm long, 1.5–12 cm wide, papyraceous to subcoriaceous, apex acuminate to caudate or acute, sometimes obtuse, base acute to obtuse or rounded, margin ± distinctly dentate to subentire, sometimes lobed or almost entire; upper surface scabrous to shortly scabrous, with very small rigid hairs, or shortly strigose brownish to strigose only on the midrib, lower surface scabrous to scabrid with very small rigid to hirtellous hairs, sometimes almost glabrous or pubescent; veins weakly prominent to flat above, prominent below; (3 in very small leaves) 5–9 (to 13 in large leaves, often more than 13 in very narrow leaves) pairs of lateral veins, including a pair of basal veins; glandular dots in the axils of basal veins and often also in the axils of other secondary veins. Petiole 0.5–2 cm long, 1–2 mm thick, pubescence similar to that of the leafy twigs. Stipules free, 0.3–0.6 cm long, whitish-brown (very shortly) puberulent to almost glabrous, caducous.

Figs 1–3 (–5) in the leaf axils or below the leaves, sometimes on old wood; peduncle 0.2–1 (–1.5) cm long, 1–2 mm thick, scabrous with sparse to rather dense hairs, white to brown, short, rigid; 2–4 small bracts scattered on the peduncle, broadly ovate, pubescence similar to that of the peduncle; 2–4 similar bracts scattered on the receptacle; receptacle globose-depressed to obovoid, 0.3–3 cm in diameter when fresh, 0.5–1.5 cm when dry, pubescence similar to that of the peduncle, (dark) red to pale red, orange, yellow or white, or especially for the larger ones, (pale) yellow to greenish at maturity; ostiole prominent, upper bracts of ostiole often ± erect when dry.

Pistillate flowers with 4–6 tepals, 1–1.5 mm long, rather narrow, acute to obtuse, glabrous or sparsely puberulent; seed flowers sessile or (depending on the fig diameter) with pedicel 1–5 mm long, style 1.5–2.5 mm long; gall flowers with pedicel reaching 2 mm, style ca. 0.7 mm long. Fruits ellipsoid, oblongoid, ovoid or obovoid, sometimes ± stipitate, 1–2 mm long; gall fruits ellipsoid to subglobose, ca. 1–1.5 mm long; fruits unicolorous, most often pale yellow, sometimes true fruits reddish. Staminate flowers with pedicel 1–2.5 mm long, perianth with 4–6 tepals 1.5–2.5 mm long, narrow, subacute, sparsely puberulent, filament ca. 1–1.3 mm long, thickening towards the anther 0.7–0.8 mm long. Pedicels puberulent to almost glabrous. – Pl. 35, p. 129.

## Discussion

In the present circumscription, F. asperifolia includes F. urceolaris and F. storthophylla, considered as distinct by earlier authors who revised the genus Ficus (Mildbr. & BURRET, 1911; HUTCH., 1916; LEBRUN & BOUTIQUE, 1948). The characters used to distinguish these species are not sufficiently important for separation at the specific level, and could only be used to recognize (regional) variations. The lianescent or climbing habit (under the binomial F. spirocaulis MILDBR. in FWTA, ed. 2, 1: 611) appears to be more common in Cameroon than in other regions.

The species is distributed from Senegal to Sudan, Uganda and Tanzania, and southward to Angola and Zambia, also in Fernando Po. It is usually found along rivers or in riparian forests (periodically inundated).

Terrestrial trees, without aerial roots. Leaves arranged in spirals. Blade scabrous or smooth above, margin usually ± distinctly dentate, basal pair of lateral veins strongly marked, tertiary venation parallel in the internervular surface to almost reticulate, glandular (waxy) dots in the axils of (basal) lateral veins. Stipules fully amplexicaul and free.

Figs solitary in the leaf axils or on ± ramified leafless branchlets on the trunk and/or on the main branches, pedunculate; receptacle relatively large, (sometimes filled with aqueous sap); basal bracts 3, in a whorl; ostiole round, covered by several bracts (± erect when dry), upper ostiolar bracts spread and interlocking, lower ones descending. Interfloral bracts lacking along the pistillate flowers.

Staminate flowers clustered near the ostiole, surrounded by 2 (–3) subtending bracts, perianth tubular, bilobed, one lobe longer curved over the other. Seed flowers and gall flowers rather distinct, perianth with 2–6 tepals, free or partially fused, often irregular in shape. Stamens 1–3, normally 2, stigmas 1 with an apical pore, elongate or truncate, free or partially fused. Fruits unicolorous brown.

Pollinators: species of the genus Ceratosolen.

In continental Africa 5 species from savanna and dense humid forest regions can be recognized in this subgenus. In Gabon, F. mucuso, F. sur and F. vogeliana are found.

These 3 species are very closely related and it is sometimes difficult to separate them. These 3 taxa have their figs on special leafless branchlets on old wood down to the base of the trunk or (in F. vogeliana) only at the base of the trunk. Figs on normal leafy branchlets can also be found in F. sur.

The structure of the staminate flowers is not entirely clear. The stamens are entirely surrounded by a tubular perianth with 2 lobes, one of the two covering the other. The perianth probably comprises 2 tepals. The staminate flower is enveloped by 2 (or 3) free or sometimes partially fused bracts. In sessile flowers, these subtending bracts can be taken for tepals; but in pedicellate flowers, their position shows that they do not belong to the perianth. The number of stamens is variable, 2 is the normal number. At a later stage (after the pollen has been collected by fig wasps?) the filaments can elongate.
