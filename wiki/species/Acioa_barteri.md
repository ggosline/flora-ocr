# *Acioa barteri* (Hooker fil. ex Oliver) Engler

**Genus**: [[Acioa]]
**Family**: [[Scytopetalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 26 : 382 (1899); Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 311 (1915); De Wild., BJBB 7 : 210 (1920); Haum., FCB 3 : 47 (1952); Keay, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 431 (1958); AUBR., FFCI, ed. 2, 1 : 190 (1959); Irvine, Woody Pl. ## Description

Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 311 (1915); De Wild., BJBB 7 : 210 (1920); Haum., FCB 3 : 47 (1952); Keay, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 431 (1958); AUBR., FFCI, ed. 2, 1 : 190 (1959); Irvine, Woody Pl. Ghana : 258 (1961); Adam, Mém. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat. Paris, Fl. descr. Monts Nimba 2 : 536, tab. 199 (1971). (Note: This species is not mentioned by Keay, Onochie & Stanfield, Nig. Trees 1 : 320, 1960.)

— Griffonia barteri Hook. f. ex Oliv., FTA 2 : 373 (1871).

— Acioa tenuiflora DINKL. & ENGL., Bot. Jahrb. 26 : 382 (1899); ENGL., Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 311 (1915); KEAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2) : 431 (1958). See note 1 below.

— Acioa lanceolata ENGL., Bot. Jahrb. 26 : 382 (1899); Pflanzenw. Afr. 3 (1) : 311 (1913), syn. nov. See note 2 below.

— Acioa trillesiana Pierre ex De Wild., BJBB 7 : 206 (1920), nom. illeg. See note 3 below.

Climbing shrub or small tree (height 12 m for diameter 25 cm, and up to 40 cm), branched at low height; trunk somewhat fluted; bark with greyish-pink cross-section, brittle; cambium yellowish, wood hard dark pink. Young shoots dark red, covered with a whitish arachnoid tomentum, early caducous. Older branches lenticellate. Young branches hispidulous then very rapidly glabrescent. Stipules often inserted near the base of the petiole, linear, 4–6 mm, pubescent then glabrescent. Petiole 3–4 mm, hispidulous then glabrescent. Blade elliptic-oblong, 3–5.5(–7) × 7–13(–15) cm, sometimes distinctly oblong (4 × 11 cm), sometimes elliptic (5 × 8 cm), sometimes oval; base acute, frequently narrowed to the upper part of the petiole, sometimes broadly acute, occasionally somewhat asymmetric; apex acute ± distinctly acuminate; midvein sparsely hispidulous then very rapidly glabrescent below near the base; lateral veins 4–6 pairs; blade glabrous sometimes scabrous above; some circular glands, on either side of the midvein, below near the base of the blade, some glands below the acumen.

Racemes solitary terminal and axillary subter­mi­nal, sometimes apparently multiple and axillary, sometimes paired, 3–4(–12) cm, bearing numerous flowers relatively small in size; peduncle, axis, bracts, pedicels, bracteoles pubérulent, with bracts and bracteoles often somewhat hispid toward the apex externally; peduncle 0–1(–4) cm, furnished with several broad tricuspid bracts at the apex. Bracts oval-lanceolate, 2–4 mm, frequently furnished toward the base or on the margins with circular glands; portion of the pedicels below the articulation slender, 6–8(–10) mm; 2 bracteoles alternate, very rarely opposite, lanceolate, 1–1.5 mm, sometimes furnished with rare glandular marginal denticulations, very rarely with stipitate glands, the terminal articulate portion of the pedicel generally reaching or exceeding the apex of the upper bracteole. Pedicel of the flower disarticulated (5–)8–12(–15) mm. Receptacle 4–6 mm, tubular or narrowly obconic and ± gibbous at the base, puberulent; sepals 4–5 mm, puberulent for the parts exposed externally in the floral bud, sometimes with ± stipitate marginal glands; petals white, oblong-obovate with base especially acute, or obtuse, 4–5 mm in height; androecium (15–)25(–30) mm, 15–20 filaments.

Drupes ovoid-compressed, 25 × 35 × 50 mm (in sicco), pointed at the apex, with pubescent then glabrous surface, with some tuberculate wrinkles toward the apex. (According to KEAY, l.c. : 431, drupe turgid "tuberculate", ± pyriform; this type of fruit appears to correspond well to that of Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl., although the specimen Andoh 5388 from Ghana cited by KEAY possesses glabrous floral receptacles (in herb. P.!), which would link it rather to Acioa scabrifolia Hua; these two species appear in fact to possess the same fruit. One cannot sufficiently draw attention to this poorly known question of Acioa fruits). — PL. 1, p. 11.

## Discussion

1) DE WILDEMAN (l.c. : 210), having not seen the syntypes of Acioa tenuiflora Dinkl. & Engl. (Dinklage 1675 and 1772 from Liberia), doubted the attribution of another specimen Dinklage 2101 (not 2021 — cf. pp. 194, 195, 210), also from Liberia, to Acioa tenuiflora Dinkl. & Engl., bringing it closer to Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl. KEAY, l.c. : 431, based on this same specimen Dinklage 2101, nevertheless admitted this synonymy. This is now clearly confirmed by the existence of the syntype Dinklage 1675 in the herbaria of Zürich(!), the Arnold Arboretum of Harvard-Cambridge University USA(!), Edinburgh(!), Stockholm(!) and which, even by its inflorescence, cannot be separated from Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl.

2) ENGLER (l.c. : 382) described Acioa lanceolata Engl. from the specimen Dinklage 925 from Cameroon, noting that this was a flowering branch from a coppice shoot; an isotype of this specimen is still available in the Hamburg herbarium(!) but no particular character, given its origin, allows it to be distinguished from Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl.; even the denticulation of the bracteoles on which ENGLER insists (comparing it moreover to that of Acioa acutifolia Engl., a taxon seemingly unknown elsewhere in the literature) offers nothing abnormal for the species in question.

3) DE WILDEMAN (l.c. : 206), placed Acioa trillesiana Pierre ex De Wild. in synonymy with Acioa scabrifolia Hua; he notes moreover: Acioa trillesiana Pierre ms. in "Bull." Mus. Paris, without further details; no mention of this species as nom. nud. appears, it seems, in the Bull. Mus. Nat. Hist. Nat. Paris from 1899 to 1905; De Wildeman more likely meant to refer to: in "Herb." Mus. Paris. In any case, Acioa trillesiana Pierre ex De Wild. cannot be placed in synonymy with Acioa scabrifolia Hua, because the holotype Trilles 22 from Gabon, with its "shortly hairy" calyx, must clearly be referred to Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl., DE WILDEMAN moreover emphasizing in his observations this fundamental difference between this species and Acioa scabrifolia Hua.

Acioa scabrifolia Hua is a very closely related species to Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl. and is essentially distinguished by its glabrous receptacle and scabrous blade above (although this character is difficult to assess); the question of the fruits of these 2 species remains confused (see above). The range of this species covers only West Africa, from Guinea to Ghana, its presence in western Cameroon (under the name Acioa lehmbachii Engl., cf. KEAY, l.c. : 431) cannot be admitted at present (see this taxon). By contrast Acioa barteri (Hook. f. ex Oliv.) Engl. has a range extending to central Africa and occurs from Sierra Leone to Gabon; its presence in Zaire had been questioned by Hauman (l.c. : 47) but it appears nonetheless to exist in Kivu (A. Léonard 5162, BR!). This species generally lives on the banks of rivers, sometimes (Ekondo Titi) behind the mangrove.

Bos 4486, Kribi, near R. Kienké (ster., May) (WAG). De Wilde W. & al. 2698, near Nyong, 35 km WSW Eka (65 km SSW?) (fl., June). Dinklage 925 (type of Acioa lanceolata), Grand Batanga near Kribi (fl., Nov.) (HBG). Fleury in Chevalier 33487, Campo (ster., wood no. 464). Letouzey 15101, Masoré, 8 km NW Ekondo Titi (ster., wood, June).
