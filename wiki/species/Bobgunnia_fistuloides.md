# *Bobgunnia fistuloides* (Harms) J.H.Kirkbr. & Wiersema Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Bobgunnia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Brittonia 49 : 3 (1997)

## Synonyms

- Swartzia fistuloides Harms, Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 65 : 305 (1910).

## Description

Tree 10–40 m tall; trunk up to 80(–120) cm in diameter; bark thin, yellowish to pale grey, scaly, sapwood yellow. Stipules oval depressed, ± 0.5 × 1–1.5 mm, with obtuse apex. Leaf: petiole 2–3 cm; rachis 12–18 cm, pubescent becoming glabrous with age; leaflets 9 to 15, with petiolule 4–7 mm, pubescent, then glabrous, with ovate to lanceolate blade, 5–13 × 3.5–6 cm, base truncate to cuneate, apex apiculate, glabrous and dark green and shiny above, mid-green and dull with slight pubescence below, especially on the midvein.

Inflorescence pendulous, in axillary panicle, multiflorous with 3 or 4 flowers per node on the axis 10–25 cm, glabrous or nearly so. Flower: pedicel 10–15 mm; calyx 8–10 mm in diameter, tearing into 2 to 4 lobes; petal with circular to transversely broadly elliptic blade, 8–20 × ± 22 mm, white with yellow base, silky adaxially, with claw 5–6 mm; stamens 30 to 40, filaments yellow, anthers orange; ovary linear, stipitate. Pod pendulous, straight or curved, cylindric or somewhat rectangular in section, slightly torulose, (6–)10–21(–80) × 2–3 cm, dark brown, with stipe 1–2 cm; pulp fibrous, pale brown. Seed lenticular, ± 15 × 10 × 6 mm, dark brown, surrounded by fibrous pulp; hilum slightly excentric; cotyledons thin, with horny albumen.

## Distribution

Guinea, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon, known from all provinces except Nyanga

## Ecology

primary and secondary forests, open forests, lakesides; in Gabon up to 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 570 m. Uses: The wood is used in joinery. The bark is ichthyotoxic, the decoction is used in medicinal baths for small children, and mixed with chili pepper to aid nursing women. The pod is toxic. The wood is said to be aphrodisiac (Towns et al. 2014)
