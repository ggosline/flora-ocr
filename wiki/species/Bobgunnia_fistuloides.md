# *Bobgunnia fistuloides* (Harms) J.H.Kirkbr. & Wiersema Planche 19

**Genus**: [[Bobgunnia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Brittonia 49 : 3 (1997)

## Description

Tree 10–40 m tall; trunk up to 80(–120) cm diameter; bark thin, yellowish to light grey, scaly, sapwood yellow. Stipules oval depressed, ± 0.5 × 1–1.5 mm, apex obtuse. Leaf: petiole 2–3 cm; rachis 12–18 cm, pubescent becoming glabrous with age; leaflets 9 to 15, petiolule 4–7 mm, pubescent, subsequently glabrous, limb oval to lanceolate, 5–13 × 3.5–6 cm, base truncate to cuneate, apex apiculate, glabrous and dark green and glossy above, mid-green and dull with slight pubescence beneath, especially on the midvein.

Inflorescence pendulous, axillary panicle, multiflowered with 3 or 4 flowers per node on the axis 10–25 cm, glabrous or nearly so. Flower: pedicel 10–15 mm; calyx 8–10 mm diameter, tearing into 2 to 4 lobes; petal with limb circular to transversely broadly elliptic, 8–20 × ± 22 mm, white with yellow base, silky adaxially, claw 5–6 mm; stamens 30 to 40, filaments yellow, anthers orange; ovary linear, stipitate. Pod pendulous, straight or curved, cylindric or somewhat rectangular in section, somewhat torulose, (6–)10–21(–80) × 2–3 cm, dark brown, with stipe 1–2 cm; pulp fibrous, pale brown. Seed lenticular, ± 15 × 10 × 6 mm, dark brown, surrounded by fibrous pulp; hilum somewhat eccentric; cotyledons thin, with horny albumen.

## Distribution

Guinea, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon, known from all provinces except Nyanga

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forests, open forests, lake margins; in Gabon up to 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 570 m. Uses: The wood is used in joinery. The bark is ichthyotoxic, the decoction serves as a medicinal bath for small children, and mixed with chilli pepper to aid nursing women. The pod is toxic. The wood is said to be aphrodisiac (Towns et al. 2014)
