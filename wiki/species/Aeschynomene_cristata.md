# *Aeschynomene cristata* Vatke Planche 4

**Genus**: [[Aeschynomene]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Oesterr. Bot. Z. 28 : 215 (1878)

## Description

Shrub or perennial herb; stem reaching 3 m in length, erect or floating on water; branches soft, silky, hairy-sticky, the hairs often with bulbous base, yellowish. Stipules lanceolate, 1.5–2.5 × 0.4–0.6 cm, longitudinally striated, with oblong appendage, 3–5 mm below the point of insertion, glabrous except for a few hairs at the node. Leaf: petiole 0.5–1 cm; rachis 10–15 cm, hirsute with stiff glandular hairs at their base; leaflets in 20 to 40 pairs, with oblong-lanceolate blade, 6–22 × 1–4 mm, mucronate, glabrous, with dark red veins beneath on glaucous green background. Inflorescence axillary, with 1 to 3 flowers, 2–8 cm in length; peduncle 2–4 cm; bracts elliptic-ovate, 4–7 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 2–3 cm, pubescent; bracteoles elliptic-ovate, 4–6 mm in length, with rare hirsute hairs, stiff and swollen at the base; calyx with tube 3–4 mm in length, upper lip obovate, 8–12 mm in length, lower lip 11–16 mm in length, tube and lobes papillose and with stiff glandular hairs at their base; corolla yellow, standard erect, suborbicular, 2 × 1.8 cm, wings obovate, oblique, 1.5 × 0.4 cm, glabrous, keel deltoid, 3 × 2 cm, obtuse, with a median crest and some stiff hairs; stamens monadelphous, glabrous, anthers all similar; ovary linear, glabrous, 15–20 mm in length; style filiform, 15–25 mm, stigma very reduced. Pod stipitate, (5–)8–11 × 0.7–0.8 cm, ± moniliform, especially in case of incomplete fertilization, with (3–)6 to 10 flattened articles, rectangular-rounded, 8 mm in length and width, glabrous but in the middle furnished with fine spines. Seed reniform, ± 6 × 4 × 2 mm, brown; hilum 2.5 mm in length, central.

## Distribution

Tropical Africa and Madagascar; in Gabon, known from the Ngounié and Nyanga

## Ecology

open humid places, in swamps, ponds, ruderal herbaceous vegetation; in Gabon, at 0–400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1050 m. Flowering and fruiting in February. b: Léonard (1954a, 1954b), Pellegrin (1949). Bibliography
