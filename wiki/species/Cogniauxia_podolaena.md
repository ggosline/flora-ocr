# *Cogniauxia podolaena* Baill.

**Genus**: [[Cogniauxia]]
**Family**: [[Cucurbitaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Mens. Soc. Linn. Paris 1: 423 (1884)

## Description

Plate 16

Herb; stem capable of reaching 8 m in length and 7–8 mm in diameter; roots capable of penetrating the soil to ±1 m. Tendrils bifid, fairly robust. Leaf: petiole 2–4 cm, puberulent; blade ovate-cordate to broadly ovate-cordate, deeply emarginate at the base, acute-acuminate at the apex, 9-18 × 10-15 cm, ±coriaceous, glabrous except on the veins of the lower surface which are shortly pubescent, with margin slightly undulate or sometimes dentate. Inflorescence: the male one in multiflorate racemes reaching 20 cm in length; bracts linear to subulate, 8-12 × 0.5-1 mm. Male flower: pedicel 1-2 cm, puberulent; receptacle 15-20 mm long; sepals elongate, 10 × 2-3 mm, acute-acuminate at the apex; petals fine, spreading at anthesis, obovate to obovate-oblong, 5-6 cm in diameter, orange-yellow, subrounded and shortly apiculate at the apex, sometimes slightly asymmetric; stamens with short and thick filament, anthers 6-8 mm long, with very sinuous lobes. Female flower: pedicel ±2 cm; staminodes 5, lamelliferous, very small, inserted toward the middle of the receptacle; ovary narrowly oblong, 18-20 mm long; style short and thick, columnar, with bilobed, thick stigma, 2 mm long. Fruit: ovoid-ellipsoidal, 12-20 × 5-8 cm, red at maturity and ornamented with clear and dark longitudinal lines; pedicel thick, ±1 cm. Seed obliquely elliptic, marginate, acuminate at the apex and almost bilobed at the base, 20 × 14-15 × 3 mm, brownish.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

in dry or riparian forests, forest edges, roadsides, fallow land; in Gabon at 0–600 m altitude, elsewhere up to 750 m

## Vernacular names

ébundja-sótó (mitsogo); ilèngè nyi nghômbè (nkomi); mururuvi (éshira)

## Discussion

Uses: Dried leaves in powder form against burns. The root is used as an extremely vigorous emeto-cathartic, with also diuretic and revulsive properties (abortive risk), also used as a decoction in palm wine for stubborn constipation, to treat ascites, edema and scrotal hernia; as an enema for hernias, urethral discharges and hematuria; the sap is crushed with sugar cane against costal and gastric pain; the pulp is applied by friction on scarifications; as a repellent against intercostal pain, rheumatism and headaches; poultices to ripen boils, bubos and breast abscesses, taking care to protect with an application of palm oil the skin of scars, if the poultice is to be kept on for a long time. The juice of the roots is insecticidal and parasiticide and it is administered in the form of a compress or enema as an anthelmintic, and in ocular instillation for filarial worms in the conjunctiva of the eye; it is also smeared on the wood of beds and cupboards as a repellent against bedbugs and cockroaches.
