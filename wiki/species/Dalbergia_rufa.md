# *Dalbergia rufa* G.Don

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Gen. Syst. 2 : 534 (1832)

## Description

, syn. nov. Liana or shrub 1.2–10 m tall, inodorous, without spines, but sometimes furnished with prehensile hooked branchlets; bark dark brown fissured longitudinally, without lenticels; young branchlets with short dense spreading tawny hairs, slightly crispy. Stipules caducous or sometimes persistent for a fairly long time, foliaceous, suborbicular to narrowly elliptic, rounded or acute at the apex, 3.5–9 × 1.5–3.5 mm, with short dense pubescence. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 0.3–1.5 cm; rachis 2.6–10.5 cm, pubescent like the branchlets; leaflets 7 to 16(–20), alternate and unequal, with petiolules 1–3 mm, lamina elliptic, ± asymmetric, except for the terminal leaflet, 2.1–7.4(–9.5) × 1.1–4.2 cm in the upper leaflets and 0.5–3.2 × 0.4–2.3 cm in the lower ones, rounded to obtuse (rarely acute) at the base, rounded to shortly acuminate at the apex, slightly coriaceous, brownish when dry and often glaucescent on the lower surface, with erect tawny hairs (rarely ± appressed) and generally crispy 0.3–0.5 mm, at first dense on both surfaces then more sparse, the upper surface sometimes glabrescent; lateral veins 3 to 10, generally inconspicuous, veinlets in rather dense and evident reticulum on the lower surface. Inflorescence in a raceme or panicle, axillary or terminal, 1.3–9 cm in length (sometimes grouped into very large false panicles on defoliated branchlets furnished with stipules that appear to be bracts), with spreading tawny, crispy, dense and short hairs; bracts caducous after flowering, narrowly elliptic, 0.8–3.8 × 0.3–1.2 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 2.5–4 mm; bracteoles caducous after flowering, linear or narrowly elliptic, 0.7–1.2 × 0.2–0.3 mm, pubescent; calyx pale green, with fairly dense tawny hairs, tube 2–3 × 1.5–2.3 mm, teeth valvate ± equal and triangular 0.5–1(–1.5) mm in length, acute or the upper ones sometimes obtuse; corolla white, petals with claw equalling or slightly exceeding the calyx, standard bent at a right angle or even slightly folded back, with claw 3–3.5 mm and lamina ± rectangular 3.5–4.5 × 2–3 mm, with emarginate apex, wings equalling the keel, with claw 2.5–3.5 mm and lamina ± rectangular 4–6.5 × 2–2.5 mm, smooth externally, with truncate base or barely auriculate, keel strongly curved, with claw 2.5–2.7 mm and lamina 5.3–6.5 × 2.5–3 mm, with truncate base or barely auriculate; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments 7–8 mm, connate for ¾ to 4/5 of their length and curved from the base in a very open S; ovary 2.5–3.5 × 0.7–0.85 mm, glabrous, with 3 ovules and curved stipe 3.5–5 mm; style linear, 1–1.5 mm, with punctiform stigma. Pod with pedicel 1.5–5 mm and stipe 5–7 mm, papyraceous, flattened and narrowly ellipsoid, (3–)3.5–7.3 × 1.1–2.2 cm, obtuse at both ends, with slightly prominent reticulation, especially at the level of the single seed, glabrous or with very rare hairs. Seed flattened, elliptic, 7.5–9.5 × 4–4.5 mm.

## Distribution

Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (Rio Muni), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo; uncommon but widely distributed in Gabon, recorded in Haut-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Secondary forests, forest margins, regrowth, forest galleries, wooded savannas and low forests on rocky ridges; up to 780 m altitude in Gabon and elsewhere. In Gabon, flowering in January, February and September; fruiting from September to December.

## Source note

The leaflets of D. rufa are quite variable in the shape of the lamina apex (generally rounded or obtuse, more rarely acuminate) and in their dimensions; the distinction between D. rufa and D. kisantuensis, based solely on leaflet size, cannot be maintained. The lamina is normally covered with fairly dense and ± erect hairs, but a specimen from the Batéké Plateaus (D. Nguema 814) is atypical in its leaflets with very sparse appressed hairs, practically limited to the midvein. The inflorescences of D. rufa most often appear on leafy branchlets. More rarely, they arise on defoliated axes bearing only stipules; this gives the plant a very different appearance, the entire axis appearing as a large inflorescence whose stipules imitate bracts. The upper buds of these axes may, after flowering, give rise to leaves. Two species very close to D. rufa exist in the Congo basin: D. grandibracteata De Wild. and D. gentilii De Wild. The latter has been recorded from Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006) through confusion with D. rufa. These two species differ from D. rufa in their flowers and bracts generally larger, and their bracteoles broadly elliptic; D. gentilii has, furthermore, symmetric leaflets with appressed hairs. b: Adam (1971), Berhaut (1976), Cronquist (1954), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Pellegrin (1949)
