## Description

Sp. pl., ed. 4, 3(2): 880 (1802). Annual herb, erect, 5–40 cm; stem slender, with sympodial growth, pseudo-di- or trichotomously branched, apparently terminating in an inflorescence with growth continuing via lateral branches generally exceeding the primary axis, with crisp to nearly spreading pubescence. Leaves alternate; petiole 1–3 mm, with crisp pubescence; blade linear-elliptic to oblanceolate, 10–60(–80) × 2–20 mm, cuneate at base, subacute to rounded at apex, with dense crisp to glabrescent pubescence on both surfaces. Inflorescence: dense terminal racemes, often globose-ovoid, 1.5–3 cm in diameter; peduncle 10–30 mm, with crisp pubescence like the rachis; bracts lanceolate, 1.5–2 mm in length, generally pubescent, persistent; bracteoles similar, but 1–1.25 mm in length. ## Distribution

widely distributed across tropical Africa: Mauritania, Ethiopia and Sudan, Mali, Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, Congo (Brazzaville), Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola, Burundi, Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ogooué-Maritime, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo and Haut-Ogooué.

## Ecology

forest, dry savanna, sandy soils, cultivated areas and roadsides; in Gabon at 0–250 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1700 m. Uses: The plant sap is squeezed into the eyes to treat headaches. Crushed leaves are used as a poultice for smallpox pustules. Powder of dried leaves and sap of fresh leaves are applied to wounds. The roots are considered to have fetish properties. Ashes mixed with castor oil are rubbed on the mouth or placed in the mouth to treat snake bites.
