# *Palisota schweinfurthii* C.B.Clarke Planche 23, Figure 5(F, H)

**Genus**: [[Palisota]]
**Family**: [[Commelinaceae]]

## Description

in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phan. 3 : 132 (1881). Perennial herb, suffrutescent or climbing, 1–2.5(–5) m tall; stem erect, sparsely branched, with upper internodes sparsely pubescent, with viscous exudate after fracture; roots thick, non-tuberous. Leaves pseudo-whorled; sheath 1–2 cm long, densely pubescent; pseudopetiole generally narrowly winged, (2–)3–8 cm, long-ciliate with red hairs, rarely ciliolate; blade elliptic to elliptic-oblong, (13–)20–40 × (4–)7–15 cm, base cuneate, apex acuminate, margins densely ciliate with reddish-brown hairs, upper surface glabrous or sometimes sparsely pubescent near margins and on midvein, lower surface almost glabrous except for midvein ± glabrous to shortly hirsute or entirely pubescent. Inflorescences 1 to 5 per terminal whorl, spicate (± 4–7 times longer than wide), 8–18 × 2–4 cm; peduncle 2–6(–11) cm, pubescent, generally with 1 or more foliaceous bracts near the base; floral bracts visible on young inflorescence, generally persistent, 10–11 × 1–2 mm, ciliate; each cincinnus with upper part densely covered with long white or light brown hairs, with withered flowers persistent. Flower: pedicel ± 4 mm; sepals equal, elliptic, 5 × 1.5 mm, smooth, white; petals somewhat longer than sepals, white with a hint of violet; stamens unequal, the 2 upper ones with filament subnull or up to 2 mm, with narrowly oblong-oval anther, ± 1.5 mm long, the lower one with filament up to 4 mm and oval anther, ± 1–1.5 mm long; staminodes as long as stamens; ovary 3-lobed, 0.5 mm diameter; style straight, 2 mm, stigma truncate. Fruit spherical, ± 7 mm diameter, cream when young, orange to red at maturity with brownish apical spots, with ± 4 seeds. Seed conical, ± 3.5 mm diameter; hilum in a broad white depression, bordered by a crest with ± 20 to 25 continuous grooves on the back; embryotega 1 mm diameter; testa light blue-gray or beige, smooth.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko), Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, South Sudan, Burundi, Uganda, Tanzania and Angola; in Gabon in the Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

secondary forest, open forest, forest edge; in Gabon from 20 to 950 m altitude, from 700 to 1800 m in East Africa, elsewhere from 100 to 600 m. Flowering from February to May, in July, October and November; fruits in February, March, from May to July and in December. Use: The leaves are used as packaging for cooked food (such as cassava) or raw for its transport

## Vernacular names

alilor (batéké) : même nom que pour P. mannii et P. alopecurus

## Source note

P. schweinfurthii is a fairly variable species of which most collections show plants with a single massive inflorescence per apical whorl. Others, such as the type specimen (Welwitsch 6599), have 2 to 5 more gracile inflorescences with strong pubescence on the pedicels, clearly visible in young inflorescences when dry. Bibliography: Bidault & van der Burg (2019), Brenan (1968b), Cabezas et al. (2014), Faden (2007a, 2012), Morton (1967), Sosef et al. (2006)
