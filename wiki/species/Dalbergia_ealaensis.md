# *Dalbergia ealaensis* De Wild.

**Genus**: [[Dalbergia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: D. altissima Baker f., J. Bot. 66, Suppl. 1 : 124 (1928)

## Description

Studies fl. Lower-Middle Congo 2(2): 151 (1907). D. altissima Baker f., J. Bot. 66, Suppl. 1: 124 (1928). Very large liane, reaching 40 m in length, with deciduous foliage, generally leafless at fruiting and sometimes also at flowering, inodorous, without spines, but sometimes bearing prehensile hook-like branchlets; stem laterally compressed at base, reaching 22 cm

in width and 4 cm in thickness; bark with red exudate at the cut; branchlets glabrous, fairly rapidly covered with dark brownish bark, without distinct lenticels. Stipules unknown, very caducous. Leaf imparipinnate; petiole 2.7–5.5 cm; rachis 7–17.5 cm, glabrous; leaflets 11 to 15, alternate and slightly unequal, petiolules 3–7 mm, limb elliptic, symmetric, 2.9–7.7 × 1–3.2 cm in the upper leaflets and 1.6–6 × 0.8–3.2 cm in the lower ones, rounded at base, rounded or very shortly emarginate at apex, coriaceous, olive-brown when dry (or blackish in young leaves), glabrous or with short appressed hairs very sparse on both surfaces; lateral veins 7 to 13, very faint and scarcely distinct from the venules, venules in dense and conspicuous reticulation in young leaves, practically invisible in mature leaves. Inflorescence in loose panicle, axillary or terminal, (3–)5–18 cm in length, glabrous or sometimes sparsely pubescent; bracts elliptic to lanceolate, glabrous except for ciliate margin, the lower ones reaching 9 × 7 mm and falling at bud opening, the upper ones 3–5 × 0.5–2 mm and falling later, but before flower opening. Flower: pedicel 2.5–9 mm; bracteoles caducous before flowering, ovate to narrowly elliptic, 2–2.3 × 0.8–1 mm, glabrous except for ciliate margin; calyx brown-red to blackish, generally glabrous except for ciliate margin (rarely hairy on outside), tube 1.7–2.5 × 1.5–2 mm, becoming slightly ridged when dry, teeth imbricate, 1–3.5 mm in length, ± equal or the lower one longer, the upper ones rounded and joined at base, the lateral and lower ones acute or rounded; corolla cream-white to pale pink, sometimes veined with purple, with a green or pale yellow patch at the base of the standard, petals with claw shorter than calyx, standard bent almost at right angle and ± folded lengthwise, with short thick claw of 1.5–2 mm and suborbicular limb of 8.5–10.5 × 8–11.5 mm, with shortly auriculate base and bilobed apex, wings exceeding the keel and almost entirely concealing it, with claw of 1.5–2 mm and very asymmetric limb of 7.5–11 × 5–6 mm, wrinkled transversely towards the base on the outer surface and truncate at the upper margin, keel strongly curved, with claw of 1.5–2 mm and limb of 5–5.8 × 3–3.5 mm, with distinct basal auricle in hook form; stamens in 2 phalanges of 5, filaments ± 7 mm, joined to the middle or slightly more, curved in C-shape from the base; ovary 3–6 × 0.8–1.3 mm, entirely covered with fairly long hairs with swollen base, with 4 or 5 ovules and stipe of 2–3 mm; style linear, 2–3 mm, with punctiform stigma. Legume with pedicel of 7–9 mm and stipe of 12–16 mm, papyraceous, flattened and ellipsoid, 10–11.5 × 2.3–2.8 cm, with prominent reticulation and scattered darker patches, appearing glabrous at first sight, but bearing a short hair in the center of each patch, with 1 seed. Seed reniform, flattened, ± 11 × 4 mm.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; rare in Gabon, recorded in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué and Woleu-Ntem.

## Ecology

Primary and secondary forests, forest galleries, river margins; in Gabon up to 700 m altitude, elsewhere up to 750 m. In Gabon, flowers from August to October, fruits in September and November. Use: In D.R. Congo, beaten barks are used in the manufacture of fabrics, and their fiber is employed in the construction of huts.

## Source note

D. ealaensis is very close to D. mayumbensis, from which it differs by slightly larger flowers, the ovary entirely pubescent and with hairs swollen at base, legumes with scattered dark patches (and not uniform in color), very caducous bracteoles and inflorescences generally glabrous (specimens from Estuaire, however, have sparsely hairy inflorescences). In the vegetative state, the two species are not always separable: although the leaflets of D. mayumbensis are often smaller, more numerous and more strongly pubescent, all these characters show some overlap. b: Cronquist (1954), Pellegrin (1949).
