# *Synsepalum dulcificum* (Schum.) Baillon..

**Genus**: [[Synsepalum]]
**Family**: [[Sapotaceae]]

## Synonyms

- Bumelia dulcifica Schum.
- Sideroxylon dulcificum A. DC.
- Bakeriella dulcifica Dubard.
- Pouteria dulcifica (Schum. et Thonn.) Baehni.
- Synsepalum glycydora Wernham.

## Description

— Baehni in Candollea IX : 276 (1942-1943). — Wernham in Journ. Bot. LV : 82 (1917). — Baker, ex Oliver F.T.A. III 503 (1877). — Engler Sap. afr. : 32 (1904). — Aubréville, F.F.S.G. : 427 et fig. (1950).

Shrub. Branchlets glabrous. Leaves elliptic to obovate-oblong, obtuse at the apex which is very obtusely acuminate and sometimes slightly emarginate, attenuate at the base which is rounded, glabrous, 5–15 cm long and 2–4 cm wide. 8–10 pairs of secondary veins prominent beneath. Very short petiole 5 mm.

Flowers white in axillary fascicles. Short ferruginous tomentose pedicels, 4 mm long. Calyx funnel-shaped ferruginous pubescent, with 5 short triangular lobes. Corolla glabrous, 5–7 mm long: tube 2–3 mm, lobes oblong 3–4 mm. Stamens with filaments 1.8 mm and anthers 1.4 mm. Staminodes 2 mm. Ovary pubescent.

Fruit ovoid, glabrous, monospermic, ca. 2 cm long, red at maturity, edible. Seed ovoid, 1.5 cm long.

Type from Upper Guinea: Thonning, Aguapim.

Shrub or small tree, reaching 4 m tall, growing along riverbanks. Very widespread in West Africa from Ghana to Cameroon and Oubangui; however not yet recorded from Gabon. Sometimes cultivated on account of its edible fruits (Ghana, Lagos, Dahomey).
