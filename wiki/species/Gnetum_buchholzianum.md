# *Gnetum buchholzianum* Engl. Planche 8

**Genus**: [[Gnetum]]
**Family**: [[Gnetaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 40 : 519 (1908)

## Synonyms

- Thoa buchholziana (Engl.) Doweld, Turczaninowia 3(4) : 32 (2000).

## Description

Sarmentose vine, dioecious, up to 10 m long or sometimes longer; branchlets slightly thickened at the nodes, glabrous. Leaves decussate or sometimes verticillate by 3; petiole approximately 1 cm, canaliculate above; blade ovate-elliptic to broadly elliptic, 8–15 × 4–8 cm, with base rounded to slightly cuneate, apex abruptly acuminate to finely apiculate or obtuse, coriaceous, glabrous, dark green above, paler beneath; lateral veins 4–8 pairs, strongly arched and forming a loop near the margin. Strobili unbranched, axillary or terminal, up to 8 cm long, articulate; peduncle 1–1.5 cm. Male strobili solitary, but often by 3 at the apex of branchlets; internodes thick, widening towards the apex; staminal column barely exsert from the envelope, bearing 2 sessile anthers. Female strobili solitary, with internodes slightly turbinate; involucre cupuliform; flowers 3 at each node, approximately 2 mm long, with moniliform hairs at the base; ovule sessile. Seed ellipsoid, 10–15 × 4–8 mm, apiculate, enclosed in the fleshy envelope orange-red at maturity, with copious endosperm.

## Distribution

Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon in the provinces of Estuaire and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

Primary forest, especially near tree-falls; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Uses: Gnetum buchholzianum is a species with uses similar to those of Gnetum africanum whose leaves are widely used as a vegetable. They are usually cooked with meat, fish and sometimes consumed in salad. The leaves chopped into fine strips are often consumed mixed with a condiment called "ragoût" based on peanut paste. In order to soften this rather hard vegetable, it is mixed with Grassé (Talinum triangulare (Jacq.) Willd.). The seeds are also consumed. The therapeutic uses are the same as those of Gnetum africanum
