# *Gnetum buchholzianum* Engl. Planche 8

**Genus**: [[Gnetum]]
**Family**: [[Gnetaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 40 : 519 (1908)

## Description

Thoa buchholziana (Engl.) Doweld, Turczaninowia 3(4) : 32 (2000). Sarmentose liane, dioecious, up to 10 m in length or sometimes more; branches slightly thickened at the nodes, glabrous. Leaves decussate or sometimes whorled by 3; petiole approximately 1 cm, canaliculate above; lamina ovate-elliptic to broadly elliptic, 8–15 × 4–8 cm, with base rounded to slightly cuneate, apex abruptly acuminate to finely apiculate or obtuse, coriaceous, glabrous, dark green above, paler below; lateral nerves 4–8 pairs, strongly arched and forming a loop near the margin. Strobili unbranched, axillary or terminal, up to 8 cm in length, articulate; peduncle 1–1.5 cm. Male strobili solitary, but at the apex of branches often in groups of 3; internodes thick, widening toward the apex; staminal column barely exsert from the envelope, bearing 2 sessile anthers. Female strobili solitary, with internodes slightly turbinate; involucre cupuliform; flowers 3 at each node, approximately 2 mm in length, with moniliform hairs at the base; ovule sessile. Seed ellipsoid, 10–15 × 4–8 mm, apiculate, enclosed in the fleshy envelope and orange-red at maturity, with copious endosperm.

## Distribution

Cameroon and Gabon; in Gabon in the provinces of Estuaire and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

primary forest, especially near tree-fall gaps; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m. Uses: Gnetum buchholzianum is a species with uses similar to those of Gnetum africanum, whose leaves are widely used as a vegetable. They are usually cooked with meat, fish, and sometimes consumed as a salad. The leaves finely shredded are often consumed mixed with a condiment called "ragout" based on peanut paste. To soften this rather tough vegetable, it is mixed with Grassé (Talinum triangulare (Jacq.) Willd.). The seeds are also consumed. The therapeutic uses are the same as those of Gnetum africanum
