# *Oxalis barrelieri* L.

**Genus**: [[Oxalis]]
**Family**: [[Oxalidaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp . pl ., éd. 2, 1 : 624 (1762)

## Synonyms

- Lotoxalis barrelieri (L.) Small, N. Amer . Fl . 25 : 49 (1907).

## Description

Herb to subshrub erect, 15–50(–60) cm in height; stem shortly branched, with subligneous base, channeled to ribbed, irregularly enlarged at the nodes, leafy from the base, pubescent. Leaves semi-fasciculate, pinnate, without stipules; petiole 1.5–4 cm long, canaliculate on the upper surface, pubescent; blade ovate to ovate-lanceolate, lateral leaflets (12–)20–35 × (5–)10–20(–24) mm, terminal leaflets (13–)30–50(–55) × (9–)20–30(–31) mm, glabrous on the upper surface, pubescent on the lower surface, base rounded, apex obtuse. Inflorescence: peduncle (2.5–)4–6(–7) cm long, compressed, pubescent; bracts subulate to linear, 1–1.5 mm long, acute, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 2–5 mm long; sepals membranous, linear-lanceolate, 3.5–5 mm long, puberulent; petals spatulate, 7–8 mm long, connate at the base, white, pink to violet; filaments connate at the base, 5 inner ones toothed at the middle, puberulent, 5 outer ones unlobed, glabrous, anthers 1.5 mm long; ovary oblong, glabrous; styles hirsute to glabrescent. Capsule ovate-oblong, 8–10 mm long, 5-locular. Seeds 3–5, ± ribbed, rugose, 2.5 mm long, white.

## Distribution

native of tropical America, but introduced into many tropical countries; in Africa: Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroun, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania; in Gabon, collected in the province of L'Estuaire and Woleu-Ntem (Monts de Cristal)

## Ecology

in gardens, along roads, plantations and villages, along rivers, in partially shaded places. Use: The leaves are eaten and appreciated for their acidic taste. B: Hiepko & Scholz (1977), Kabuye (1971), Lejoly (1980), Veldkamp BIBLIOGRAPHY (1971)
