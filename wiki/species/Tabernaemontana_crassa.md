# *Tabernaemontana crassa* Benth. Planche 111 ; Figure 12(F-H)

**Genus**: [[Tabernaemontana]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]

## Description

in Hook., Niger Fl.: 447 (1849). Shrub or tree reaching 20 m in height; bark with large lenticels; wood tender, pale yellowish; branchlets glabrous. Leaves of one pair equal or unequal, the largest up to times as long as the other but not proportionally narrower; petiole of ± mm, with a well-visible ochreate base; limb broadly to narrowly elliptic, ± – times as long as wide, ± × ± cm, rounded at the base, acuminate and rounded at the apex, coriaceous (even when fresh), glabrous on both faces; secondary nerves 7 to 17 pairs, rather straight at the base and generally abruptly curved toward the top near the margin. Inflorescence erect, ± × ± cm, ramified times, rather lax to dense but never very dense, generally with numerous flowers; peduncle 3.5–11.5 cm, rather robust. Flower fragrant; pedicel 3–20 mm; calyx with thick lobes, 2.5–8 × 3–7 mm, glabrous, the inner ones with 1 to 3 dense rows of 6 to 20 small colleters crowded basally in the middle; corolla generally with pale green tube and white lobes, thick and fleshy, bud cylindric with broadly ovoid or subglobose head, ± of the length of the bud, almost glabrous on the outside, partially pubescent on the outside, densely pubescent on the inside; tube long 34–100 mm, subcylindric, not angular, inflexed around the insertion of the stamens, wide ± mm, twisted at the base, with twisted lobes, 0.5–0.95 times the length of the tube, 24–55 × 6–20 mm; stamens deeply included, inserted less than 0.4 times the length of the tube, anther sessile, 9.5–12.5 mm long; pistil 8–20 mm long, glabrous. Fruit: mericarps distinct, obliquely subglobose, 5–12 × 4–11 × 4–10 cm, dehiscent, pale green or glaucous, with several seeds; exocarp 9–40 mm thick; aril white. Seed ± × ± × ± mm, up to with weak longitudinal striations. Distribution from Sierra Leone to Nigeria, from Cameroon to Equatorial Guinea, from Gabon to Republic of the Congo, to Democratic Republic of the Congo, to Central African Republic and to northern Angola; in Gabon Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué, Ivindo, Lolo, Woleu-Ntem. Ecology: secondary forest, montane forest, gallery forest; in Gabon up to m altitude; elsewhere up to m. Uses: The leaves and latex have several uses, essentially as a local anesthetic. The latex is also applied as a hemostatic by forming a pellicle on the wound and used internally as a vermifuge. Leaf decoction is taken as a tonic to stimulate appetite, against fever and as an aphrodisiac. The latex enters into the composition of an arrow poison. The latex from the bark produces a rubber of inferior quality. In Gabon one places this latex on the thatch of house roofs with the large leaves, because a belief has it that their bitter taste repels cockroaches. The wood is suitable for carpentry in general, for crosspieces and coffins. The plant is considered ornamental. Vernacular names: Adam's apple, flowerangle, tuia, nompilot, efang.
