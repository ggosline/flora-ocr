# *Dorstenia poinsetiifolia* Engler

**Genus**: [[Dorstenia]]
**Family**: [[Moraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 20: 142 (1894); ENGL., Mon. Afr. Pfl. 1, Moraceae: 18 (1898); RENDLE in HUTCH. & RENDLE, FTA 6 (2): 45 (1916); DE WILD., Pl. Bequaert. 6: 54 (1932)

## Description

RENDLE in HUTCH. & RENDLE, FTA 6 (2): 45 (1916); DE WILD., Pl. Bequaert. 6: 54 (1932)

Herbs with creeping rhizomes thick 2–5 mm. Ascending stems, 30–100 cm high; base woody; upper part glabrous or with short white uncinate hairs or comb-shaped, scattered to very dense, often mixed or buried under (yellowish-)white larger hairs, reaching 0.75 mm in length; internodes 1–5 cm long, 2–5 mm thick; nodes sometimes swollen. Leaves spiral. Limb oblong to elliptic to lanceolate, sometimes wider in upper half, often narrowing towards base, 4–20 × 2–8 cm, chartaceous,

acute to acuminate with rounded or acute acumen; base subobtuse or subcordate, sometimes cuneate; margin entire to sinuate, or denticulate to coarsely toothed with 1–10 coarse teeth, especially towards apex; upper surface glabrous; lower surface nearly glabrous or, especially on veins, puberulent with small white dome-shaped hairs, or ± appressed uncinate hairs, ± often mixed with white recurved hairs, larger, more scattered, reaching 0.5 mm in length; midvein and secondary veins impressed above, prominent below; 6–13 pairs of secondary veins. Petiole 5–25 (–35) mm long and c. 1.5 mm thick, with indumentum similar to that of stem. Stipules caducous or persistent, subulate, 1–6 (–10) mm long and c. 0.5 mm wide at base, with ± uncinate hairs, scattered to fairly dense; sometimes stipules grouped at stem apex.

Inflorescences solitary or grouped 2–4 in leaf axils, dark purple to yellowish-green or grayish, generally ± zygomorphic. Peduncle passing ± progressively to receptacle, either 5–10 (–15) mm long and then most often 1 mm in diameter, or (15–) 20–40 (–60) mm long and then most often 0.5 mm in diameter, glabrous to puberulent with short white dome-shaped or uncinate hairs and larger white recurved hairs. Receptacle discoid and concave, ± flat or convex, or sometimes broadly infundibuliform and then often compressed laterally; floriferous surface elliptic, suborbicular or 3–4-angular or sometimes 5–6-angular 6–15 (–25) × 2–18 mm; margin 0.1–5 (–6) mm wide, entire or weakly to strongly lobed; primary appendages 2, sometimes 3–5, rarely up to 6, 0.5–10 (–18) cm long (lateral appendages often shorter than median ones), 1–2 mm wide, often enlarged at apex or even lobed; secondary (lateral) appendages 8–30, 2–40 × 0.5–2 mm; upper surface of margin and appendages glabrous or with small white dome-shaped or uncinate hairs; lower surface of receptacle and appendages with small white dome-shaped or uncinate hairs, scattered to dense, the latter especially on well-marked median ribs that extend into primary appendages, mixed with larger white recurved hairs. Flowers ± numerous; perianth 3-lobed, c. 0.5–0.75 mm in diameter; stamens 3, with filaments 0.15–0.75 mm long, 0.05 mm thick, curved in several directions at anthesis, and anthers c. 0.2–0.35 × 0.2–0.35 mm, with connective as wide as locules. ♀ flowers 1–30; perianth c. 0.5 mm in diameter, c. 0.25 mm high; stigmas 2, linear, c. 0.5 mm long.

Stone c. 4 × 2 mm, smooth.

## Discussion

The species is variable in many characters, especially the shape and dimensions of the inflorescence, indumentum of stem, petioles, lower leaf surface and inflorescence, stipule length and number of ♀ flowers. Seven varieties can be recognized for Cameroon and Gabon.

D. tessmannii Engl. (Bot. Jahrb. 46: 274, 1911), described from poor material from Río Muni is probably conspecific with D. poinsettiifolia and in that case belongs to var. poinsettiifolia, because of the plant's indumentum and inflorescence form.

D. yangambiensis LÉONARD (BJBB 19: 227, 1949), based on material from Zaïre is very close to D. poinsettiifolia and could be conspecific. It shows affinities especially with var. poinsettiifolia. It differs from it by ± trilobed leaves.

D. poinsettiifolia is quite close to D. brieyi mentioned as probably synonymous. Moreover, D. poinsettiifolia shows resemblances with D. bequaertii De Wild. (Pl. Bequaert. 6: 23, 1932), D. gaussenii Trochain & Koechlin (Bull. Soc. Bot. France 106: 142, 1959) and D. paucibracteata De Wild. (Pl. Bequaert. 6: 50, 1932), all described from material collected in Zaïre and Congo. D. poinsettiifolia is also, but more distantly, related to D. dinklagei and cited in a remark on that species. D. sereti De Wild. resembles D. poinsettiifolia in its characters. With D. soerensenii Friis (Norw. Journ. Bot. 21: 108, 1964), from Ethiopia, all these species constitute a group of ± closely related taxa, having especially in common zygomorphic inflorescences.

KEY TO THE VARIETIES

1. Plants with white uncinate hairs, scattered to dense, always mixed with large white recurved hairs; receptacle with, most often, 2 primary appendages.

2. Receptacle discoid and flat, or slightly turbinate in fruit; floriferous surface orbicular to elliptic.

3. Margin 1–5 mm wide, wavy or with few to many secondary appendages, ± triangular, reaching 3 mm..... a. var. poinsettiifolia.

3'. Margin 1 mm at most wide, with many linear secondary appendages, reaching 23 (–40) mm ..... b. var. longicauda.

2'. Receptacle infundibuliform or almost discoid but then floriferous surface angular.

4. Margin 1 mm at most wide, not reflexed, entire or weakly lobed; receptacle discoid and concave or infundibuliform ..... c. var. angusta.

4'. Margin 1–5 mm wide, reflexed, distinctly lobed; receptacle discoid and convex ..... d. var. librevillensis.

1'. Plants glabrous to puberulent, only with short white uncinate hairs e. var. glabrescens.

MORACEAE

a. var. poinsettiifolia

- D. poinsettiifolia ENGL. var. grossedentata ENGL., Mon. Afr. Pfl. 1, Moraceae: 18 (1898).

- D. poinsettiifolia ENGL. var. subdentata ENGL., l.c.: 18 (1898). TYPE: lectotype of D. poinsettiifolia.

- D. poinsettiifolia ENGL. var. undulata ENGL., l.c.: 18 (1898). TYPE: Zenker 981, Cameroon (lecto-, B).

- D. gabunensis ENGL., l.c.: 14 (1898). TYPE: Soyaux 26, Gabon (holo-, B; iso-, K; photo-, BM).

Stems and petioles with short white recurved hairs, dense, reaching 0.5 mm, mixed with white uncinate hairs, more scattered, distinctly shorter. Lower surface of limb with same indumentum as stems on veins, but sometimes less dense. Stipules caducous or persistent, 2–7 (–10) mm.

Inflorescences in pairs, green or purplish. Peduncle 5–12 mm long, c. 1 mm in diameter, widened towards receptacle. Receptacle discoid and flat but somewhat turbinate in fruit; floriferous surface elliptic sometimes triangular, with sinuous or lobed margin, 1–5 mm wide; secondary appendages reaching c. 3 mm in length and 0.5–1 mm in width; primary appendages 2, or sometimes 3, (1–) 3–20 mm long, the 3rd situated laterally, generally shorter than the other 2 (up to c. 12 mm); upper surface of margin and appendages glabrous or with small white dome-shaped hairs; lower surface of receptacle and appendages as well as peduncle, with fairly dense short white uncinate hairs, especially on well-marked median ribs extending into primary appendages, mixed with larger white recurved hairs. ♀ flowers c. 10. — Pl. 24, fig. 1–5, p. 85.

The type of D. gabunensis (Soyaux 26) contains material probably belonging to D. turbinata and material belonging to var. poinsettiifolia. It is based on the latter that D. gabunensis was described. Although the inflorescences are small and young, they already show the characters of var. poinsettiifolia.

In some specimens with widely margined inflorescences the appendages are very short to almost absent.

Var. poinsettiifolia is known from Cameroon and Gabon where it is generally found in forest understory, often along watercourses or in marshy places, up to 1,150 m altitude.

This variety is known from Cameroon and Gabon where it is generally found in the understory (or at the margins) of primary or secondary forests, up to 800 m altitude.

This variety is known from Cameroon and Gabon where it is found most often in dense riparian or littoral forests.

Specimen Hallé N. 729 differs from other samples by the large number of flowers (c. 10). Sample Hallé N. & Villiers J.-F. 4557 has green inflorescences whereas they are probably purplish for specimens Hallé N. 729 and Hallé N. & Villiers J.-F. 4548.

This variety is known from Cameroon and Gabon, where it is found in the understory of dense humid forests.

e. var. glabrescens M. Hijman & C.C. Berg

Adansonia, sér. 2, 16 (4): 438 (1977).

Plants glabrous or with small white uncinate or comb-shaped hairs, scattered. Stipules caducous, 2–3.5 mm long; often stipules grouped at stem apex, longer (up to c. 6 mm).

Inflorescences solitary, green. Peduncle 10–27 mm long and c. 0.5 mm in diameter. Receptacle discoid and strongly convex; floriferous surface elliptic; margin c. 0.5 mm wide, reflexed; primary appendages 2, 20–80 mm long, reflexed; some secondary appendages, 1–5 (–12) mm long. ♀ flowers c. 12.

This variety is known only from Gabon (Cristal Mountains) where it is found in dense and humid forests.
