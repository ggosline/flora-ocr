# *Vitex thyrsiflora* Baker

**Genus**: [[Vitex]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1895 : 152 (1895)

## Description

Shrub sarmentose or lianescent, very much branched, reaching 4 m in height; branches hollow (inhabited by ants), pierced by a hole at each node, strongly quadrangular, with subwinged angles. Leaf: petiole 10–18 cm; leaflets 5, with petiolules 5–10 mm, well distinct from the blade, blade elliptic to obovate-elliptic, 10–18 × 4–9 cm, rounded or cuneate at the base, abruptly acuminate at the apex, entire, upper surface dark green, scabrous (hairs very short with thickened base), lower surface pale green, sprinkled with prominent yellow glands, glabrous except for the papillose veins; secondary veins (5 to) 7 or 8 (to 10) on each side of the principal vein, network prominent above. Inflorescence formed of thyrses, at the leaf axils and grouped into a large panicle at the apex of the stem; axes quadrangular, glabrous or shortly pubescent. Flower: calyx cupuliform, 2–3 mm long, truncate or with very short teeth, with numerous yellow glands; corolla 4–6 mm long, glandular, tube bent, whitish; stamens included. Fruit globose, ± 7 mm in diameter, somewhat depressed, stipitate, yellow-orange, subtended by the spread calyx.

## Distribution

West and central tropical Africa, from Guinea to the Democratic Republic of the Congo; species not yet found in Gabon, but occurring in Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea very close to the Gabonese border. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

dense forests, forest galleries

## Source note

This species is myrmecophilous, developing an exclusive symbiosis with the ant Tetraponera tessmannii (Djiéto-Lordon et al. 2005). B: Djiéto-Lordon et al. (2005), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Huber et al. (1963), Li i B liography sowski (2009), Meerts (2020a, 2020b), Moldenke (1958)
