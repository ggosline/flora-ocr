# *Platysepalum violaceum* Welw. ex Baker var. vanhouttei (De Wild.) Hauman

**Genus**: [[Platysepalum]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. Congo Belge 5 : 65 (1954)

## Description

Tree or shrub reaching 15 m in height; bark smooth, red. Stipules caducous, 3–4 mm in length. Leaf: petiole 4–6 cm; rachis 4–11 cm; stipels filiform, 2–3 mm; leaflets 7 to 9, petiolule 3–4 mm, blade obovate to elliptic, 9–13 × 3–5.5 cm, base long-attenuate, apex with acumen of 1–2 cm, pubescent, but glabrescent beneath. Flower: pedicel 2–3 mm; bracteoles fairly persistent, oblong to ovate, 3–5 mm in length, tomentose; calyx with tube of 2 mm in length, upper lip 12–14 × 13–15 mm, with 4 stout nerves, lateral lips 6.5 × 3 mm, lower lip 8 × 2 mm; corolla mauve to reddish or yellowish-white, standard 15 mm in length, white striped with red, wings 15 mm in length, keel 17 mm in length of which 4.5 mm for the claw; stamens 10–12 mm; ovary 10–11 mm in length, hairy. Legume linear to narrowly elliptic, 9–11 × 1.5–2.4 cm, with 2 or 3 seeds, with strong sutures, velvety, yellowish-brown. Seed suborbicular, reaching 15 mm, black.

## Distribution

Southern Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo to northern Angola; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Nyanga, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

primary or secondary forest, in dry terrain or in swamps, on inselbergs: rocks with humus pockets, on brown sand; in Gabon at 110–760 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Flowers in February. Use: The wood is hard, resembling ebony and is used in the Democratic Republic of the Congo to make traps. The red resin of the branches becomes hard and black in air

## Source note

Var. violaceum, with bracteoles of 7–10 mm in length, is found in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. B: Burkill (1995), Hauman (1954a), Maesen (2010), Pellegrin (1949), Raponda-Walker & Bibliography Sillans (1961)
