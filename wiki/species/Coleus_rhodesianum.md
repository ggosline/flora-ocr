# *Coleus rhodesianum* (N.E.Br.) A.J.Paton

**Genus**: [[Coleus]]
**Protologue**: Phytokeys 129 : 88 (2019)

## Synonyms

- Englerastrum schweinfurthii Briq ., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 19 : 178 (1894), non C. schweinfurthii Vatke.

## Description

E. djalonense A.Chev., J. Bot. (Morot) 22 : 126 (1909), non C. djalonensis A.Chev. E. rhodesianum N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1922 : 31 (1922).

Plectranthus djalonensis (A.Chev.) A.J.Paton, Fl. trop. E. Afr., Lamiac. : 286 (2009).

Annual herb, 10–100 cm tall, aromatic; stem erect or climbing, slightly succulent, ± branched, pubescent with short hairs, spreading or directed downward, and pale sessile glands, becoming less pubescent, sometimes with glandular hairs on the inflorescence axis. Leaf: petiole 0–6 mm; blade ovate to broadly ovate, 1–4 × 0.6–2(–2.5) cm, rounded to broadly cuneate or attenuate at base, acute to obtuse at apex, entire or toothed, slightly pubescent with pale sessile glands. Inflorescence mainly axillary, lax, branched with slender branches, rarely with 2 inflorescence axes arising from each upper leaf scar; bracts linear to elliptic, 1 mm long; cymes 1-flowered, opposite toward apex, often spiralled below, strongly unilateral, often clustered toward the end of the axis. Flower: pedicel 1–3 mm; calyx shortly tubular-campanulate, 1 mm long, 2.5–3.5 mm long in fruit, pubescent, with pale sessile glands, lobes ± equal, narrowly triangular; corolla 4–5 mm long, white, blue or purple, with scattered pale sessile glands, tube slightly or scarcely sigmoid, lower lip 1–2 mm long, cucullate, enclosing the stamens; stamens with fused filaments. Fruit: achenes broadly ovoid, slightly flattened, 1 mm long, brown, with darker spots, producing abundant mucilage.

## Distribution

Widely distributed in tropical Africa, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon: Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié and Ogooué–Ivindo. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

Pioneer of disturbed soils; in Gabon at 100–630 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1250 m. B: Morton (1963), Paton et al. (2009, 2013). Bibliography
