Large shrub or tree reaching up to 20 m in height, similar to the preceding species from which it differs essentially only in the leaves and inflorescences. Leaves broadly elliptic or often obovate, coriaceous, 10-30(-40) × 4-11(-13.5) cm, acute or acuminate (the obovate leaves often have a sudden and sharp acumen, the length of which can exceed 3.5 cm), apex shortly mucronate, base obtuse or cuneate; midrib strong and clearly prominent beneath, longitudinally wrinkled when dry, slightly marked and often depressed above; secondary nerves < 10, regularly spaced at most 5 cm apart, prominent beneath, depressed above, slightly arched from the midrib and terminating in a loop near the margin; microveinlets loosely reticulate, indistinct on the upper surface.

Inflorescences ordinarily in simple racemes, sometimes branched axillary or sub-terminal, slender, reaching 15 cm at flowering, final length 26 cm, without leaves but bearing minute prophylls at the base.

## Discussion

Along the Gulf of Guinea, this species is limited to regions near the coast: it is known from southern Guinea-Bissau to northern Gabon. The type of B. macrophylla is the only specimen collected in Gabon: it comes from the vicinity of Libreville and dates from the end of the nineteenth century: as no more recent collection is known for this country, one may wonder whether it still exists there at present.

No local name is known for this species in Gabon; nevertheless, given its close resemblance to B. tholloniana, it is probable that the two species bear the same vernacular names.
