# *Campylospermum vogelii* (Hook.f.) var. costatum (Tiegh.) Farron, Bull. Jard. Bot. État

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bruxelles 35 : 403 (1965)

## Synonyms

- Ouratea bracteata Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 264 (1903).

## Description

Tree reaching 6 m in height; branches with pale bark. Stipules persistent to caducous, triangular to narrowly triangular, 4–10 mm in length. Leaf: petiole 3–7 mm, stout; blade narrowly elliptic to narrowly obovate, (11–)14–35(–46) × (4–)6–12(–15) cm, ratio 3–3.5, cuneate to rounded at base, apex acute to acuminate, coriaceous, plane, with regularly serrate margin; main vein prominent on both surfaces, lateral veins in 10 to 25 pairs, prominent on both surfaces, tertiary venation scalariform to reticulate, distinct on both surfaces. Inflorescence terminal, borne on a very robust and flattened branch, branched, lax to rather dense, main axis (4–)5–18 cm; racemes 3 to 6, (2–)6–7(–13) cm, with flattened axes; gemmiparous scales present; bracts caducous, ovate, 1.5–4 mm in length; cymules distant 0.5–2 cm, 3- to 7-flowered. Flower: pedicel 3–7 mm, stout, articulate 1–2 mm from base; sepals narrowly ovate, 7–8 × 2–4 mm in flower, 10 × 5 mm in fruit and erect-spreading; petals obovate, 7–9 × 4–5 mm, cuneate at base, rounded at apex; stamens: anthers 4–6 mm in length; style 4–6 mm. Fruit: receptacle 4–6 mm in width; 3 to 5 well-developed drupels per receptacle, globose to broadly ellipsoid, 6–8 × 5–7 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Ivory Coast? (to be confirmed), Central African Republic (Sangha-Mbaéré), Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, uncommon, known from Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

secondary forest, degraded forest; in Gabon and elsewhere at 100–700 m elevation. Taxonomic note: Farron (1965, 1985) treated C. costatum as a variety of C. vogelii, but it differs from C. vogelii in leaves that are generally longer and wider, in a stout petiole, an inflorescence borne on a very robust and flattened branch and pedicels that are often shorter and stout. b: Bamps & Farron (1967), Bissiengou (2014), Farron (1963, 1965, 1985), Bibliography Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006)

Tree reaching 13 m in height; trunk up to 7(–10) cm in diameter; branches with whitish-brown bark. Stipules caducous, leaving a scar at base of petiole, triangular, 1–5 mm in length. Leaf: petiole 2–5 mm; blade elliptic to narrowly elliptic or narrowly elliptic-obovate, (7–)9–23(–27) × (2–)3–9(–10) cm, ratio (2.2–)2.4–3.7, cuneate to attenuate at base, acute to acuminate at apex, coriaceous to papyraceous, smooth or slightly bullate, margin serrate to serrulate; main vein prominent on upper surface, prominent to slightly prominent on lower surface, lateral veins in 10 to 25 pairs, prominent on both surfaces, tertiary veins scalariform, perpendicular to main vein, connected by nervilles, distinct to slightly distinct above, distinct below. Inflorescence terminal, branched, lax, main axis 3–14(–22) cm, compressed to flattened, zigzag; gemmiparous scales absent; racemes (1–)3 to 7, (2–)5–7(–13) cm; cymules distant 1–2 cm, 2- to 7-flowered; bracts caducous, triangular, ± 1 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 6–13 mm, articulate 3–7 mm from base; sepals narrowly ovate, 5–8 × 1.5–3 mm in flowers, 10–15 × 5–6 mm in fruits, covering the drupels; petals obovate, 3–10 × 3–6 mm, cuneate at base, rounded to slightly emarginate at apex; stamens: anthers 4–6 mm in length; style 5–6 mm. Fruit: receptacle ± 3 mm thick; drupels 1 to 4 well-developed per receptacle, ellipsoid to ellipsoid-oblong, 7–10 × 5–6 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

### Distribution

Sierra Leone, Guinea, Liberia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, southern Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola, Uganda, Burundi, western Tanzania and northern Zambia; in Gabon, poorly distributed throughout the country

### Ecology

primary and secondary forest, open forest, gallery forest and swamp zone, coastal forest; on coastal rocks and white sandy soil; in Gabon, up to 750 m elevation, elsewhere up to 1800 m. Use: A decorative plant with yellow flowers (Burkill 1997). The wood is used as firewood and for making planks (Bamps & Farron 1967). Taxonomic note: C. vogelii can easily be confused with C. reticulatum, but is distinguished by the peduncle and inflorescence axis compressed to flattened, by tertiary veins that are not reticulate but scalariform perpendicular to the main vein and connected by nervilles. b: Bamps & Farron (1967), Bissiengou (2014), Exell & Mendonça (1951), Bibliography Farron (1963, 1965, 1968, 1985), Hawthorne & Jongkind (2006), Robson (1963), Verdcourt (2005)
