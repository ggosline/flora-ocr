# *Blighia welwitschii* (Hiern) Radlkofer

**Genus**: [[Blighia]]
**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]

## Description

in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 1146 (1933); WILCZEK, Bull. Jard. Bot. Etat Bruxelles 21 : 155 (1951); HAUMAN, Fl. Congo 9 : 315 (1960).

— Phialodiscus welwitschii HIERN, Cat. Pl. Welw. : 171 (1896).

— Blighia wildemaniana GILG ex DE WILD., Ann. Mus. Congo Belge, ser. 5, 2 : 296

— Blighia laurentii DE WILD., l.c. 3 : 113 (1909).

— Blighia mildbraedii RADLK., in MILDBRAED, Deutsch. Zentr. Afr. Exped. 1907-08 2 : 481 (1912).

— Phialodiscus mortehanii DE WILD., Bull. Jard. Bot. Etat Bruxelles 4 : 361 (1914).

— Blighia kamerunensis RADLK., in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 1145 (1933).

— Phialodiscus bancoensis AUBR. & PELLEGRIN, Bull. Soc. Bot. France 85 : 291 (1938).

Tree with weak buttresses reaching 35–40 m, trunk 20–30 m under branches, 0.8–1 m in diameter (HAUMAN, LETOUZEY); rhytidome greyish-green, bark with orange streak, sapwood 4 cm pale orange-yellow, heartwood dark orange. Leaves 3–4-jugate; petiole very narrowly winged; wings 0.5 mm, raised upward; basal leaflets smaller, 6 × 3 cm; following ones larger, 12–25 (−30) × 4–8 (−10) cm, coriaceous, glabrous, obovate-lanceolate, acuminate, 10–14 pairs of veins fading toward the margin without distinct anastomosing arc, slightly discolorous, upper surface more glossy, reddish, lower surface dull, brownish.

Inflorescences in axillary racemes 4–12 cm, simple or with 1–2 branches bearing sessile cymules of 1–3 (−5) flowers arranged in a 2/5 spiral, bracts 1 mm. Flower with pedicel 5–6 mm and sepals 2–3 mm, puberulent; petals with scale sometimes longer, sometimes shorter than the limb, 2–3 mm; disk puberulent, in lobulate crown or divided into 5 rounded lobes. Axes of infructescences 10–14 cm; fruit pendulous, fresh orange-red, glabrous, with thick fleshy walls, 4–5 mm, 7 mm at the split. Seeds green, then dark purplish, 28 × 19 × 14 mm; arillus cup-shaped lobed, fleshy, yellow, reaching 25 × 17 mm when fresh, very retracted when dry. PL. 43, 1–7', p. 185.

Hard wood used in cabinet-making; crushed fruits serve fishermen as a fish stupefacient.

## Type

Welwitsch 4517, Angola (P)

## Discussion

Tree recorded from Liberia to Angola, across Côte d'Ivoire, Sierra Leone, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Gabon, Congo and Zaire. In Côte d'Ivoire a variety bancoensis Aubr. & Pellegrin is encountered. According to the authors "the fruit of this variety is half smaller than normal, which brings it closer to the fruit of B. unigata Bak., but it remains distinct by the interior of the locules, hairy and not glabrous."
