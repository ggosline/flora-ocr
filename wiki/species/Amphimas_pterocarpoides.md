# *Amphimas pterocarpoides* Harms Planche 9

**Genus**: [[Amphimas]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg. 12 : 12 (1913)

## Description

Tree reaching 50 m in height; trunk up to 1.5 m in diameter; bark greyish-green, with red exudate; branchlets and petioles tomentellous-tawny. Stipules lanceolate, reaching 2 cm in length. Leaf: petiole 3–10 cm; rachis 15–40 cm; stipels filiform, 2–5(–8) mm in length; leaflets 13 to 19, opposite to alternate, with petiolule of ± 5 mm, with blade oval-elliptic to oval-lanceolate or elliptic-oblong, 3.5–13 × 2–5 cm, base rounded to obtuse-truncate, apex ± acuminate to obtuse, glabrous, shining above, dull below; lateral veins in 9 to 15 pairs, slightly prominent and puberulent below. Inflorescence robust, 10–18 cm in length, sometimes concentrated at the branch tips; axes dark brown and tomentellous; bracts caducous, ovate, 3 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 1–1.5 mm, articulate in the middle, thick; calyx with tube of ± 2 mm in length, lobes short, rounded; petals 5 mm in length, cream to yellow; anthers ovate; ovary glabrous, flattened; style filiform, stigma small, capitate. Legume with short stipe, ellipsoid, 15–18 × 4–6 cm, central part with reticulate veins finer and more prominent than the other longitudinal veins. Seed 1.5–2 × 0.7 cm.

## Distribution

West Africa, from Guinea to Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Central African Republic, Sudan and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Estuaire and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

Dense forest, palm groves; in Gabon up to 500 m in altitude, elsewhere up to 900 m. Uses: The wood is used for various purposes in interior construction (especially local) and in veneers. The reddish resin is used in traditional medicine

## Vernacular names

ikókódi (bapunu, bayaka) ; mbikódi (mitsogo) ; mouvouka (bayaka). b : Tchinda & Tané (2008), Wilczek (1952). ibliographie
