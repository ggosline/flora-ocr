# *Millettia drastica* Welw. ex Baker Planche 93

**Genus**: [[Millettia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. trop. Africa 2 : 128 (1871)

## Description

Shrub or tree, up to 20 m tall; trunk up to 25 cm diameter; bark brown to pale gray; branchlets with cream lenticels. Stipules persistent or caducous, oblong to triangular, 4–8 mm long. Leaf: petiole 2–7 cm, with black pulvinus; rachis (15–)20–25 cm, channelled above; stipels setaceous, 1–2 mm; leaflets 15 to 21, petiolule 3–5 mm, blade oval-oblanceolate, 4–8(–11) × 2–5 cm, base oblique, ± rounded, apex with acumen up to 1.5 cm, rounded-mucronate, glabrescent above but midvein pubescent, glabrescent below, with small translucent areas in the meshes of the tertiary venation; lateral veins in 6 to 8(–10) pairs, plane below. Inflorescence axillary, at the end of branches, 11–27 cm long; peduncle 3–6 cm; brachyblasts globose, 1–5 mm; bracts oval, ± 1 mm long. Flower: pedicel 1–3 mm; bracteoles below the calyx, caducous, oval-pointed, ± 1 mm long; calyx velvety, brown, tube 4–5 mm long, lobes spaced, 0.5–1 mm long; corolla violaceous, standard with claw 3 mm, blade broadly oval, 14–17 × 13 mm, apex emarginate and hooded, the back brown velvety, wings with claw 6–7 mm, blade oblong, 12–13 × 5 mm, glabrous, keel with claw 6–7 mm, blade 12–13 × 8 mm, with uncinate auricles; stamens ± 16 mm, of which 4–5 mm free and curved, the vexillary free at the base at the end of flowering; ovary linear, pubescent, multiovulate; style glabrous. Pod oblong, 9–18 cm × 1.4–2 cm, with 5 or 6 seeds, glabrous, finely wrinkled, sutures slightly thickened. Seed flattened, orbicular, 6–7 mm diameter, dark purple, smooth.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola; in Gabon, known from Nyanga, Ogooué-Lolo and Ogooué-Maritime.

## Ecology

Mangrove edge and sunny stations, but also in rainforest, wooded savanas or Imperata grasslands; in Gabon at 100–250 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1850 m. Flowering in April, October and November. Use: Considered a powerful laxative. The wood is hard. The ground seeds have been used as a vulnerary.

## Vernacular names

kibayu kivambaka

## Source note

The species is very similar to M. eetveldeana (Micheli) Hauman, from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, but M. drastica has pedicels shorter than the calyx and leaflets with blade bearing small translucent areas in the meshes of the venation (with light and strong magnification). b: Banzouzi et al. (2008), Hauman (1954a). Bibliography
