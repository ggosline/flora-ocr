# *Heisteria trillesiana* Pierre

**Family**: [[Olacaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Heisteria]]

*Rev. Cult. Colon.* 11: 258, tab. 1–3 (1902); 13: 1, tab. 1 (1903).

## Description

Shrub or small tree 3–5 m tall, or small tree 10–15 m with unbranched trunk. Bark ash-grey with reddish undertone, scaly on surface. Twigs finely striated and weakly two-winged longitudinally. Petiole glabrous, 5–15 mm long, canaliculate above, striated below. Blade glabrous on both faces, glossy dark greenish-brown above, dull below, oblong, oblong-elliptic, elliptic or lanceolate, 10–18.5 × 3.5–7.5 cm; base cuneiform, apex obtusely acuminate. Midrib weakly concave or weakly prominent above, prominent below. Secondary nerves 6–8 pairs, ascending, curved, anastomosing 1.5–2 mm from blade margin, weakly prominent on upper face, prominent on lower face. Tertiary nerves in fairly loose network, obscurely visible on upper face, weakly prominent on lower face.

**Inflorescence**: Axillary, many-flowered fascicle. Flower pedicel 3–4 mm long. Flower bud globose with valvate petals. Flower pentamerous.

**Flowers**: Calyx with 5 glabrous sepals on both faces, triangular, apex acute marked with a black spot sometimes absent. Petals glabrous externally, pubescent in the middle of the inner face, 2.5 mm long, oblong-lanceolate, thicker at base, slightly coherent, apex weakly obtuse and slightly apiculate. Stamens 10 in two cycles; filaments of alternipetalous stamens flat, narrowed at apex, 1 mm long; filaments of epipetalous stamens adnate to petals, narrowing at apex, 0.5 mm long; anther with 2 locules and longitudinal dehiscence slits. Disc short, crowning the ovary, lobed. Ovary superior, globose, glabrous, with 3 incomplete uniovulate locules. Central placenta erect. Style short, terminated by a trilobed stigma.

**Fruit**: Solitary drupe surrounded by strongly accrescent calyx forming a 5-lobed collar with triangular lobes wider than long, apex obtuse or somewhat acute, red in colour. Drupe ovoid, black and wrinkled. Endocarp hard and lignified, 1 mm thick. Seed solitary with abundant albumen. Embryo apical.

## Distribution

**Range**: Cameroon and Gabon.

**Cameroon**: Collected near Ebéa, 26 km N of Kribi, on the Lokundję river.

**Gabon**: Known from numerous localities including the Komo basin, the Ogoué river region, Libreville, and the Cristal Mountains area.

## Habitat and ecology

This species is distributed in mountainous regions and along streams and rivers. Flowering occurs from July to November; fruits ripen from November to March. Fruits fall and decompose immediately after ripening.

## Notes

This species appears intermediate between *H. parvifolia* SMITH and *H. zimmereri* ENGL., and is very difficult to distinguish from these two species in the absence of fruit.

The wood is cream-yellow tinged with rose, firm, and splits easily. The seed has a hazelnut flavour and is eaten raw, though more commonly boiled. It contains numerous oily inclusions providing a fluid oil for consumption, but this becomes rancid very rapidly, developing an unpleasant taste that may cause colic. Oil yield from the seed is 63%.
