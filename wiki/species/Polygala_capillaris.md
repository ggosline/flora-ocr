# *Polygala capillaris* E.Mey. ex Harv.

**Genus**: [[Polygala]]
**Family**: [[Polygalaceae]]
**Protologue**: Harv. & Sond., Fl. Cap. 1 : 93 (1860)

## Description

subsp. capillaris Plate 23(3) Annual herb, (15–)20–50 cm; stem simple to strongly branched, filiform, glabrous or with a few sessile glands. Leaves alternate, subsessile; blade linear to ribbon-like, 3–15 × 0.2–1 mm, cuneate at base, acute at apex, glabrous. Inflorescence: terminal racemes loose or dense, sometimes a few lateral ones, 6–10(–20) cm long; rachis glabrous; bracts linear, 0.6–0.8 mm long, glabrous, early caducous; bracteoles similar, but 0.3–0.5 mm long. Flower pink, purplish or greenish-white; pedicel 0.25–0.5 mm, glabrous; sepals glabrous, the upper one linear, 1–1.5 mm long, winged sepals elliptic or oblanceolate, (1.5–)1.75–2 × 0.75–1 mm, lower outer sepals linear, 0.75–1 mm long, free; upper petals lanceolate or elliptic, 1.5–2 mm long, keel 1.2–1.5 mm long, crest 0.5 mm long, ± fimbriate; stamens 8. Fruit ellipsoid to subglobose, 1–1.5 × 1–1.25 mm, without wings, glabrous. Seed ellipsoid, (0.75–)1.0 × 0.5–0.6 mm, with minute appressed white hairs, not covering the base; caruncle absent.

## Distribution

widely distributed in tropical and subtropical Africa and in Madagascar; in Gabon observed in Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

gramineous formations in humid season, marsh edges, coastal herbaceous savanna; in Gabon at 0–150 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m. Use: The plant is purgative.

Taxonomic notes: P. capillaris subsp. perrottetiana (Paiva) Paiva, from West Africa, has leafy stems and strongly branched, covered with sessile glands; the seeds have white appressed hairs. In contrast, the typical subspecies, known from tropical and subtropical Africa, from east to west and from north to south, as well as in Madagascar, has glabrous stems, simple or weakly branched and loosely leafy, rarely with a few glands; the seeds have white appressed hairs or rarely a few glochidiate hairs. In a few stations in the western part of Central Africa, it is not easy to separate the two, although the latter is generally an erect unbranched herb and the former an erect strongly branched herb. The material J. Vosmer 209 (WAG), collected in the Gamba area (Ogooué-Maritime) has naked seeds, a character I have never seen elsewhere in this species. B: Burkill (1997b), Exell (1960), Gilbert (2000), Keay & Taylor (1954), Bibliography: Paiva (1998, 2007), Petit (1958).
