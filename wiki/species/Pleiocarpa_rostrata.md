# *Pleiocarpa rostrata* Benth. Planche 93 ; Figure 9(G)

**Genus**: [[Pleiocarpa]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Hooker’s Icon. Pl. 12 : 71, t. 1182 (1876)

## Description

Carpodinopsis rostrata (Benth.) Pichon, Bol. Soc. Brot. sér. 2, 27 : 147 (1953). Shrub or small tree reaching 5 m in height, with a semi-spreading habit. Leaves opposite; petiole narrowly winged to winged, narrowly elliptic to obovate, 0.5–2 times as long as wide, 10.6–28 × 3.8–11.3 cm, acute or decurrent at the base on the petiole, acuminate, rarely obtuse, at the apex; secondary veins 7 to 12 pairs, oblique, very prominent beneath, forming an angle of 45–70° with the midvein and joining in a submarginal nerve running along the entire blade or only in the upper half. Inflorescence axillary, rarely terminal, ramified 5–12 × 3–8 cm with minute, squamiform bracts on the flowers. Flower: sepal 0.5–1 mm, calyx with 4–6 lobes as long or longer than wide, 0.5–1.2 × 1.2–3 mm; corolla white, cream to pale green, 13–37 mm long in the mature bud, forming a relatively broad oblong head, 0.3–0.5 of the length of the bud, rounded at the apex; tube subcylindric, 8–22 mm long, 1–3 mm wide above the base, narrowed over 0.5–2 mm wide under the insertion of the stamens, enlarged again over 0.5–3.5 mm at the level of the anthers, yellow at the narrow throat, lobes spreading to recurved, obovate to elliptic, 0.5–1 times as long as the tube, 1–2 times as long as wide, 8–15 × 4–15 mm; stamens included, apex reaching 3–8 mm below the throat, inserted above the middle of the tube, anther 0.5–1 times as long as wide, 1–3 × 1–2 mm; pistil 10–13.8 mm long, with the apex situated 1–5 mm below the stamens, ovary with 3 to 5 (or 6) free carpels; style 6.5–11 mm. Fruit with 3 to 5 free mericarps, narrowly ellipsoid to ovoid, slightly gibbous, 12–20 × 7–10 × 5–8 mm, rounded or rostrate, orange-red, waxy, smooth to rugose, with 1 to 5 seeds. Seed 14–17 × 8–11 × 4–5 mm, orange, smooth.

Distribution: Nigeria, Cameroon, and Gabon; in Gabon known from a single flowering specimen from the Ogooué-Lolo. Ecology: in forest understory in Gabon at 550 m altitude, elsewhere up to 680 m.

## Source note

The main range of P. rostrata is located in Nigeria and Cameroon; the sole Gabon collection, Le Testu 8871, also cited by Omino, is geographically rather distant from the rest. B: Jongkind & Lachenaud (2022), Omino (1996). BIBLIOGRAPHY
