# *Hypoxis angustifolia* Lamarck

**Family**: [[Hypoxidaceae]] · **Genus**: [[Hypoxis]]

Encycl. Meth. Bot. 3 : 182 (1789).

## Synonymy

- *H. djalonensis* Hutch., FWTA, ed. 1, 2 : 394 (1936)
- *H. luzuloides* Robyns & Tournay, BJBB 25: 254 (1955)

## Description

**Bulb**: solid, 0.5–2 × 0.5–1.5 cm.

**Leaves**: in rosette, linear, grass-like, 2–6 mm wide, gradually elongating to 35 cm, far exceeding the flowers at maturity; sparsely hairy with long paired hairs, especially on the midvein and along the margins.

**Inflorescence**: covered with white to golden hairs. Lowest pedicel 0.5–2 cm long; bracts reaching 1 cm in length. Flowers 1–5, star-shaped.

**Flowers**: tepals lanceolate, approximately 5.5 × 2.2 mm; outer tepals sometimes with a red midvein on the abaxial face. Filaments filiform with versatile anthers, both approximately 2 mm long; anthers with a crest at the apex between the two lobes. Style slender, reaching 3.5 mm; stigma 0.7–2 mm.

**Fruit**: fairly long and slender, reaching 15 mm in length, containing approximately up to 25 seeds. Seeds brown and dull; seed coat papillose, covered with distinct cuticle folds, typically composed of radial structures visible as ridges, with smaller undulate structures between them.

## Distribution

**Range**: Widely represented in tropical Africa, occurring in Gabon among other regions.

**Gabon**: Cristal Mountains (Kinguélé), Owendo, Ogooué, Mfôa, Tchibanga, Ofooué.

## Habitat and ecology

Found in different types of grasslands, wooded areas, forest clearings, and along rivers. Often collected in cultivated or burned soils, up to approximately 2500 m altitude.

## Notes

The seed coat structure (testa) is described as very characteristic for this wide-ranging species. However, two specimens from Gabon (Hallé N. & Le Thomas 250, Thollon 742) are morphologically consistent with *H. angustifolia* but differ in this character, having glabrous papillae without the distinctive pleated structure. The source raises the question of whether these latter taxa merit taxonomic separation.
