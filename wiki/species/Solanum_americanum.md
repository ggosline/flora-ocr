# *Solanum americanum* Mill. Planche 36, Figure 7(I)

**Genus**: [[Solanum]]
**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gard. Dict., ed. 8 : no. 5 (1768)

## Description

S. americanum Mill. var. nodiflorum (Jacq.) Edmonds, J. Arn. Arbor. 52 : 634 (1971). S. nodiflorum Jacq. subsp. nutans R.J.F.Hend., Contr. Queensl. Herb. 16 : 30 (1974). S. americanum Mill. var. patulum (L.) Edmonds, J. Linn. Soc. 75(2) : 171 (1977). S. americanum Mill. subsp. nodiflorum (Jacq.) R.J.F.Hend., Austrobail. 2(5) : 555 (1988). Annual or perennial herb, erect or weakly climbing, up to 1.5 m in height, unarmed or with spinescent stems with age, pubescence of simple hairs; stems spreading, cylindrical or furrowed and appearing angular, pubescent, rapidly glabrescens. Leaf: petiole (0.3–)2–4 cm, sparsely pubescent; blade ovate to elliptic, 3.5–10.5 × 1–4.5 cm, attenuate at the base, acute at the apex, margin entire or sometimes sinuate-dentate, unarmed, concolorous, sparsely pubescent above, more densely pubescent below. Inflorescence lateral, 0.6–2.5 cm in length, simple or more rarely branched once, with 3 to 8 flowers all bisexual; peduncle (0.5–)1–2 cm; axes sparsely pubescent. Flower 5-merous; pedicel 3–9 mm, pubescent; calyx with tube 0.8–1.3 mm in length, sparsely pubescent, with broadly triangular lobes, 0.3–0.5 mm in length, obtuse at the apex; corolla stellate, 3–6 mm in diameter, white and yellow-green towards the centre, divided to two-thirds, with lobes spreading or strongly reflexed, triangular, 2–3.2 × 1–2.5 mm, densely papillose below; stamens equal, filament pubescent, anther 0.8–1.5 mm in length; ovary glabrous; style included to exsert, 2–2.5 mm, densely pubescent on the lower half. Fruit spherical, 4–9(–12) mm in diameter, purple-black at maturity, with 30 to 50 seeds; sclerites generally absent, but if present, 2 to 4(–6) per berry. Seed ovoid, 1–1.5 × 0.8–1.3 mm, pale yellow.

## Distribution

Pantropical with the zone of origin remaining unknown; present throughout sub-Saharan Africa; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem, and probably throughout the other provinces.

## Ecology

Very common weed; humid and dry zones, and in the latter case, close to water sources; in Gabon from sea level to 650 m elevation, elsewhere up to 2000 m. Use: The stems and young leaves are cooked and eaten like spinach. The fruits reputed to be non-edible are nonetheless often consumed raw or cooked by local populations. Leaf extract is used to treat conjunctivitis and wounds; in Nigeria, the fruits are macerated to serve as a vermifuge for poultry.

## Source note

Historically the species S. americanum and S. nigrum L. were considered as widely distributed throughout the Old World as a whole; they were notably recorded as present in Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006). But the lack of clear delimitation for these two species (and more broadly for the species of the group to which they belong, the 'Morelloid' clade) was at the origin of a long series of confusions. The name S. nigrum in particular was often used to designate all or part of the species of the 'Morelloid' clade. The most recent taxonomic and phylogenetic studies (Särkinen et al. 2015, 2018) have made it possible to precisely circumscribe S. americanum and S. nigrum, both in their morphology and in their distribution. The two species are distinguished notably by the size of their anthers, < 1.5 mm for S. americanum and > 2 mm for S. nigrum (see Särkinen et al. 2018 for more detail). Geographically, S. americanum is present throughout the tropical zones of the globe while S. nigrum is found mostly in the temperate and subtropical zones of the Old World. The two species are sympatric only in tropical Asia and only S. americanum is present in sub-Saharan Africa and therefore in Gabon. Bibliography: Edmonds (2012), Heine (1963), Manoko & van der Weerden (2004), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Särkinen et al. (2015, 2018)
