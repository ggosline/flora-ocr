TYPE: Cameroon, Moloundou region, Dja river, Mildbraed 3913 (holo-, B delet.; lecto-, HBG; isolecto-, BM).

– Pittosporum bicrurium Schinz & Durand, in Durand & Schinz, Et. Fl. État Indép. du Congo: 65 (1896). BRETELER, MLW 82 (8): 83, syn. nov. (1982). TYPE: Zaire, Jawubu, Demeuse 420 (holo-, BR).

Liana of small to medium size, sometimes very large liana with diameter reaching 22 cm, scandent shrub, shrub, sometimes small tree of ≤ 15 m. Stem whitish to pale brown with 5 rows of longitudinal lenticels. Entire xylem cylinder (?). Old branches glabrous or glabrescent with or without scattered lenticels, rarely hollow. Young branches pubescent-puberulent, hairs appressed, soon glabrescent or not.

Stipules early caducous, narrow and triangular, 1–3 (4) mm long, appressed pubescence-puberulence.

Leaves. Petiole sub- to hemicylindric, sulcate above or not, 1–6 (10) mm long, pubescent-puberulent, hairs appressed, glabrescent or not. Blade papyraceous sometimes coriaceous, drooping or not, generally dull below, obovate to elliptic, sometimes oblong or ovate, (1.5) 2–3 (4) times longer than wide, (3) 4–15 (22) × (1) 1.5–5 (12) cm; margin revolute or not; base rounded to cuneate; acumen present, often weakly marked, acute to obtuse, mucronate or not, ≤ 1.5 cm long; young leaves with subappressed pubescence on both sides of the I midvein, often also on both sides of the main II veins in very young leaves, subsequently early glabrescent or not; main II veins, (4) 5–7 (8) pairs, generally prominent on both sides, glabrous or nearly so; I midvein often ± impressed or sulcate above in its lower half, especially on young dry leaves; glands most often present only below, small, ± concentrated near the base and sometimes also at the apex.

Inflorescences, axillary or grouped on axillary shoots without leaves, cymose, generally distinctly pedunculate, ≤ 4 orders of ramification distinct and well spaced (the first mostly as long as or longer than the peduncle), few- to very multiflowered; most often with pubescence-puberulence formed of dense and appressed hairs. Peduncle 1–15 (30) mm long. Bracts and bracteoles narrow and triangular, ≤ 2 mm long. Pedicel slender ≤ 6 mm, upper part very distinct, ≤ (0.5) 1–3.5 mm, subappressed puberulence.

Flowers. Sepals reflexed, free or nearly so, obovate-elliptic to oblong, 1.5–3 × 0.7–1 (1.5) mm; exterior with appressed-puberulent to tomentellous hairs; interior with some hairs in the apical region. Petals slightly to completely spreading or even reflexed, shortly fused by their base to the filaments, generally obovate in shape, (2) 2.5–3 (4) mm long, split for (1) 1.5–2 mm (half their length), glabrous or with very rare external hairs below the slit; lobes concave often spreading. Stamens suberect, often slightly longer than the petals, 2.5–3.5 (4) mm long, glabrous; anthers 0.3–0.6 mm long, connective distinct. Staminodes subquadrate to oblong-ovate, ≤ 0.4 × 0.2 mm, glabrous or with some internal hairs in the apical region. Pistil 2 (3)-merous, (2) 2.5–3.5 (4) mm long; ovary covered with dense woolly hairs; style glabrous or woolly at its lower part, terminated by 2 (3) lobes generally ≤ 1 mm, rarely bifid along its entire length.

Fruits 1 (2)-seeded, hairs very short generally dense, velvety to tomentellous, orange at maturity; aborted locules often marked by a slightly swollen rib, beak rarely distinct; 1-seeded fruits ellipsoid-ovoid to reniform, 2–4.5 × 1–2.5 cm; exo- and mesocarp together, ≤ 4 mm thick; endocarp bony to woody, ≤ 1 mm thick, exterior fairly smooth to somewhat wrinkled, interior smooth and glabrous. Seeds ellipsoid, somewhat laterally compressed, ≤ 30 × 15 mm; testa black, thin to fairly thick, brittle or not, ≤ 0.5 mm thick.

Seedling with strong taproot; epicotyl 6–7 cm with subappressed pubescence, bearing a few cataphylls; first pair of leaves alternate and the first leaf distinctly smaller and proportionally wider than the following ones. – Pl. 58, p. 193.

## Type

Zenker & Staudt 243, Yaoundé, Cameroun

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Congo, Zaire, Angola, Kenya, Tanzania

Although very widely distributed, this species is quite constant and is easily distinguished by its reflexed sepals and its 2-merous pistil.

## Ecology

Evergreen and semi-deciduous rainforests, forest galleries, dry lowland forests, montane forests ≤ 1500 m
