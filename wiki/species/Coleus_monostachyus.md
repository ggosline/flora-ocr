# *Coleus monostachyus* (P.Beauv.) A.J.Paton subsp. monostachyus

**Genus**: [[Coleus]]
**Protologue**: Phytokeys 129 : 76 (2019)

## Synonyms

- Solenostemon ocymoides Schumach. & Thonn. in Schumach., Beskr. Guin. pl. : 271 (1827).

## Description

S. monostachyus (P.Beauv.) Briq. in Engl. & Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenfam. 4(3a) : 359 (1897).

Plectranthus monostachyus (P.Beauv.) B.J.Pollard, Kew Bull. 56 : 980 (2001).

Annual herb, 0.20–1 m tall; stem erect or ascending, more or less pubescent, with hairs directed toward the base and appressed. Leaf: petiole 15–30(–40) mm; blade ovate, 3–9.5 × 3–6 cm, attenuate at the base, acute to obtuse at the apex, dentate to crenate, sometimes marked with brown or reddish on the upper surface, puberulent with reddish sessile glands on the lower surface. Inflorescence terminal, unbranched, lax, with verticillasters distant up to 20(–25) mm; bracts small, 5 mm long, eventually reflexed and caducous. Flower: pedicel 2–4 mm; calyx funnel-shaped, 2–3 mm long and 3–5 mm in fruit, pubescent, with brownish sessile glands, upper lip curved upward, ovate, acuminate, lateral lobes narrowly triangular, lower teeth curved upward and closing the throat, fused, obovate, subentire or with 2 apiculate teeth; corolla 4–7 mm long, blue or violet marked with white, lower lip 2–4 mm long; stamens with filaments fused at the base. Fruit: ovoid achenes, 1 mm long, brown, speckled darker.

## Distribution

widespread in West and West-Central Africa, including Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea (Bioko, Annobon), São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, Central African Republic, Republic of the Congo, and Angola (Cabinda); naturalized in parts of tropical Asia; in Gabon: Estuaire, Haut Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Ivindo, and Woleu-Ntem. Conservation status: Widely distributed; probably Least Concern (LC)

## Ecology

disturbed areas on rocky or sandy soil, or former cultivated areas, humid grasslands (also on oil palm trunk), becoming invasive in Southeast Asia; in Gabon at 140–580 m elevation, at 60–1300 m in neighboring countries. Uses: Used against abdominal pain. The leaves are eaten with courgette seeds. In Congo, the juice of leaves is given to children as eye drops to treat convulsions and as a sedative drink. It is also used there against snake bites.

## Vernacular names

ava (fang) ; etsipo-a-kyètu (mitsogo)

## Source note

The second subspecies, subsp. perennis J.K.Morton, is found in Sierra Leone, Côte-d'Ivoire, Ghana, and Nigeria. Decumbent perennial material, previously identified as Coleus (or Plectranthus) monostachyus subsp. perennis from Gabon, actually represents C. calaminthoides with stem hairs directed upward rather than downward and appears to be restricted to coastal areas. B: Morales (2011), Morton (1963), Pollard et al. (2001, 2006), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Vande weghe et al. (2016)
