# *Aucoumea klaineana* Pierre

**Family**: [[Burseraceae]] · **Genus**: [[Aucoumea]]

## Description

Large tree 35–40 m, trunk to 2 m diameter, with variable buttresses and a light pyramidal crown; young leaves bright red. Leaves imparipinnate, 3–6-jugate; rachis to 40 cm with a narrow deep adaxial groove; leaflets ovate-oblong, acuminate, rounded at base, 14–30 × 4–7 cm, coriaceous and glabrous, with 12–16 pairs of looped secondary veins; petiolules 3–4 cm. Panicles 10–20 cm, axillary or terminal, ferruginous-pubescent. Flowers 5-merous; sepals 4–5 mm, petals 5.5–6 mm, both tomentose; disc extrastaminal, of five bilobed glands; stamens (9–)10; ovary 5-locular, two ovules per locule.

Capsule top-shaped, 3.5–5 × 1.8–3 cm, opening from the base into five spoon-shaped valves. Columella club-shaped and pentagonal, bearing five ovoid seeds, each extended by a 2–3 cm wing. Flowers October–November; fruits February.

## Distribution and ecology

Nearly endemic to Gabon, extending into Equatorial Guinea and slightly beyond the Kouilou in the Republic of the Congo. A strongly light-demanding, fast-growing tree regenerating in clearings, roadsides and abandoned cultivation; large stands in apparently primary forest are interpreted as old secondary forest.

## Uses

Okoumé is Gabon’s principal timber, especially for veneer and plywood, as well as joinery and canoes. The aromatic resin is used for torches.

## Specimens examined

Holotype: Klaine 34, Libreville. Other cited Gabon material includes Griffon du Bellay 198; Klaine 100; Fleury 26502, 26510, 26695, 26891; Le Testu 1188; Heitz II; Aubréville 34.

## Vernacular names

*okoumé*; *angouma* (Fang); *nkoumi* (Vili, Yaka).
