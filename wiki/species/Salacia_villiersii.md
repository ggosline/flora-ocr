# *Salacia villiersii* Hallé, sp. nov.

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]

## Description

S. erectae (G. Don) Walp. affinis, sed foliis obscure dentatis, petiolo marginibus leviter undulatis, acumine insignite angustato, supra in longum convoluto, inflorescentiis multifloribus in glomerulis axillaribus sessilibus, sepalis paulum inaequalibus denticulatis, petalis oblongis, reflexis, disco viridi pulvinato, mediocriter crasso et marginato, antheris rimis obtuse conjunctis, ovulis 2 superpositis in quoque loculo, floribus S. cornifolia similibus sed minoribus, differt. It inhabits the Gabonese Crystalline Mountains. Named after a poet.

TYPE (sole material known): Hallé N. & Villiers J.-F. 4279, Gabon, Mbel on Komo, 10.1.1968 (fl.) (holo-, K!).

Glabrous liane, lacking rubber; young internodes with 4 fine ridges. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole 6–9 mm with canal margins almost straight or moderately undulate. Blade when dry brownish, dark and varnished above, elliptic, 5.5–11 × 2.5–5 cm, with base very briefly subacute, with curiously acuminate apex: acumen 3–6 × 1 mm with margins narrowly bordered above; blade margins weakly or indistinctly dentate. Secondary veins 6–10 pairs; fine venation slightly apparent, or more apparent above than below.

Inflorescences in multiflorous sessile axillary glomérules forming spherical clusters nearly 2 cm in diameter at each node. Pedicels 5–6 mm. Ovoid buds of 1.6–1.9 × 1.7–2 mm. Flowers yellow 3.5–4.5 mm in diameter. Sepals ovate, denticulate and somewhat unequal, 0.7–1 mm wide. Petals ovate or oblong with parallel principal veins, approximately 2.5 × 1.5 mm, spreading then reflexed at anthesis. Disc green, circular, pulvinate 1.5–1.7 mm in diameter by 0.4 mm thick, margined at mid-height, not clearly provided with a perigynous furrow and scarcely fossulate beneath the anther locules which are in contact with it only in the very young bud. Stamens 1–1.2 mm, twisted upon themselves, spaced at base, and furnished with an adaxial pit; anthers with connivate locules, 0.5 mm wide, dehiscing by a fissure forming a very open angle. Pistil 0.8–1.3 mm, pyramidal subulate over 0.7 mm, with locules half or less than half-sunken. Ovules two superposed in each locule.

Fruit unknown. – Pl. 34, fig. 1–12, p. 125.

The unique collection was made along a river bank. The aspect of the flowers, much smaller, resembles that of S. cornifolia, but floral analysis brings the species closer to S. erecta.

26 bis. Salacia sulphur Loesener & Winkler

Probable liane, with sulfur-yellow sap (whence the name). Leaves opposite oblong 10–12 × 5 cm, with petiole 10–12 mm (cm in the diagnosis). Blade acute at base, acuminate at apex, with the apex of the acumen slightly retuse; margin subentire, indistinctly crenulate. Secondary veins 9–12 pairs.

Axillary floral fascicles, few-flowered. Flowers mediocre, with pedicel 13–15 mm. Sepals 5 unequal, orbicular. Petals 5 suborbicular approximately 3 mm, with blade narrowed and translucent on each side of the base. Disc disciform slightly thick, weakly pentagonal, and without perigynous furrow. Stamens 3 with filaments broadened at base, revolute at anthesis; anthers small with confluent locules. Ovary sunken in the disc, with 3 ovules per locule; short pyramidal trigonous pistil, with punctiform stigma.

Fruit unknown.

## Discussion

This species remains enigmatic in the absence of any reference material. The proximity to S. togoica Loes. (Bot. Jahrb. 44: 163, 1910) is not significant. The only affinity indicated is that of S. adolfi-fridericii Loes. ex Harms (Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Berlin 15: 672, 1942).

PL. 34. – Salacia villiersii Hallé: 1, bud; 2, 3, flower, diam. 4.5 mm; 4, calyx seen from below; 5, petals 2.5 mm long; 6–9, discs, diam. 0.9–1.7 mm; 10, 11, stamens; 12, locule with two ovules (Hallé N. & Villiers J.-F. 4279). – S. adolfi-friderici Loes.: 13, bud; 14, flower, diam. 11 mm; 15, calyx seen from below; 16, petal 5 mm long; 17, 18, disc, diam. approx. 3 mm; 19–22, stamens; 23, 24, locules with many ovules (13, 15, 19, 22, 23, Breteler 2830; 16, 17, 20, 24, Mildbraed 5418).
