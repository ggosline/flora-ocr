# *Clitoria ternatea* L. Planche 25.4–9

**Genus**: [[Clitoria]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 753 (1753)

## Description

Climbing herb; stems slender, at first pubescent. Stipules lanceolate, 4–10 mm long. Leaf: petiole 7–8 cm, barely pubescent; rachis 1–6 cm; stipels subulate, 1–2 mm long; leaflets 5 to 7, with petiolule 1–3 mm, limb elliptic-oblong, 3–4.5 × 1.5–3 cm, rounded to acute at base and apex, slightly pubescent beneath; secondary veins in 5 pairs. Inflorescence uniflorous; peduncle 0.5–1 cm, glabrescent; bracts lanceolate, 2–3 mm long, striate. Flower: bracteoles at base of calyx, broadly ovate to suborbicular, 5–8 mm long; calyx with tube 8–14 mm long, teeth oblong-lanceolate, 7–12 mm long; corolla deep blue to violet, sometimes marked with white and yellow in the centre, or rarely white, standard 2.5–5.5 cm long; stamens with staminal column 17–19 mm, anthers 1 mm long; ovary linear, ± 8 mm long, densely hairy. Pod linear to falcate, flattened, with calyx long persistent, 6–12 × 0.8–1.2 cm, smooth, apex acute, with (6–)8 to 12 seeds, each lodged in a circular alveole. Seed somewhat reniform, flattened, 4.5–7 × 3–4 mm, dark brown.

## Distribution

probably native to the tropics of the Old World, today cultivated and spontaneous throughout tropical regions; in Gabon, known from Estuaire and Moyen-Ogooué

## Ecology

cultivated in gardens and subspontaneous in bushes, fallows, riparian forest; in Gabon at low altitude, elsewhere up to 1500 m. Uses: An ornamental species that could also serve as fodder or cover plant. The plant and fruits contain minerals and vitamins

## Vernacular names

liane madame, pois bleu, pois des haies, pois sauvage, pois savane, pois tonnelle, (fr.) ; blue pea, butterfly pea, cordofan pea (angl.)

## Source note

Recently recorded in Gabon, very little collected. Bibliography: Botton & Hallé (1958), Fantz (1980), Pellegrin (1949), Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), Verdcourt (1971c), Wilczek (1954)
