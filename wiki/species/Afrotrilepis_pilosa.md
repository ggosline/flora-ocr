## Also treated in vol 44

**Pages**: 13-15 · **Source**: `sources/Cyperaceae_vol44_liteparse`

Robust herb, sometimes 1 m tall, with a horizontal stem at the base, branched and erect above, stem bases surrounded by a compact mass formed from the remains of old leaves mixed with adventitious roots. Leaves arranged in a spiral; sheath closed; blade 10‒45 cm × 4‒8 mm, glabrous or hairy below, persistent for a long time. Inflorescence a lax and well-developed panicle, occupying approximately half the length of the stems; with spikelets arranged in glomérules at the ends of the branches, each having a bract at its base. Spikelet usually with 2 flowers, either with two male flowers or with one male and one female flower. Male flower reduced to 2 or 3 stamens. Female flower more complex, the pistil being placed in a ring of hairs fused at the base, and falling with the achene at maturity. Achene 2.5‒3 mm long, with an elongated beak and usually very scabrous; large empty space inside the seed.

### Distribution

Mali, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroun and Gabon; widespread in Gabon in the Woleu-Ntem, but also found in the Nyanga and Ogooué-Ivindo (14 collections)

### Ecology

Inselbergs and rocky outcrops in forest; in Gabon, at 450‒750 m altitude

### Vernacular names

Devil grass (Engl.)

### Uses

In Gabon and Sierra Leone, the plant is used to make thatched roofs. B: Brink (2011a), Haines & Lye (1983), Napper (1971), Raynal (1963). Bibliography
