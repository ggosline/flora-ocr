# *Melothria sphaerocarpa* (Cogn.) H.Schaef. & S.S.Renner

**Genus**: [[Melothria]]
**Family**: [[Cucurbitaceae]]
**Protologue**: Taxon 60: 134 (2011)

## Description

Plate 25; Figure 3(D)

Cucumeropsis edulis (Hook.f.) Cogn. in A.DC. & C.DC., Monogr. phan. 3: 518 (1881).

Monoecious herb

- monoecious; stem branched. Tendrils slender, simple, slender, rather short, puberulent. Leaf: petiole 5–12 cm, pubescent-hirsute, glabrescent; blade with broadly ovate, pentagonal outline, with 3 to 5 angles or more or less deeply 3- to 5-lobed, (6–9)–18–(21) × 7–15–(21) cm, deeply cordate at base, membranous to rigid, slightly scabrous on both surfaces, bright green above, lighter green below, with finely wavy-denticulate margins, with lobes (if present) triangular, separated by ± deep sinuses, the basal one deep 2–4 cm. Inflorescence: the male with 10 to 15 flowers, at the tip of a common peduncle 3–4 cm; the female with solitary flower axillary to the male inflorescence. Male flower: pedicel slender, without bract, rather short, puberulent; receptacle campanulate, rounded at base, slightly narrowed at throat, 7–8 × 3–4 mm, finely pubescent on both surfaces; sepals spreading then reflexed, 3 mm long, acute at apex; petals broadly ovate, 3–6 mm long and wide, yellow, with 3 nerves, pubescent on both surfaces; stamens 3, subsessile, inserted at throat of receptacle, this bearing dense hairs, 2 bithecous and 1 monothecous, anther extrorse, 2 mm long, with lobes curved at apex, bordered by hairs, with broad connective; base of receptacle thickened into a glandular pistillode. Female flower: staminodes absent; ovary ovoid to ellipsoid, covered with scattered hairs, containing numerous horizontal ovules, with 3 placentae; style columnar, 2 mm long, terminated by 3 bilobed stigmas. Fruit ellipsoid, ovoid or club-shaped, up to 30 × 10 cm, smooth, fleshy. Seed ovate, 16–18 × 9 × 2 mm, attenuate at apex, white, with thin margins, but without thickened margin.

## Distribution

of American origin, cultivated in West and central Africa, from Guinea-Bissau to southern Sudan, Uganda and Angola; in Gabon: Estuaire, Ngounié, Nyanga and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

cultivated and sometimes escaped in forest, savanna, fallow land; in Gabon up to 670 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1000 m

Uses: The fruit flesh is edible raw. The seeds are used, raw or cooked (rich in oil) or also roasted, as a snack. The seed oil is used in cooking. The young tender leaves are cooked and consumed as a vegetable. The fruit juice mixed with other ingredients is applied to the navel of a newborn. The leaves are used in maceration to purge a breastfed baby either by direct administration to the baby, or by placing them on the mother's breasts before feeding.

## Vernacular names

égousi, egousi-itoo, gousi (fr.); dark egusi, egusi-itoo, white seed melon (angl.)

## Source note

Although there exist two older basionyms than that used by Schaefer & Renner (2011b) to form the specific epithet, the combination M. sphaerocarpa is correct due to the existence of M. mannii Cogn. and M. edulis Raf. in the genus Melothria
