# *Cyperus tenuiculmis* Boeckeler Planche 48, Figure 4a

**Genus**: [[Cyperus]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Linnaea 36 : 286 (1870)

## Description

C. zollingeri non Steud., sensu C.B.Clarke in Fl. trop. Afr. Perennial herb, medium-sized, with a rather thick creeping rhizome and swollen stem base; stems 30–85 cm × 0.7–3 mm, trigonous, almost glabrous. Leaf: sheath green or brown; main blades 10–40 cm × 2–4 mm, flat, scabrous on the margin and principal nerves. Inflorescence an anthela formed of one sessile spike and 2–9 spikes on rays 4–25 cm long, almost glabrous (rarely with a secondary spike at the base of a primary spike); involucral bracts 3–4, foliaceous, erect or spreading, the main one 8–30 cm × 1.5–4 mm; spikes 1.5–3.5 × 1.4–4 cm, with 3–13 spikelets erect or spreading. Spikelet linear–lanceolate, 8–30(–40 with the naked rachis) × 1.3–2 mm (4 mm with the glumes spreading), golden or yellowish-brown, rarely reddish-brown, with 6–20 flowers; glumes ovate, 2.8–4 mm long (highly variable on the same plant), yellowish-brown with or without a slight reddish-brown tint or with darker dots, midvein green, scarcely exceeding or not exceeding the glume. Flower: style with 3 branches. Achene obovate, 1.4–1.8 × 1–1.2 mm, trigonous, blackish, with finely papillose surface.

## Distribution

widely distributed in the tropical regions of Africa and Asia; common in Gabon, found in Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem (22 collections)

## Ecology

in seasonally humid herbaceous grasslands, usually on sandy soil; in Gabon, at 0–700 m altitude, up to 1800 m in East Africa. Taxonomic note: recognized by its thick creeping rhizome and inflorescence with linear–lanceolate spikelets, often golden. Three varieties are often recognized, but they differ only in minor characters. C. tenuiculmis var. schweinfurthianus (Boeckeler) Hooper differs in its robust scabrous stems and broader leaves. It has been found in Nyanga. B: Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972). ibliography
