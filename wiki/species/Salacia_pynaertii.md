# *Salacia pynaertii* De Wildeman

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Musée Congo Belge, Bot., sér. 5, 2 : 295 (1908) ; Robson, FZ 2 (2) : 402 (1966)

## Synonyms

- Salacia elegans WELW. ex OLIV. var. pynaertii (DE WILD.) WILCZEK. FCB 9: 196 (1960).

## Description

– S. mildbraediana LOES., Bot. Jahrb. 44: 170 (1910); Deutsch. Zentr.-Afr. Exped. 1907-1908, 2: 470, tab. 62, fig. A (1912). TYPE: Mildbraed 2139, Zaire (probably lost).

Glabrous liane, 2–20 m high, containing rubber threads. Young branches smooth, brownish when dry; older branches becoming light russet or beige ochraceous, with bark ± fissured lengthwise. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole (4)5–8 mm, with canal margins straight. Lamina subcoriaceous, brown or brownish and ± discolorous when dry, glossy above, elliptic, 5.5–14(17) × 2.5–7 cm, with acute to very briefly cuneiform base, acuminate apex, finely dentate to entire margin. Secondary veins 6–9(10) pairs, moderately ascending.

Inflorescences axillary, sessile or with small peduncle up to 2(4) mm, 4–25 flowers. Bud approximately 1.5 mm, subglobose or briefly ovoid. Pedicel 2.5–5 mm. Flower 2.5–4(5) mm diameter, with corolla often half or somewhat erect, yellow or yellow-green, more rarely whitish. Sepals unequal, obtuse or acute, 0.7–1.7 mm wide, entire or subentire. Petals ovate or elliptic, 1.5–2 × 1–1.5 mm. Disk small and low, 1.3–1.4 × 0.2 mm, in ring 6-fossulate at anther contact areas in bud. Stamens 0.5–1.5 mm, spaced and inserted in small perigynous groove; anthers with longitudinal thecae acute at apex, 0.4–0.6 mm wide. Pistil triquetrous, attenuate and trifurcate at apex. Locules not sunken and containing 2 superposed ovules. One of the sexes is sometimes reduced or ± aborted, the other appearing dominant.

Fruits globose (rarely ovate), smooth, sometimes ± irregularly tuberculate, orange, orange-pink or red, 1–2.7 cm diameter and containing 1–3 seeds. At juvenile stage the 3 stigmatic lobes, nearly 1 mm, persist briefly. – Pl. 43, fig. 1–5, 7, 8, 10, 12, p. 151.

## Discussion

Note. – If the synonymy of S. mildbraediana proposed above without certainty can be confirmed, the name could be usefully retained in order to group at varietal rank numerous materials with ± sessile inflorescences; the type of DE WILDEMAN would characterize the typical variety with longer peduncles. Other varietal forms with dark brown leaves when dry, elongate and sometimes lanceolate, with still imprecise boundaries might perhaps be described. S. gerrardii Harv., from South Africa is related; it is distinguished by coarser leaf margin teeth and fewer flowers and somewhat larger ones.
