# *Salacia pynaertii* De Wildeman

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: Ann. Musée Congo Belge, Bot., sér. 5, 2 : 295 (1908) ; Robson, FZ 2 (2) : 402 (1966)

## Description

- Salacia elegans WELW. ex OLIV. var. pynaertii (DE WILD.) WILCZEK. FCB 9: 196 (1960).

– S. mildbraediana LOES., Bot. Jahrb. 44: 170 (1910); Deutsch. Zentr.-Afr. Exped. 1907-1908, 2: 470, tab. 62, fig. A (1912). TYPE: Mildbraed 2139, Zaire (probably lost).

Glabrous liane, 2–20 m tall, containing rubber threads. Young branches smooth, brownish when dry; older branches becoming pale russet or ochraceous beige, with bark ± longitudinally fissured. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole (4)5–8 mm, with straight canal margins. Lamina subcoriaceous, brown or brownish and ± discolorous when dry, glossy above, elliptic, 5.5–14(17) × 2.5–7 cm, base acute to very briefly cuneate, apex acuminate, margin finely dentate to entire. Secondary venation 6–9(10) pairs, moderately ascending.

Inflorescences axillary, sessile or with a small peduncle reaching up to 2(4) mm, 4–25 flowers. Bud approximately 1.5 mm, subglobose or briefly ovoid. Pedicel 2.5–5 mm. Flower 2.5–4(5) mm in diameter, corolla often half or somewhat erect, yellow or yellow-green, more rarely whitish. Sepals unequal, obtuse or acute, 0.7–1.7 mm wide, entire or subentire. Petals ovate or elliptic, $ 1.5–2 \times 1–1.5 $ mm. Disc small and low, $ 1.3–1.4 \times 0.2 $ mm, a 6-foveolate ring at the contact areas of the theca in bud. Stamens 0.5–1.5 mm, spaced and inserted in a small perigynous groove; anthers with acute thecae at the apex, 0.4–0.6 mm wide. Pistil triquetrous, attenuate and trifurcate at the apex. Locules not sunken and containing 2 superposed ovules. One of the sexes is sometimes reduced or ± aborted, the other appearing dominant.

Fruits globose (rarely ovate), smooth, sometimes ± irregularly tuberculate, orange, orange-pink or red, 1–2.7 cm in diameter, containing 1–3 seeds. At the juvenile stage the 3 stigmatic lobes, nearly 1 mm, persist for a while. - Pl. 43, fig. 1–5, 7, 8, 10, 12, p. 151.

## Discussion

Note. – If the synonymy of S. mildbraediana, proposed above without certainty, can be confirmed, this name could usefully be retained in order to group at the varietal rank numerous materials with ± sessile inflorescences; the type of DE WILDEMAN would characterize the typical variety with longer peduncles. Other varietal forms with dark brown leaves when dry, elongate and sometimes lanceolate, with still imprecise limits might perhaps be described. S. gerrardii Harv., from South Africa, is related; it is distinguished by coarser leaf margin teeth and fewer, slightly larger flowers.

Nigeria (Talbot s.n., Oban), Cameroon, Gabon, Central African Republic (Tisserant 2334, Le Testu 4449), Zaire, Angola, Zambia, Mozambique and Usambara (Scheffler 46). This species is absent from the most humid forests or low-lying coasts; collections range between 500 and 1,500 m elevation.
