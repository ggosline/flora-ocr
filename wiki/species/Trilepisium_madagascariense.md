Trees usually reaching 20–25 m, sometimes 40 m in height, generally terrestrial, sometimes epiphytic and strangler figs; latex white, becoming red or brownish; small leafy branchlets, thick (0.5–)1–4 mm, glabrous. Blade elliptic to oblong to lanceolate, generally wider in the upper half, symmetric or slightly asymmetric, 4–16 × 1–6.5 cm, coriaceous or subcoriaceous, generally shortly or long acuminate, sometimes subcaudate or subacute; base acute to obtuse, often subauriculed; margin entire, glabrous on both sides; upper surface often with pluricellular oblong-capitate hairs, white, small; veins slightly prominent to plane above, midvein sometimes slightly impressed; midvein prominent below, the others less prominent to plane; 4–10 (–12) pairs of secondary veins, the lower pair of secondary veins generally distinct and forming with the midvein angles more acute than the other secondary veins not distinctly grouped in pairs; no parallel tertiary veins. Petiole 3–15 mm long. Stipules caducous, 2–12 mm long, coriaceous, glabrous or sometimes with small scattered uncinate and/or ciliolate hairs.

Inflorescences discoid to turbinate, 6–8 (–10) mm in diameter. Peduncle 3–) 5–12 mm long, glabrous. Receptacle glabrous; involucre 6–8-lobed to 6–8-fid, ciliolate. Stamens numerous, often 2 or more connate at the base, with filaments 3–6 mm long and anthers c. (1–) 1.5–2 × 0.4–0.6 mm, often apiculate. Female flowers sometimes 2, with perianth c. 2 mm high, 4-lobed or irregularly incised to fimbriate, ciliolate; style 2–4 mm long; stigmas 7–9 mm long, straight or twisted.

Infructescences oblong to globose, often slightly oblique or curved, c. 2–3 cm long and c. 1.2–1.7 cm in diameter; receptacle fleshy, becoming yellow, brownish or reddish, generally purplish or blue-black at full maturity; peduncle reaching 25 (–30) mm in length; seed ellipsoid to subglobose, c. 10–16 × 8–14 mm. - Pl. 31, p. 107.

## Discussion

T. madagascariense unites the 13 species described in the genus Bosqueia. Despite considerable variation, no infraspecific taxon can be recognized. In Cameroun and Gabon the material is fairly uniform. It is related to the West and Central African representatives of this species.

T. madagascariense ranges from Guinée to Madagascar and the Seychelles. On the continent individuals are found in evergreen and semi-deciduous forests, primary and secondary, often along watercourses, sometimes as solitary trees in cultivated areas.

The West African representatives of the species are connected to those of central Zaire by the semi-deciduous forest belt of Cameroun and Central Africa. The populations of this species in Gabon are connected to those of central Zaire through the Lower Congo region.
