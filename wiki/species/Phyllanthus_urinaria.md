# *Phyllanthus urinaria* L.

**Genus**: [[Phyllanthus]]
**Family**: [[Phyllanthaceae]]
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 982 (1753)

## Description

Herb ± annual, monoecious, up to 50 cm tall. Stipules triangular, 1–1.5 mm long. Leaf: petiole 0.4–0.6 mm; blade ± oblong, 7–22 × 2.5–5.5 mm, rounded at base, ± acute at apex, with thick nerve-edged margin and 2–3 rows of small rigid hairs, with 4–6 pairs of lateral nerves. Male flower ± sessile; sepals 6, 0.4–0.6 mm long; disk with 6 elements alternisepalous; stamens 3, ± 0.3 mm long, with connate filaments. Female flower: pedicel 0.4–0.6 mm; sepals 6, 0.25–0.35 long; disk entire with finely crenulate margin; ovary inflated to smooth; styles 3, very short, bilobed. Fruit capsular, reaching 2.3 mm in diameter. Seeds claw-shaped spherical, brownish, dorsally ornamented with 10–12 transverse ridges.

## Distribution

species with circumtropical distribution; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

secondary vegetation areas, fallows; in Gabon, at 0–650 m altitude. Uses: Throughout the world, P. urinaria is used for its diuretic and purgative properties in the treatment of all manner of urogenital ailments, diarrhea and diabetes, as well as a bitter tonic and febrifuge, including against malaria. Several other medicinal applications are known (see Mosango 2008)

## Vernacular names

curanellie rouge, kikilé, petit tamarin rouge, urinaire de Malabar (fr.) ; chamber bitter, common leaf-flower, shatterstone (angl.). b : Brunel (1987), Keay (1958), Mosango (2008). ibliographie
