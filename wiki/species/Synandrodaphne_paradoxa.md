# *Synandrodaphne paradoxa* Gilg

**Genus**: [[Synandrodaphne]]
**Family**: [[Thymeleaceae]]
**Protologue**: Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. 53 : 362 (1915); Pflanzw. Afr. 3 (2) : 628 (1921)

## Synonyms

- Gilgiodaphne paradoxa (Gilg) Domke, Bibl. Bot.: 120 (1934). — Staner, Ann. Soc. Sc. Brux. B. 56: 242 (1936).

## Description

Pflanzw. Afr. 3 (2) : 628 (1921). — Lanjouw et coll., Regn. Veget. 23 : 289 (1961).

Subshrub 40–80 cm tall, scarcely bushy, with erect branches (and ascending?), sparsely leafy or denuded at the base. Bark grey or slightly reddish, reticulate, very pubescent or even hirsute when young, glabrescent or glabrous later, containing long solid fibres, silvered at the fracture; wood fairly hard, dull.

Leaves alternate, distichous, petiolate, without stipules. Petiole 3–5 mm, nearly cylindrical, not or scarcely canaliculate, densely covered with stiff, grey hairs. Blade very thin, membranaceous, oblong or ovate-oblong, symmetric, with greatest width towards the middle, regularly attenuate-cuneate at the base, entire throughout, glabrous or glabrescent, slightly discolorous, 5–10 cm long, 2–4 cm wide, apiculate at the apex; apex triangular, acute. Veins clearly visible on both surfaces, often slightly prominent on the upper surface, grey-silvered on the lower surface (numerous very short, stiff, appressed hairs). Lateral veins 5 to 10 on each side, at first straight at approximately 45° to the main vein, then regularly inflexed towards the apex at the leaf margin to which they become subparallel, the upper ones generally very finely anastomosing; tertiary network very fine, forming an irregular reticulum visible by transparency. Leaf margins most often plane.

Inflorescences terminal on the leafy branches, situated in the axil of the upper leaves and appearing quite sessile, or borne on more or less long peduncles, secondarily defoliate, consisting of 1–2 pseudo-capitules closely contiguous or separated from one another; peduncles densely pubescent-hirsute or even somewhat silky-villous at the apex. 8–15 to 25–30 flowers very densely involucrate by 5–15 foliaceous or somewhat scarious bracts, slightly carinate, ovate-rounded, ovate-lanceolate or lanceolate-apiculate, 5–10 mm long, 3–8 mm wide, covered with very dense silky-silvered pubescence. 4 sepals almost entirely free, the two outer ones (opposite to the axillary bracteole) broadly ovate, somewhat spoon-shaped, 4–5 mm long, 2–3 mm wide, covered with long silky hairs on the outside, subglabrous on the inside; the two inner ones somewhat smaller and narrower (very rarely 4 sepals + 1 much smaller scale-like sepal).

Androecium characteristic, with 4 (sometimes 5) stamens with filaments fused to the external wall of an androecial tube occupying, around the ovary, the centre of the flower. This tube, cylindrical, terminates apically in 4 (sometimes 5) lobes, themselves broadly rectangular, rounded at the apex and mucronate or apiculate, very densely silky-villous. In the young floral bud, the ovoid-elongate anthers are lodged in the sinuses of the androecial tube; during development the filaments lengthen to reach 1.5 times the length of the tube, and the anthers are thus widely exserted. Mature flower with tube approximately 2–4 mm long; filaments (alternating with the lobes) 5–10 mm; anthers introrse, dorsifixed, 1–1.5 mm long. Filaments and anthers glabrous.

Ovary globose-spheric, sessile and broadly adnate to the receptacle, scarcely or not adnate to the androecial tube, covered with long silky hairs. Style filiform but fairly stout, exserted even in the very young flower; stigma globose-flattened, papillose, exserted but less than the stamens in the mature flower. Ovary with two locules.

Fruit also very characteristic, very small (less than 5 mm), a bivalvate capsule, dehiscent by two slits situated in the diameter opposite to that of the ovarian septum; valves curved or slightly carinate, bearing internally the trace of the half-septum, often with thickened margins and strongly ciliate. Most often a single developed seed, very fusiform, ovoid at its base, with brown or blackish testa, densely covered with stiff, short, yellowish or golden hairs, presenting a more glabrous depressed zone where the fibrous structure of the testa often appears. Embryo included in two thick, acuminate cotyledons; albumen absent or scarcely abundant.

Type described from Cameroon (Mildbraed : 4591 and 4965).

The species is currently represented by isolated collections from Cameroon to Ubangi; it is the genus of Thymelaeaceae in intertropical Africa with the most restricted distribution. This plant was collected very close to the northern and eastern borders of Gabon.
