# *Cassipourea barteri* (Hook.f. ex Oliv.) Engl. & Brehmer Planche 29.1, 30.1

**Genus**: [[Cassipourea]]
**Family**: [[Rhizophoraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1894 : 5 (1894)

## Synonyms

- Dactylopetalum barteri Hook.f. ex Oliv., Fl. trop. Afr. 2 : 412 (1871).

## Description

Large shrub or small tree 3–6 m tall, glabrous; trunk to 15 cm diameter; bark smooth, greyish, with clear exudation smelling of mushroom; branches strongly swollen at the nodes, dark brownish with paler lenticels. Stipules early caducous. Leaves decussate; petiole 6–12 mm; lamina elliptic, 11–18 × 5–10 cm, 1.5–2 times longer than wide, rounded at base, acute and ± apiculate at apex, thick, glabrous, greenish above, brownish below, main vein red below, lateral veins 8–10 pairs. Inflorescence axillary, fasciculate, dense, with dozens of small flowers almost entirely masking the node, on a warty boss exuding a pale yellow gum. Flower small, 5-merous; pedicel 1 mm and stipe to 2 mm; calyx campanulate-elongate to tubular, 3–4.5 × 1–1.5 mm, greenish, with 5 short, erect, triangular lobes, 1.5 mm long; petals spatulate, 9–10 × 1–1.5 mm, smooth, white to cream-white, fringed-laciniate from 4 mm from the base into 10–12 filiform languettes; stamens 10, white, glabrous, slightly connate at base into a crown bearing 5 rectangular teeth 1 × 0.5 mm, epipetalous filaments 7.5 mm, alternipetalous 6 mm, anthers narrowly ovoid, 1 × 0.5 mm; ovary subglobose, 1 mm diameter, tomentose; style ± 6 mm, slender; stigma rounded. Fruit globose, 6–7 × 3–5 mm, puberulent, with stipe 2–3 mm. Seeds 2, ovoid, 3 × 1.5–2 mm, red-brown, topped by an orange aril covering almost entirely one face.

## Distribution

from Sierra Leone to Cameroon, southward to Angola; in Gabon, very common in the coastal plain: provinces of Estuaire, Ogooué-Maritime and Nyanga. Schurmans, reproduced from Liben (1987), Meise Botanical Garden ©

## Ecology

abundant in coastal thickets on sandy soils, savannas, emergent parts of mangroves and swampy forests; in Gabon and elsewhere, at ± 1–50 m altitude. Flowering from February (buds in January) to July; fruits from March to September

## Vernacular names

dinkandou (bapouno). b : Breteler (2006, 2008), Keay (1954), Liben (1987). ibliographie
