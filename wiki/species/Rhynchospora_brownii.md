# *Rhynchospora brownii* Roem. & Schult. Planche 118, Figure 21b

**Genus**: [[Rhynchospora]]
**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Protologue**: Syst. veg. 2 : 86 (1817)

## Description

, nom . illeg ., non Vahl (1805). R. rugosa sensu Hooper, Fl. W. trop. Afr. ed. 2, 3 : 333 (1972), non (Vahl) Gale (1944). R. glauca sensu C.B.Clarke in Dyer, Fl. trop. Afr. 8 : 482 (1902), non Vahl (1805), nom . illeg . superfl . Perennial herb, in tufts, with short rhizome ; stem 40–100 cm × 0.4–1.8 mm, triangular, grooved, scabrous on some ridges, base covered with shiny brown scales and remains of old leaves. Leaf : sheath entire or tearing late ; blade 5–40 cm × 1–3 mm, rigid, plane or folded, with scabrous margin, apex triangular in section. Inflorescence a slender panicle, with the main branches 2–10 cm long, exceeding the upper leaf sheaths and ending in small corymbiform clusters, usually with 5–15 spikelets. Spikelet ovoid, 4–5 × 1.2–2 mm, acute, brown ; glumes brown with usually the central nerve prominent, the 3–5 lower ones sterile, 1.5–2.8 mm long, above which a few fertile glumes 3–4 mm long ; bisexual flowers 1–3. Flower : perianth with 6 bristles of unequal length, shorter than the achene, often with a ring of short hairs at the base ; stamens 2–3 ; style divided almost to the base into two branches. Achene urn-shaped, 1.5–2 (cap and pedicel excluded) × 1–1.2 mm, greyish to pale brown, with faint transverse undulations, topped with a conical cap about 0.8 mm long, darker than the achene, set within a whitish ± distinct rim ; "pedicel" persistent at the base of the achene, 0.2–0.3 mm long.

## Distribution

scattered in tropical Africa (Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Zambia, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Angola), more common in South Africa and Madagascar ; also present in Asia and Australia ; rare or little known in Gabon, found only in Moyen-Ogooué, Ogooué-Ivindo and Ogooué-Maritime (3 collections)

## Ecology

swamps and riverbanks ; in Gabon, at 0–100 m altitude, up to 1800 m in East Africa. b : Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Bibliography Raynal (1971), Robinson (1961a)
