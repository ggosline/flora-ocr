# *Dracaena aubryana* Brongn. ex E.Morren Planche 4, Figure 1(D, E)

**Genus**: [[Dracaena]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Belgique Hort. 10 : 348 (1860)

## Description

Pleomele thalioides (Makoy ex Regel) N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1914 : 279 (1914). Monocaulous shrub, up to 2.50 m tall; trunk generally simple; woody rhizome with orange roots. Leaves equitant, leaving the stem visible only in the basal 20 cm in older specimens; sheath embracing the stem over clearly more than the circumference; pseudapetiole longer than the blade, channeled above, marbled with grey; blade oblong-ovate, up to 150 × 13 cm, base rounded to truncate-attenuate, often asymmetric, apex gradually narrowed into a mucro of 0.5–1 cm, coriaceous, distinctly channeled/grooved longitudinally, solid green, paler beneath. Inflorescence erect, paniculate, up to 80 cm long, with axes green to purplish, some branches more than 5 cm long; bracts below the branches caducous, narrowly ovate, up to 3 × 0.7 cm; fascicles with 3 to 5 flowers, subtended by triangular bracts, 1–1.5 mm long, scarious. Flower: pedicel 0–2 mm; perianth 23–38 mm long, white spotted with red-green near the apex of the lobes, lobes longer than the tube, reflexed; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 2 mm shorter than the lobes, filament white, swollen, apex subulate, anther 2–2.5 mm long, pale yellow; ovary ellipsoid-oblong, up to 3 × 2 mm; style exsert for up to 5 mm, white, stigma ± 1 mm in diameter. Fruit entire or deeply 2- or 3-lobed, ± 18 × 9 mm, smooth and shiny, bright orange, lobes ovoid, divergent, subsessile. Seed ovoid, ± 12 × 6 mm.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola; in Gabon in the Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

understory, often deeply shaded; in Gabon as elsewhere at 0–450 m altitude. Flowering recorded from June to December

## Source note

D. tholloniana and D. kindtiana are close to D. aubryana. They are treated as synonyms according to Bos (1984). Here, we treat them as distinct species, using the characters mentioned in the key.
