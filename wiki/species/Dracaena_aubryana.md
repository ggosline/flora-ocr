# *Dracaena aubryana* Brongn. ex E.Morren Planche 4, Figure 1(D, E)

**Genus**: [[Dracaena]]
**Family**: [[Dracaenaceae]]
**Protologue**: Belgique Hort. 10 : 348 (1860)

## Synonyms

- Pleomele thalioides (Makoy ex Regel) N.E.Br., Bull. Misc. Inform. Kew 1914 : 279 (1914).

## Description

Monocaulous shrub, up to 2.50 m tall; trunk generally simple; rhizome woody, with orange roots. Leaves equitant, leaving the stem visible only in the basal 20 cm in older specimens; sheath embracing the stem over distinctly more than the circumference; pseudpetiole longer than the blade, channelled above, marbled with grey; blade oblong-ovate, up to 150 × 13 cm, base rounded to truncate-attenuate, often asymmetric, apex gradually narrowed into a mucro 0.5–1 cm, coriaceous, distinctly channelled/grooved longitudinally, uniform green, paler below. Inflorescence erect, paniculate, up to 80 cm long, with green to violaceous axes, some branches more than 5 cm long; bracts under the branches caducous, narrowly ovate, up to 3 × 0.7 cm; fascicles with 3 to 5 flowers, subtended by triangular bracts, 1–1.5 mm long, scarious. Flower: pedicel 0–2 mm; perianth 23–38 mm long, white spotted with red-green near the apex of the lobes, lobes longer than the tube, reflexed; stamens inserted near the throat, up to 2 mm shorter than the lobes, filament white, swollen, apex subulate, anther 2–2.5 mm long, pale yellow; ovary ellipsoid-oblong, up to 3 × 2 mm; style exsert up to 5 mm, white, stigma ± 1 mm in diameter. Fruit entire or deeply 2- or 3-lobed, ± 18 × 9 mm, smooth and shiny, bright orange, lobes ovoid, divergent, subsessile. Seed ovoid, ± 12 × 6 mm.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola; in Gabon in l'Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Nyanga and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

understory, often deeply shaded; in Gabon as elsewhere at 0–450 m altitude. Flowering recorded from June to December

## Source note

D. tholloniana and D. kindtiana are close to D. aubryana. They are treated as synonyms according to Bos (1984). Here, we treat them as distinct species, using the characters mentioned in the key.
