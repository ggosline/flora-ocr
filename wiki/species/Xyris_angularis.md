# *Xyris angularis* N.E.Br.

**Genus**: [[Xyris]]
**Family**: [[Xyridaceae]]
**Protologue**: X. vanderystii Malme, Ark. Bot. 22A, 4 : 3 (1928)

## Description

in A.W.Hill, Fl. trop. Afr. 8 : 22 (1901). X. vanderystii Malme, Ark. Bot. 22A, 4 : 3 (1928). X. decipiens N.E.Br. var. vanderystii (Malme) Malme, Ark. Bot. 24A, 5 : 2 (1931). X. decipiens sensu Hepper (1967) & Lewis (1981) p.p., non N.E.Br. Perennial herb in spreading tufts. Leaf: sheath slightly furrowed, brown, glabrous, with narrow scarious margins; ligule acute, approximately 1 mm in length; blade linear, 8–15(–40) cm × 3–4 mm, glabrous, smooth or somewhat rugose on one surface, apex acute, straight or slightly incurved, serrulate, sometimes with a few hairs. Inflorescence: peduncle 20–50(–85) cm × 1–1.5 mm, round in section, with 4–6 longitudinal striae, glabrous; spike subspheric to broadly ellipsoid, reaching 8 × 8 mm at maturity, reddish-brown to brown; sterile bracts 4, broadly ovate, approximately 5 × 4 mm, coriaceous, reddish-brown to brown with a very distinct grey dorsal mark, the lower ones smaller, margin laciniately-ciliate, sometimes with an apical tuft of arachnoid hairs but glabrescent; fertile bracts similar. Flower: lateral sepals arched, 4.5 × 1 mm, keel ciliate-dentate, sometimes with an apical tuft of hairs; corolla yellow, tube approximately 3 mm in length, lobes oblong, approximately 3.5 × 1.5 mm, dentate at apex; stamens approximately 1 mm in length, anther 0.5 mm in length; staminodes consisting of a small tuft of hairs; ovary oblong, approximately 2 × 0.5 mm; style 4 mm, 3-branched in the upper third, stigmas multiply lobed. Fruit obovoid to oblong, 3–6 mm in length, apiculate, smooth. Seeds narrowly ellipsoid, approximately 1 × 0.25 mm, with several irregular longitudinal striae or reticulations.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Gabon, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Angola, Uganda, Tanzania, Burundi and Zambia; in Gabon known only from Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

herbaceous swamps, seasonally flooded grasslands, clearings in equatorial forests (ésobé), often on sandy soils; in Gabon at approximately 20 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1300 m. B: Lewis (1981), Lisowski et al. (2001, as X. vanderystii), Lock (1999, bibliography 2010)
