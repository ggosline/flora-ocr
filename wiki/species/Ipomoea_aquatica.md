# *Ipomoea aquatica* Forsk. Planche 11

**Genus**: [[Ipomoea]]
**Family**: [[Convolvulaceae]]
**Protologue**: Fl. aegypt.-arab. : 44 (1775)

## Synonyms

- Ipomoea reptans Poir. in Lam., Encycl. Suppl. 3 : 460 (1814).

## Description

Perennial or annual herb; stems thick, fistulous or spongy, prostrate or floating, rooting at the glabrous or hairy nodes. Leaf: petiole (1–)3–8 cm, glabrous; blade of variable form, ovate to triangular or sometimes lanceolate or linear, (1–)2–15 × 1–6 cm, glabrous; basal nerves, ± 3 pairs, lateral nerves, 6–8 pairs. Inflorescence glabrous, in cymes of (1–)2–5 flowers; peduncle 3–5 cm; bracts thin, ± ovate, approximately 2 mm in length. Flower: pedicel 1–3 cm, glabrous; sepals ± equal, ovate, 6–8 mm in length, with thin hyaline margin; corolla funnelform, 4–5 cm in length, white, glabrous; stamens included; pistil glabrous; ovary 2-locular, 4-ovulate; style approximately 10–12 mm. Fruit globose, 8–10 mm in diameter, glabrous. Seeds pubescent.

## Distribution

Tropical species of Africa, America and Asia; in Gabon, known only from Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

Lake and river margins, marshy places; in Gabon, at low altitude, elsewhere, up to 1500 m

## Vernacular names

kangkong, liseron d’eau, patate aquatique (fr) ; kangkong, kangkung, swamp spinach, swamp morning glory, water convolvulus, water spinach (angl.)

## Uses

Young shoots and leaves of kangkong are harvested as a green vegetable, especially in Southeast Asia and China where it is very popular and frequently cultivated. B: Lejoly & Lisowski (1992), Grubben (2004). Bibliography
