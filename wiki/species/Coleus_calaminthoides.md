# *Coleus calaminthoides* (Baker) A.J.Paton Planche 11

**Genus**: [[Coleus]]
**Protologue**: Phytokeys 129 : 30 (2019)

## Description

S. monostachyus (P.Beauv.) Briq. subsp. perennis J.K.Morton, J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 58 : 272 (1962), pro parte. Perennial herb, trailing, slightly succulent, reaching 1.5 m in height; flowering stem ascending, ± pubescent with appressed ascending hairs, rarely with additional spreading hairs. Leaf: petiole 5–30 mm; blade narrowly to broadly ovate, 1.5–5(–6) × 1.5–4 cm, attenuate at base, obtuse to rounded or rarely acute at apex, subentire to slightly dentate at margin, glabrous to puberulent with reddish sessile glands on lower surface. Inflorescence loose, with verticillasters spaced up to 30 mm apart; cymes sessile, with axis slightly accrescent in fruit, with 6 to 14 flowers; bracts small, cordate, 3.5–5 mm long, acuminate. Flower: pedicel 3–4 mm; calyx funnel-shaped, 2–3 mm long, 4–6 mm in fruit, pubescent, with brownish sessile glands, upper lip ovate, acuminate, lateral lobes narrowly triangular, lower teeth fused, obovate, subentire or emarginate, curved upward and closing the throat; corolla 8–)10–15 mm long, dark blue, purple or violet, lower lip 5–8 mm long; stamens fused at base. Fruit: achenes 1 mm long, brown, with darker spots.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Republic of the Congo; in Gabon: Estuaire, Nyanga and Ogooué-Maritime

## Ecology

open zones in coastal forest, river banks, disturbed soils, on sand or sandy soils; in Gabon and elsewhere at 0–100 m altitude. Conservation status: A preference for open disturbed habitat may reduce risks from human activities in coastal regions and moreover the species has a wide distribution. However, a comprehensive assessment of this species hitherto neglected is desirable

## Source note

Differs from C. monostachyus by the trailing habit, stem with ascending hairs and larger flowers of darker colour. Limited to low altitudes and coastal zones. C. calaminthoides has not been mentioned in recent taxonomic works, being previously identified as C. monostachyus subsp. perennis J.K.Morton. This subspecies occurs in Sierra Leone, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana and Nigeria and has stems with downward-directed hairs. B: Baker (1900), Paton et al. (2019). Bibliography
