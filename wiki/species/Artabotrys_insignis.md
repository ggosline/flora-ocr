## Description

Bot.Jahrb.39:483(19o7).-Pellegrin,Bull. Soc.Bot.Fr.94:256(1947).- Boutique,FI. Congo Belge 2 : 318 (1951).- Keay,F.W.T.A.,ed. 2,1 : 40 (1954).-R.E. Fries in Engler et Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenf.,ed. 2,17 a, 2:123 (1959).-Le Thomas,Adansonia,ser.2,5:448 (1965).

Liana with glabrous branches, pubescent-appressed branchlets, russet. Leaves with petiole 3-5 mm long, canaliculate, puberulent; lamina subcoriaceous, oblong, elliptic-oblong to elliptic 7-20 cm long, 2.2-8 cm wide, acute to subrounded at the base, ± long-acuminate at the apex; upper surface glabrous and shiny, lower surface pubescent-appressed on the veins; 8-14 pairs of lateral, ascending, oblique veins, joined in arches near the margin.

Inflorescences in opposite-leafed cymes, with axis and peduncle recurved into a woody hook, 1-2 cm long, sparsely appressed-pubescent. Flowers fleshy, green, with thick pedicel, flattened, 2-5 mm long, puberulent. Sepals triangular acute, 10-15 mm long, 5-8 mm wide, spreading, appressed-puberulent. Petals appressed-pubescent, with short hairs on both surfaces, more densely at the base and on the exterior; the outer ones lanceolate, 3-3.5 cm long, 0.7-1.2 cm wide, narrowed towards the rounded and concave base; the inner ones lanceolate-oblong, 1.5-2.5 cm long, 2-6 mm wide, fused at the concave base in the form of a small spoon. Stamens numerous, about 2 mm long, oblong, connectives enlarged and truncate below the anthers. Carpels 12-17, 3-4 mm long, oblong narrow, ribbed, entirely hairy or only on the margins; stigmas oblong, recurved outwards, pubescent. 2 basal ovules. Receptacle hirsute.

Fruits very shortly pedicellate, mericarps red at maturity, ellipsoid-obovoid, 1.7-2.5 cm long by 0.8-1.2 cm wide, marked with a longitudinal suture line, with a ± long point at the apex. 2 seeds per mericarp, ellipsoid flattened on the side of the suture.

## Discussion

Liana of very humid dense forest very widely distributed in Africa, in Sierra Leone, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Dahomey, Cameroon, Gabon and Congo-Kinshasa.

Species of humid dense forest and forest galleries, widely distributed in Africa, from Ghana to Congo-Kinshasa.

It is very close to A. velutinus Sc. Elliot, but differs from it by the papyraceous lamina of the leaves, the outer petals linear suddenly enlarged at the base into a sort of concave shield, the inner petals with a flat section and their pubescence less dense, longer and appressed.
