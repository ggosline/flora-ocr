# *Motandra lujaei* De Wild. & T.Durand Planche 75

**Genus**: [[Motandra]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belgique 40 : 23 (1901)

## Description

Liane reaching 10 m in height; branches with small lenticels; branchlets with dense dark brown pubescence, glabrescent. Leaf: petiole 3–10 mm; blade 3.5–13 × 1.2–4.9 cm, often with small domatia on the first or first two pairs of a ramification, constituted of a tuft of pale brown hairs. Inflorescence condensed, 1.5–5(–7.5) × 1–2.5 cm; peduncle and axes with dark brown pubescence, sometimes also with some long glabrous hairs; numerous bracts, ovate to narrowly ovate, 1–16 × 0.2–10 mm, with dark brown external pubescence. Flower: pedicel 1–4 mm; calyx with lobes 2–2.3 × 1.2–1.5 mm, dark brown, without or with 1 to 3 colleters per lobe; corolla white, less often yellow or dark brown, long in the mature bud, with cylindrical tube 4.2–10.2 × 1–2.2 mm, the interior with tufts of hairs 1.9–2.8 mm from the base, with lobes 0.2–0.5 times the tube length, 2–2.7 × 1–1.5 mm, the exterior densely pubescent and often presenting glabrous patches there, the interior slightly pubescent to glabrous; stamens inserted near the base of the tube, anther long with hairs at the summit; disk with pubescent lobes; pistil long 3–3.7 mm. Fruit: follicles forming an angle of, often with numerous lenticels, often subtended by the persistent calyx and bracts, with up to seeds in each follicle; exocarp up to mm in thickness, with tomentum 1–2 mm in thickness, dark brown, caducous, able to hide the wings; wings undulate, in the mature fruit 1–2 mm wide. Seed 12–15 mm long; coma 15–30 mm long. Distribution: Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda); in Gabon: Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem.

## Distribution

from Guinea to Uganda and Angola; in Gabon: Nyanga and Ogooué-Ivindo

## Ecology

clear deciduous or secondary forest, gallery forest, secondary regrowth, on sandy or clayey soils and on rocky outcrops; in Gabon to ± m altitude; elsewhere up to m. Use: In West Africa, the leaf juice is applied to treat eye infections and toothache. B: Boone (2008), de Kruif (1984). BIBLIOGRAPHY
