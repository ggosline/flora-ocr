# *Campylospermum glomeratum* (Tiegh.) Biss.

**Genus**: [[Campylospermum]]
**Family**: [[Ochnaceae]]
**Protologue**: Blumea 58 : 6 (2013)

## Description

Monelasmum glomeratum Tiegh., Ann. Sci. Nat., sér. 8, Bot. 16 : 330 (Dec. 1902). Ouratea reticulata (P.Beauv.) Engl. ex Gilg var. angustifolia Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 17 : 81 (1893). Ouratea angustifolia (Engl.) Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 269 (Mar. 1903). Ouratea conrauana Engl. & Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 260 (Mar. 1903). Ouratea dusenii Engl. & Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 33 : 260 (Mar. 1903). Ouratea gilgiana H.Winkl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 41 : 282 (1908). Campylospermum dusenii (Engl. & Gilg) Biss. & Sosef, Blumea 53(3) : 631 (2008). Small tree reaching 6 m in height; branches erect, with brownish-white bark. Stipules persistent, triangular, 1–2 mm long. Leaf: petiole 4–6 mm; blade narrowly to very narrowly elliptic, (4–)6–10(–14) × (0.8–)1–2(–3) cm, ratio 4–6, cuneate at base, acute at apex, papyraceous to subcoriaceous, with margin slightly serrulate to spaced teeth; primary vein distinctly prominent on upper surface, prominent on lower surface, lateral veins in 4 to 7 pairs, more prominent below than above, tertiary venation scalariform, indistinct above, distinct below. Inflorescence terminal, erect, branched, rather dense, main axis (2–)4–9(–13) cm; gemmate scales persistent, triangular, short; racemes 2 or 3, ascending, 3–6(–9) cm; cymules distant by 2–13 mm, 1- to 4-flowered; bracts persistent, 2 or 3, narrowly triangular, at base of cymules. Flower: pedicel (8–)10–11(–13) mm, articulate at 2–3(–4) mm from base; sepals ovate, 8–9 × 3–4 mm in flower, 8–10 × 3–4 mm in fruit; petals obovate, 11–13(–15) × 7–11 mm, narrowly cuneate at base, slightly emarginate at apex; stamens: anthers 4–8 mm long; style 5–6 mm. Fruit: receptacle 4–6 mm thick; drupels 1 or 2 well developed per receptacle, ellipsoid, 6–4 × 3–4 mm; cotyledons incumbent, unequal with small external cotyledon.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon; in Gabon, known only from the banks of the Ogooué (Middle-Ogooué and Ogooué-Ivindo).

## Ecology

Periodically inundated riverine forest, river banks, rocky shores, on rocky soil or white sand; in Gabon, at 80–120 m elevation, elsewhere at 10–600 m. Taxonomic note: This species resembles C. paucinervatum by its relatively small and narrowly elliptic leaves. It differs, however, by its bracts at the base of the cymules, its long and ascending racemes, as well as its blade bearing more lateral veins.
