# *Heckeldora zenkeri* (Harms) Staner Planche 8(E–M)

**Genus**: [[Heckeldora]]
**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Protologue**: Bull. Jard. Bot. État Bruxelles 16 : 207 (1941)

## Synonyms

- Guarea zenkeri Harms, Bot. Jahrb. Syst . 23 : 158 (1896).

## Description

Shrub sparsely branched, reaching 4 m in height. Leaf: petiole 3.5–12(–19) cm and rachis 13.5–25(–36) cm, both glabrescenct, rounded but slightly channeled above and with short, stiff indument; petiolules 1–5 mm; leaflets (3–)7–11(–13), opposite to subopposite or alternate, narrowly elliptic to elliptic or obovate, 4–21 × 2.5–7 cm, with cuneiform or obtuse base, with acuminate or sometimes nearly acute apex, glabrescenct on both surfaces except the midrib generally densely hairy above or rarely glabrous. Inflorescence solitary or fascicled by 2–3, paniculate, sparsely branched, up to 60(–80) cm in length, with ramifications reaching 17 cm in length; sometimes the flowers nearly sessile and the inflorescence resembling a raceme. Functionally male flower: pedicel articulate, 0.5–1.5 mm, puberulent; calyx cupuliform, truncate or weakly (3–)4-lobed or -toothed, 2–3.5 × 3–4 mm, puberulent and with some brownish glands; petals (3–)4, narrowly obovate to narrowly oblong, 8–11 × 2.5–4 mm; staminal tube 8–10 mm in length, glabrous, with 8–9 anthers; gynoecium clearly developed, slender, with glabrous stipe 0.5–2.5 mm in length, swollen towards the middle or upper part; ovary ellipsoid, 1–2 mm in diameter, densely hairy; style 3–4 mm. Functionally female flower similar, gynophore swollen and narrowly annular at the base of the ovary; ovary 1.5–2.5(–3) mm in diameter, densely hairy. Fruit stipitate, ovoid to obovoid, often asymmetric, 3–7 × 2–5 cm, attenuate towards the apex over approximately 1 cm, yellowish, shortly hairy.

## Distribution

Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo and Woleu-Ntem

## Ecology

Understory of evergreen and semi-deciduous forests, old secondary forest; in Gabon and elsewhere, up to 800 m in altitude. Bibliography: Hutchinson et al. (1958), Staner (1941), de Wilde (2007).
