# *Garcinia lucida* Vesque

**Genus**: [[Garcinia]]
**Family**: [[Clusiaceae]]
**Protologue**: G. nobilis Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 40 : 566 (1908)

## Description

in DC., Monogr. phan. 8 : 311 (1893). G. nobilis Engl., Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 40 : 566 (1908). Tree or shrub, sometimes scandent, dioecious, up to 20 m tall; trunk up to 15 cm in diameter; bark rugose to fissured, with large lenticels, brown or grey to beige; latex white to yellowish or cream pinkish; twigs angular, sometimes slightly winged. Leaves opposite, glabrous; petiole 1–1.5(–2.5) cm, angular, wrinkled transversely, fovea 6–9 mm long; blade elliptic to elliptic-obovate, sometimes narrowly elliptic, (14–)25–57 × (5–)8–23 cm, base rounded to slightly cordate, apex acute to acuminate, coriaceous, shiny, pale green to whitish-green beneath; midvein slightly rounded above, beneath prominent and acute, lateral veins 13–20 pairs, secretory canals indistinct, reticulation distinct when dry. Inflorescence terminal or sometimes axillary, up to 1.8 m long, first-order branching normally unbranched and of very variable size, also up to 1.8 m long. Flowers unisexual, in small glomerules, sessile. Male flower: sepals 4, suborbicular, whitish, the inner ones approximately 1 mm, the outer ones 2–3 mm in diameter; petals 4–5, circular to broadly elliptic-obovate, 5–6 mm long, white; stamen phalanges 4–5, oblong, 4–6 mm long, filaments free at the apex, each phalange with 5–10 ovoid and non-locellate anthers; rudimentary ovary irregularly lobed, 3 mm in diameter. Female flower: sepals 3–4 mm in diameter; petals 6–9 mm long; stamen phalanges well developed (bisexual flowers); ovary 4–5-locular, broadly ellipsoid, 3–4 mm long; stigma sessile, lobed. Fruit subglobose to obpyriform, 3.5–5 × 1.5–5 cm, verrucose, light brown to brown. Seeds 3–4, reniform, 2–3.5 × 1–2 cm, red to pinkish-red.

## Distribution

Cameroon, Gabon and west of the Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, fairly common in the western half of the country

## Ecology

primary forest or old secondary forest, especially on slopes and hills, often forming fairly dense stands; in Gabon, at 70–675 m altitude, elsewhere up to 1200 m

## Vernacular names

essok, otoka (mitsogho)

## Uses

The bitter bark is used as an additive in the production of palm wine. The fruits and bark are also used against stomach pains and to prevent food poisoning. Taxonomic notes: While in Garcinia the number of sepals and petals (and stamen phalanges) is normally fixed and is also a stable and important character for identification, in this one, the number of petals and stamen phalanges is 4 or 5.
