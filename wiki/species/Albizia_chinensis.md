# *Albizia chinensis* (Osbeck) Merrill

**Genus**: [[Albizia]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Amer. Journ. Bot. 3: 575 (1916)

## Synonyms

Tree reaching ± 7 m tall for a diameter of 20 cm, attaining 20 m for a diameter of 40 cm. Mature branchlets glabrous, dark brown with brown lenticels, with fine longitudinal ridges. Young branchlets puberulent pubescent to tomentose, light brown. Stipules early deciduous, densely pubescent tomentose on both faces, asymmetrically lanceolate, reaching a length of 1.5 cm; asymmetric base: adpetiolar margin strongly rounded and abpetiolar margin very strongly cordate; apex acute. Petiole pubescent yellowish, 3.5–6 cm long, upper face ± plane then channeled (channel bordered by 2 longitudinal ridges) bearing between the midpoint and the base a strongly prominent ellipsoid gland. Rachis pubescent, 12–22.5 cm long, upper face with broad keel, channeled bearing prominent ellipsoid to ± conic glands at the level of insertion of the 1–3 pairs of terminal pinnae. Pinnae 4–14 pairs. Pinna axis pubescent, 4–10.5 cm long, upper face keeled bearing up to 3 ellipsoid glands at the level of insertion of the pairs of terminal leaflets. Leaflets (10) 20–30 pairs, with basal pair reduced; with short pubescent petiolule above, 0.5–0.75 mm long. Lamina glabrous above and ± pubescent below (hairs long ± appressed), weakly glaucous green above and pale green below on the living plant, blue-green above and green below on the dried specimen; ± oblong subulate, reaching 10 × 3 mm; asymmetric base: proximal margin rounded-truncate and distal margin weakly attenuate linear; margin ciliate; apex acute. Principal vein strongly eccentric toward the distal or submarginous margin, concave above and prominent pubescent below. Secondary veins, approximately 3, basal, radiating, not visible above, very obscurely prominent below.

Inflorescences in panicles of capitate spikes, axillary or terminal. Panicle axis densely pubescent tomentose yellowish. Spikes fasciculate. Base of spikes surrounded by stipules as described above and often axillate by a reduced leaf, reduced to a pubescent axis 1–6.5 mm long, terminated by a circular gland. Spike peduncle pubescent tomentose, 1.5–4.5 cm long. Bracteole pubescent, narrowly elliptic to linear, apex acute. Floral bud obovoid. Basal flowers sessile or subsessile. Calyx yellowish-green to yellow on the living plant, pubescent, obconic, 2.5–2.75 mm long; free lobes broadly triangular, ± 0.5 mm long, apex acute. Corolla pubescent externally becoming progressively glabrous toward the base, 5.5–6.25 mm long; free lobes ovate triangular, 1.50–2 mm long, apex acute. Stamens white on the living plant, 25–28.5 mm long, united into a non-exsert tube 3–3.80 mm long; free filaments slender; anthers oblongoid, ± 0.15 mm long. Pistil with short stipe 0.25–± 0.5 mm long; ovary with micropubescence in the upper half and a few hairs toward the apex, narrowly oblongoid, 2–2.5 mm long, with 14 ovules; style slender reaching 34 mm, terminating in a short flattened stigma. Terminal flower solitary, sessile. Calyx pubescent ± cylindric, 3 mm long, free lobes triangular, 0.5–0.75 mm long, apex acute. Corolla pubescent externally, progressively glabrous toward the base, distinctly obconic above the calyx, 7.5 mm long; free lobes ovate triangular, 1.5–2 mm long, apex acute. Stamens 21 mm long, united into an exsert tube 10 mm long, base enlarged. Pistil not stipitate; ovary resting on a flattened lobed disc, with pubescence on the upper half and a few longer hairs at the apex, ellipsoid, 2 mm long, with 10 ovules; style 23 mm long.

Legume chartaceous, glabrous or glabrescent, mat brown on the dried specimen, 15 × 2 cm; base attenuate, sometimes stipitate; sutural margins thickened; lateral faces with very effaced or absent venation; apex obtuse. Seed elliptic, 7 × 4.5 mm; aril circular, near the micropyle, with a diameter of 1 mm.

## Type

Osbeck s.n., Chine, près Wampoa (non retrouvé)

## Discussion

A. chinensis is an Asian species whose range extends from India to southern China to the east, to Ceylon, Thailand and Java to the south. It is planted in Gabon as a shade tree in plantations.
