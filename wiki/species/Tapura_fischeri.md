## Description

KLAY, FWTA, ed. 2, 1 (2): 439 (1958); HAUMAN, FZRB 7: 347 (1958), KLAY, ONOCHIE & STANFIELD, Nigerian Trees 1: 327 (1960); WHITE, For. Fl. N-Rhodesia: 185 (1962); ROSS, Fl. Natal: 219 (1972); BRETELER, AUWP 86 (3): 56-61 (1986). TYPE: Tanzania, Kagehi, Fischer 282 (holo-, B delet.; lecto-, K).

– var. pubescens VERDCOURT & TORRE in TORRE, Bol. Soc. Brot., ser. 2, 36: 69 (1982); FZ 2 (1): 328 (1963). TYPE: Mozambique, Mossurize, Simao 1173 (holo-, EA; iso-, LISC).

- T. lujae DE WILD., Bull. Jard. Bot. Brux. 5: 101, "lujai" (1915); HAUMAN, FZRB 7: 348 (1958). TYPE: Zaire, Cataractes, Luja 149 (holo-, BR).

– Dichapetalum dummeri Moss, Kew Bull.: 123 (1928); BRETELER, MLW 78 (10): 62 (1978). TYPE: Uganda, Mabira, Mulange, Dummer 4469 (holo-, BM).

– D. corradii CHIOV., Webbia 8: 232 (1952); CUFODONTIS, Bull. Jard. Bot. Brux. 26, suppl.: 412 (1956); BRETELER, MLW 78 (10): 23 (1978). TYPE: Ethiopia, Omo Valley, Lake Murle, Corradi 8400 (holo-, Fl.; iso-, WAG).

Shrub to small tree with abundant and often low branching, reaching a height of 20 m and trunk diameter of 45 cm; branches often horizontal or even spreading. Bark grey-brown, fairly smooth. Sapwood whitish, rapidly turning yellow or a darker shade when exposed to air. Older twigs grey-brown, glabrous or nearly so, often bearing very small lenticels. Young twigs with ± appressed hairs, often becoming glabrous relatively early.

Stipules early caducous, triangular and generally narrow, 1-5 mm long, with subappressed hairs and sometimes glandular margins.

Leaves. Petiole semi- to subcylindrical, furrowed above or not, (1) 2-10 (17) mm long (generally somewhat longer when fused to the inflorescence peduncle) and with subappressed pubescence. Blade papyraceous, elliptic to obovate, (1.5) 2-3 (3.5) times longer than wide, (2) 4-10 (16) × (1) 2-5 (7) cm; base cuneate to rounded and often asymmetric; acumen often short ≤ 1 (2) cm, rounded to acute at the apex; midvein and the 4-7 (8) pairs of principal secondary veins not or very slightly prominent above and much more distinctly so below; upper surface glabrous to pubescent, then often becoming glabrous relatively early; pubescence often denser below, especially on the midvein and principal lateral veins, subsequently glabrous or not, sometimes with persistent tufts of hairs at the axils of the principal lateral veins; glands, when present, only on the lower surface, generally few and rather widely distributed, but sometimes only near the base.

Inflorescences subumbellate, sometimes with one or two distinct ramifications, ≤ 35-flowered, subglabrous or bearing ± dense subappressed pubescence. Peduncle, partially to completely fused with the petiole, sometimes free, (4) 5-8 (13) mm long. Bracts and bracteoles ovate-triangular, 1 (1.5) mm long. Pedicel 3 (6) mm long, upper part 0-0.5 (1) mm, generally glabrous.

Flowers 2.5-3.5 mm long. Sepals erect, subappressed, free or nearly so, somewhat unequal in shape and strongly unequal in size, suborbicular to ovate-elliptic, ≤ 1 × 1 mm, somewhat concave; exterior glabrous to bearing scattered hairs, interior glabrous; margin ciliate. Petals fused to the stamens in a tube 0.5-1 (1.5) mm tall, thin, glabrous to pubescent with free apical parts; 1 large abaxial petal 2-2.5 mm long, basal part hyaline, upper part deflexed, outer side pubescent, apex split for 0.5-1 mm into 2 somewhat spreading cucullate lobes; the 2 lateral petals are shorter ≈ 2 mm, split for 0.3-0.5 mm, their lobes generally both cucullate or at least the abaxial lobe (the longer one), their outer pubescence appressed; the 2 adaxial petals are often slightly shorter still than the lateral ones, their apical part, entire, narrow and triangular, 0.5-1 mm long, their outer pubescence similar. Fertile stamens 2, rarely 3, equal to the large petal or somewhat shorter, rarely somewhat longer than it, long fused to the adjacent lateral petal, but shortly fused to the large petal; free part of filaments 0.5 (1) mm long, glabrous; anthers cordate, 0.5 mm long, connective distinct or only slightly so, muriculate or not, sometimes slightly projecting behind the thecae at the apex. Sterile stamens 1-3 (the adaxial or the lateral ones may be absent or rudimentary), the free part filiform, 0.5-1 mm long, pubescent. Basal staminodes fused into a ring often lobed around the ovary and open on the side of the large petal, or rarely free; they are 0.3-0.7 mm tall, glabrous and fused by their base to the tube. Pistil 3 (2)-merous, 2-3 (3.5) mm long; ovary glabrous to covered with dense hairs; pubescence appressed on the upper part of the style, more scattered or absent on the lower part; apex with (2) 3 papillose lobes of often unequal length.

Fruits ovoid to oblique ellipsoid, 5 × 4 mm, sometimes subspheric, 1-3-seeded, ± distinctly lobed if they have more than one seed, glabrous or covered with dense villous tomentum, dehiscent, showing an endocarp surrounded by a scarlet mesocarp; exo- and mesocarp thin; endocarp osseous, somewhat rugose on the outside, glabrous and finely striated on the inside. Seed subellipsoid, 3-4 × 1-1.5 mm, rounded to subacute at both ends, testa brown and thin. Embryo enveloped by a layer of albumen 0.2 mm thick, cotyledons flattened 0.2 mm thick, radicle exsert.

T. fischeri is the first species of Dichapetalaceae with albuminous seed known in Africa. The embryo is also slightly distinguished by the protrusion of its radicle. – Pl. 63, p. 207.

## Distribution

Ivory Coast, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Congo, Zaire, Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, South Africa

Although no collection of this species from Gabon is known to date, we deemed it necessary to include it in this flora: given its wide distribution in Africa and especially the fact that it has already been collected in southeastern Cameroon, Congo and Zaire, its presence in Gabon is more than likely (Editor's note).
