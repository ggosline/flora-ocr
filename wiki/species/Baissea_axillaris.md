# *Baissea axillaris* (Benth.) Hua Planche 15

**Genus**: [[Baissea]]
**Family**: [[Apocynaceae]]
**Protologue**: Compt. Rend. Acad. Sci. Paris 134 : 857 (1902)

## Description

Vine reaching 15 m in height; branches densely pubescent. Leaves: petiole 1–8 mm, densely pubescent; lamina ovate to oblong, often panduriform, sometimes deltoid, ± as long as wide to longer than wide of (0.3–)1–7.8 × 0.3–2.9 cm, rounded to cordate at the base, obtuse to acute at the apex, pubescent to almost glabrous on both faces, secondary nerves 10 pairs; domatia present. Inflorescence axillary and sometimes also terminal, 0.5–2.5 cm long, in a cyme with 4–8 flowers, pedicel 0.5–2 mm, pubescent. Flower: pedicel 1–3 mm, calyx with lobes 1–3 times as long as wide of 1–3 × 0.5–1.5 mm, pubescent to puberulent on the exterior, ciliolate, glabrous on the interior, with 3 to 6 (mainly 5) colleters at the base; corolla with tube yellow or orange, with or without red spots or longitudinal stripes, internally funnel-shaped or sometimes campanulate, 1.5–3 times as long as the calyx, (1.9–)2.9–4.9 mm long, diversely puberulent or pubescent on the exterior, with tufts of hairs on the interior, with lobes elliptic to oblong, 0.5–1 times as long as the tube, 1–2 times as long as wide of 1–2 × 0.5–1 mm, often enlarged at the base, obtuse at the apex, pubescent on the exterior and glabrous on the interior; stamens inserted 0.5–1.2 mm from the base of the tube, anther 1.3–1.6 mm long, glabrous or sometimes puberulent on the exterior; ovary pubescent at the apex. Fruit: follicles 10.8–38.5 × 0.5–2.8 cm, densely pubescent. Distribution from Benin to the Central African Republic, to the Democratic Republic of the Congo and to Angola, in Gabon common but not yet recorded from Ngounié and Ogooué Maritime. Ecology: Primary and secondary forest, forest edges, in Gabon up to 400 m altitude, elsewhere up to 700 m. Use: A decoction of leafy branches is used to treat kidney diseases. A decoction of roots associated with the bark of the stem is used to treat colic without triggering diarrhea. The bark is used to make a violent poison for hunting. Notes: Whereas in Gabon the corolla is always yellow, there exist regions in Cameroon and to the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo where the corolla is described as white according to field notes. The plants with white flowers from Cameroon might represent a different species. The western African specimens cited in the revision by van Dilst belong to other species of Baissea or are too poor to be identified with certainty. It is therefore very probable that B. axillaris is not present to the west of Benin. BIBLIOGRAPHY
