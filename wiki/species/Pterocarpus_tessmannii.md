# *Pterocarpus tessmannii* Harms Planche 110

**Genus**: [[Pterocarpus]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. Syst. 45 : 311 (1910)

## Description

, pro parte (flowers). Semi-deciduous tree after fruiting, reaching 35 m in height; trunk fluted at the base, up to 1.5 m in diameter; bark detaching in broad scales; branchlets glabrous or almost so. Stipules oblong, 17–20 × 2–3 mm, acute to obtuse. Leaf: petiole 1–2 cm; rachis 4–8 cm; leaflets 5 to 9, with blackish petiolule, 4–8 mm, limb oblong to lanceolate-oblong, 4–8 × 2–3 cm, base obtuse to cuneate, apex acuminate, short and obtuse, ± emarginate, glabrous, shiny above. Inflorescence in racemes or elongate pseudoracemes solitary or fascicled and forming a false panicle with short axis, arranged toward the apex of glabrous branchlets, flowers 1 to 3 per fascicle; bracts caducous, minute. Flower: pedicel 4–7 mm; calyx red or dark purple, with infundibuliform tube, 6–7 mm long, with large teeth 1–2 mm long, margin shortly ciliate; standard erect, with broadly oval limb, 7–8 mm long, apex scarcely emarginate, yellow with red base, claw 2–5 mm, wings oblique, 6–7 × 5 mm, yellow, auriculate at base, rounded at apex, claw 4–5 mm, keel reddish; stamens diadelphous, 8 mm; ovary stipitate, narrow, oblong, with 3 ovules, with a few scattered hairs, stipe hairy; style 2 mm, glabrous, stigma minute. Pod oblong to falcate, 11.5–13.5 × 3.8–4.8 cm, with 1 or 2 seeds, brown, surface reticulate, but less so on the boss. Seed reniform, 1.5 cm long, chocolate brown, flattened and smooth.

## Distribution

Equatorial Guinea, Gabon and Democratic Republic of the Congo; in Gabon, known from Estuaire, Ngounié and Ogooué-Lolo

## Ecology

semi-deciduous and evergreen forests, riparian, forest edges, places more humid than P. soyauxii; in Gabon up to 500 m altitude. Flowers in February. Uses: The red wood is similar to that of P. soyauxii, it is probably sold as such and used for carpentry and cabinetmaking. Maceration of the wood produces a red dye that serves to embellish the body

## Source note

Pellegrin (1949) indicates that this species could present itself as a liana, but this is very probably in error. Rojo (1972) had excluded P. tessmannii from the genus Pterocarpus, but without having seen the fruits. B: Evrard (1988), van der Maesen (2013), Pellegrin (1949), Rojo (1972), Velayos et al.
