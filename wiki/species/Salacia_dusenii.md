# *Salacia dusenii* Loesener

**Genus**: [[Salacia]]
**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 19 : 242 (1894) ; l.c. 44 : 188 (1910) ; HUTCH. & MOSS, FWTA 1 : 452 (1928) ; HALLÉ, Mon. Hipp. Afr. occid., Thèse : 174 (1958) ; Mém. IFAN 64 : 176 (1962)

## Description

l.c. 44 : 188 (1910) ; HUTCH. & MOSS, FWTA 1 : 452 (1928) ; HALLÉ, Mon. Hipp. Afr. occid., Thèse : 174 (1958) ; Mém. IFAN 64 : 176 (1962).

Large liane (or tree ?) reaching 8-15 m, entirely glabrous. Branchlets light grey to brownish. Leaves opposite or subopposite. Petiole 6-10 mm with a narrow grooved channel. Limb olivaceous when dry, somewhat paler beneath, 7-16(-20) × 4-8(-10) cm, elliptic, with entire margin bordered below; base acute or obtuse; apex with a distinct acumen, sometimes short. Midvein prominent above. Secondary veins 6-9 pairs long-curved; fine reticulation apparent, not dense and often subtransverse.

Axillary glomerules entirely sessile, pluri- or multiflorous, with dense and very small obtuse bracteoles. Pedicels slender 7-20 mm. Buds subglobose 1.5-1.8 mm in diameter. Flower rotate, (4)5(6.5) mm in diameter, yellow to reddish-yellow, orange or brown. Sepals subequal 1 mm wide, slightly distinctly microciliolate, ± obtuse and short. Petals oblong, narrow at the base, about 3 mm long, distinctly microciliolate. Disk subpentagonal, thick, with upper face somewhat raised at the 5 apices; deep perigynous groove in which are inserted the narrow stamens, about 1.5 mm long, with anther transversely elliptic and erect in the bud. Ovary lageniform, with locules sunken somewhat lower than the insertion level of the stamens; 2 superposed ovules per locule.

Fruit ± globose about 3 cm in diameter, up to 4.5 cm long, somewhat attenuate toward the base, with perianth tardily caducous. - Pl. 21, fig. 1-13, p. 81.

## Discussion

Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon and Zaire (forms with short pedicel: Pauwels 5842).

The depth of the perigynous groove is remarkable and clearly distinguishes the species from the other two: S. staudtiana whose glomerules are not sessile, and S. pyriformioides whose capitate multiflorous axillary glomerules are much larger. But S. dewevrei De Wild. & Th. Dur. (Bull. Soc. Roy. Bot. Belg. 38(2): 79, 1899 ; WILCZEK, FCB 9 : 209, 1960, TYPE : Dewèvre 1070, BR!) from Zaire, is still much closer and even differs only slightly: the 5 apices of the disk are more narrowly lobed and raised; the sepals are sometimes more acute. A conspecificity that would accord to this Salacia a rank of subspecies of dusenii is not to be excluded.
