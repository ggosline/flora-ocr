Annual herbaceous or briefly perennial plant, reaching 2 m in height, armed with stipular spines, often branched from the base; main stem dominant, rigidly erect. Plant puberulent and provided with glands to pubescent; stem glabrescenct in the lower portion; woody base reaching 1 cm in diameter. Stipular spines reaching up to 5 mm in length, straight or slightly curved, triangular in lateral view, swollen on the sides, shiny straw-yellow with age, glabrous except at the base; their size decreases toward the apex of the stems; they are present even on the racemes and sometimes even on the lower floral bracts. Spines and acicles (gradually passing to hairs) are often present on the lower surface of the petioles and the midvein of the leaflets.

Leaves (3-) 5-7 (-9) -foliolate, long-petiolate. Leaflets lanceolate to narrowly lanceolate, acuminate, most often attenuate, symmetric or nearly so, dark olive on both surfaces or slightly paler below; median leaflet 5-12 × 1.5-2 cm; lateral leaflets somewhat smaller. Lateral veins of the median leaflet generally 17-38, sometimes 12-14 at the level of the basal leaves. Median and lateral veins prominent below, depressed above. Median vein often provided with acicular or setiform hairs below, glabrous above. Upper surface of leaflets provided with small glandular hairs between the veins; lower surface generally glabrous between the veins. Petiolules 0.5-3 mm long, canaliculate and densely puberulent on the upper surface and generally glabrous on the lower surface, or slightly pubescent on both surfaces. Petioles of the lower leaves reaching up to 14 cm in length, whereas below the racemes they are only about 2-3 cm; they are slender, distinctly channeled on the upper surface, sometimes provided with hairs on both surfaces, but most often glandular-puberulent to pubescent above and provided with a few prickles and acicular hairs below.

Inflorescence dense in its apical portion, reaching 10 cm in width at anthesis and 65 cm in length while becoming lax at fruiting; primary racemes ordinarily more developed than lateral racemes. Floral bracts simple or sometimes 3-foliolate (the most inferior ones); simple bracts, lanceolate, 2 or 3 times longer than wide, subsessile or shortly petiolate, attenuate, cuneate or rounded at base, persistent; floral bracts 2-2.5 cm long at the level of the lowermost flowers, increasingly smaller toward the top of the raceme; stipular spines generally absent, prickles sometimes present on the most inferior bracts. Pedicels 3.5-4 cm long at fruiting, equal to or shorter than the gynophore, finely glandular-puberulent. Sepals 4, yellowish-green, narrowly lanceolate, 5.5-7 × 1-1.5 mm before anthesis, glabrous or finely glandular-puberulent on the dorsal surface, united at the base, strongly reflexed from the base at anthesis, tardily caducous. Petals uniformly pink or white, ascending, oblanceolate; the 2 pairs have almost equal size, 1.6-2.8 × 0.4-1 cm (smaller on poorly developed specimens), rounded and mucronulate at apex; limb narrowed into a basal claw equal to or slightly shorter than the sepals, glabrous or sometimes provided with a few glands on the dorsal surface; these petals are caducous. Androgynophore short, bulbous and fleshy at anthesis, dark olive to blackish when dry, reaching 1-2 (-3) mm at fruiting, glabrous, smooth or vaguely striated, bearing staminiferous scars all at about the same level. Stamens 6, all fertile, inclined, long-exserted and very fragile; filaments 2.2-8 cm long (shorter on poorly developed flowers), glabrous,

whitish, often becoming purple or pink in the upper half; anthers linear, very fragile, 4-6 mm long, becoming purple before dehiscence, dorsibasiflxed, curved at the apex at the time of anthesis, early caducous. Gynophore slender, glabrous, 3-6-6.5 mm long at fruiting, having 1 to 2 times the length of the pedicel. Ovary linear, glabrous or sometimes provided with sparse and fine glands; stigma subsessile, very small, truncate.

Capsule relatively thick at maturity, 4.5-6.5 cm in length and up to 0.9-1.2 cm in width, linear, slightly swollen at the margins, acute at both ends, straight or slightly curved, green at maturity and then generally somewhat drooping; replum glabrous. Valves papyraceous, striated with about 60 fine nervules, not prominent and very anastomosed; fruit wall and nervules glabrous; style ordinarily absent, sometimes 1 mm long; stigma prominent, truncate. Seeds suborbicular-reniform in lateral view, subspherical; 2.5-2.75 mm in diameter; hilar slit distinct, short and open, elaiosome absent; testa grayish-green matte, glabrous, smooth or most often provided with a few very small tubercles near the obtuse extremity of the cotyledons.

## Discussion

C. afrospina is the sole African representative of a section (sect. Tarenaya) that is otherwise neotropical and endemic. The species most closely related to it is C. trachycarpa Kl. ex Eichler, which lives in Brazil and Paraguay. ILTIS (1967) hypothesized that the origin of C. afrospina could be a small population introduced by birds, which would have evolved to give the present species. Section Tarenaya comprises some species fairly easy to confuse. Two of them were recently introduced to Africa: C. spinosa Jacq. and C. hassleriana Chod.; the former was found in Gabon where it has naturalized, but the latter is entirely unknown there. The possibility of gene exchange between C. afrospina and these newly introduced species cannot be ruled out in the future.

C. afrospina is distinguished from related species by the numerous lateral veins of the leaflets, its thick capsule, and also by various characters of the seed. However, the Gabonese material is ± atypical compared to that from adjacent territories: variation for essential characters has still been found there. Given this unexpected variation, the key published in the Cameroon Flora is less useful for determining collections from Gabon.

The variation highlighted in the account that follows can serve as a key to determine the material from Gabon, which can be classified into 3 morphological entities (namely 3, 3' and 1' in this account). The material from Cameroon corresponds to the combinations of characters listed under numbers 2 and 3 of the account.

KEY TO THE VARIATION OF Cleome afrospina

1. Lateral veins 17-28 (median leaflet); petals white or pink, 16-28 mm long; anthers 5-6 mm long (Gabon, Cameroon and elsewhere).

2. Gynophore and pedicel substantially equal (Cameroon and elsewhere except Gabon).

2'. Gynophore about 2 times longer than the pedicel (as in C. spinosa and C. hassleriana) (Gabon).

3. Pedicels subglabrous on the lower surface (with the exception of prickles and acicular hairs); racemes without stipular spines or present only at the level of the most inferior bracts (Cameroon and elsewhere; Gabon: Hallé & Cours 870 with pink petals, Louis & al. 1021 with white petals).

3'. Pedicels abundantly and entirely puberulent; racemes armed in their lower portion at least as far as bracts no. 7-10, prickles smaller on the upper bracts, sometimes able to disappear completely (as in C. hassleriana) (Gabon: de Wilde & al. 696, pink petals).

1. Lateral veins fewer in number, 7-14 (as in C. spinosa and C. hassleriana); petals small, 5-6 mm long, white; anthers 3 mm long (atypical characters for C. afrospina, recalling a poorly developed C. spinosa); pedicel and gynophore of equal length; pedicel subglabrous on the lower surface, racemes armed from the base, character typical for C. afrospina (Gabon: Dybowsky 130, Descoings 6229): one may doubt whether they belong to this species.

The specimens listed under 1' are entirely distinct from the type of C. afrospina by their few lateral veins and their small petals. These collections are poor and incomplete: they lack ripe fruits and seem to correspond to poorly developed individuals, perhaps because they come from impoverished and dry soils. They differ from C. spinosa by their lanceolate floral bracts and by the presence of various prickles on the leaflets and petioles. They were assigned, not without some hesitation, to C. afrospina. The rest of the specimens are remarkable because they present, like C. spinosa and C. hassleriana, a gynophore twice as long as the pedicel; such variation is still very imperfectly known in Gabon.

C. afrospina is found in secondary forest, on river bed banks, in swampy places or periodically flooded or remaining very humid for long periods. Its flowering has been noted in February, May and November; it probably flowers during most of the year. In Gabon only a few collections are known, all from the Southwest. No use of this species is known in Africa.

Widely distributed in the western part of tropical Africa, it is known notably from Nigeria, Cameroon, Gabon, Congo and Zaire. Its introduction must have been ancient: the first known collection was made in Zaire in 1819; the first collection from Cameroon dates back to 1892; as for Gabon, the atypical specimen (1' in the account) was collected in 1894 at Fernan Vaz; another from 1960 comes from the south of N'Dende; the other 3 collections are from 1983.
