Annual herbaceous or briefly perennial plant reaching 40 cm in height, very much branched from the base; the primary stem usually dominant; lateral branches decumbent or ascending. Stems rigid or not, finely pubescent or glabrescent, green or tinged with purple, grooved toward the top, cylindrical and becoming woody toward the base. Hairs non-glandular, scanty, spreading, reaching 2 mm, generally reddish with a broad base and swollen at the margins.

Leaves 3 (-5) foliolate, petiolate, reducing toward the top of the branches and passing gradually into bracts. Leaflets subsessile, lanceolate, oblanceolate to narrowly obovate, sometimes linear, rounded or acute sometimes acuminate, in some cases truncate on the upper leaves, often attenuate at the base, dark green above, somewhat paler below; median leaflet symmetric, 30–35 × 8–12 mm on the lower leaves, 3–4 times longer than wide; lateral leaflets smaller, symmetric or not on 3-foliolate leaves; outermost leaflets of 5-foliolate leaves very asymmetric at the base and much smaller than the others, otherwise similar to the median leaflet. Midrib prominent beneath, depressed above; lateral veins 5–8. Lower surface of leaflets puberulent or pubescent on the median and lateral veins, glabrous elsewhere; upper surface bearing hairs regularly spaced between the veins and venules glabrous. Petioles reaching 3.3 cm in length on the lower leaves, progressively shorter going up the stem, 5 mm long below the lowest flower, green or tinged with purple, finely puberulent. Stipules glandiform, obovate, reaching 0.25 mm in length.

Inflorescences poorly defined initially, rather well defined after the elongation of the shoots; primary raceme reaching 30 cm at fruiting often slender, lateral racemes ordinarily shorter. Floral bracts 3-foliolate, shortly petiolate to subsessile, progressively shorter toward the top of the raceme, persistent. Flowers zygomorphic, rather small. Pedicel 10–17 mm in length at fruiting, often tinged with purple, very finely puberulent particularly toward the top, more often glabrous toward the base. Sepals 2 mm long, lanceolate to subulate, attenuate, somewhat distant at anthesis, puberulent, dark purple most often on the margins and apex, deciduous. Petals pink to dark purple, ascending at anthesis; the upper pair (inner) provided with a distinct transverse band, bordered in violet near the claw; petals lanceolate to oblong, 6–7 (–9) × 1.5–2 mm, rounded and mucronulate at the apex, gradually narrowed into a basal claw equal to or shorter than the sepals, glabrous, caducous. Androgynophore absent. Stamens 6, all fertile, inclined, slightly exserted; filaments 6–7 mm long, glabrous, whitish, hyaline, fragile; anthers linear, approximately 2 mm in length before dehiscence, dorsobasifixed, early caducous. Gynophore glabrous, (4)–5–7(–13) mm in length at fruiting, then equal or inferior to the pedicel. Ovary provided with dense or scattered pubérulence with stipitate glands, upper half ± incurved at anthesis; style absent or reaching 1 mm in length, straight and glabrous; stigma small, capitate or truncate.

Capsule linear, thin, cylindrical, ordinarily 35–50 × 2.5–3 mm, attenuate at both ends or acute at the apex, horizontal or pendulous, green at maturity; replum glabrous or provided with very small hairs; valves striated with 7–10 clearly prominent veins, little anastomosed; walls of the fruit provided, between the veins, with narrow nerve-like glabrous bars, becoming straw-colored with age. Style 0 to 2–4 mm in length, filiform, straight, glabrous; stigma very small swollen-capitate. Seeds suborbicular-reniform viewed laterally, slightly compressed laterally, hilum reaching the center of the seed covered initially with an elaiosome early caducous; testa dark brown to blackish at maturity, ornamented with slightly marked longitudinal striations and numerous prominent transverse ridges for the most part interrupted, without pubérulence along the upper margin.

## Discussion

Cleome iberidella is a polymorphic species composed of numerous ± geographically isolated populations. The species is here accepted in the broad sense as in the Flora of Cameroon. It was thought that this species and C. rutidosperma could be interfertile; a few specimens bearing the characters of these 2 taxa have been collected in Cameroon and Gabon. This atypical material has been cited following C. rutidosperma.

The main recognition characters of C. iberidella are the occasional presence of 5-foliolate leaves, distinctly defined inflorescences and the nerve-like thickenings of the fruit walls; these narrow reliefs alternate with the veins with which they can easily be confused: they are however glabrous while the true veins are pubescent and anastomosed. The Gabonese material is reduced to a single collection lacking its basal portion; consequently it was necessary to complete its description using the characters mentioned in the Flora of Cameroon.

C. iberidella is found in mountains or in rough regions occupied by shrubby savannas or montane forests. The majority of collections from Africa come from rocky or gravelly soils or from periodically humid soils. In Gabon, it was found in a gravel pit near a track, close to a humid ravine shaded by forest at 600 m altitude.

C. iberidella is limited to Africa where its range is very extensive but also very fragmented; this range is mainly the periphery of the Congo Basin, but the species is present not only in Zaire, Angola and Zambia but also in Guinea Bissau, Nigeria, Cameroon, Uganda as well as the extreme North of Namibia (South-West Africa).
