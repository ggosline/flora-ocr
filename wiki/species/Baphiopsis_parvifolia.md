# *Baphiopsis parvifolia* Benth. ex Bak.

**Genus**: [[Baphiopsis]]
**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Protologue**: Baker in Oliv. F.T.A. 2:256 (1871)

## Synonyms

- Baker in Oliv. F.T.A. 2:256 (1871).

## Description

Trees, shrubs. Leaves unifoliolate, broadly elliptic or ovate-elliptic or oblong or lanceolate, obtusely acuminate or conversely long acuminate-acute, with base generally rounded or obtuse, 4 to 20 cm in length by 4–10 cm in width, glabrous. Lateral veins 5–8 pairs united in arches, prominent. Reticulum of veinlets and venules prominent on both surfaces, very characteristic. Petiole 1–4 cm, swollen at each extremity.

Flowers white. Pedicels slender, somewhat pubescent, up to 1.5 cm in length. Buds ellipsoid, hairy at the apex, about 3.5 mm long. Petals 6, subequal. Stamens 13–18 (–41 in Congo), free; filaments white and anthers yellow. Ovary sessile, hairy. Legumes oblong, 3–4 cm in length.

## Type

Mann 715, Ambas Bay (isotype P)

## Discussion

Polymorphic species. Specimens collected at Yangambi in Congo have very hairy buds. The shape of the leaves varies from broadly elliptic to lanceolate; the petioles are long, but sometimes short; they may be thick or slender. Perhaps it will be possible to separate several subspecies when herbarium material becomes more abundant. It remains, however, easy to recognize, even when sterile, by its unifoliolate leaves and the prominent network of its veinlets.

This shrubby species, which does not appear to be very common in the Guineo-Congolian region, has a very vast distribution, since it traverses almost all of Africa from the maritime sectors of Cameroun and Gabon to Uganda and Tanganika (east region of Lake Victoria). Present in the Congo Basin.

## Specimens examined

Aubréville : 180 Mayumba, dans les fourrés littoraux, sur sable. Hallé et A. Le Thomas : 243, bords de l'Ogooué à Booué (fl. juil.). Le Testu : 7947, Lastoursville (fl. mars).
