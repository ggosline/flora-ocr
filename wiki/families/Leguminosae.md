# Leguminosae

**Authority**: Juss.
**Also known as**: Fabaceae (the conserved alternative name)
**Order**: Fabales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Légumineuses*

The most thoroughly treated family in Flore du Gabon, and the only large one
**covered in full** — all three traditional subfamilies, across three volumes and
**48 years**: Caesalpinioideae (1968), Mimosoideae (1989) and Papilionoideae
(2016). Together roughly **138 genera and 476 species**.

It is also the ecologically dominant family of the Guineo-Congolian rainforest.
The genera in the vol 15 treatment — *Gilbertiodendron*, *Julbernardia*,
*Berlinia*, *Microberlinia*, *Brachystegia*, *Monopetalanthus* — include the
canopy trees that structure much of the region's forest, and *Gilbertiodendron*
is the largest genus in that volume with 14 species.

## The three treatments

### Vol 15 (1968) — Caesalpinioideae

**Editor**: N. Hallé · 54 genera, 144 species · `sources/Leguminosae_vol15_paddle`

The rainforest core. Largest genera: *Gilbertiodendron* (14), *Monopetalanthus*
(8), *Dialium* (7), *Cassia* (6), and *Cynometra*, *Guibourtia*, *Didelotia*,
*Berlinia* (5 each). Also holds several monotypic or near-monotypic African
endemics — *Duparquetia*, *Zenkerella*, *Librevillea*, *Stemonocoleus*,
*Augouardia*, *Toubaouate*, *Neochevalierodendron*.

### Vol 31 (1989) — Mimosoideae

**Editor**: J.-F. Floret · 20 genera, 48 species (+4 infraspecific) ·
`sources/Leguminosae_vol31_paddle`

The smallest of the three. *Albizia* (7), *Calpocalyx* (6), *Acacia* (5) and
*Newtonia* (5) lead; *Parkia*, *Pentaclethra*, *Entada* and *Tetrapleura* are all
present. Includes introduced and cultivated genera — *Leucaena*, *Calliandra*,
*Adenanthera*, *Mimosa*.

### Vol 49 (2016) — Papilionoideae

**Editors**: M.S.M. Sosef, J. Florence, L. Ngok Banak, H.P. Bourobou Bourobou &
P. Bissiengou · Margraf Publishers · 67 genera, 284 species ·
`sources/Millettiaspeciesfabaceae_vol49_liteparse`

By far the largest treatment, and the most recent thing in the corpus — a modern
volume from a different publisher and editorial team, nearly half a century after
vol 15. Largest genera: *Millettia* (38), *Dalbergia* (27), *Indigofera* (17),
*Vigna* (16), *Crotalaria* (15), *Baphia* (14), *Desmodium* (13), *Leptoderris*
(11), *Eriosema* (10). Being born-digital, its OCR is markedly cleaner than the
scanned volumes.

***Physostigma venenosum*** is the Calabar bean, source of **physostigmine** — the

## Diagnosis

Not restated here — each volume gives its subfamily's characters rather than a
single family-wide account, a consequence of the treatments being written decades
apart. The traditional separation holds across the three: Caesalpinioideae with
mostly zygomorphic flowers and free stamens; Mimosoideae with actinomorphic
flowers in heads or spikes and exserted stamens; Papilionoideae with papilionate
corollas and diadelphous stamens.

## Notes

**The subfamily framework these volumes use has been superseded.** The 2017
legume classification recognises six subfamilies, and traditional
Caesalpinioideae — the whole of vol 15 — is paraphyletic. Most of its
Guineo-Congolian rainforest genera (*Gilbertiodendron*, *Julbernardia*,
*Berlinia*, *Brachystegia*, *Guibourtia*, *Afzelia*, *Daniellia*, *Copaifera*,
*Detarium*, *Cryptosepalum*, *Didelotia*, *Monopetalanthus*, *Aphanocalyx*,
*Microberlinia*) now sit in **Detarioideae**; *Dialium* in **Dialioideae**;
*Griffonia*, *Gigasiphon* and *Piliostigma* in **Cercidoideae**; and
**Mimosoideae** — the whole of vol 31 — is now a clade *within* a recircumscribed
Caesalpinioideae rather than a subfamily beside it. Only Papilionoideae comes
through essentially intact.

So vol 15's single subfamily is distributed across four of the six modern ones.
The generic treatments remain usable; the subfamily headings do not. This should
be checked against a current source before being relied on for nomenclature.

***Duparquetia* is its own subfamily.** *Duparquetia orchidacea*, one species in
vol 15, is now placed in the monogeneric **Duparquetioideae** — one of the six —
and is endemic to West and Central Africa. A single unremarkable entry in the
1968 treatment turns out to be a lineage of its own.

**Genera treated twice, under different subfamilies.** *Amphimas* and
*Baphiopsis* appear in both vol 15 (as Caesalpinioideae) and vol 49 (as
Papilionoideae) — the 2016 team placing them where the 1968 one did not. Vol 49's
accounts supersede vol 15's for these two. Anyone counting genera across the
volumes should deduplicate them.

**Economically and conservation-significant genera are prominent.** *Dalbergia*
(27 species here) is the rosewood genus, listed on CITES Appendix II; *Millettia*
(38) includes the wenge timbers; *Pterocarpus*, *Afzelia*, *Guibourtia* and
*Erythrophleum* are all major regional timbers. Vol 49 also covers cultivated
food legumes — *Arachis*, *Cajanus*, *Vigna*, *Psophocarpus*, *Sphenostylis*.

**An OCR artefact**: vol 49's source directory is named
`Millettiaspeciesfabaceae_vol49_liteparse`, a mangled family heading from an
earlier split, not a taxonomic statement. The volume is Leguminosae –
Papilionoideae entire.
