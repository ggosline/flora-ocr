## Notes

***Barteria* is one of the best-studied ant-plants in Africa.** *Barteria
fistulosa* and its relatives have **hollow, swollen internodes inhabited by
*Tetraponera* ants**, which patrol the foliage and attack herbivores — and, in
the classic accounts, sting vertebrates with notable ferocity and kill
encroaching vegetation around the host. The association is obligate and the trees
are effectively unarmed without it. Four species here make Lower Guinea the
centre of the genus.

**The family is now more broadly circumscribed.** APG includes **Turneraceae**
and Malesherbiaceae within Passifloraceae, so [[Turneraceae]] — treated
separately in [[vol42]] of this corpus — belongs here on current classification,
as does *Paropsia*, historically shuffled between the two.

*Adenia* is the largest genus, comprising lianas and caudiciform succulents, and
several species are notably toxic. *Passiflora* here is largely introduced or
cultivated — the granadillas and passion fruits are Neotropical.
