## Notes

**This is the only gymnosperm family in the entire corpus.** Every other page in
this wiki treats flowering plants. *Gnetum* is a lianescent gymnosperm with
broad, net-veined, opposite leaves that look thoroughly angiospermous — the
resemblance is convergent, and the Gnetales' relationship to flowering plants was
one of the long-running problems of seed-plant phylogeny before molecular data
placed them within the conifers.

***Gnetum africanum* is one of the most heavily traded non-timber forest products
in Central Africa.** Known as **koko**, **eru**, **okok** or **m'fumbua**, the
leaves are a staple leafy vegetable across Cameroon, Gabon, Congo and Nigeria,
harvested from the wild and traded internationally in quantity. Harvesting
pressure is intense and the vines are slow-growing, so wild populations are
declining and the species is a recurring subject of domestication and
sustainable-harvest research.

Of everything in this corpus, this is the species where a wiki page has the most
immediate practical relevance to livelihoods and conservation.
