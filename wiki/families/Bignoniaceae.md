## Notes

**African Bignoniaceae are trees; Neotropical ones are lianas.** The family's
great radiation is in tropical America, where the tribe Bignonieae comprises
several hundred liana species. Africa has none of that: every genus here is a
tree or shrub, which is why a family of some 800 species worldwide contributes
only 15 to this flora.

***Kigelia africana*** is the sausage tree, unmistakable for its huge pendulous
woody fruits on long cords, bat-pollinated, and widely used medicinally across
the continent.

***Spathodea campanulata***, the African tulip tree, is native here and one of
the **most invasive trees in the world's tropics** — extensively naturalised in
the Pacific, the Caribbean, Asia and Australia after being planted as an
ornamental. It is a case of a Lower Guinea native becoming a serious problem
elsewhere, which is worth noting since the corpus otherwise records invasion in
the opposite direction.

*Newbouldia laevis* is planted throughout West Africa as a living fence and
carries considerable ritual and medicinal significance.
