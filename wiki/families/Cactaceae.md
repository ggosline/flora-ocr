## Notes

***Rhipsalis baccifera* is the only cactus native to the Old World.** Cactaceae
are otherwise an exclusively American family, and this single pendulous epiphyte
— leafless, spineless, with slender cylindrical stems and white berries — occurs
naturally in African and Malagasy forest as well as in the Neotropics.

How it got there is a genuine and unresolved question. The main hypotheses are
long-distance dispersal by migratory birds, which eat the mucilaginous fruits,
and human introduction in relatively recent times; the balance of evidence
favours natural dispersal, but the case is not closed. Either way it is one of
the more striking biogeographic anomalies in the African flora, and it is
recorded here in a Gabonese flora as a native.

The plant grows as an epiphyte in humid forest, a habit shared with a minority of
cacti and quite unlike the desert succulents the family is associated with.
