## Notes

**Malpighiaceae is the classic oil-flower family**, and the African species tell
the more interesting half of the story. Neotropical Malpighiaceae bear paired
**elaiophores** — oil glands on the calyx — and are pollinated by specialist
oil-collecting bees (Centridini and relatives) whose distribution the family's
floral form tracks closely. Those bees do not occur in the Old World, and African
Malpighiaceae have correspondingly **lost or reduced the oil glands**, shifting
to generalist pollination. The family is a textbook case of a floral syndrome
breaking down when its pollinator guild is absent, and the genera here are on the
losing side of that transition.

The distinctive clawed, butterfly-like petals persist even where the glands have
gone, which makes the family recognisable in the field regardless.

*Acridocarpus* is the largest genus here and the most characteristically African
— lianas and shrubs of forest edge and savanna with conspicuous yellow flowers
and samaroid fruits.
