## Notes

***Hypodaphnis zenkeri* is the most significant plant on this page.** It is the
only species of its genus, endemic to West and Central African forest, and
molecular work places it as **sister to all other Lauraceae** — the earliest
diverging lineage in a family of some 3,000 species. It is also the only member
of the family with a truly inferior ovary. A single-line entry in a 1960s flora
turns out to be of considerable phylogenetic importance. (The OCR renders the
name "Hypodaphns"; searches on the source text should allow for that.)

***Cassytha* is a parasite** — a leafless, twining, yellow-stemmed vine
attaching by haustoria, and so similar to *Cuscuta* (Convolvulaceae) in
appearance that the two are routinely confused in the field. It is a striking
outlier in a family otherwise of trees.

*Beilschmiedia*, with 22 of the 32 species, is a difficult genus of forest trees
whose species are largely separated on fruit and bark characters.
