# Marantaceae

**Authority**: R.Br.
**Order**: Zingiberales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Marantacées*

Eleven genera and 34 species. Ecologically far more important in the region than
that count suggests: Marantaceae form the dense understorey thickets — "Marantaceae
forest" — that characterise large areas of Gabonese and Congolese forest and
strongly influence regeneration and large-mammal habitat.

## Notes

***Thaumatococcus daniellii* is the source of thaumatin**, the intensely sweet
protein used as a commercial sweetener and flavour modifier — by weight some
2,000 times sweeter than sucrose, and the sweetest substance known from a plant.
This is the second such protein in the corpus, alongside brazzein from
*Pentadiplandra brazzeana* ([[Pentadiplandraceae]], [[vol20]]); both come from
West/Central African forest plants, which is a striking regional coincidence
worth recording.

**The large-leaved genera are used throughout the region as wrapping leaves**,
particularly *Megaphrynium macrostachyum* and *Marantochloa* — for food packaging,
roofing and thatch. This is one of the most widely used non-timber forest
products in Lower Guinea.

*Maranta* and *Thalia* are Neotropical genera; their presence here is as
introductions or cultivated plants rather than as native elements. *Haumania*,
*Ataenidia*, *Afrocalathea* and *Sarcophrynium* are African endemics.
