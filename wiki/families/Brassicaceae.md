**Authority**: Burnett

## Notes

**Brassicaceae is essentially a temperate family**, and its near-absence from
Lower Guinea is the point of this page. Of some 4,000 species worldwide, seven
reach the region, and most of those are **cultivated or weedy introductions** —
*Brassica* (cabbages, mustards), *Raphanus* (radish) and *Lepidium* are here
through agriculture rather than as native flora. *Cardamine* and *Rorippa* are
the most plausibly native, in damp montane and riverine ground.

**Vol 30 is a compact illustration of the Brassicales split.** Capparaceae s.l.
proved paraphyletic with respect to Brassicaceae, and the modern arrangement is
three families — Capparaceae, **Cleomaceae**, Brassicaceae — with Cleomaceae
sister to Brassicaceae. This volume treats two of the three side by side, and
*Cleome*, now the type of the middle family, sits on the [[Capparidaceae]] page.

All three produce **glucosinolates**, the mustard oils that define Brassicales.
