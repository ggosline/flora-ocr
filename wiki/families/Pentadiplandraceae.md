# Pentadiplandraceae

**Authority**: Hutch. & Dalziel
**Order**: Brassicales (APG IV placement)
**Name in source (FR)**: *Pentadiplandracées*

A **monogeneric, monospecific family endemic to tropical Africa** — the whole
family is [[Pentadiplandra brazzeana]]. The smallest of the five treatments in
[[vol20]].

## Diagnosis

An erect or climbing shrub. Leaves simple, entire, alternate, exstipulate,
pinnately veined. Flowers actinomorphic, hermaphrodite, male and female, in
many-flowered axillary or terminal racemes. Sepals free, imbricate in bud.
Petals free except at the base, which bears a membranous ligule. Stamens 9–13,
free, inserted on an androgynophore; anthers introrse, opening by longitudinal
slits; replaced by staminodes in female flowers. Ovary superior, reduced in male
flowers; 4–5 many-ovuled locules in female flowers. Placentation parietal.
Ovules campylotropous. Fruit a globose berry with several one-seeded locules.
Seeds reniform.

The **androgynophore** and the ligulate petal base are the diagnostic
combination; with parietal placentation and campylotropous ovules they point to
Brassicales, where APG IV places the family — not to any of the Santalales
families it sits beside in this volume.

## Distribution

Endemic to tropical Africa, from Nigeria through Cameroon and Gabon into the
Congo basin.

## Notes

*Pentadiplandra brazzeana* is the source of **brazzein**, an intensely sweet
protein isolated from its fruit, and the plant is known in West and Central
Africa as a sweetener — the 1973 treatment does not mention this, the compound
having been characterised two decades later. Worth recording here because it is
the main reason the species appears in non-floristic literature.
