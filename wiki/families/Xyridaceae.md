# Xyridaceae

**Authority**: C.Agardh
**Order**: Poales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Xyridacées*

One genus, eight species — all *Xyris*, the yellow-eyed grasses.

## Genera in region

| Genus | Species |
|-------|---------|
| [[Xyris]] | 8 |

## Notes

**Xyridaceae share the habitat and the conservation profile of
[[Eriocaulaceae]]** — seasonally wet sand, seepage, marsh margins and the
drainage tracks of inselbergs. The two families grow together throughout the
region, and both are Poales, though not each other's closest relatives.

*Xyris* plants are tufted rosettes with a dense terminal cone of bracts, from
which the three yellow petals emerge for a few hours in the morning. The flowers
are ephemeral, which makes the genus troublesome to collect in good condition and
contributes to persistent taxonomic difficulty.

As with Eriocaulaceae, the habitat is naturally patchy and vulnerable to
drainage alteration and quarrying rather than to logging, and species are likely
under-recorded.
