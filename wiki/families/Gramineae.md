# Gramineae

**Genera in region**: 76 · **Species in region**: 173

## Diagnosis

(63 genera, 140 species)

BY

J. KOECHLIN

Research Fellow at O.R.S.T.O.M.

Assistant Lecturer at the Faculty of Sciences, Paris

(Orsay Section).

Annual or perennial herbs, sometimes woody plants (Bamboos). Stems (culms) generally branched at the base and forming erect or more or less creeping tufts. Culms articulate, generally hollow but closed at the level of the nodes. Leaves alternate, distichous, often grouped in tufts at the base of the culms, composed of a sheath, a ligule and the blade. Sheath open longitudinally; ligule constituted by a membrane or a line of hairs at the junction of the blade and the sheath, rarely absent. Blades entire, generally linear, sometimes oval or cordate at the base.

Inflorescence terminal, formed by spikelets arranged variously, in panicles, in heads, in racemes or in simple or compound spikes.

Spikelet formed by a rachis bearing 3 to several alternate, distichous bracts: the two lower ones (lower glume and upper glume) form an involucre around the spikelet. The two following pieces, the glumelles, surround the flower: lower glumelle, lemma or valve, and upper glumelle, palea or valvule. These two pieces, generally differing from the glumes by their size

---

and their structure, form together with the flower proper a false flower ("floret" of English-language authors) but which, for convenience, will be called a flower in the pages that follow.

These flowers may be hermaphrodite or unisexual, sometimes sterile and then reduced to the glumelles. The sexual organs are still surrounded by a pair of small bracts often fleshy, the lodicules, or glumellules. Apparently there are often three of them because of the division of the lower glumellule into two. In practice, one most often sees two pieces that are arranged between the ovary and the lower glumelle. They are quite often absent.

Stamens hypogynous, generally three, filaments very thin, most often free; anthers with two locules with almost always longitudinal dehiscence. Ovary entire, one-locule, two styles, sometimes 1 or 3, stigmas hairy. One ovule, anatropous. The fruit (caryopsis, grain) has a pericarp adhering to the seed, the latter sometimes free and surrounded by a utricle. At maturity, the caryopsis often detaches from the spikelet with the glumelles to which it adheres.

The spikelets may all be similar, or different by sex and also by structure. When the spikelets are bisexual, the flowers may all be hermaphrodite, or hermaphrodite and male, or female and male. In unisexual spikelets, the plant may be monoecious or dioecious. At maturity, the spikelets may fall entire, singly or grouped, or break above the glumes. At the point of insertion of the glume or lower glumelle on the pedicel or rachis, these pieces often extend into an appendage that ensures their attachment, the callus.
