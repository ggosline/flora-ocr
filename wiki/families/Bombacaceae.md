## Notes

**Bombacaceae is part of Malvaceae s.l.**, as subfamily **Bombacoideae** —
the same merger that absorbed [[Sterculiaceae]] ([[vol02]]) and Tiliaceae. This
corpus therefore holds three separate treatments that a modern account combines
into one family, alongside [[Malvaceae]] s.s. in [[vol45]].

***Adansonia digitata*, the baobab**, is the most recognisable tree in the
family and one of the most culturally significant in Africa — long-lived,
massively pachycaul, and used for fruit, fibre, leaves and water storage. It is a
savanna rather than a forest tree, so its presence in a Gabonese flora reflects
the drier margins of the region.

***Ceiba pentandra*** is the kapok or fromager — a giant buttressed emergent
found across West and Central African forest, and the source of kapok fibre. It
is also one of the very few tree species with a natural transatlantic
distribution, occurring in both African and American tropics.
