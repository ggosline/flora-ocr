# Myristicaceae

**Authority**: R.Br.
**Order**: Magnoliales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Myristicacées*

Four genera and five species by R. Fouilloy — a small treatment of
disproportionate commercial weight. Shares [[vol10]] with [[Lauraceae]] and
[[Monimiaceae]], a volume of aromatic magnoliid trees.

## Notes

**Every genus here is a commercial timber.** *Pycnanthus angolensis* (ilomba) is
among the most widely exported Gabonese woods; ekoune, ossoko and niové are all
established trade timbers. For a family of five species this is an unusually high
proportion, and it makes Myristicaceae one of the corpus's most economically
significant treatments relative to its size.

The family is the **nutmeg family** — *Myristica fragrans*, the source of nutmeg
and mace, is Indonesian and absent here, but the characteristic features are
shared: aromatic, dioecious trees, red or watery sap, and a seed enclosed in a
lacerate **aril** (the mace of commerce).

**A segmentation artefact worth knowing.** The volume prints the vernacular name
as a heading immediately after each species heading, so `## Ilomba`, `## Ossoko`
and `## Ekoune` are picked up by the ingest as if they were genus names, while
the actual genera go unrecognised. The OCR also renders *Coelocaryon* as
"Colecaryon". The table above gives the correct names; a naive index of this
treatment will not.

Older names in the source: *Pycnanthus angolensis* also appears as *Myristica
angolensis*, and *P. microcephalus* as *M. microcephala*.
