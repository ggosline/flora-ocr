# Hymenophyllaceae

**Authority**: Gaudichaud
**Genera in region**: 2 · **Species in region**: 11

## Diagnosis

Terrestrial or epiphytic plants, with thin rhizome, elongately creeping, covered with hairs when young, often rootless. Fronds of very delicate texture, generally small in size, rarely simple or divided, formed of a single row of cells, with free veins, sometimes presenting false veins not united to the true ones. Sori marginal, with elongated receptacle; sporangia borne in basipetal succession. Indusium campanulate or bilabiate, covering the base of the receptacle. Sporangia with oblique ring. Prothallus monoecious, rarely dioecious, filamentous.

Highly specialized hygrophilous plants. Comprising approximately 600 species, mostly tropical, but also found in subtropical and temperate regions of the Southern Hemisphere.

Copeland, who published numerous fascicles concerning this family, divides it into 33 genera which, in our opinion, have only the value of subgenera. We retain here the older classification into 2 genera:
