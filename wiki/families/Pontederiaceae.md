## Notes

***Eichhornia crassipes* is the water hyacinth — among the most damaging
invasive plants in the world**, and the single most consequential species on any
page in this corpus for practical management. Native to the Amazon basin, it was
introduced to Africa as an ornamental and now blankets rivers, lakes and
reservoirs across the continent, blocking navigation and fishing, deoxygenating
water, and displacing native aquatic vegetation. Infestations on the Congo and
Niger systems and on Lake Victoria have required sustained biological control
programmes.

**The name has changed**: molecular work showed *Eichhornia* to be polyphyletic,
and the species is now generally placed in ***Pontederia*** as *Pontederia
crassipes*. Both names are in current use, and the older one dominates the
management and ecological literature.

*Heteranthera* is likewise Neotropical in origin. This is a family present in the
region almost entirely through introduction.
