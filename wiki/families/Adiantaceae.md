# Adiantaceae

**Authority**: Ching
**Genera in region**: 7 · **Species in region**: 13

## Diagnosis

Terrestrial ferns, rarely aquatic (Ceratopteris), with shortly creeping or erect rhizome. Petiole often black, glossy. Fronds simple or deltoid (Doryopteris), or pinnate, not articulated to the rhizome. Veins free or anastomosing, without included veinlets. Sori marginal, without indusium, often protected by a reflexed lobe of the lamina, rounded and terminal on the veins, or elongate along the veins, or continuous along the margins.

This family, rather heterogeneous and grouping genera sometimes united in the ADIANTACEAE and PTERIDACEAE, would be related to the SCHIZAEACEAE. Certain genera are particularly adapted to dry climates.

The name Adiantaceae was employed by Presl (Tent. Pter.: 139, 1836) as a tribe. Lindley, the same year (Nat. Syst. Bot., ed. 2: 401, 1836) made it a "suborder". We unite here, as Alston did, genera that Ching (Sunyatsenia l.c. 1940) placed in 4 different families: Adiantaceae, Gymnogrammaceae, Pteridaceae, Ceratopteridaceae. Pichi-Sermolli assigns to this family even more restricted limits, comprising in it only a single genus: Adiantum; he makes Ceratopteris the family Parkeriaceae, following in this Hooker (Exot. Fl. 2, t. 147, 1825) who made it an order.
