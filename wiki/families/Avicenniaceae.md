## Notes

***Avicennia germinans*, the black mangrove, is one of the two structural
mangrove trees of the West African coast**, with [[Rhizophora]]. It occupies the
landward, more saline and less frequently flooded zone, and is recognisable by
the dense carpets of **pneumatophores** — pencil-like aerial roots projecting
from the mud — and by the salt it excretes onto the upper leaf surface.

**The family is no longer recognised.** *Avicennia* is now placed within
**Acanthaceae**, as subfamily Avicennioideae — a placement that is well supported
but consistently surprising, since [[Acanthaceae]] as treated in [[vol13]] is a
family of forest-floor herbs with nothing of the mangrove about it. The two pages
in this corpus are one family on current classification.

With [[Rhizophoraceae]] and the *Laguncularia*/*Conocarpus* of
[[Combretaceae]], this completes the region's mangrove flora — a habitat under
heavy pressure from clearance, aquaculture and coastal development along the
Atlantic seaboard.
