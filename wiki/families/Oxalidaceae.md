# Oxalidaceae

**Authority**: R.Br.
**Order**: Oxalidales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Oxalidacées*

Two genera, five species — small herbs of damp and disturbed ground.

## Genera in region

| Genus | Species |
|-------|---------|
| [[Biophytum]] | 3 |
| [[Oxalis]] | 2 |

## Notes

***Biophytum* leaves are thigmonastic** — the leaflets fold together within
seconds of being touched, as in *Mimosa pudica*, and also close at nightfall. The
movement is driven by turgor changes in a pulvinus at the leaflet base. It is one
of the few plants in the region with visible, rapid movement, and the two genera
that show it here belong to unrelated families ([[Mimosa]] is in the
[[Leguminosae]] vol 31 treatment), making it a clear case of convergence.

*Oxalis* — the wood sorrels — accumulate oxalic acid, which gives the foliage its
sharp taste and the family its name. Several species in the region are
introduced weeds.

The family sits in **Oxalidales**, alongside [[Connaraceae]] ([[vol33]]), a
placement from molecular data rather than from any obvious shared morphology.
