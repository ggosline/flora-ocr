# Lentibulariaceae

**Authority**: Rich.
**Order**: Lamiales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Lentibulariacées*

Two genera, 17 species — **the corpus's only carnivorous plant family**, of
marsh, seepage and shallow water.

## Genera in region

| Genus | Species |
|-------|---------|
| [[Utricularia]] | 16 |
| [[Genlisea]] | 1 |

## Notes

**Both genera trap animals, by entirely different mechanisms.**

*Utricularia*, the bladderworts, catch prey in **suction bladders**: a sealed
trap held under negative pressure fires when trigger hairs are touched, drawing
in water and prey in well under a millisecond. It is among the fastest movements
known in plants.

*Genlisea*, the corkscrew plants, use **eel traps** — subterranean, forked,
spiral-slit structures that admit soil protozoa and other microfauna but not let
them out. The trapping organs are modified leaves, not roots; *Genlisea* has no
roots at all.

**Both genera are of major interest for genome biology.** *Genlisea* and
*Utricularia* include the **smallest genomes recorded in flowering plants**, an
extreme reduction that has made them models for asking how much non-coding DNA a
plant can dispense with. That an unremarkable single entry in a Gabonese flora
belongs to this group is worth recording.

*Utricularia* with 16 species is well represented in the region's seasonally wet
savannas and inselberg seepages, habitats that are poorly collected and where
further species are likely.
