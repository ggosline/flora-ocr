# Opiliaceae

**Authority**: Valeton
**Order**: Santalales (APG IV placement)
**Name in source (FR)**: *Opiliacées*

A strictly tropical family, well represented in Africa and Asia and sparse in
the Americas. Two genera and six species occur in Lower Guinea — **five in
Gabon, four in Cameroon**. One of five families in [[vol20]].

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or woody lianas. Leaves simple, alternate, exstipulate,
pinnately veined. Flowers hermaphrodite, tetra- or pentamerous, actinomorphic,
pedicellate. Calyx small. Petals united at the base in the open flower, valvate.
Stamens free or fused to the base of the petals, epipetalous and equal to them
in number; anthers introrse, with two pollen sacs opening by longitudinal slits.
Glands alternipetalous, several-lobed or forming a continuous disc. Ovary
superior, one-locular, with a single pendulous or erect ovule. Fruit a drupe,
often soft, one-seeded. Seed with abundant endosperm and a small embryo.

The generic key turns on a single character — the **inflorescence rachis**.
[[Opilia]] has an elongate raceme with an unthickened rachis; [[Rhopalopilia]]
has a short raceme or umbel with a clavate rachis, thickened at the flower
pedicels.

## Distribution

Pantropical, with its centre of diversity in Africa and Asia. In Lower Guinea
the family reaches both Cameroon and Gabon, with Gabon holding one more species.

## Notes

Opiliaceae are root hemiparasites, like most of Santalales — a point the 1973
treatment does not make but which explains the family's placement beside
[[Olacaceae]] and [[Octoknemaceae]] in the same volume. All three are Santalales
in APG IV, so the volume's grouping, made on morphology, has largely held up.
