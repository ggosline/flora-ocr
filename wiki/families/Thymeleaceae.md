## Notes

**Thymelaeaceae have exceptionally tough bark**, the fibres long used for cordage
and bark-cloth across the tropics — the same property that makes the Asian
*Edgeworthia* and *Daphne* paper plants. Many members are also toxic, containing
daphnane and tigliane diterpenes, which are potent skin irritants and among the
more biologically active compounds in the order.

*Octolepis* has sometimes been segregated as Octolepidaceae, but is now kept
within Thymelaeaceae, in subfamily Octolepidoideae. *Synandrodaphne* is a
monotypic West/Central African endemic — the name is misleading, as it has
nothing to do with the lauraceous *Daphne*-like genera it echoes.

*Dicranolepis*, with 7 of the 15 species, is the characteristic forest
understorey genus of the family in the region.
