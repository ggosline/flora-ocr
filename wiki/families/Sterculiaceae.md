# Sterculiaceae

**Authority**: Vent.
**Order**: Malvales
**Modern placement**: **subsumed into Malvaceae *sensu lato***
**Name in source (FR)**: *Sterculiacées*

Eleven genera and 48 species, the first scanned volume in the corpus (1961, ed.
A. Aubréville). *Cola* alone holds 30 of them.

## Notes

**Sterculiaceae no longer exists as a family.** Molecular work established that
the traditional Malvales families — Sterculiaceae, Tiliaceae, Bombacaceae and
Malvaceae s.s. — are not mutually monophyletic, and APG merges all four into a
broadly circumscribed **Malvaceae**. The genera above are distributed among its
subfamilies as tabulated. Sterculioideae and Byttnerioideae are both well
supported; the placement of *Nesogordonia* in Dombeyoideae is the least settled
of these.

Note that [[Bombacaceae]], treated in [[vol22]], goes the same way — both
families in this corpus are now parts of one Malvaceae.

***Tarrietia* is a synonym of *Heritiera***. *Tarrietia utilis* is **niangon**, a
major West African timber, and will be found as *Heritiera utilis* in any modern
source.

**Two of the region's most important timber and crop genera are here.**
*Triplochiton scleroxylon* — **obeche**, also traded as ayous, samba or wawa — is
among the most heavily exploited timbers in West and Central Africa, and this
single-species entry understates its economic weight considerably. *Cola*
contains the kola nuts, *C. nitida* and *C. acuminata*, of major cultural and
commercial importance across West Africa; with 30 species it is the largest
genus in the volume and would repay a genus page of its own.
