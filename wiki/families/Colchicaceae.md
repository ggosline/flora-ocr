## Notes

***Gloriosa superba*, the flame lily, is both spectacular and lethal.** A
climbing herb from a tuber, it ascends by tendrils formed at the leaf tips —
unusual among monocots — and bears large reflexed red-and-yellow flowers.

The whole plant, and especially the tuber, contains **colchicine**, and
poisonings — accidental, medicinal and deliberate — are well documented across
Africa and South Asia. Colchicine is also a pharmaceutical of real importance,
used for gout and familial Mediterranean fever, and a standard laboratory reagent
for inducing polyploidy by disrupting spindle formation. Wild *Gloriosa* is
harvested commercially as a colchicine source, which has put pressure on
populations in parts of its range.

Colchicaceae were formerly included in a broadly circumscribed Liliaceae; the
family is now well established in **Liliales**.
