# Orchidaceae

**Genera in region**: 53 · **Species in region**: 223

## Diagnosis

(52 genera, 222 species in Gabon)

JUSSIEU, Gen. Pl. : 64. (1789) ; LINDLEY, Gen. Sp. Orch. Pl. : 553 pp. (1840) ; SCHLECHTER, Orchideen : 836 pp. (1915) ; DRESSLER, Orchids, Nat. Hist. Classif. : 332 pp. (1981) and Phyl. Classif. Orchid. Fam. : 312 pp. (1993) ; SZLACHETKO, Syst. Orchid. : 152 pp. (1995) ; SZLACHETKO & RUTKOWSKI, Gynost. Orchid. 1 : 380 pp. (2000) ; SZLACHETKO & MARGOŃSKA, Gynost. Orchid. 2 : 275 pp. (2002).

Herbaceous plants terrestrial or epiphytic, sarmentose or bushy, sympodial and monopodial, autotrophic or occasionally saprophytic. Roots grouped at the base of a stem or scattered along a stem or rhizome, with or without velamen. Storage organs of varied forms.

Leaves either folded, or convolute or duplicate or cylindrical, sometimes squamiform, cauline or basal, sessile or petiolate, articulate or not.

Inflorescence terminal or axillary, in a spike, raceme or panicle, 1-n-flowered.

Flowers of varied size and form, resupinate or not. Median sepal of the inner cycle transformed into a labellum usually distinct from the others. Ovary 1(-3)-locular. Gynostemium: often with a basal foot of the column free or fused with the apex of the ovary. Columnar part generally well developed and formed by complete or partial fusion of the staminodes, filament and style, certain groups excepted. Stigma wet, 3-2-1-lobed, often highly modified, concave to convex, entire or split into 2 parts. Median stigmatic lobe transformed into a rostellum, a structure of varied form and size producing a cellular or sometimes semi-fluid viscidium unique but also double in certain genera. The viscidium sometimes emits a unciform structure named hamulus. The tegula arises from the abaxial surface of the rostellum. The unique fertile anther, which corresponds to the median stamen of the external cycle, can be erect, reflected or deflexed, fixed or mobile or even detachable. Connective thick, fleshy or broad, separating or covering the two locules. Pollinium generally compact or sectile, rarely granular, formed of pollen in

The staminodes correspond to the lateral anthers of the internal cycle; rarely free, they are digitiform or aliform and very often fused to the column; their apex often free varies in its form, size and function. – Pl. 1, p. 13.

Fruit capsular, somewhat fleshy. Seeds very fine, adapted to anemochory, exceptionally to zoo- or hydrochory.

EDITORIAL NOTE

It is with regrettable delay compared to the Flora of Cameroon, that the two volumes of Orchidaceae from the Flora of Gabon finally appear, nevertheless written by the same team.

Indeed, after the publication of the third volume of this family for Cameroon in 2001, whose enlarged scope covered a large part of tropical Africa, and thus the majority of Gabon's species, it proved necessary, on the one hand, to restrict this time the subject to species strictly from Gabon (or to be so with near certainty), and, on the other hand, to take account of advances in knowledge in this field. Thus, three taxa absent from the Cameroon edition appear.

In the meantime, the first author, handicapped by health problems, retired leaving his work unfinished.

Nevertheless, the additional delay offered by the resumption of editorial work was used by the authors to correct a number of errors concerning the nature, the location of deposit, indeed even the typification itself, of species types existing simultaneously in Cameroon and Gabon.

These corrections are indicated in this Flora.

Furthermore, the conception of taxonomic limits within the family has clearly evolved between the publication of the two Floras, since the genera Diaphananthe, Chamaeangis and Microcellia, do not belong to the same subtribes according to the works.

It is with satisfaction mixed with regret at not having been able to obtain better iconography (the originals having been lost) that we present one of the most important families of Gabon.

GLOSSARY

Acuminate: with an apex (leaf or floral part) marked by a narrowing and ending in a point.

albumen (m): reserve tissue of many seeds; absent in Orchidaceae.

anther (f): male reproductive organ producing pollen grains. There is only a single fertile anther in ORCHIDACEAE; in the other families of the order ORCHIDALES, there are two (CYPRIPEDIACEAE, APOSTASIACEAE p.p.) or even three (APOSTASIACEAE p.p.).

antherophores (m): strongly elongated basal parts of the anther locules, extended by the caudicles and known only in Habenariinae.

apiculate: provided with a short, sharp and flexible point: the apex of the labellum of certain species bears a short apicule.

auricles (f): appendages of the anthers situated on the back of the base of the locules; these generally prominent structures are formed of swollen cells with raphides inside and they exist only in Orchidoideae.
