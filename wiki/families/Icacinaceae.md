# Icacinaceae

**Authority**: Miers
**Order**: Icacinales (APG IV placement; see Notes — the family as treated here
is now split across four orders)
**Name in source (FR)**: *Icacinacées*

A pantropical family of 58 genera and about 350 species. Twelve genera and 45
species occur in Lower Guinea, and the treatment is explicitly binational:
**12 genera and 37 species in Cameroon, 11 genera and 35 species in Gabon**.
It is the largest of the five families in [[vol20]].

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or lianas, monoecious or dioecious. Leaves alternate — opposite
only in [[Iodes]] — entire or sinuate, petiolate, exstipulate, with palmate or
pinnate venation. Hydathodes present in some genera. Inflorescences various:
racemes, fascicles, panicles, spikes, cymes, glomerules or umbels, borne
axillary, supra-axillary, cauline, terminal or oppositifolious. Flowers
actinomorphic, tri- to penta- or occasionally hexamerous. Sepals present or
absent, alternipetalous. Petals free or united, valvate, glabrous or pubescent
within. Stamens free or fused to the corolla, gynobasic, alternipetalous and
equal in number to the petals, absent from female flowers and sometimes replaced
by staminodes; anthers introrse, 2-locular, opening by longitudinal slits. Ovary
superior, unilocular, with 2 pendulous ovules. Style variable in form. Fruit a
one-seeded drupe, sometimes enclosed by an accrescent organ. Endocarp woody or
crustaceous, ornamented on its inner face in some genera. Embryo small and
apical, or large and occupying the whole length of the seed.

Two characters carry most of the generic key: **habit** (tree or shrub versus
liana), taken first, and then **inflorescence architecture** — umbel, fascicle,
raceme, spike or decussate cyme. Hydathodes and the presence of an accrescent
organ around the drupe separate the liana genera, a parallel to the accrescent
organ that organises [[Olacaceae]] in the same volume.

## Distribution

Pantropical and subtropical. In Lower Guinea the family is well represented in
both Cameroon and Gabon, with Cameroon holding one more genus and two more
species than Gabon. Ranges of individual species extend well beyond the region —
several reach West Africa and the Congo basin, and *Leptaulus holstii* extends
to East Africa.

The first and largest of the five families in the volume, followed by

Villiers wrote all five. The treatment gives counts for Cameroon and Gabon
separately throughout, which is why this page can carry per-country figures that
most Flore du Gabon treatments cannot. 32 figures accompany the treatment.

## Notes

**The family has since been dismembered.** Icacinaceae as circumscribed here is
polyphyletic, and APG IV distributes these genera across several families and
orders: most remain in Icacinaceae s.s. (Icacinales, lamiids), but
[[Lasianthera]] belongs to Stemonuraceae and [[Leptaulus]] to Cardiopteridaceae,
both in Aquifoliales. Anyone using this treatment for a modern determination
should expect the generic key to work while the family boundary does not. These
placements should be checked against a current source before being relied on.

An OCR artefact worth knowing: the scan renders *Rhaphiostylis beninensis* as
"Rhapiostylis", so the genus appears twice in a naive index. It is one genus of
five species.
