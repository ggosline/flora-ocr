# Rubiaceae

**Authority**: Juss.
**Order**: Gentianales (APG IV placement; the source places it in "Rubiales")
**Name in source (FR)**: *Rubiacées*

The largest family treated in Flore du Gabon and one of the largest flowering
plant families anywhere: **about 500 genera and more than 4,500 species**
worldwide, of which 140–150 genera are African. Nicolas and Francis Hallé counted
**75 genera in Gabon**.

**The treatment is incomplete.** Two parts appeared — vol 12 (1966) and vol 17
(1970) — covering 52 genera and 206 species between them, roughly two-thirds of
the Gabonese genera. No further part followed. *Coffea* and *Psychotria*, two of
the largest and most consequential genera in the region, are in neither part.

## Diagnosis

Gamopetalous, with the ovary usually inferior, anthers distinct and leaves
opposite. Within the order, Rubiaceae are distinguished — with some exceptions —
from Valerianaceae and Dipsacaceae by having **equal numbers of petals and
stamens**; from Caprifoliaceae by their **interpetiolar stipules**; and from
Adoxaceae by their stipules together with entire leaves. Flowers in those related
families are often zygomorphic, whereas in Rubiaceae they are actinomorphic with
very few exceptions.

Habit spans the range: large trees, but predominantly small-diameter woody
plants — shrubs, bushes and subshrubs — along with woody lianas and herbs, annual
or perennial, erect, creeping or scrambling.

## Vegetative architecture

The general-characters chapter devotes a full illustrated section (Pl. 1) to
vegetative architecture, which is unusual for a flora of this period and worth
noting on its own. It analyses the epicotyl trunk or **orthotropic axis** —
rarely solitary, as in *Bertiera simplicicaulis*; more often bearing lateral
branches; sometimes lianescent, sometimes developing into a substantial bole
(*Brenania*, *Nauclea*, *Mitragyna*, *Tricalysia macrophylla*) — and the
**plagiotropic** secondary branches with their characteristic horizontal growth.
Cauliflory, with inflorescences borne below the leafy crown, is treated as part
of the same architectural scheme.

Francis Hallé, co-author of this chapter, went on to become the principal figure
in plant architectural analysis; this 1966 treatment predates the standard work
on architectural models. Whether it should be read as an early statement of that
programme is a question for someone with the bibliography to hand — the
connection is noted here, not asserted.

## Distribution

Pantropical, extending into temperate regions. Rubiaceae and Loganiaceae are the
only essentially tropical families of the order as the source circumscribes it,
the remainder being temperate specialists. In Lower Guinea the family is
ubiquitous, from the herbaceous *Oldenlandia* and *Kohautia* of drier ground to
forest trees and canopy lianas.

Gardenieae focus.

## Notes

**Economically significant genera are well represented.** *Pausinystalia* (the
source of yohimbe), *Mitragyna*, *Nauclea* and *Corynanthe* are all timber or
medicinal genera of regional importance, and *Gardenia* and *Rothmannia* are
widely grown ornamentals. The absence of *Coffea* from both parts is the most
consequential gap in the treatment.

**OCR artefacts in these two volumes.** The scans are noisier than most, and
several genus names are corrupted in the raw text: *Virectaria* appears as
"Vrectaria", *Hymenodictyon* as "Hymenodietyon", *Pausinystalia* as
"Pausinstalia", *Dictyandra* as "Dietyandra", *Tarenna* as "Tarena", and
*Stelechantha* as "Stelecantha"/"Telecantha". A "Materiel" heading is also picked
up as a genus. Names are given corrected above; anyone grepping the source text
should search for both forms.

Species totals for a few genera could not be read reliably from the segmented
text and are left blank rather than guessed.
