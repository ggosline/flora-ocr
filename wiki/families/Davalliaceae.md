# Davalliaceae

**Authority**: Gaud. emend. Alst
**Genera in region**: 5 · **Species in region**: 7

## Diagnosis

Epiphytes, rarely terrestrial, with long-creeping rhizome, with peltate scales, more rarely erect (Nephrolepis). Petiole articulate, except in Nephrolepis and Arthropteris where the pinnae are articulate. Frond simple, or pinnate, or multipinnate. Segments with unequal bases. Veins free. Sori generally terminal on the veins (except in Oleandra), with a basal indusium, opening outward. Sporangia with 12-14 cells in the annulus. Spores monolete, without perispore.

Approximately 200 species, chiefly from Asia and Malesia.

Family rather heterogeneous, the degree of frond dissection and the form of the indusium being quite variable. It shows affinities with the LINDSAEACEAE.

1. Frond entire, sori situated near the costa..... 1. Oleandra.

1'. Frond pinnate or multipinnate.

2. Pinnae articulate to the rachis.

3. Rhizome erect, petiole not articulate; frond pinnate, with dimidiate pinnae, auriculate at the upper base..... 2. Nephrolepis.

3'. Rhizome long-creeping, petiole articulate, frond bipinnatifid..... 3. Arthropteris.

2'. Pinnae not articulate to the rachis; frond triangular.

4. Indusium attached by the base and sides..... 4. Davallia.

4'. Indusium attached by the base only..... 5. Humata.
