# Podostemaceae

**Authority**: Rich. ex Kunth
**Order**: Malpighiales
**Name in source (FR)**: *Podostémacées*

Four genera and 19 species — **the most morphologically extraordinary family in
the corpus**, and the one most acutely threatened.

## Notes

**Podostemaceae are rheophytes that grow only on rock in fast water** —
rapids, cataracts and waterfall splash zones. They are submerged and vegetative
through the high-water season and flower as the water falls, and their body plan
is so reduced and modified that the distinction between root, stem and leaf
largely breaks down; the plant body is often a thallus-like crust adhering to
rock by haptera. Nineteenth-century botanists compared them to algae and
liverworts, and their placement in **Malpighiales**, close to Hypericaceae and
Clusiaceae, was only settled molecularly.

**They are the family in this flora most directly threatened by hydropower.**
Species are frequently restricted to a single rapid or falls system, so a dam or
a flow alteration can extinguish an entire species. *Ledermanniella* and
*Inversodicraea* in particular contain narrow endemics of Lower Guinea rivers.
Any conservation assessment drawn from this wiki should treat these 19 species as
the highest-priority group in the corpus.

Their extreme reduction also makes them hard to collect and harder to identify,
so regional species counts are likely underestimates.
