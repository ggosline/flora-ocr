## Notes

**Polygalaceae flowers mimic legume flowers** — two enlarged, petaloid inner
sepals form what look like wings, and the lower petal is keeled and often
crested, producing a strongly papilionoid appearance. The resemblance is
convergent but it is not coincidental in placement: molecular data put
Polygalaceae in **Fabales**, alongside [[Leguminosae]], so the two families are
genuinely related even though the floral similarity evolved independently.

***Carpolobia lutea* and relatives are widely traded** in West and Central Africa
as chewing-sticks and for their roots' medicinal use; the stems are also cut as
walking sticks, and harvesting pressure on wild populations is significant.

*Polygala* is a very large genus worldwide and the species here are small
savanna and grassland herbs, easily overlooked.
