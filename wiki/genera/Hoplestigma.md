# *Hoplestigma* Pierre

**Family**: [[Boraginaceae]]
**Authority**: Pierre
**Protologue**: Bull. Mens. Soc. Linn. Paris, sér. 2, 1: 116 (1899)

## Diagnosis

Trees pubescent or glabrous. Leaves alternate, petiolate; lamina obovate, with cuneate base and apiculate apex, entire margin, ± hairy on the lower surface. Inflorescences terminal, in dichasial panicles, with subscorpioid cymes; bracts absent. Flowers bisexual; calyx with (2–3 or 4 asymmetric, ovoid, non-ridged, pubescent lobes; corolla with tube reaching the $ \frac{1}{3} $ or the $ \frac{1}{2} $ of the total length of the corolla, with 11 to 14 imbricate, obovate lobes, rounded at apex, white; stamens 20 to 35 in ± 3 irregular rows, inserted in the tube, anther dorsifixed, thick, elongate, 4-locular; ovary ovoid, with 2 carpels, with very thick wall and a single locule, with 4 anatropous ovules (horizontal); style with 2 long branches, stigma thick, irregularly curved in bud. Fruits: drupes slightly flattened laterally, fairly large, with thick and bony endocarp, entire, containing 4 central seeds and two sterile lateral locules; calyx persistent, accrescent and becoming coriaceous.

Genus with two or three species (see Notes) in West Africa (Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana) and Cameroon, in Equatorial Guinea and Gabon. For Gabon, one species.

Notes: Hoplestigma was first placed in Flacourtiaceae by Pierre (1899), then in its own family Hoplestigmataceae by Gilg (1908). The latter was placed successively in Ebenales, Bixales and finally Violales by Cronquist. While Göttschling et al. (2016) place the genus in Ehretiaceae and Luebert et al. (2016) place it in Cordiaceae, the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group, (2016), or APG IV, no longer recognizes these families and places the genus in Boraginaceae sensu lato.

H. pierreanum Gilg is known from southern Cameroon, the type specimen was collected at Bipindi; the species included in the key below should therefore be sought in Gabon. According to Gilg (1908), the differences with H. klaineanum are: larger fruits, larger leaves more densely furnished with softer hairs, whereas H. klaineanum has more rigid appressed sparse hairs. Heine (1963) adds two other characters to this: longer petiole and more numerous lateral veins. I examined specimens of these two species from Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire. I saw no differences between these two species with respect to the size of the lamina, petiole and number of veins, nor the size of the fruits. Only pubescence remains, and one may wonder whether the two species should be recognized as distinct.
