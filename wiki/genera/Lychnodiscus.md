# *Lychnodiscus* Radlkofer

**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Authority**: Radlkofer
**Protologue**: Sitzungsber. Bayer. Akad. Wiss. München 8 : 271 (1878); 9 : 605 (1879); 20 : 244, 285 (1890); in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 1137 (1933)

## Diagnosis

Trees or shrubs with branched habit; branchlets tomentulose or ferruginous pubescent; leaves 3-8-jugate; leaflets entire or dentate, often glandulous beneath.

Inflorescences axillary or terminal, superior to or equal to the leaves, in simple or branched racemes of scorpioid cymes, 4-7 or multi-flowered; cymules sessile or briefly stipitate with bracts sometimes inserted several millimetres below the cymule (L. grandifolius). Flowers regular; buds 4-8 mm in diameter, tomentulose; 5 sepals subvalvate, separated over 3/4 or 4/5 of the calyx height, glabrous within; corolla with 5 petals funnel-shaped over 1/4 or 1/2 of their length; scale hairy; double disc, the inner cup taller than the outer, resembling the shape of an oil lamp (Greek: lychnos = lamp); androecium of 10-18 stamens, filament rarely hairy, somewhat geniculate in the bud; anthers elongate, with emarginate base; pistil 3 (-4)-locular, hairy, with style equal to or shorter than the ovary. Capsule with 3-(4) locules, loculicidal, tomentulose then glabrescent externally, glabrous and coloured red internally, 2-3 cm in diameter, containing 3 seeds or fewer by abortion. Seeds ellipsoid, completely covered with an orange or red fleshy envelope, drupaceous testa (PELLEGRIN), aril (RADLKOFER, HUTCHINSON & DALZIEL) or thick tegument, waxy simulating an aril (HAUMAN); the latter opinion seems best; cotyledons superposed, amylaceous; radicle punctiform dorsal, opposite to the hilum.

TYPE SPECIES: Lychnodiscus reticulatus Radlkofer.

---

RADLKOFER described 6 species growing from Cameroon to Zaïre; AUBRÉ-VILLE & PELLEGRIN added L. dananensis from Côte d'Ivoire. Some were reduced to the rank of varieties by HAUMAN. On the other hand, var. brevibracteatus Pellegrin seems worthy of being elevated to the rank of species. It may be noted that RADLKOFER presents hairy filaments as a generic character, this arising from the examination of the first described species L. reticulatus; however it turns out that all other Lychnodiscus have glabrous filaments, except the type of L. grandifolius (Zenker 3524), whose filaments are puberulent.
