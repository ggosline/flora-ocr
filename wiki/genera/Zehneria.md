# *Zehneria* Endl.

**Family**: [[Cucurbitaceae]]
**Authority**: Endl.
**Protologue**: Prodr. fl. Norfolk.: 69 (1833)

## Diagnosis

Herbs annual or perennial, monoecious or dioecious, climbing or prostrate. Tendrils simple. Leaves petiolate; blade entire to palmatilobate, ovate-cordate to triangular or hastate. Inflorescences: the male ones in ± umbelliform racemes, sessile or pedunculate, sometimes with solitary or fasciculate flowers; the female ones with solitary flowers or in pauciflorous racemes, often axillated by a male flower. Flowers relatively small. Male flowers pedicellate; receptacle campanulate to cupuliform; sepals 5, dentiform; petals 5, slightly united at the base, white; stamens 3 or sometimes 2 or 5, inserted on the lower part of the receptacle, with filament short, anthers all bithecous or monothecous, with straight locules, duplicated or triplicated. Female flowers: perianth similar to that of male flowers; staminodes 3; ovary globose to ellipsoidal or fusiform, smooth, containing numerous horizontal ovules; style short, with 2 or 3 stigmas. Fruits solitary or in tufts, subsessile or pedicellate, bacciform, globose, ellipsoidal or fusiform, indehiscent, green, white or red at maturity. Seeds small, lenticular, sometimes marginate.

Genus comprising approximately 70 species in Africa and Madagascar, in Asia, in Australia and in the Pacific, of which about thirty in Africa and Madagascar and five in Gabon.

Notes: The genera Anangia, Neoachmandra and Pilogyne are all part of Zehneria on a morphological and molecular basis. The taxonomy of the group is detailed in de Boer et al. (2015) and Dwivedi et al. (2018).

BIBLIOGRAPHY: de Boer et al. (2015), de Wilde & Duyfjes (2010), Dwivedi et al. (2018), Jeffrey (1967), Keraudren (1967), Keraudren-Aymonin (1975), Schaefer & Renner (2011a, 2011b).
