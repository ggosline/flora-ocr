Trees or shrubs terrestrial or semi-epiphytic ("stranglers"), sometimes lianescent or with lianescent roots; aerial roots present or absent. Leaves arranged in spirals or in some cases distichous, subalternate or subverticillate. Blade smooth or scabrous above, margin entire, (crenate-)dentate to lobed; secondary veins pinnate and brochidodromous, the basal pair often prominent; tertiary veins reticulate to very parallel to the preceding ones or ± parallel to each other and across the internervular field; on the lower surface, either several glandular (waxy) points at the axil of the secondary veins (or of the basal ones only), or 1 (-2) at the base of the midvein. Stipules wholly or semi-amplexicaul with free margins or fused at the base, caducous or persistent.

Inflorescences (syconia, figs) solitary or grouped in pairs at the leaf axils or just below, or borne on sorts of ± developed spurs, far removed from the leaves on older branches or even on the trunk; inflorescences are sometimes found on ± branched aphyllous branches borne by the main branches and/or by the trunk. The figs are either sessile or pedunculate, presenting themselves as a urceolate receptacle with 2 or 3 verticillate bracts at the base ("basal bracts") or 1-5 bracts spaced toward the top of the peduncle; in sect. Sycidium, the external surface of the "receptacle" itself bears some bracts. The apical opening of the fig, the ostiole, may be circular and obstructed by 3 visible bracts at least, followed by "ostiolar bracts" nested within each other and increasingly oriented downward; the ostiole may

---

also form 2 lips or simply a small slit and in this case, all the ostiolar bracts are descending and invisible. Within the cavity of the fig are usually found "interfloral bracts" and most often staminate flowers and pistillate flowers, some of which produce seeds (seed flowers) and others galls in which fig wasp larvae incubate (pollinating agents): these are gall flowers. Sometimes (in sect. *Sycidium*) the receptacle contains either staminate flowers accompanied by gall flowers or only seed flowers. The staminate flowers may be grouped near the ostiole or dispersed over the entire internal surface of the receptacle; they are pedicellate or sessile with 2-5 (-7) free or ± connate tepals and 1, 2, or 3 stamens. The seed flowers and gall flowers are ± difficult to distinguish from each other: the seed flowers are most often sessile or with a relatively short pedicel whereas the gall flowers are usually more longly pedicellate; the perianth has 2-5 free or ± connate tepals and the style of variable length is relatively developed in seed flowers; the stigma, most often unique, sometimes 2, is linear, sub-penicillate or pore-shaped; when there are two, they often constitute a synstigma. The fruits, which contain the seeds, differ greatly in size and shape from gall fruits which are often stipitate; the seed fruits have 1 or 2 colors and in this case the lower part is brown (dark brown) and the upper part yellowish; the outer layer of the latter is (perhaps?) mucilaginous. The very thin testa contains an albumen, a small embryo straight slightly curved with flat cotyledons.

                        - **23′.** Ficus L. subg. Sycidium (Miq.) MILDBR. & BURRET, Bot. Jahrb. 46: 174 (1911). Shrubs to small trees, sometimes climbing. Leaves distichous, or subopposite to subwhorled. Limb usually rugulose above, margin dentate to lobed; basal pair of lateral veins distinctly separate, tertiary venation parallel across the internervural surface, to reticulate; glandular (waxy) patches at the axil of the lateral (basal) veins. Stipules free, semi-amplexicaul. Figs in pairs in the leaf axils or below the leaves, and then more often clustered on short spurs, pedunculate; bracts mostly scattered on the peduncle, some bracts on the external surface of the receptacle, ostiole closed by a few bracts, upper ostiolar bracts spreading and overlapping; lower ones descending, interflorals bracts few or absent (?), receptacle containing each both staminate and gall flowers or only seed flowers (the two fig types probably on separate plants). Staminate flowers grouped near the ostiole; stamens 1 or sometimes 2 or 3, pistillode often present. Seed and gall flowers rather similar, except for the different length of the style; perianth with 4–6 free or basally connate tepals; stigma 1, with an apical pore; stigmas free. Fruits one-coloured, usually pale yellow, those containing seeds ± drupaceous with a crustaceous endocarp, sometimes reddish. Pedicels and tepals with normally short hairs on the margins eventually. Pollinators: species of Kradibia. 4 species probably in continental Africa, mostly in savanna zones, but also in humid dense forest areas; often along watercourses. In Gabon: F. exasperata and F. asperifolia. The species of section Sycidium are morphologically so similar that it is sometimes very difficult to distinguish them. The species found in Gabon appear to be rather distinctly ecological. F. asperifolia with a shrubby habit, is normally encountered along watercourses in forested zones. F. exasperata, with a tree-like habit, is not associated with riverbanks. Another species in this section is F. pygmaea Welw., known from Angola and South Africa. It is characterized by its shrubby habit, small leaves with a coarsely crenate to lobed margin and a very short petiole.
