# *Capsicum* L.

**Family**: [[Solanaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 1 : 188 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Annual or perennial herbs, or small shrubs sometimes climbing, unarmed; stems sometimes angular and furrowed, pubescent or glabrescent with simple or sometimes branched hairs, glandular or not. Leaves alternate or paired toward the apex, simple, petiolate; blade with entire margin. Inflorescences axillary or terminal, in fascicles of 2 to 8 flowers or with solitary flowers. Flowers bisexual, 5-merous, actinomorphic; pedicel erect or recurved downward; calyx cupuliform to campanulate, truncate or with 5(–10) small lobes, sometimes accrescent on the fruit; corolla rotate to stellate, with short tube and deeply lobed, with valvate prefloration; stamens equal, inserted at the base of the tube, base of filament with 2 small lateral appendices, anthers basifixed, connate, oblongoid, dehiscing by longitudinal slits; disk small and annular or absent; ovary 2- or 3-locular, glabrous; style filiform, glabrous, with capitate stigma, sometimes bilobed. Fruits: weakly fleshy berries, erect to pendulous, with numerous seeds. Seeds flattened, reniform to ovoid; embryo curved.

Genus of approximately 20 species, originally distributed from the southern United States to central Argentina. Five of the species have been domesticated and have been cultivated for a long time in South America; two of them, C. annuum L. and C. frutescens, are widely cultivated throughout the world; in Gabon, only C. frutescens is reported as escaped from cultivation; only this species is therefore treated below.
