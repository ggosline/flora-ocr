# *Calanthe* R.Br.

**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Authority**: R.Br.
**Protologue**: Bot. Reg. 7 : tab. 573 (1821)

## Diagnosis

Terrestrial herb, large or medium-sized, sometimes epiphytic. Pseudobulbs conical, cylindrical or very weakly marked, 1–n nodes. Leaves at the apex of pseudobulbs, plicate, thin, petiolate, generally very large, deciduous or persistent. Inflorescence basal, erect or arched in its apical half, dense or lax. Flowers numerous, often showy, fairly large. Sepals and petals subsimilar. Labellum generally deeply trilobed, rarely entire, fused to the base of the gynostemium, furnished with a central verrulose or cristate callus and a spur. Gynostemium short, massive, erect. Pollinia 8, claviform, unequal in shape and size, each with apical sticky caudicles. Rostellum ligulate, suberect, producing a single viscidium. – Pl. 50, p. 107.

The floral structure with a particularly long and narrow spur and a colour generally white or red indicates that these species of Calanthe are pollinated by flies or butterflies.

An important genus of approximately 200 species widely represented in the tropics and northward to Japan, the majority living in Australasia and Madagascar. Africa and America each have only one species.
