# *Citrus* L.

**Family**: [[Rutaceae]]
**Authority**: L.

## Diagnosis

Among the family Rutaceae a special place may be reserved for the genus Citrus, whose species, originating for the most part from tropical and subtropical South-East Asia, have been widely introduced, multiplied, transformed by cultivation, hybridizations and mutations in all tropical and warm temperate regions of the globe; the ensemble of these species is designated by the general term Citrus fruits.

The genus Citrus groups together small trees and shrubs with young angular branches becoming cylindrical later; simple spines garnish the leaf axils but the older branches are often unarmed; the alternate leaves are unifoliolate, ordinarily thin, not coriaceous, with some lateral veins, without a well-marked network of veinlets but they are studded with translucent glandular points; the petioles are generally ± winged and articulate with the limb, the latter is ± denticulate. The flowers are, at the leaf axils, solitary or arranged in short corymbiform racemes; they are hermaphrodite or ♂ by ± pronounced abortion of the pistil; calyx cup-shaped 4–5 lobed; petals 4–8 (ordinarily 5), imbricate in the floral bud, thick, linear, strongly furnished with translucent glandular points, most often white at anthesis; stamens generally 4 times as numerous as the petals but for several species this proportion rises to 6–10 times, filaments lanceolate, free or ± connate, anthers sagittate, rather introrse; annular disk, thick; ovary subglobose and clearly distinct from the narrower style, or ovary truncate, fusiform, or subcylindric and prolonging gradually in a style almost as wide as the upper portion of the ovary; 8–18 locules (ordinarily 10–14), with ovules, whose number rises to 4–8 and more per locule, arranged in 2 collateral rows; style cylindric, enlarging abruptly into a subglobose or flattened spheroid stigma. Berry-like fruit, globose or ellipsoid, multilocular ("hesperidium") whose "segments" contain seeds or "pips" near their inner angle while the rest of the space is filled with stipitate fusiform vesicles, formed by very aqueous pulpy tissue with large cells; around the "segments" is found a white mesocarp, not very thick, itself surrounded by a coriaceous exocarp ("zest") furnished with very numerous glands containing essential oil; the external surface of the fruit turns yellow or orange at complete maturity; the seeds are obovoid or flattened, or angular and contain one or several white or green embryos.
