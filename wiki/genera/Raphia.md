# *Raphia* P.Beauv.

**Family**: [[Arecaceae]]
**Authority**: P.Beauv.
**Protologue**: Fl. Oware 1 : 75. (1806)

## Diagnosis

(by: Suzanne MOGUE KAMKA, Thomas L.P. COUVREUR and Terry C.H. SUNDERLAND)

Fl. Oware 1 : 75. (1806).

Palms with solitary or caespitose stems, sometimes acaulous, spiny, monocarpic, monoecious; stem generally covered with the remains of leaf sheaths and straight or curved fibres. Leaves very large, pinnate; sheath lacking spines, tubular, splitting opposite the petiole and disintegrating into fibres; petiole short or long, lacking spines, concave above, rounded below; rachis margined with spines or not; blade with numerous leaflets, linear, arranged in several directions giving a feathery aspect, margins and central nerve armed with spines. Inflorescences interfoliar, pendulous, semi-erect or erect, twice-branched; peduncle short, with a tubular prophyll and ± 6 bracts; rachis longer than the peduncle; flowering branches numerous, slender to robust, with male flowers present on the apical ¾, female ones at the base. Male flowers: calyx tubular, slightly 3-lobed; corolla much longer than the calyx, tubular at the base, 3-lobed; stamens 6 to 30, filaments adnate to the corolla; pistillode absent or minute. Female flowers: calyx tubular, slightly 3-lobed; corolla longer than the calyx, tubular for half its length, 3-lobed; staminodes 6 to 16, united in an epipetalous ring; gynoecium 3-locular, ovoid or conical, covered with scales, with 1(–3) basal ovule per locule; style short, stigma 3-lobed. Fruits spherical to ellipsoid or ovoid, apex with stigmatic remains, covered with scales in longitudinal rows, seeds 1(–3); endocarp thick, oleaginous; endocarp not differentiated. Seed with furrowed surface; albumen ruminate.

The genus Raphia is the most diverse in continental Africa, with about twenty species known to date (Tuley 1995; Stauffer et al. 2014), one of which is present in South America (R. taegidera (Mart.) Mart). The taxonomy of the genus is complicated (Stauffer et al. 2017, Mogue et al. 2018), and until recently, only two species were recorded in Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006). In this flora we describe eight species, two of which were recently described (Mogue et al. 2018). The descriptions for certain species remain imperfect, but will suffice for the identification of specimens.
