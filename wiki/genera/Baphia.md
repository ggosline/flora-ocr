# *Baphia* Afzel. ex Lodd.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] (Papilionoideae)
**Authority**: Afzel. ex Lodd. — Bot. Cab. 4: t. 367 (1820)
**Treatment by**: Frans J. Breteler

Fourteen species.

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or lianas. Stipules early caducous. **Leaves unifoliolate** — the
most immediately useful character, unusual in a family of pinnate leaves — with
the petiole bearing two pulvini, united or separate; stipels absent.
Inflorescences axillary or (sub)terminal, in racemes, panicles, fascicles or
rarely cymes, sometimes the flowers solitary; bracts ± early caducous. Flowers
with the pedicel bearing bracteoles inserted against or well below the calyx and
sometimes alternate; **calyx ovoid or ellipsoid, often ± falcate, opening by one
or two longitudinal splits**; corolla white, the standard suborbicular to
reniform **with a yellow blotch at the base**, the wings obliquely oblong or
obovate.

The spathaceous calyx splitting longitudinally, together with unifoliolate leaves
and a yellow-blotched white standard, identifies the genus readily.

## Notes

***Baphia nitida* is camwood**, the source of a red dye long traded from West
Africa — the genus name is from the Greek *baphe*, dye. The heartwood yields
santalin-type pigments used for textiles, cosmetics and body decoration, and
camwood was an article of Atlantic commerce before synthetic dyes displaced it.
It remains in local use.

The genus's affinities are with the Baphieae, an African-centred tribe of
Papilionoideae whose unifoliolate leaves and spathaceous calyx set them apart
from the rest of the subfamily.
