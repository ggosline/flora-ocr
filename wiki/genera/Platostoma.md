# *Platostoma* P.Beauv.

**Authority**: P.Beauv.
**Protologue**: Fl. Oware 2 : 61, t. 95 (1818)

## Diagnosis

(by Benedict John POLLARD)

Fl. Oware 2 : 61, t. 95 (1818).

Annual or perennial herbs, non-aromatic or slightly aromatic. Leaves simple, petiolate; blade of upper leaves generally pale or coloured at the base. Inflorescences terminal, lax or condensed, with flowers arranged in verticillasters, generally with more than 6 flowers; bracts generally coloured at the base; bracteoles absent. Flowers pedicellate; calyx bilabiate, funnel-shaped or rounded tubular in cross-section, upper lip with 1 or 3 lobes of which the median is decurrent or not, lower lip with 1 to 5 lobes, lateral lobes either midway between the lower and upper lips or closer to the upper lip, but not in the same plane, throat glabrous; corolla bilabiate, short, dorsally gibbous, slightly curved downward, with dilated throat, upper lip erect, 3- or 4-lobed, median lobes slightly narrower, lower lip ± linear, slightly cucullate, horizontal, becoming deflexed but not thickened and articulate at the base, not folding back at anthesis; stamens 4, declined, slightly exsert, upper filaments pubescent, swollen at the base, inserted below the midpoint of the tube, lower filaments inserted above the midpoint of the tube, anthers globose, dorsifixed, with equal thecae; disc 4-lobed, with the lower lobe slightly larger; ovary deeply 4-lobed; style gynobasic, apex bifid. Fruits: 4 achenes, black or dark brown, with minute reticulate ridges and a small white basal scar, not producing mucilage when wet.

Genus of 45 species, present in the tropics of the Old World. The genus is most diverse in Asia. Three species occur in Gabon.

Notes: Platostoma is frequently confused with Ocimum, but differs by verticillasters with more than 6 flowers, bracts coloured at the base, and finely reticulate achenes.

Although the fruits are characteristic of this genus, they do not have diagnostic characters between the species.

B: Paton (1997b), Paton et al. (2009, 2013).
