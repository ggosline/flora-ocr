# *Xyris* L.

**Family**: [[Xyridaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 1 : 42 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Herbs annual or perennial, often in tufts; rhizomes (if present) short, horizontal or ascending. Leaves distichous, few to numerous, almost all basal; blade ribbon-like or cylindrical; ligule present or absent. Inflorescence: short spike, with flowers in the axil of dense and often coriaceous bracts; peduncle elongate and often provided with longitudinal furrows; basal bracts of the inflorescence sterile, sometimes elongate or reflexed. Flowers solitary in the axil of fertile bracts, wilting rapidly; sepals unequal, free (sometimes fused at the base in some American species), persistent; corolla with three broad and spreading lobes, often yellow; staminodes normally divided at the apex and furnished with numerous moniliform hairs; ovary (1–)3-locular, with parietal placentation (in Africa); style sometimes divided into three at the apex. Fruits loculicidal. Seeds often longitudinally striated.

Genus comprising approximately 300 species, of which about sixty are restricted to tropical and subtropical Africa, from Senegal to South Africa; nine species in Gabon.

Taxonomic notes: The treatment of the family for Cameroon (Lewis, 1981) shows interesting differences between the species found in Cameroon and Gabon. Cameroon has montane species, such as X. obscura N.E.Br. and also a taxon misidentified by Lewis as X. welwitschii Rendle (perhaps a new species, see Lock, 1998). Gabon has some species from the south, such as X. imitatrix

– Xyris straminea L.A.Nilsson is a small annual plant with a narrowly elliptic inflorescence, pale yellow-brown. The blade is weakly wrinkled towards the base. This species grows on rocks with shallow soil. It is widespread in regions of Africa with marked seasons.

– Xyris aristata N.E.Br. is a perennial plant in tufts. It possesses dark brown bracts. The lower bracts are long mucronate or long acuminate and much longer than the upper ones.
