# *Manilkara* Adans.

**Authority**: Adans., *Fam. pl.* **2**: 166 (1763), *nom. cons.*  
**Family**: [[Sapotaceae]]

## Diagnosis

The genus is immediately recognisable in the Gabon treatment by its floral
architecture: a double calyx of **3 + 3 sepals**, a corolla of **6 petals**
each bearing **2 dorsal appendages**, **6 stamens**, and **6 staminodes**.
The ovary usually has about **12 locules**, more rarely about **6** as in
[[Manilkara_le_testui]]. Fruits, when known, are small ellipsoid berries with
1-2 seeds; the seeds are marked by a narrow basiventral scar.

The treatment notes that African humid-forest *Manilkara* can be grouped by
leaf architecture into species with many fine parallel lateral veins and those
with larger blades and more spaced venation. It also stresses that pedicel
form and floral details are often more reliable than foliage alone.

The species of the group with numerous, fine, parallel lateral venation, not very visible, whose lower surface of the limb is grayish-white, the remnant of tomentum from the juvenile form worn away in the adult form, pose very delicate problems of specific discrimination whose solutions are never entirely satisfactory. With at least provisional exception of Manilkara Pellegriniana, a poorly known species whose ecology is unknown to us, the other species of this group are an example of those sequences of ecophyletic species that are frequent in tropical African flora. These are plant forms which by the morphology of the leaf, flower and fruit are separated only by secondary characters, yet specific ones; but there exist moreover between them intermediate types. Despite the latter, the characteristic types of each of these species are distinctly distinct. Moreover, from the point of view of the plant habit and their ecology, the distinctions are evident. There is a correlation between habit and ecology on one hand and foliar and floral morphology on the other. In the case of this group of three Manilkara, M. lacera, M. multinervis and M. ## Treatments

### Vol 1 (1961)

**Author**: A. Aubreville  
**Pages**: 27-40  
**Source**: `sources/Sapotaceae_vol01_paddle`

This opening genus block of the Sapotaceae monograph mixes clear floral
diagnosis with repeated warnings that foliage alone can be deceptive. Several
species are still imperfectly known, and the author explicitly treats the
coastal and inland forms around [[Manilkara_lacera]] as a difficult continuum.
