# *Manilkara* Adans.

**Authority**: Adans., *Fam. pl.* **2**: 166 (1763), *nom. cons.*  
**Family**: [[Sapotaceae]]

## Diagnosis

The genus is immediately recognisable in the Gabon treatment by its floral
architecture: a double calyx of **3 + 3 sepals**, a corolla of **6 petals**
each bearing **2 dorsal appendages**, **6 stamens**, and **6 staminodes**.
The ovary usually has about **12 locules**, more rarely about **6** as in
[[Manilkara_le_testui]]. Fruits, when known, are small ellipsoid berries with
1-2 seeds; the seeds are marked by a narrow basiventral scar.

The treatment notes that African humid-forest *Manilkara* can be grouped by
leaf architecture into species with many fine parallel lateral veins and those
with larger blades and more spaced venation. It also stresses that pedicel
form and floral details are often more reliable than foliage alone.

The fruits of most of these species are unknown. Nothing is known either of the ecology of certain species. Those best known are shrubs or small trees of littoral thickets, M. lacera, M. Welwitschii. M. multinervis is in the wet dense forest zone, a tall tree, from river margins and periodically inundated forest. M. Fouilloyana is by contrast a tall tree of upland forest. Among the species cited here, two have been encountered thus far <<<---SECTION-BREAK--->>> only in the Oubangui forest, Boukoko region, M. Aubrevillei and M. Pellegriniana, a third has been recorded only from the Brazzaville region M. Koechlini, and a fourth is a species from South Cameroon, M. Zenkeri. With 10 known species, equatorial Africa in its western part is clearly the richest in Manilkara of the entire Guineo-Congolian forest. The species of the group with numerous, fine, parallel lateral venation, scarcely visible, whose lower leaf surface is greyish white, the remainder of tomentum from the juvenile form worn away in the adult form, pose very delicate problems of specific discrimination whose solutions are never entirely satisfactory. With at least provisional exception of Manilkara Pellegriniana, a poorly known species whose ecology we ignore, the other species of this group are an example of these series of ecophyletic species that are frequent in tropical African flora. These are plant forms which by the morphology of the leaf, flower and fruit differ only by secondary characters, yet specifically distinct; but there exist moreover between them intermediate types. Despite the latter, the characteristic types of each of these species are distinctly separate. Furthermore, from the point of view of plant habit and ecology, the distinctions are evident. There is a correlation between habit and ecology on the one hand and foliar and floral morphology on the other. In the case of this group of three Manilkara, M. lacera, M. multinervis and M. Fouilloyana, the first is a shrub or small tree of beach thicket, sometimes preceded by inextricable bushy forms, incontestably of the same species. The suborbicular obovate form of the leaves is specifically remarkable. In the interior, along rivers and streams, and also in periodically inundated forest, one may encounter stands of a tall tree, with perfectly straight bole, which contrasts by its lofty upright stature and light canopy foliage with the generally curved or tortuous trunks, the very leafy crowns of trees of riparian or flood-prone forest. The leaf is oblong, attenuate at both ends, evidently different from that of M. lacera of the beaches. We have referred this species to Manilkara multinervis. There is nevertheless variability in leaf form. These are sometimes more or less obovate, without however becoming broadly obovate or even suborbicular as those of M. lacera. This tree ascends rivers to the forest margins, and even follows forest galleries in savanna regions. The leaf form becomes increasingly distinctly oblong, sometimes narrowly oblong. But curiously in these countries of wooded savanna and open forests with semi-arid climate, this species whose presence is incontestably linked to the proximity of running water, readily leaves the forest galleries and spreads in dry terrain. It is encountered in abundance with the habit of a small tree or shrub on plateaus and in rocky areas in mountains. As furthermore this Manilkara appears to exist in a considerable range covering all of western and central Africa, and as moreover the action of bush fires causes individual variations in individuals living in wooded savannas, the systematist observes in the abundant herbaria of the species the presence of highly variable leaf forms. Thus many species have already been described or simply named: M. djalonensis A. Chev., M. atacorensis A. Chev., M. Poissoni Pierre, M. Maclaudi Pierre, M. argentea (Pierre) Dubard, M. remotifolia Pierre, M. Schweinjurthii (Engl.) Dubard, M. dahomeyensis Pierre, M. sublacera A. Chev. It is difficult to separate them specifically from M. multinervis, the earliest described species (1877), of which the type is from the Niger (Nupe, Baikie's Nig. Exped.). Only the study of germinations and seedlings would perhaps allow one to separate with certainty taxonomic types within all these multiple ecological or geographic forms. It remains evidently remarkable to give the same specific name to a tall tree with straight bole growing in stands in inundated forest in the full equatorial forest zone and to a shrub of rocky terrain in semi-arid country. <<<---SECTION-BREAK--->>> arid. In any case these variations in habit and leaf morphology, in relation to environment, merit being highlighted and studied more thoroughly. The third species of the group, M. Fouilloyana, distinguishes itself fairly well from the two preceding by its larger flowers, borne on thicker pedicels. The very coriaceous leaves are obovate oblong, generally larger than those of other Manilkara of the same group. Their most marked type is then very distinct from that of M. lacera and M. multinervis. But here too there are doubtful cases. M. Fouilloyana is a very tall tree, with straight bole, fairly common in Gabon in the primary forest of the Monts de Cristal. An upland species therefore but of very humid and very rainy region. In Côte d'Ivoire in upland forest, we have also distinguished an M. sylvestris Aubr. et Pellegr. distinct by its distinctly smaller leaves, but ecologically equivalent to the Gabonese M. Fouilloyana. As shown by the drawings of the 3 species: Pellegriniana, multinervis, Fouilloyana, the study of floral structure hardly allows sure separation of the species. For these three species, it is the pedicels that give fairly precise separative characters. ^k5

## Treatments

### Vol 1 (1961)

**Author**: A. Aubreville  
**Pages**: 27-40  
**Source**: `sources/Sapotaceae_vol01_paddle`

This opening genus block of the Sapotaceae monograph mixes clear floral
diagnosis with repeated warnings that foliage alone can be deceptive. Several
species are still imperfectly known, and the author explicitly treats the
coastal and inland forms around [[Manilkara_lacera]] as a difficult continuum.
