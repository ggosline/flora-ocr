# *Duranta* L .

**Family**: [[Verbenaceae]]
**Authority**: L .
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. : 637 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Shrubs or small trees, deciduous, often spiny, non-aromatic. Leaves simple, opposite or whorled, petiolate. Inflorescences terminal and axillary, in an elongate, lax spike; bracts small. Flowers generally pedicellate; calyx cylindric-tubular or campanulate, with 5 nerves, with 5 teeth, persistent, accrescent and enclosing the fruit; corolla tubular, campanulate or hypocrateriform, ± actinomorphic, blue, violet or white, with tube straight or incurved, limb two-lipped, with 5 unequal lobes; stamens 4, generally with 1 staminode, slightly didynamous, inserted at or above the middle of the tube, included, anther dorsifixed, with parallel thecae; ovary globose, 4-carpellate, each carpel 2-locular, each locule bearing a single erect anatropous ovule; style equal to or shorter than the corolla tube, caducous or persistent, with capitate stigma, unequally 4-lobed. Fruits drupaceous, fleshy, with 4 pyrenes with 2 seeds, enclosed in the accrescent calyx. Seeds erect, without endosperm.

This genus of about 31 species is native to (sub)tropical America. One species is widely cultivated and often naturalized in (sub)tropical Africa, Asia and the Pacific.
