# *Khaya* A.Juss.

**Family**: [[Meliaceae]]
**Authority**: A.Juss.
**Protologue**: Bull. Sci. Nat. Géol . 23 : 238 (1830)

## Diagnosis

Dioecious trees, often of large size, base furnished with buttresses. Leaves in tufts towards the end of the branches, compound, paripinnate, with 2–7 pairs of leaflets; leaflets opposite to subopposite, glabrous, with entire margin. Inflorescences supra-axillary, in the form of large and well-branched panicles. Flowers 4–5-merous, functionally unisexual but with vestiges of the opposite sex well developed; calyx deeply lobed, lobes semicircular and imbricate; petals free, imbricate, rounded at the apex, sometimes cucullate, glabrous, white; staminal tube urceolate, glabrous, with margin 8–10-lobed, anthers (or anthérodes) 8 or 10, alternating with the lobes and entirely included in the tube; disc cushion-shaped cupuliform, fleshy, the base fused to the gynoecium; ovary rectangular-ovoid, 4–5-locular, each locule with 8–18 ovules. Fruit a subglobose capsule, erect above the foliage, woody, opening in 4 or 5 valves, valves remaining united at their base; central axis (columella) truncate at the apex, not extending to the top of the capsule, 4–5-angular at the winged sharp-edged margins delimiting the 4 or 5 locules. Seed disciform, flattened, with margins furnished with a narrow membranous wing.

African genus grouping 3 or 4 species in continental Africa and 1 in Madagascar and the Comoros. From Gabon, only a single species is known.

Taxonomic note: 1. Khaya senegalensis A.Juss., cited in Check-list of vascular plants of Gabon (Sosef et al., 2006) refers to a collection gathered by Klaine (n° 2836) of a plant cultivated in Libreville (Pellegrin, 1911).

2. The only species currently known from Gabon, namely K. ivorensis A.Chev., is easily confused with K. anthotheca (Welw.) C.DC. The latter species, widely distributed throughout the semi-deciduous forest zone of Africa, appears to be replaced by K. ivorensis in the evergreen rainforest. According to Aubréville (1959), in regions where the ranges touch, they can be found in mixture and the two species coexist in Gabon under the collective name African mahogany. In herbaria, on the other hand, we have found only K. ivorensis, but collections of Khaya of Gabonese origin are poorly represented there. Also noted in Cameroon, the presence of K. anthotheca (Plate 10A–E) in Gabon is thus to be verified. For this reason both taxa are included in the key below.
