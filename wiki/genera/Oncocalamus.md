# *Oncocalamus* (G.Mann & H.Wendl.) H.Wendl.

**Family**: [[Arecaceae]]
**Authority**: (G.Mann & H.Wendl.) H.Wendl.
**Protologue**: Calamus L. subgenus Oncocalamus G.Mann & H.Wendl., Trans. Linn. Soc. London 24 : 436 (1864)

## Diagnosis

in De Kerchove de Denterghem, Palmiers: 36, 252 (1878).

Calamus L. subgenus Oncocalamus G.Mann & H.Wendl., Trans. Linn. Soc. London 24: 436 (1864).

Lianescent, cespitose, spiny palms, polycarpic, monoecious. Mature leaves pinnate, bifid in juveniles, with a terminal cirrus; tubular sheath, armed with fragile and flattened spines; ochrea distinctly visible, truncate at apex, armed with spines like the sheath; knee absent, but sometimes rounded swelling present; petiole present but very short; rachis armed with spines like those of the sheath; blade with more or less numerous leaflets, linear to elliptic or sigmoid, margins armed with robust spines, basal leaflets smaller and reflexed around the stem; cirrus furnished with spine-like leaflets of 2–5 cm, with rachis lacking spines. Inflorescences axillary, once branched; peduncle enclosed within the leaf sheath, base of sheath not fused to the internode, with ± 4 bracts; prophyll tubular, shorter than the sheath; rachis longer than the peduncle; floriferous branches with up to 11 flowers in clusters, composed of a central female flower flanked by two lateral cincinni each with one female flower and 3 or 4 male flowers. Male flowers: calyx tubular, 3-lobed; corolla scarcely larger than the calyx, very shortly tubular, broadly 3-lobed; stamens 6, filaments united into a free staminal ring, anthers latrose; pistillode narrow, slightly exceeding the staminal ring. Female flowers similar to male flowers, staminal ring bearing reduced sterile anthers; gynoecium of 3 free carpels, ellipsoid, with 1 ovule per carpel, covered with scales; stigma 3-lobed. Fruits spherical, covered with scales in vertical rows, monosperms, basal seed; mesocarp very thin; endocarp not differentiated. Seeds subglobose, smooth to verrucose or foveolate.

Genus comprising four species in tropical Africa from Benin to Angola, two of which are represented in Gabon.

Notes: Oncocalamus was first considered as a subgenus of Calamus (Mann and Wendland 1864), then was elevated to generic rank by Wendland in De Kerchove de Denterghem (1878).

B: Faye et al. (2014), Sunderland (2007, 2012).
