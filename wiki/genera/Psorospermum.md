# *Psorospermum* Spach

**Family**: [[Hypericaceae]]
**Authority**: Spach
**Protologue**: Ann. Sci. Nat., Bot. II, 5 : 157 (1836)

## Diagnosis

Suffrutex, shrub or small tree; latex orange. Leaves opposite, subopposite or alternate, without stipules; blade glabrous or with stellate hairs, often punctate with black glands.

Inflorescences terminal or sometimes pseudo-axillary, in corymbiform or umbelliform cymes with very reduced axes. Flowers: sepals 5, persistent, with longitudinal glands; petals 5, caducous, villous on the inner surface, glandular; staminodial fascicles with 2–∞ stamens, filaments partially free, anthers subglobose, staminodes 5, fleshy, scale-shaped, alternating with the fascicles; ovary 5-locular, with 1 ovule per locule; styles 5, free. Fruits: berries with (3–)5 seeds. Seeds ovoid to broadly ellipsoid; testa with numerous large black glands; embryo isocotylous with plane-convex accumbent cotyledons, or heterocotylous with subplane-convex incumbent cotyledons, or with enrolled cotyledons.

Genus with about fifty species distributed in tropical Africa and Madagascar; in Gabon 4 species.

Taxonomic note: This genus was divided into three subgenera by Bamps (1966). The subgenus Psorospermum with accumbent cotyledons is found only in Madagascar. The subgenus Parapsorospermum, with the Gabonese species P. membranaceum, has incumbent cotyledons and the subgenus Afropsorospermum, with the Gabonese species P. febrifugum and P. tenuifolium, has enrolled cotyledons.

P. staudtii Engl. appears in the Check-list of vascular plants of Gabon (Sosef et al., 2006), but all the specimens cited belong to P. membranaceum. As it is nonetheless quite likely that P. staudtii is present in Gabon, it appears in the key below.

B: Bamps (1966, 1970), Milne-Redhead (1953).
