# Dacryodes

**Authority**: Vahl (1810) · **Family**: [[Burseraceae]]

## Diagnosis

Resinous trees with imparipinnate leaves and predominantly 3-merous unisexual flowers: three sepals, three petals, six stamens and a two-locular ovary. The Gabon species are separated chiefly by leaflet number and shape, simple versus stellate or scaly hairs, and the form and shield-like markings of the endocarp.

## Key to the species


KEY BASED ON LEAVES AND THEIR PUBESCENCE

**1.** Small leaflets lanceolate, acuminate, glabrous. Flowers rather long-pedicellate (8 mm) → D. osika, osika. ^k1

**1′.** Leaflets lanceolate, long acuminate acute, densely covered below with a persistent tomentum of russet stellate scaly hairs → 1. D. Bittneri, ozigo. 1". Leaflets narrowly to broadly obovate, acuminate sometimes very acute.

**2.** Thick tomentum of hairs with fine radiating branches at the apex of a long pedicel, on the lower face of the leaflets and on the rachis at least in the juvenile form → 2. D. Normandii, ossabel. ^k2

**2′.** Stellate hairs not stipitate.

**3.** Absence of simple hairs. ^k3

**4.** Thick tomentum of stellate hairs, at least on young leaves. ^k4

**5.** Thick tomentum of russet stellate hairs, dense on the midvein, disappearing almost completely in time → 3. D. heterotricha, mouganga ^k5

**5′.** Persistent tomentum of stellate hairs with fine, supple branches → D. pubescens, safoukala.

**4′.** Minute scaly hairs, scattered on the rachis and lower face of the leaflets practically glabrous.

**6.** Among the russet scaly hairs, very high density of white points (stomata). Leaves practically glabrous. Petiolules often swollen at the extremities, rather long (1-2 cm) ^k6

**6′.** Leaves practically glabrous but existence of varieties having scattered stellate hairs on the lower face of the leaflets.

**7.** Petiolules ordinarily rather long (1-3 cm), swollen at both extremities, but sometimes short and without distinct swelling → 5. D. Klaineana, adjouaba. ^k7

**7′.** Petiolules not swollen at the extremities. Rachis and petiolules covered with scaly hairs. Blade below practically glabrous, sometimes with traces of a few caducous stellate hairs → 6. D. edulis, safoutier, atanga, olem.

**3′.** Presence of long simple supple hairs in ± great abundance, at least on the rachis, capable of disappearing in time on leaflets which become then practically glabrous. Sometimes mixed with scaly hairs.

**8.** Simple hairs, on the rachis, the midvein above, and on the lower face of the blade. Leaflets subsessile..... ^k8

**8′.** Simple hairs below the blade and small scattered scaly hairs, regularly distributed, applied against the blade, persistent 8. D. igagang, igagang 8". Simple hairs on the rachis, the petiolules, the midvein below, often very sparse. Mixed in with a few small scaly hairs on the blade. Persistence of scaly hairs on the petiolules and the rachis. Sometimes leaflets practically glabrous → 6. D. edulis var. hirsuta, safoutier, atanga, olem.
