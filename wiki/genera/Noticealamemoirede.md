GEORGES LE TESTU

(1877-1967)

GREAT BOTANIST COLLECTOR

AND FRIEND OF GABON

Georges le Testu was born in Caen on 8 January 1877. He received his early education there while his parents ran a business. In Paris he obtained the degrees of agricultural engineer and licenciate in sciences; he further pursued studies in law. He knew the Tunisian bled during his military service in 1898-99. Professionally he began as a cultural agent in Dahomey from 1900 to 1902, then in Mozambique from 1903 to 1906. Eager to travel and explore, he subsequently became administrator in Gabon, in Haut-Oubangui and again in Gabon where he was successively entrusted with numerous posts.

In 1934 a retirement much regretted interrupted the tropical activity of this tireless friend of Gabon. A bachelor, living by correspondence with numerous friends, moving about rarely and only by train, he remained until the end of his life in his house on rue Caponière in Caen. There he worked with his books and herbaria while fulfilling the duties of Conservator of the botanical collections of the University of Caen. Having retained an activity incompatible with his advanced age, he died on 14 April 1967 following a fracture of the femoral neck.

The vocation of botanist of G. Le Testu appeared during his secondary studies. As soon as he resided in Africa, he devoted all his leisure to studying the vegetation of the regions he travelled and assembled copious herbaria which he entrusted to specialists. He

---

also collected zoological and geological specimens; he observed the inhabitants and noted their customs. He corresponded with numerous scholars, who studied his collections and who shared the sacred fire he carried for Natural History. He was appointed in 1919 correspondent of the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle. At the Laboratory of Phanerogamae of the Muséum he made the acquaintance of the Parisian F. Pellegrin who remained until the end — Le Testu outlived him by two years — among his best friends. It was Pellegrin who undertook the scientific exploitation of the greater part of the Phanerogams in the Le Testu collections. Many other researchers who were his friends are mentioned by R. Meslin in his notice on the life and work of his friend from Caen.
