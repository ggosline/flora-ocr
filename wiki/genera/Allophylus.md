# *Allophylus* Linné

**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Authority**: Linné
**Protologue**: Fl. Zeyl.: 58 (1747); RADLK., in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 455 (1932)

## Diagnosis

Erect shrubs (2–4 m) or sarmentose (4–5 m: A. hallæi, lastours-villensis, hamatus, oyemensis; up to 20 m: A. altescandens), sometimes trees (5–10 (–20) m: A. zenkeri, africanus, bullatus, poungouensis, longicuneatus). Young branches often pubescent and lenticellate, rarely armed with spines (A. hamatus).

Leaves alternate (2/5 arrangement) without stipules, with petiole cylindrical or ± canaliculate above, generally bearing 3 leaflets (rarely a single one due to abortion of the lateral leaflets: A. megaphyllus, nigericus, hirtellus), with petiolules ± long; even in unifoliolate leaves the petiolule is

---

distinguished from the petiole by transverse ridges in the bark. Sometimes the petiolules and the base of the petioles retract and narrow when drying, being swollen with sap when fresh (motor swelling?: A. schweinfurthii, oyemensis, longicuneatus, camptoneurus). The lamina is ± thick, ± pubescent, flat or goffered (A. bullatus), concolorous or discolorous. The margin is entire, all secondary veins curving at the apex and anastomosing to the following vein, or toothed, the secondary veins reaching the margin and terminating there either in a sinus or by a mucro at the apex of a tooth. The teeth are either poorly marked (1 mm) or distinct and acute (2–3 mm), present along the entire margin or only in the upper half of the lamina. The median leaflet possesses bilateral symmetry; in contrast the lateral leaflets are asymmetrical, the basiscopic half being wider than the acroscopic half, and most often shorter than the median leaflet. The length of the leaflets varies from a few cm to several dm.

The inflorescences are ± contracted cymules, grouped in simple or branched axillary racemes, solitary (rarely 2 at the axil of the subtending leaf). Simple racemes (2–50 cm) have a peduncle generally reaching 1/3 of the total length. Branched racemes comprise 1 to 12 branches, most often 2–3, arising at different heights on the main axis. Sometimes the last subtending leaves are much reduced and the last simple racemes simulate a branched raceme: the bud at the apex of the central stem shows that these are lateral simple racemes (A. ngounyensis). The cymules, subtended by bracts of 1–5 mm, are arranged along the axis of the racemes according to a divergence more complicated than 2/5, ± crowded (2 to 8 per cm), ± stipitate (0.3–3 mm). The pubescence is generally the same on the axis of the raceme and on the cymules up to the articulation of the pedicels. Each uniporous, scorpioid cymule, curved downward, comprises 2 to 10 flowers, subtended by small bracteoles. The fall of flowers makes it possible to see the degree of contraction of the cymules. The pedicels then form either a rounded head bearing contiguous scars (A. senegalensis, hamatus), or a scape with disjunct scars (A. hallæi), or a kind of claw (A. zenkeri, oyemensis...). The cymules are most often monoecious: ♀ flowers at the base and ♂ at the apex (A. africanus). Certain species are dioecious: A. persicifolius, schweinfurthii,...
