# *Berlinia* Sol. ex Hook.f. & Benth.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] — Caesalpinioideae in [[vol15]], **Detarioideae**
in current classification
**Authority**: Sol. ex Hook.f. & Benth., nom. cons. — Niger Flora: 326 (1849)
**Synonyms**: *Westia* Vahl (1810), nom. rej.; *Macroberlinia* (Harms) Hauman
(1952)

Five species.

## Diagnosis

Trees and shrubs. Leaves 2–5-jugate. Leaflets opposite, sometimes subopposite,
petiolulate. **Large white flowers, over 4 cm long** — among the showiest in the
family in the region, and the character that most readily separates *Berlinia*
from the allied genera.

## Key to the species

**1.** Five large petals, 4–6 cm long, subequal, the limb of the median petal being broader; strong terminal racemes; long bracts 5–7 cm → [[Berlinia_bracteosa\|*B. bracteosa*]]

**1′.** A single large petal with a long claw, the others distinctly much smaller or rudimentary; very short bracts at most 5 mm long.

**2.** Bracteoles densely velutinous silvery within; forest galleries of the northern peripheral guinea-congolian domain and of the sudanian region → B. grandiflora (Nord-Cameroun).

**2′.** Bracteoles finely pubescent or nearly glabrous within.

**3.** Bracteoles relatively short, about 2 cm long by 1 cm wide; 2–3 pairs of leaflets; shrubs, small trees.

**3′.** Bracteoles more than 3 cm long.

**4.** Relatively narrow, 8–15 mm wide; floral buds oblanceolate.

**5.** (2–) 3–5 pairs of leaflets; bracteoles 10–15 mm wide → [[Berlinia_confusa\|*B. confusa*]]

**5′.** 2 (–3) pairs of leaflets; bracteoles 8–11 mm wide → [[Berlinia_congolensis\|*B. congolensis*]]

**4′.** Very broad, 2 cm wide or more; very large flowers; buds broadly obovoid; calyx tube densely tomentose without; bracteoles obovate, 4.5 cm long by 2–3 cm wide; 4–6 pairs of leaflets → [[Berlinia_craibiana\|*B. craibiana*]]

## Species in region

*B. bracteosa*, *B. auriculata*, *B. confusa*, *B. congolensis*, *B. craibiana*
— 5 in all.

## Notes

*Berlinia* belongs to the **ectomycorrhizal Detarioideae alliance** that
structures much of Guineo-Congolian and miombo forest — with
[[Gilbertiodendron]], [[Julbernardia]], [[Brachystegia]] and [[Microberlinia]].
That shared mycorrhizal habit is central to the monodominance those genera
display in places, and it distinguishes them from most tropical trees, which are
arbuscular-mycorrhizal.

*Macroberlinia*, treated here as a synonym, is sometimes maintained; the source
lists it in synonymy, and current usage should be checked.

Several species yield useful timber, traded as **ebiara** or **abem**.
