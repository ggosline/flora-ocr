# *Cynodon* Pers.

**Family**: [[Gramineae]]
**Authority**: Pers.
**Protologue**: Pers. Syn. I: 85 (1805)

## Diagnosis

Perennial herbs with creeping stolons, rooting at the nodes and then producing tufts of erect stems. Leaves distichous, narrowly linear, with imbricate sheaths. Racemes linear, unilateral, 2 to 6 in terminal umbels. Spikelets muticous, one-flowered, inserted in 2 series on the rachis of the racemes, disarticulating above the glumes. Hermaphrodite flower, with narrow, keeled, acuminate or mucronate glumes and the lower one persistent. Lemma longer than the glumes, with 3 nerves, keel ciliate. Palea shorter, bicarinate; 2 lodicules; 3 stamens.

This genus comprises two species, one of which, Cynodon dactylon Pers., the type of the genus, undoubtedly occurs in Gabon. It can be used for making lawns which must be mowed frequently. It is propagated by cutting of stolons. Vernacular names (after Walker and Sillans): Ekanda (Mpongwé); obur (Fang).
