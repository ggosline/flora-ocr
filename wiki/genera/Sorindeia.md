# *Sorindeia* Thouars

**Family**: [[Anacardiaceae]]
**Authority**: Thouars
**Protologue**: Gen. nova Madag. : 23 (1806)

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or lianes; exudate rare, clear. Leaves generally imparipinnate, sometimes 3-foliolate or 1-foliolate; leaflets with tertiary venation sometimes meeting in a fine oblique vein (see Plate 7) between two lateral veins (except S. grandifolia Engl. and S. madagascariensis DC., from East Africa and Madagascar). Inflorescences terminal or axillary or arising just below the leaves or on the trunk, in panicles. Flowers unisexual, dioecious, 5-merous; pedicel short, articulate; sepals united at the base or at most for half their length; petals ± free, 2–4(–5) times as long as the sepals, valvate or imbricate, ± erect or spreading. Male flowers with 6 to 20 stamens, with filaments often shorter than the anther; pistillode absent. Female flowers with 5 to 10 staminodes; ovary glabrous or pubescent, ± ovoid, 1-locular, with 1 pendulous ovule; style short, stigma 3-lobed to subglobose. Fruits up to 3.5 × 2 cm, 1-seeded, style generally persistent.

Genus of tropical Africa with 9 species, 8 of which concentrated on the continent, the last in East Africa and Madagascar; in Gabon, 6 species.

Notes: The distribution of S. grandifolia Engl. is found north of the equator and it has not yet been recorded in Gabon. Nevertheless, it appears in the key below, but is not treated. In Check-List of the Vascular Plants of Gabon (Sosef et al. 2006) several collections are mentioned under S. grandifolia whereas these belong to other Sorindeia, notably to S. juglandifolia.

Bibliography

B: Bourobou-Bourobou & Breteler (1997, 1999), Breteler (2003), Fernandes & Fernandes (1966), Kokwaro (1986), Pell et al. (2011), Van der Veken (1960a).
