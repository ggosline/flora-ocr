# *Drypetes* Vahl

**Family**: [[Putranjivaceae]]
**Authority**: Vahl
**Protologue**: Eclog. amer. 3:49 (1807)

## Diagnosis

Shrubs or trees dioecious, exceptionally monoecious, with orthotropic trunk and sometimes plagiotropic branches; hairs uni- to multicellular, or rarely stellate; bark sometimes with spicy odour, horseradish-flavoured; branchlets cylindric to slightly flattened, often longitudinally furrowed, glabrous. Leaves: blade often coriaceous, without glands, base generally oblique (slightly asymmetric) or distinctly asymmetric, margin entire or subentire to spinose-dentate; secondary veins ± arched, tertiary venation reticulate in irregular polygons. Inflorescences with flowers agglomerated in dense tufts, their bases often forming outgrowths (especially on branches and trunk). Male flowers pedicellate; sepals (3 or) 4 or 5 (to 7), usually strongly imbricate even during anthesis, sometimes apparently open, persistent or caducous after anthesis, ligulate to suborbicular, generally concave and unequal, apex obtuse to acute, often with three vascular traces, margin ciliate (although often the fringes are little differentiated from the rest of the indumentum); stamens 3 to 20(−50), either in a single whorl and surrounding the outline of the disc, or obscurely arranged and ± deeply enclosed by the folds of the disc, filaments free, anthers erect, basifixed to slightly dorsifixed, generally introrse, rarely latrorse or extrorse, usually glabrous; disc present, plane to slightly concave (cupuliform) or convex (dome-shaped), smooth or rugose, sometimes plicate, foveolate or convolute-tuberculate, often fleshy, nectariferous, generally fragrant. Female flowers with general structure similar to the males; disc annular, concave, convolute-tuberculate, sometimes plicate, often fleshy, nectariferous; ovary with 1 to 4 (to 7) fertile carpels, often 2, with short, little-visible gynophore; styles generally short or almost none, with stigma pulvinate-capitate, reniform, fan-shaped, bifid, ligulate or obtriangular, usually thick, sometimes subpeltate or subsessile, non-papillose. Fruits globose, ovoid, ellipsoid or rarely angulate; exocarp hard or slightly fleshy, becoming hard on drying; endocarp coriaceous, chartaceous or osseous, with 1 seed per locule or per fruit by abortion. Seeds without caruncle, sarcotesta slender or absent.

Pantropical genus of ±210 species from dry and wet forests, sometimes in savannas. Among these, 70 species in Africa and Madagascar; on the continent, these are distributed over a vast territory that comprises tropical West and Central Africa, where the genus shows its maximum diversity, up to the Central African Republic and Sudan in the north-east, and to East Africa in the east and southern Africa; absent from a vast area in the south-west. In Gabon, Drypetes currently comprises 29 species.
