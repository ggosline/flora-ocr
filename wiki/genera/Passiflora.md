# *Passiflora* L.

**Family**: [[Passifloraceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 995 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Herbaceous or woody lianes provided with simple tendrils. Stipules sometimes very well developed and foliaceous. Leaves alternate, with petiole provided with glands or not; limb entire or deeply lobed, generally palmatinerved. Axillary inflorescences, sessile or not, reduced to 1 or 2 flowers on a peduncle provided with a terminal involucre of 3 foliaceous bracts or strongly divided. Hermaphrodite flowers, 5-merous, pedicellate; hypanthium patelliform or cupuliform, nectariferous ring present or not at the base; sepals free or partially connate into a tube; petals free; membranous operculum covering the internal part of the flower; corona composed of several whorls of filaments, the 1 or 2 external ones with very long filaments; stamens 5, anthers dorsifixed, versatile; ovary on an androgynophore, with 3 parietal placentas; styles 3(–4), free or connate at the base, with capitate stigmas. Fruits bacciform. Seeds generally numerous, flattened.

Genus comprising approximately 370 species, the great majority of which occur in tropical America and a few in tropical Asia and Oceania; in Gabon, 2 common and naturalized species, several others in cultivation could escape, two of which are treated here.

Taxonomic note: In Gabon, all Passiflora are introduced. In gardens, other ornamental species may be encountered, outside of those treated here. The presence of P. quadrangularis, mentioned by Raponda-Walker & Sillans (1961), could not be confirmed, while another, of uncertain identity, is treated here briefly as Passiflora sp.

Bibliography: De Wilde (1975), Fernandes & Fernandes (1978), Hutchinson & Dalziel (1954), Killip (1938), Robyns (1995), Sinsin (2006), Zepernick & Timler (1984).
