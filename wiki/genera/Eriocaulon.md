# *Eriocaulon* L.

**Family**: [[Eriocaulaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 1 : 87 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Perennial, annual or ephemeral herbs, leaves, sheaths and scapes glabrous. Leaves septate, arranged in a tuft or basal rosette or, in floating aquatic species, scattered along a stem. Inflorescence: scapes 3–7-furrowed; capitulum subglobose to ovoid, 2–6 mm wide; involucral bracts uniseriate to multiseriate, spreading or reflexed at maturity, scarious to coriaceous; floral bracts frequently with white pilosity toward the apex; receptacle glabrous or pilose. Flowers 3-merous or more rarely 2-merous, sessile or pedicellate, the male and female flowers mixed or the latter at the periphery (particularly in ephemerophytes); sepals often navicular, sometimes winged; petals free, furnished with a black apical or subapical gland on the inner face, glands sometimes reduced or rarely absent, frequent white pilosity on the inner face and at the apex. Male flowers: sepals ± connate, a fleshy infundibuliform or columnar stipe between the sepals and petals; petals at the apex of the stipe, very small (in Gabon); rudimentary gynoecium present; stamens 4 or 6, twice as numerous as the petals, the inner cycle epipetalous, anthers generally black, rarely cream-white. Female flowers: sepals free; stigmas 3, without appendages. Seeds relatively large, ellipsoid, yellowish to reddish-brown, smooth or with distinct pattern.

Genus with worldwide distribution in tropical and subtropical regions; a few species in temperate northeastern North America, temperate eastern Asia and one in western Europe. The exact number of species is truly poorly known, but generally 400 are indicated, of which approximately 90 in Africa; in Gabon, 2 species and 2 others to be sought.

Taxonomic notes: Although only two species are currently known from Gabon, one may expect to find more in open, swampy or sandy stations, insofar as several other species are present in Cameroon and Congo.

The identification of most Eriocaulon is not easy because of the very small size of the flowers which nevertheless show a wide range of important differences. Species with usually comparable external appearance possess great variation in their floral morphology.

For dissection, the flowers are easily rehydrated in a drop of water, without needing to boil them. Measurements of the floral parts are taken on the flowers
