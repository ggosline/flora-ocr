# *Gilbertiodendron* J.Léonard

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] — Caesalpinioideae in [[vol15]], **Detarioideae**
in current classification
**Authority**: J.Léonard — Bull. Jard. Bot. État Brux. 22: 188 (1952)
**Synonyms**: *Youapa* sect. *Pentisomeris* Baill. (1865); *Berlinia* sect.
*Berliniella* Taub. (1894); *Macrolobium* sensu Pellegrin (1948), non Schreb.

Fourteen species — **the largest genus in [[vol15]]**, and ecologically the most
important in the corpus.

## Diagnosis

Trees and shrubs. Leaves unijugate to multijugate, stipulate. Stipules
lanceolate, fused into an intrapetiolar blade, sometimes bearing auricle-shaped
appendages; caducous or persistent. Leaflets opposite, rarely alternate.

## Notes

***Gilbertiodendron dewevrei* forms the most extensive monodominant tropical
rainforest in the world.** Across the Congo basin it occurs in near-pure stands —
often 60–90% of canopy basal area — covering thousands of square kilometres, in a
biome otherwise defined by extreme tree diversity. Explaining that monodominance
is a long-standing problem in tropical ecology: ectomycorrhizal association,
heavy shade-tolerant seedling banks, large recalcitrant seeds with limited
dispersal, and slow leaf-litter decomposition have all been implicated, and no
single explanation is settled.

The consequences are substantial. *Gilbertiodendron* forest has different soil
chemistry, carbon dynamics, light regime and faunal community from adjacent
mixed forest, and it regenerates on a different trajectory after disturbance. Any
vegetation mapping or carbon accounting in the Congo basin has to treat it
separately.

The genus is **ectomycorrhizal**, unusual among tropical trees and shared with
other Detarioideae — *Berlinia*, *Julbernardia*, *Brachystegia*, *Microberlinia*
— which is likely central to the monodominance question and links this genus to
the miombo woodlands further south, where the same alliance dominates.

Several species yield useful timber, but the genus's importance is ecological
rather than economic.
