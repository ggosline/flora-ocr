# *Bulbophyllum* Thouars

**Family**: [[Orchidaceae]]
**Authority**: Thouars
**Protologue**: Hist. Orchid. [Fl., Iles Austr. Afr.] : tab. gen., fig. 93-97 (1822), nom. conserv. - FTWA : 226-242 (1968) ; FAC. 2 : 307-374 (1992) ; FCAM 2 : 380 (2001) ; GYNOR 2 : 124. (2002)

## Diagnosis

Pseudobulbs with a single internode, clustered or spaced along the rhizome, apex usually 1- but sometimes 2-3-leaved. Leaves coriaceous or fleshy. Inflorescence at the base of the pseudobulbs, few- to many-flowered. Rachis sometimes flattened or swollen. Flowers generally small, resupinate. Sepals and petals dissimilar, the lateral ones fused to the foot of the column forming a prominent mentum. Labellum generally smaller than the sepals, articulated at the apex of the column foot, mobile, fleshy, thick, very often ligulate to oblong, often furnished with long hairs. Column foot prominent, generally long, massive and curved upward toward the apex. Gynostemium short, massive. Pollinia most often 4 in two pairs, of equal or very unequal size, sometimes only 2, flattened laterally, ellipsoidal or ovoid. Rostellum truncate, short, straight or nearly so. Viscidium if present, single, often thick, fleshy, sticky. – Pl. 64, p. 133.

The flowers attract flies by dull insipid colours either greenish or brown-purple, often spotted or striped, sometimes covered on the external side by fur or club-shaped mobile hairs vibrating in the wind, e.g. B. barbigerum. They do not present nectar to insects, but sometimes they produce oil and sugar at the base of the labellum or sepals or a thick sticky layer on the part turned upward on the underside of the lateral sepals. The flowers often emit a putrid odour. They are characterized by their labellum which, like a spring-loaded seesaw, is articulated by a narrow elastic claw that returns it to its equilibrium position when this has been disrupted. This articulated labellum pushes against the gynostemium poorly oriented flies, so that they play a truly effective role as pollinators. In species with a flat, widened and often purplescent rachis, it functions as a first landing area which allows the gradual approach of the insect toward the flower.

This genus exceeds 1100 species widely distributed in the tropical and subtropical regions of the New and Old World: nearly 70 species occur in continental Africa. In a recent revision of these, VERMEULEN (1987) proposed no infrageneric classification of the genus. CRIBB (1984) adopted the division into sections of SUMMERHAYES (1935), yet far from being satisfactory. Faced with such a situation, we have divided this genus into 4 artificial groups for a primarily practical purpose and with no phylogenetic significance.

**2′.** Lip globally linear or lanceolate and progressively narrowed toward the apex.

**3.** Lower bracts ≥15 mm.

**4′.** Lip without a basal adaxial tooth → [[Bulbophyllum_schinzianum\|*B. schinzianum*]]
