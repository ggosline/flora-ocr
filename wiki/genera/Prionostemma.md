# *Prionostemma* Miers

**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Authority**: Miers
**Protologue**: Trans. Linn. Soc. London 28: 330, 354-396 tab. 17 (1872); HALLÉ, Bull. Mus. natn. Hist. nat., Paris, 4° sér., B, 3, Adansonia (1): 7-8 (1981)

## Diagnosis

Glabrous lianes (except the American species which is scabrous), often tall, with cylindrical woody core. No rubber filaments. Leaves with entire or sometimes dentate blade.

Inflorescences often paniculate, branched and lax in several species. Buds subglobose, wider than tall. Flowers pedicellate, rather large, rotate, (6)8–13 mm in diameter. Sepals ± unequal, quincuncial. Petals orbicular raised by a distinct narrow claw. Disk fleshy ± patellifom, either broadly trapezoidal or spreading in a flattened cushion, zoned with an external margin micropapilliferous or nectariferous part that is not in contact with the base of the filaments. Pistil pyramidal, style narrowly conical. 4–20 ovules per locule.

Fruit tricarpellate dehiscent in 6 caducous valves. Seeds winged with raphé crossing the wing. Placental tubules insufficiently known. Cotyledons fused at least in the American species.

TYPE SPECIES: Prionostemma aspera (Lam.) Miers, Guiana. It was chosen by A.C. SMITH who revised the genus by judiciously excluding heterogeneous materials originally grouped by MIERS. But SMITH's generic conception (Brittonia 3: 391–396, 1940) was too narrow and delayed the grouping of Old World species.

Etymology: from Greek prion, to saw, with the sense of denticulate, and stemma, crown; denticulate crown evoking the corolla.

Genus of 5 species: 1 from America, 1 from Asia (P. arnottiana (Wight) Hallé, from India) and 3 species from Africa.
