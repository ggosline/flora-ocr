# *Chytranthus* Hooker fil. ¹

**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Authority**: Hooker fil. ¹

## Diagnosis

— Glossolepis GILG, Bot. Jahrb. 24 : 299 (1897); Nat. Pflanzenf., Nachtr. 1 : 228 (1897).

Small trees or monocaulous shrubs polygamous or dioecious, probably not monoecious. Leaves often large, pluri- or multifoliolate, paripinnate, without stipules.

Inflorescences cauline ± scattered along the trunk or grouped around its base, in spiciform racemes approximately 5–50(–80) cm long; rachis bracts moderate and sometimes recaulescent, bearing at their axils brief sessile or pedunculate cymes, most often short and contracted. The raceme very rarely presents branching toward its base.

Flowers zygomorphic, often only 3–6 per small cyme, sometimes all ± oriented to the same side of the raceme; calyx ± urceolate, clothed with felted hairs on the exterior and interior, with (4–) 5 lobes narrowly quincuncial in bud, short (1/2 to 1/4 of the calyx) and opening little, ± coloured pale green, white, cream, brown, brownish, pink or red; 4 or 5, rarely 6 white or yellowish petals inconspicuous at anthesis, ± pubescent, base sometimes narrowed into a claw, apex spatulate sometimes crenulate, furnished on the inner face with a ligule, emarginate or not, forming a sort of pocket whose axis is sometimes ± united; an appendage or appendicule, digitate or ± laminate, sometimes appears on the abaxial face of the ligule; 6–15 stamens with narrow, hairy, slightly subulate filament at the apex; anther with 2 introrse lobes separated by a longitudinal groove, dorsifixed between the lower third and lower fifth of its height; thecae glabrous or pubescent; connective emarginate at the base and sometimes slightly at the apex, plane or convex, sometimes provided with a prominent glandular boss dorsally or apically; extrastaminal disc yellow, fleshy, crescent-shaped with sinuous outline, fleshy, convex or depressed at the middle, rarely accompanied by a satellite (Ch. setosus), very exceptionally annular; ovary hirsute or glabrous, with 3–9 uniovulate locules, outline ± lobed, reduced by abortion in the flower called ♂; style developed sometimes curved in the ♀ flower; stigma not or scarcely distinctly lobed.

Fruit fleshy, succulent at maturity, green, yellow, orange or ± purplish, adorned with ribs: either one rib per locule, or additionally with meridional intercalary ribs ± prominent.   - **2′.** Stamens 11-15 → Sect. 3. Glossolepis. Remarks:
    - **1.** The section Chytranthus is the most complex in the entire genus. The characters of the type species having particular need to be reconsidered, we provide its analysis based on a specimen from San Tomé (Chevalier 14251, P!, region of Boa Entrada): Calyx 10 mm high; 4 petals with rather short ligule, free and devoid of appendicle. 7-8 stamens with thecae appearing glabrous but provided nevertheless toward the apex or base with a few isolated hairs; connective somewhat gibbous above the attachment; ovary hirsute with 3 locules. Inflorescences sometimes provided toward the base with 1-2 branches that may exceed 5 cm, a character not appearing so clearly in any other species of the genus. The floriferous cymules are situated in the axil of acute bracts 2-5 mm and often pedunculate for 0.5-1 cm. According to EXELL the species reaches 5 m in height, the flowers are cream-colored and the fruits edible. The essential foliar characters are the large size of the leaflets, the attenuate lanceolate form of the apex and the length of the acumen.
      - **2.** In the section Chytranthus, at least 13 species have been described on continental materials, many of them are closely related to Ch. mannii. We thought it was more useful, despite the numerous difficulties this raises, to maintain a somewhat narrow conception of the species, provided precise morphological data are presented, possibly new or revised; for this reason very numerous analyses, notably of types or isotypes, were redone.
        - **3.** The number of ovary locules is, with rare exceptions, recognizable in all flowers, even those called ♂ and even in young buds. Herbarium material is well suited to this examination which is sometimes delicate but practically always possible. Magnification 25 is recommended.
          - **4.** The number of locules of the aborted ovary of the ♂ flower is always similar, or always presents the same limits of variation as that of the ♀ flower (or of the fruit) of the same species.
            - **5.** The appendicle which appears between the petal and its ligule is typically a prolongation of the fusion tissue connecting these two parts, but it is sometimes rather a distinctly ligular supplementary lobe, a fold or diverticulum of the abaxial face of the ligule. The appendicle is an organ very subject to variation in its dimensions and in its aspect, as much in the individual as in the species.
