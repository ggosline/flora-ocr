# *Crotalaria* L.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] (Papilionoideae)
**Authority**: L. — Sp. pl. 2: 714 (1753)

Fifteen species.

## Diagnosis

Herbs, shrubs or subshrubs. Stipules filiform to foliaceous or absent. Leaves
simple or 1- or 3-foliolate (up to 7-foliolate outside Gabon), palmate, generally
petiolate; stipels absent. Inflorescences axillary, terminal or leaf-opposed, in
racemes or heads, sometimes in pseudopanicles; bracts present or sometimes
absent, in pairs. Flowers with bracteoles present or absent, on the pedicel or
below the calyx; calyx with (4–)5 subequal lobes; corolla most often yellow, or
sometimes white or blue, the petals striate and clawed, **the standard almost
always with two internal appendages near the base**.

The name is from the Greek for a rattle: the seeds come loose in the inflated
dry pod and rattle, which identifies the genus instantly in fruit.

## Species in region

*C. axillaris*, *C. brevidens*, *C. calycina*, *C. cylindrocarpa*,
*C. dewildemaniana*, *C. glauca*, *C. goreensis*, *C. ochroleuca*,
*C. oligostachya*, *C. ononoides*, *C. pallida*, *C. retusa*, *C. subcapitata*,
*C. tchibangensis*, *C. verrucosa* — 15 in all.

## Notes

***Crotalaria* is a significant poisonous plant genus.** Many species accumulate
**pyrrolizidine alkaloids**, which are cumulative hepatotoxins and carcinogens.
Livestock poisoning is well documented across Africa, and human poisoning has
occurred where *Crotalaria* seed contaminates grain — the cause of outbreaks of
veno-occlusive liver disease. *C. retusa* and *C. pallida*, both here, are
among the implicated species.

The same genus is nonetheless widely used as a **green manure and cover crop**,
grown for nitrogen fixation and nematode suppression, so its agricultural role is
genuinely double-edged and depends on the species and the use.
