# *Cledelavariationde* Cleome afrospina

**Family**: [[Brassicaceae]]
**Authority**: Cleome afrospina

## Diagnosis

1. Lateral nerves 17-28 (median leaflet); petals white or pink, 16-28 mm long; anthers 5-6 mm long (Gabon, Cameroon and elsewhere).

2. Gynophore and pedicel approximately equal (Cameroon and elsewhere except Gabon).

2'. Gynophore approximately 2 times longer than the pedicel (as in C. spinosa and C. hassleriana) (Gabon).

3. Pedicels subglabrous on the lower surface (except for prickles and acicular hairs); racemes without stipular spines or present only at the level of the lowermost bracts (Cameroon and elsewhere; Gabon: Hallé & Cours 870 with pink petals, Louis & al. 1021 with white petals).

3'. Pedicels abundantly and entirely puberulent; racemes armed in their lower part at least as far as bracts no. 7-10, prickles smaller on the upper bracts, sometimes able to disappear completely (as in C. hassleriana) (Gabon: de Wilde & al. 696, pink petals).

1. Lateral nerves fewer in number 7-14 (as in C. spinosa and C. hassleriana); petals small, 5-6 mm long, white; anthers 3 mm long (atypical characters for C. afrospina, recalling a poorly developed C. spinosa); pedicel and gynophore of equal length; pedicel subglabrous on the lower surface, racemes spiny from the base, character typical for C. afrospina (Gabon: Dybowsky 130, Descoings 6229): one may doubt that they belong to this species.

The specimens listed in 1' are entirely distinct from the type of C. afrospina, by their few lateral nerves and their small petals. These collections are poor and incomplete: they lack ripe fruits and seem to correspond to poorly developed individuals, perhaps because they come from impoverished and dry soils. They differ from C. spinosa by their lanceolate floral bracts and by the presence of various prickles on the leaflets and petioles. They were assigned, not without some hesitation, to C. afrospina. The rest of the specimens is remarkable because they present, like C. spinosa and C. hassleriana, a gynophore twice as long as the pedicel; such variation is still only very imperfectly known in Gabon.
