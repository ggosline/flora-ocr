# *Sindora* Miquel

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: Miquel
**Protologue**: Miquel, Fl. Ind. Bat., Suppl. 1 : 287 (1860)

## Diagnosis

Resiniferous trees. Leaves pinnate, opposite (Asian species), or alternate (Gabonese species), ± riddled with translucent dots. Main vein ordinarily terminated by a small apical gland visible on the lower surface of the lamina. Dense reticulation of veinlets. Panicles; bracteoles small, not enveloping the bud. Flowers distichous. Receptacle short. Sepals 4, subvalvate, unequal in width. Petal 1, well developed, sessile, sometimes accompanied by rudimentary petals. Stamens (9–) 10, of which 1 free and 9 unequal, united to each other at the base, of which 2 are larger. Anthers from large to absent. Ovary shortly stipitate, 1–7 ovules. Fruits flattened, dehiscent; valves woody, generally spiny, sometimes unarmed.

Type species: S. sumatrana Miq. from Sumatra.

Indomalayan genus, of approximately 18 species, distributed in Malesia and in southeastern Asia. A single African species, localized exclusively in the coastal region of Gabon.

The disjunction between Indo-Malayan species and the Gabonese species is so extraordinary that one was tempted to recognize in it two genera close but distinct. Separative characters are found in the anatomy of the wood (Normand) (1). But comparative studies of the flowers, fruits and leaflets, undertaken by Pellegrin, De Wit and Léonard, lead to affirming the generic unity of the Asian and African species.
