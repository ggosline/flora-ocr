# *Fimbristylis* Vahl

**Family**: [[Cyperaceae]]
**Authority**: Vahl
**Protologue**: Enum. pl. 2 : 285 (1805)

## Diagnosis

Annual or perennial herbs, small or medium-sized, with stems 15–80 (–120) cm long. Leaves sometimes reduced to sheaths, glabrous; ligule sometimes present; blades flat and linear, scabrous or pilose. Inflorescence usually in a compound anthela, rarely reduced to a single spikelet (F. schoenoides). Spikelets with glumes arranged in a spiral, the 1–2 lower ones usually empty, then bisexual and the upper ones male or sterile; glumes of variable shape, often oval, obovate or broadly lanceolate, apex shortly or longly mucronate, acute or obtuse, margins entire or ciliate.

Flower: perianth segments always absent; stamens 1–3; style with 2–3 branches, in some species the style is very flattened with fringed margin, base of style enlarged, but falling with the rest. Achenes biconvex or trigonous according to the number of style branches, surface smooth, tuberculate or longitudinally striated, never transversely wrinkled as in Bulbostylis; embryo of Fimbristylis type.

Genus of more than 100 species widely distributed in tropical and warm temperate regions. Some are subcosmopolitan (F. dichotoma), while others are highly localized. Most grow in humid habitats, often on the margins of swamps, watercourses, seasonal ponds or damp meadows; 15 species in Gabon.

Taxonomic note: Although very similar, the genera Fimbristylis and Bulbostylis have very different embryos. In Gabon, however, the difference is easier: all Fimbristylis species have glabrous leaves, whereas those of Bulbostylis have tufts of long hairs at the collar of the sheath.

Bibliography: Goetghebeur & Coudijzer (1984a), Haines & Lye (1983), Hoenselaar et al. (2010), Hooper & Napper (1972), Lye (1995, 1997).
