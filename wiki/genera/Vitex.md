# *Vitex* L.

**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 638 [comme « 938 »] (1753) ; Gen. pl. ed. 5 : 285 (1754)

## Diagnosis

(by Pierre MEERTS)

Sp. pl. 2 : 638 [as "938"] (1753) ; Gen. pl. ed. 5 : 285 (1754).

Trees, shrubs or more rarely lianes; stem quadrangular. Leaves opposite or whorled by 3s, petiolate, palmatifid; leaflets (1–)3 to 7, with entire, toothed or more rarely lobed blade, often bearing sessile glands. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia or thyrses at the leaf axils or grouped in loose panicles; bracts small, generally not exceeding the calyx. Flowers: calyx campanulate, with 5 lobes, sometimes slightly bilabiate or truncate; corolla zygomorphic, bilabiate, of varied color, often whitish to bluish, with straight or bent tube, upper lip bilobed, lower lip trilobed; stamens 4, didynamous, inserted in the tube, exserted or included; ovary before anthesis with 2 imperfect locules then with 4 uniovulate locules; style terminal. Fruit a drupe (within the territory of this Flora) containing 1 pyrene with 4 locules; calyx persistent, strongly accrescent, saucer-shaped or cupuliform.

Genus of approximately 250 species principally in the tropical regions of all continents. In Gabon: eleven species and one to be sought.

Notes: The genus Vitex was long placed in the family Verbenaceae, subfamily Viticoideae. The placement of Viticoideae within Labiatae has been well established since Harley et al. (2004). Very recently, the subfamily was reduced to three genera following phylogenetic analyses by Li et al. (2016). Vitex is the only genus of this subfamily represented in Africa.

The genus Vitex is awaiting revision for Africa (Cabral 2013). The most useful synthesis is that of Pieper (1928). Moldenke devoted numerous notes to the genus (notably between 1955 and 1958) which have not always helped to clarify the taxonomy; reference to it will be made, where appropriate, only in the bibliography of certain species.

Apart from the species treated below, Vitex trifolia L. has been collected in Gabon, a species from southeastern Asia and the islands of the Pacific Ocean, sometimes cultivated in Africa, and locally naturalized. This species is easily recognized by its leaflets strongly discolorous, tomentose-greyish on the lower face and subglabrous on the upper face, and by its paniculate inflorescence at the extremity of the branches; the number of leaflets varies from 1 to 5; the single specimen from Gabon has unifoliolate leaves.

B: Aubréville (1950, 1956), Baker & Stapf (1900), Briquet (1895), Cabral (2013), Li et al.
