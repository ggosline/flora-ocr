# *Vitex* L.

**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 638 [comme « 938 »] (1753) ; Gen. pl. ed. 5 : 285 (1754)

## Diagnosis

(by Pierre MEERTS)

Sp. pl. 2 : 638 [as "938"] (1753) ; Gen. pl. ed. 5 : 285 (1754).

Trees, shrubs or more rarely lianes; stem quadrangular. Leaves opposite or whorled by 3s, petiolate, palmatifid; leaflets (1–)3 to 7, with entire, toothed or more rarely lobed blade, often bearing sessile glands. Inflorescence formed of axillary dichasia or thyrses at the leaf axils or grouped in loose panicles; bracts small, generally not exceeding the calyx. Flowers: calyx campanulate, with 5 lobes, sometimes slightly bilabiate or truncate; corolla zygomorphic, bilabiate, of varied color, often whitish to bluish, with straight or bent tube, upper lip bilobed, lower lip trilobed; stamens 4, didynamous, inserted in the tube, exserted or included; ovary before anthesis with 2 imperfect locules then with 4 uniovulate locules; style terminal. Fruit a drupe (within the territory of this Flora) containing 1 pyrene with 4 locules; calyx persistent, strongly accrescent, saucer-shaped or cupuliform.

Genus of approximately 250 species principally in the tropical regions of all continents. In Gabon: eleven species and one to be sought.

Notes: The genus Vitex was long placed in the family Verbenaceae, subfamily Viticoideae. The placement of Viticoideae within Labiatae has been well established since Harley et al. (2004). Very recently, the subfamily was reduced to three genera following phylogenetic analyses by Li et al. (2016). Vitex is the only genus of this subfamily represented in Africa.

The genus Vitex is awaiting revision for Africa (Cabral 2013). The most useful synthesis is that of Pieper (1928). Moldenke devoted numerous notes to the genus (notably between 1955 and 1958) which have not always helped to clarify the taxonomy; reference to it will be made, where appropriate, only in the bibliography of certain species.

Apart from the species treated below, Vitex trifolia L. has been collected in Gabon, a species from southeastern Asia and the islands of the Pacific Ocean, sometimes cultivated in Africa, and locally naturalized. This species is easily recognized by its leaflets strongly discolorous, tomentose-greyish on the lower face and subglabrous on the upper face, and by its paniculate inflorescence at the extremity of the branches; the number of leaflets varies from 1 to 5; the single specimen from Gabon has unifoliolate leaves.

B: Aubréville (1950, 1956), Baker & Stapf (1900), Briquet (1895), Cabral (2013), Li et al.

  - **2′.** Leaflets 3, the median with lamina bearing 4 or 5 secondary veins on each side of the midvein → V. agelaeifolia var. agelaeifolia
    - **3.** A single leaflet (rarely accompanied by a second vestigial leaflet), with lamina obtuse-rounded at the base → V. madiensis subsp. madiensis

      - **4′.** Lamina of leaflets with entire margin (rarely somewhat crenate in V. ciliata ) → couplet **6**

        - **5′.** Petiole and inflorescence axes with short papillose hairs; leaflets (3 to)5, with petiolules scarcely unequal, with lamina practically glabrous, thin, long-acuminate at the apex → [[Vitex_oxycuspis\|*V. oxycuspis*]]

            - **7′.** Dichasia on leafy nodes; ovary setose with hairs, with conical apex; calyx
              - **3.** 4(–5) mm long; branches hollow, occupied by ants → [[Vitex_grandifolia\|*V. grandifolia*]]

                - **8′.** Calyx 1.5–2(–2.5) mm long at anthesis → couplet **11**
                  - **9.** Calyx tomentose with long fine crisp ochre or tawny hairs; lower surface of leaflets and bracts bearing ochre hairs; upper surface of leaflets and bracts blackish-green in the herbarium → [[Vitex_ferruginea\|*V. ferruginea*]]
                  - **9′.** Calyx with short appressed hairs; leaflets and bracts glabrous or with very short grayish hairs on the lower surface; upper surface of lamina and bracts greenish to brownish → couplet **10**
                    - **10.** Ovary crowned with stiff hairs; calyx entirely covered with hairs; inflorescence always with two branches at each node; leaflets thick and coriaceous, with rounded or obtusely acuminate apex; lamina generally concolorous → [[Vitex_doniana\|*V. doniana*]]
                    - **10′.** Ovary glabrous; calyx with scattered hairs; inflorescence with 3 or 4 branches at some nodes; leaflets thin, always with an acute and narrow acumen; lamina strongly discolorous, very pale grayish-green on the lower surface → [[Vitex_zenkeri\|*V. zenkeri*]]
                      - **11.** Leaflets setose-tomentose, soft to the touch; inflorescence axes and calyx setose with tawny or reddish, long and spreading hairs .. V. congolensis var. congolensis
                      - **11′.** Leaflets glabrous except on the veins or somewhat strigulose on the upper surface; inflorescence lacking long tawny spreading hairs or these rare → couplet **12**
                        - **12.** Petiolules 0–2(–4) mm; leaflets spatulate, with their greatest width in the upper third, very long-attenuate at the base, rounded-truncate at the apex and cuspidate, with 9 to 12 secondary veins on each side of the midvein; lower surface with veins pale brownish, generally forming a pattern contrasting with the rest of the lamina; calyx with glands absent or masked by hairs → [[Vitex_ciliata\|*V. ciliata*]]
                        - **12′.** Petiolules 5–15(–25) mm; leaflets with their greatest width ± at the middle, short-attenuate at the base, bearing a fine acumen 1–3 cm long, with 10 to 20 secondary veins on each side of the midvein; lower surface with veins not forming a brownish pattern contrasting with the rest of the lamina; calyx with numerous sessile glands not masked by hairs → [[Vitex_rivularis\|*V. rivularis*]]
