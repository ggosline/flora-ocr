# *Microcalamus* Franch.

**Family**: [[Gramineae]]
**Authority**: Franch.
**Protologue**: FRANCH., Journ. de Bot. III : 282 (1889)

## Diagnosis

Perennial grasses with extravaginal innovations; culms surrounded at the base by a number of leaf sheaths lacking a blade (cataphylls), the first internode often long and creeping. Leaves few in number, blade lanceolate, contracted and articulate at the base. Racemes often reduced, solitary or paniculate.

Spikelets ovoid-lanceolate, in pairs or threes, or solitary.

Glumes oval, with 5 or 7 nerves, shorter than the spikelet; lower floret male or sterile, lemma similar to the glumes, but longer, with 7 nerves; palea bicarinate, somewhat shorter; 2 lodicules. Upper floret hermaphrodite; lemma coriaceous, subulate-acuminate, with 5 nerves; palea bicarinate, shorter, 2 lodicules, 3 stamens. Three species are recorded from Gabon, where two of them appear to be endemic; they are distinguished only by vegetative characters; the herbarium material relating to these species is very limited. 1. From 2 to 8 leaves per flowering culm, glabrous on the upper surface, finely pubescent on the lower surface toward the base, or glabrous; venation very dense, from 12 to 14 nerves per centimetre. ..... 1. M. barbinodis.

1'. 1 to 3 complete leaves per flowering culm, venation less dense (from 7 to 10 nerves per cm). 2. Leaf blades glabrous on the upper surface, pubescent below, at least toward the base or when young ..... 2. M. aspidistrula. 2'. Leaf blades sparsely pubescent on the upper surface with tuberculate hairs at the base, glabrous or nearly so on the lower surface.

## Key to the species

1. Leaf blades glabrous above, pubescent below ..... M. barbinodis
1'. Leaf blades pubescent above and below, often purple ..... M. convallarioides

38.2 Microcalamus convallarioides Stapf

Fl. Gab.: 20.

I doubt that this species, distinguished by its indumentum and by its leaves often colored purple, is synonymous with M. barbinodis, as proposed by Clayton & Renvoize (1986).
