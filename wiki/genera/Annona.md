# *Annona* L.

**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp.Pl.,cd.1: 536 (1753);Gen. Pl.,ed.5: 241 (1954)1.-Engler et Dieis,in Engler,Monogr.Afr. Pflanzenf.6:76 (19o1).--R.E. Fries,Act. Hort.Berg. 10,2:197 (1931).-Rob.ct Ghesq.,Bull.Soc.Roy.Bot.Belg.67:7-5o(1934). -R.E.Frics in Engl.et Prantl, Nat.Pflanzenf.,ed.2,17a,2: 142 (1959)

## Diagnosis

Small trees, shrubs or rhizomatous suffrutices, glabrous or with indumentum of simple hairs. Buds globose, conical or triquetrous. Flowers generally solitary or in pauciflorous inflorescences, terminal, opposite to the leaves or extra-axillary. Bracteoles persistent or caducous. Sepals 3, valvate, much smaller than the petals. Petals 6, in two equal or unequal whorls, free or connate at the base, the outer ones valvate, the inner ones valvate or imbricate, sometimes rudimentary or absent. Stamens very numerous, linear-oblong; filament short, thecae extrorse; connective dilated above the anthers, often papillose. Carpels numerous, free in the flower or ± connate, cylindrical, stigmas claviform, ± muriculate. 1 ovule only, erect.

Syncarpous fruits, fleshy, ovoid or globose to cylindrical. Seeds numerous embedded in the pulp. Aril visible or absent.

Type species: A. muricata L.

Tropical genus comprising about a hundred species, most of them American. Only 4 indigenous species are present in Africa and Madagascar with several subspecies and varieties: A. glauca Thonn. has a very restricted range in West Africa, A. glabra L. is a mangrove species, whilst A. senegalensis Pers. and A. stenophylla Engl. et Diels are savanna species much more widely distributed. Four other species, originating from America, are introduced and cultivated in Africa.

1.- For the spelling Annona versus Anona (Miller Ph., Gardeners Dict. 4, fig. 23 (1755) see J.E. Dandy, Regnum vegetabile, 51:27 (1967).

Like Anonidium, Annona are part of an evolutionary series containing genera among the most evolved in the family, tending towards true syncarpium.

Shrub or small tree reaching 10 m in height; twigs glabrous. Leaves with petiole 8–15 (20) mm long, channelled above, glabrous; limb papyraceous, ovate-elliptic to oblong-elliptic, 5–15 cm long, 2–7 cm wide, rounded to acute at the base, slightly decurrent on the petiole, attenuate-acute to shortly acuminate at the apex, glabrous on both faces. Lateral nerves 9–14 pairs, prominent on both faces; reticulum well distinct.

Flowers yellowish, solitary, extra-axillary, with pedicel 1.5–2 cm long, glabrous, slightly bearing below the middle a small ovate bracteole, 1–1.5 mm long. Sepals broadly ovate, apiculate at the apex, 3–5 mm long, glabrous. Petals glabrous, the outer ones broadly ovate, 1.5–2.5 (3) cm long, the inner ones somewhat shorter. Stamens oblong, 3–4 mm long, filament 0.6–0.8 mm long, anthers linear extrorse, connective dilated above the thecae and papillose. Carpels oblong, glabrous, as long as the stamens, agglutinate to one another, stigmas claviform. Receptacle ovoid-conical.

Fruits green, ovoid-globose, smooth or barely areolate, 5–12 cm long. Seeds numerous, oblong, flattened, marginate, 15–18 mm long, 10–14 mm wide, with testa dull beige-ochraceous; aril not visible.

Type: Catesby, Nat. Hist. Carolina, Florida and Bahama Isl. 2: 64, tab. 64 (1730–48).

Species of mangrove and marshy terrain, found along the entire coast of West and Equatorial Africa, from Senegal to Congo. Probably native to America where it is widely distributed along the Atlantic coast, from Florida to southern Brazil; this species, which occupies a very vast range of distribution, can be considered indigenous in Africa.

Vernacular name: Sea custard apple.

The fruit is edible but rather insipid.
