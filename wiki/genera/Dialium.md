# *Dialium* Linné

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: Linné
**Protologue**: Linné, Mantissa plantarum 1 : 3 (1767)

## Diagnosis

Trees. Leaves imparipinnate; 3-21 leaflets generally alternate, sometimes subalternate, rarely opposite; often with dense reticulum of veinlets and veins. Inflorescences in terminal panicles, sometimes axillary, often densely flowered. Buds pyramidal, generally pubescent, or ellipsoid. Flowers ♀ generally zygomorphic. Sepals 5 (-6) subequal, imbricate. Petals 0, or 1 (-3), rarely 5, very small, caducous, inserted on the margin of a relatively large and pubescent disc. Stamens generally 2, rarely 5, exceptionally 10 (D. Schlechteri Harms from East Africa); anthers basifixed, dehiscent by longitudinal slits opening at the apex. Ovary sessile or shortly stipitate, 2-ovulate. Fruits ± subglobose, ellipsoid or discoid, indehiscent, of small dimensions. Exocarp hard, thin, generally velvety dark brown, rarely smooth and glabrous. Mesocarp pulpy whitish, enclosing one (-2) compressed seed.

Type species of the genus: Dialium indum L. Java.

The Dialium are very easy to recognize by their racemes of small fruits flat or globose, generally velvety black, and also by their small flowers, with the typical structure where the stamens and the very small petals (when present) are inserted on the margin of the disc. At the centre is found the hairy ovary with its style curved towards the stamens.

The species are separated with difficulty by the flowers and fruits. Distinctions based on leaves are sometimes imprecise. The number of leaflets is a fairly reliable criterion. The density of the reticulum provides good characters. This is sometimes finely

---

meshed and prominent, often on both surfaces. Sometimes on the contrary it is rather lax. But there are intermediate cases that are difficult. These imprecise characters of specification, and insufficient documentation, make the classification of species very delicate. The one we propose, quite different from that of Pellegrin for Gabon and Cameroon, or that of Steyaert for Congo, is not free from uncertainties.

The Dialium are generally medium-sized trees. The bark, when cut, generally exudes a sticky, reddish liquid. It is a good practical character. The wood is very hard, dark brown in colour, with an unpleasant odour in the fresh state; it is remarkable for the tiered structure of the rays and for the dense network of circum-medullary lines. These are often species of water margins. The acidulous-sweet pulp of the fruit is sometimes edible and sought after by animals.
