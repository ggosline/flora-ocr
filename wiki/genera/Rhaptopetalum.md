## Diagnosis

Shrubs or small trees; bark fibrous with pink or red sapwood; heartwood reddish; branches with nodosities (ramiflory); young branches rarely angular, glabrous or papillose; terminal bud enclosed in a young leaf rolled into a ± tapering cornet and making the growth, normally sympodial in Scytopetalacea, appear monopodial; mass of blunt cataphylls flattened, pressed against the branch, supra-axillary on mature branches and sometimes giving rise (cf. Breteler 2754) to leafy innovations. Leaves, shortly petiolate (0–10 mm); blade coriaceous, sometimes subcoriaceous, rarely papyraceous, elliptic or elliptic-oblong or obovate, acuminate, often of fairly large dimensions, 4–12 pairs of lateral curved nervures anastomosing near the margin, the latter entire, parallel nervilles in a ± perpendicular reticulum to the median nervure, very often glandular punctations beneath the blade.

Racemes very contracted into pseudofascicles, pauci- or pluriflorous, generally fragrant, on defoliated branches (ramiflory) and also supra-axillary, sometimes axillary; bracts and bracteoles blunt, minute, early caducous, pedicels articulate at the apex. Calyx patelliforme, cupuliform or flared cupuliform, sessile or with base narrowed ± longly, with margin entire or crenulate-festooned, often split into sectors sometimes rounded at the sides with rolled margin¹, or clearly lobed; corolla bud white,

pink or red, ellipsoid, often constricted at the base, pointed or rounded at the apex, smooth, opening into 3–4(–5) lobes, thick and coriaceous, turned outward at anthesis and bearing the longitudinal striations of the androecium; numerous (30–40) yellow stamens attached to the internal base of the corolla, short filaments ± connate with each other and long anthers with poricidal dehiscence at the apex; ovary superior or semi-inferior, 3–5(–6?) locules with 2–3 or up to 12–15 ovules per locule, style filiform with undifferentiated stigma.

Capsular fruits crustaceous or subcoriaceous drupaceous, globulous, sometimes ellipsoid or ovoid, often with seemingly late dehiscence, by 3–5 valves or irregular ruptures; 1 to several seeds in a mass surrounded by a mucilaginous tegmen and with ruminate albumen.

TYPE SPECIES: Rhaptopetalum coriaceum Oliv.

This genus, essentially of Central Africa, is represented in Cameroon by 5 species (R. breteleri, R. coriaceum, R. depressum, R. pachyphyllum, R. sessilifolium), one of which (R. coriaceum) is found again in south-eastern Nigeria, in Macias Nguema (former Fernando Po), as well as in Gabon where R. pachyphyllum also exists as well as 2 particular species (R. belingense, R. sindarense). In Cameroon the presence of a species was formerly mentioned, but which appears doubtful, (R. roseum) from the mountains of eastern Zaire; a ninth species (R. evrardii Germain) remains to this day considered as endemic to Zaire. In West Africa (Ivory Coast, Ghana), the last species of the genus is encountered (R. beguei Mangenot).
