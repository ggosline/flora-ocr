# *Schumanniophyton* Harms

**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Authority**: Harms
**Protologue**: Harms, Pflanzenf. Nachtr. 1:313 (1897)

## Diagnosis

Shrub with straight trunk, reaching 2–17 m in height and 5–15 cm in diameter; orthotropic stem always single, exceptionally forked by accident. Lateral branches unbranched, regularly whorled in 3, 4 or 5 and comprising either a single elongated internode (subgenus Schumanniophyton), or several by sympodium (subgenus Chalazocarpus). The branches are caducous and leave at regular levels on the trunk, oval scars that remain visible for a long time. Interpetiolar stipules triangular, protecting the terminal bud of the stem,

---

then ± caducous. Large or very large petiolate or sessile leaves, some axilling the branches, others grouped on the latter where they may be ± asymmetrical.

Sessile inflorescences arising only at the apices of lateral axes (before the regrowth of caducous leaves in Chalazocarpus). A voluminous stipular system protects the young inflorescence which is preformed very far in advance. Prefloration contorted. Calyx tubular. Corolla large with very long and narrow tube bearing 5–11 lobes. Stamens sessile medifixed with 2 pollen sacs oriented somewhat laterally. Pollen in simple tricolporate grains ± on the axes. Style slender. Stigma subcapitate scarcely divided into 2–4 thick lobes. Ovary with 2–5 locules each containing a peltate placenta with axile linear insertion. Numerous anatropous ovules strongly attached, globular, unitegmic and tenuinucellate.

Fruits spheroid with calyx tube persistent or not, thick-walled ± dry and indehiscent. Seeds embedded in fleshy placental tissue. Embryo straight surrounded by albumen. Epigeal germination.

The type species is Schumanniophyton magnificum which comprises several varieties and forms. Outside of Gabon two other species are sometimes considered as belonging to distinct genera: Assidora A. Chev. and Chalazocarpus Hiern. The range of the genus thus extends from Sierra Leone to Angola and over almost the entire Congo Basin.

The Schumanniophyton are plants remarkable for their habit of perfect regularity, for their very reduced number of aerial vegetative meristems, for their whorls, the great dimension of the laminas and especially for their phyllomorphic branches; they have aroused the wonder of numerous botanists even when certain of their most remarkable peculiarities were not known. In 1962, Francis Hallé discovered the internal rhythm of preformation of the inflorescences; in 1966 the same researcher demonstrated the existence of an endophytic Siphymocete developing in the cerids of the sealed cavities that constitute all the preformed buds of the plant. Description common to the different varieties or forms:

Shrub attaining 9–17 m in height and 15 cm in diameter, with spherical or pyramidal crown. The wood is golden yellow, semi-hard, with fine grain. The pith is voluminous, quadrangular in cross-section. The trunk is simple, accidentally forked; it is composed of a succession of short internodes, and regularly ornamented by the oval scars left by the fall of verticillate branches and those, triangular, of the leaves of the primary axis. Terminal bud large, covered very tightly by the stipules of the last developed leaves which are verticillate by (3) 4 (5). Triangular acute stipules 1–2 cm long, sheltering in a viscous orangey wax the leaf primordia of the axillary branches. These latter develop ± horizontally and are all approximately equal, 40–50 cm long, comprising a single developed internode terminated by a pseudoverticil of three leaves. Two of these leaves are arranged laterally in a horizontal plane, and their very reduced stipules have an apex often twisted into a hook; the third leaf belongs to a different node separated from the first by a zero internode, it has no opposite foliar organ, an extreme case of anisophylly, and its stipules are fused into a large conical hood, with acuminate apex often curved into a hook. This stipular hood shelters tightly the inflorescence primordia which are invariably preformed even at the juvenile stage of the branch.

Leaves of the stem and leaves of the branches of approximately similar dimensions. The lateral leaves of the branches differ by the asymmetry of the lamina base; the other leaves are entirely symmetric. Petiole variable, sometimes null, often short and thickened, rarely attaining 2–5 cm in length, with margins of the

Lamina variable ± narrowly obovate and attenuate in its lower half, attaining 50–140 × 15–50 cm or only 35 × 11 in the smallest flowering specimens of the forma trimerum. Base of the lamina highly variable, acute in var. magnificum, ± narrowly rounded or cordate in the other varieties; apex terminated by a brief acute acumen. Upper surface glabrous; lower surface very finely pubérulent or glabrous according to the varieties, frequently with scattered hairs on the nerves and on the margins. 18–40 pairs of secondary nerves; fine reticule ± apparent.

Inflorescences terminal on the lateral branches, formed by a capitate group of 10–53 flowers with synchronous opening, entomogamous, sessile, juxtaposed without apparent order on a glabrous circular surface 5–50 mm in diam., forming the apical platform of the branch. The stipular hood persists, torn ± regularly. Calyces subprismatic from reciprocal pressure, 8–12 mm long, tubulous for 2–6 mm, pubescent or rarely glabrous (f. Letestuanum), with small acute pubescent lobes. Corolla white or white-yellow with tube 6–12 cm in length and 3–6 mm in diam., very fragrant, with abundant sweet nectar, ± pubérulent externally, finely pubescent internally; 5–9 (11) lobes attaining 12–30 × 5–11 mm, attenuate acute, contorted. Anthers 9–15 × 1.5–2 mm, medifixed, sessile, hidden at the level of the throat or even slightly below, yellowish; connective massive, thickened dorsally, prominent ventrally between the two thecae, massive at the extremities for env. 1 mm, acute or obtuse and sometimes slightly pilose at the summit. Pollen whitish 3–4-porate. Style filiform with ± scattered pubescence in its upper part; stigma thickened attaining the upper level of the anthers or exsert at the level of the throat, with 3–4(5) stigmatic lobes bossy fleshy, yellowish, briefly divergent. Ovary with (2)3–4(5) locules. Peltate placentas 1–2 mm, ovoid, subcordate, foveolate; numerous ovoid ovules.

Fruits brownish or grayish subsessile or entirely sessile, grouped up to 7–9 at a time and long persistent. One can find on the mother plant, at successive levels, infructescences of different age (6 months or 1 year difference?). The fruit wall is ± sieve-like with small lipped lenticels; the calycinal limb is ± degraded by decrepitude and encircles the apical depression of the fruit: this depression zone is sometimes minimal but can increase through accretion to 15 mm in diam. The fruit shape, somewhat variable, ± globular 2–6 cm in diam., recalls the medlar or the fig. Cross-section shows a thick wall, yellow ochraceous. Fleshy placental mass, golden yellow, embracing irregularly the seeds, and divided by very thin partitions ordinarily numbering 3 or 4; the number of carpels is always variable in well-furnished infructescences. Seeds numbering 10–54 in a fruit, brown to black, with dull testa. White and hard albumen; embryo yellow 9 × 5.5 mm; radicle straight; cotyledons suborbicular cordate at the base, with 5 basal nerves, with pairs of free stipules, not fused in the interpetiolar space. Germination capacity evaluated at one month. Epigeal germination. Seedling with opposite leaves to a height of 30–40 cm, then verticillate by 3, finally by 4 toward 1 m.
