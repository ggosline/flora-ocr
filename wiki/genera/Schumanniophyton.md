# *Schumanniophyton* Harms

**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Authority**: Harms
**Protologue**: Harms, Pflanzenf. Nachtr. 1:313 (1897)

## Diagnosis

Shrub with straight trunk, reaching 2–17 m in height and 5–15 cm in diameter; orthotropic stem always single, exceptionally forked by accident. Lateral branches unbranched, regularly whorled in 3, 4 or 5 and comprising either a single elongated internode (subgenus Schumanniophyton), or several by sympodium (subgenus Chalazocarpus). The branches are caducous and leave at regular levels on the trunk, oval scars that remain visible for a long time. Interpetiolar stipules triangular, protecting the terminal bud of the stem,

---

then ± caducous. Large or very large petiolate or sessile leaves, some axilling the branches, others grouped on the latter where they may be ± asymmetrical.

Sessile inflorescences arising only at the apices of lateral axes (before the regrowth of caducous leaves in Chalazocarpus). A voluminous stipular system protects the young inflorescence which is preformed very far in advance. Prefloration contorted. Calyx tubular. Corolla large with very long and narrow tube bearing 5–11 lobes. Stamens sessile medifixed with 2 pollen sacs oriented somewhat laterally. Pollen in simple tricolporate grains ± on the axes. Style slender. Stigma subcapitate scarcely divided into 2–4 thick lobes. Ovary with 2–5 locules each containing a peltate placenta with axile linear insertion. Numerous anatropous ovules strongly attached, globular, unitegmic and tenuinucellate.

Fruits spheroid with calyx tube persistent or not, thick-walled ± dry and indehiscent. Seeds embedded in fleshy placental tissue. Embryo straight surrounded by albumen. Epigeal germination.

The type species is Schumanniophyton magnificum which comprises several varieties and forms. Outside of Gabon two other species are sometimes considered as belonging to distinct genera: Assidora A. Chev. and Chalazocarpus Hiern. The range of the genus thus extends from Sierra Leone to Angola and over almost the entire Congo Basin.

The Schumanniophyton are plants remarkable for their habit of perfect regularity, for their very reduced number of aerial vegetative meristems, for their whorls, the great dimension of the laminas and especially for their phyllomorphic branches; they have aroused the wonder of numerous botanists even when certain of their most remarkable peculiarities were not known. In 1962, Francis Hallé discovered the internal rhythm of preformation of the inflorescences; in 1966 the same researcher demonstrated the existence of an endophytic Siphymocete developing in the cerids of the sealed cavities that constitute all the preformed buds of the plant.
