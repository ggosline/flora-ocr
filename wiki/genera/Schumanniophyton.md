# *Schumanniophyton* Harms

**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Authority**: Harms
**Protologue**: Harms, Pflanzenf. Nachtr. 1:313 (1897)

## Diagnosis

Shrub with straight trunk, reaching 2–17 m in height and 5–15 cm in diameter; orthotropic stem always single, exceptionally forked by accident. Lateral branches unbranched, regularly whorled in 3, 4 or 5 and comprising either a single elongated internode (subgenus Schumanniophyton), or several by sympodium (subgenus Chalazocarpus). The branches are caducous and leave at regular levels on the trunk, oval scars that remain visible for a long time. Interpetiolar stipules triangular, protecting the terminal bud of the stem,

---

then ± caducous. Large or very large petiolate or sessile leaves, some axilling the branches, others grouped on the latter where they may be ± asymmetrical.

Sessile inflorescences arising only at the apices of lateral axes (before the regrowth of caducous leaves in Chalazocarpus). A voluminous stipular system protects the young inflorescence which is preformed very far in advance. Prefloration contorted. Calyx tubular. Corolla large with very long and narrow tube bearing 5–11 lobes. Stamens sessile medifixed with 2 pollen sacs oriented somewhat laterally. Pollen in simple tricolporate grains ± on the axes. Style slender. Stigma subcapitate scarcely divided into 2–4 thick lobes. Ovary with 2–5 locules each containing a peltate placenta with axile linear insertion. Numerous anatropous ovules strongly attached, globular, unitegmic and tenuinucellate.

Fruits spheroid with calyx tube persistent or not, thick-walled ± dry and indehiscent. Seeds embedded in fleshy placental tissue. Embryo straight surrounded by albumen. Epigeal germination.

The type species is Schumanniophyton magnificum which comprises several varieties and forms. Outside of Gabon two other species are sometimes considered as belonging to distinct genera: Assidora A. Chev. and Chalazocarpus Hiern. The range of the genus thus extends from Sierra Leone to Angola and over almost the entire Congo Basin.

The Schumanniophyton are plants remarkable for their habit of perfect regularity, for their very reduced number of aerial vegetative meristems, for their whorls, the great dimension of the laminas and especially for their phyllomorphic branches; they have aroused the wonder of numerous botanists even when certain of their most remarkable peculiarities were not known. In 1962, Francis Hallé discovered the internal rhythm of preformation of the inflorescences; in 1966 the same researcher demonstrated the existence of an endophytic Siphymocete developing in the cerids of the sealed cavities that constitute all the preformed buds of the plant. Description common to the different varieties or forms: Shrub reaching 9–17 m in height and 15 cm in diameter, with a spherical or pyramidal crown. The wood is golden yellow, semi-hard, fine-grained. The pith is voluminous, quadrangular in cross-section. The trunk is simple, occasionally forked; it is composed of a succession of short internodes, and regularly ornamented by the oval scars left by the fall of verticillate branches and those, triangular, of the leaves of the primary axis. Terminal bud large, tightly covered by the stipules of the last developed leaves which are verticillate by (3) 4 (5). Triangular acute stipules 1–2 cm long, sheltering in an orangish viscous wax the leaf primordia of the axillary branches. These latter develop ± horizontally and are all roughly equal, 25–50 cm long, bearing only one developed internode terminated by a pseudoverticil of three leaves. Two of these leaves are arranged laterally in a horizontal plane, and their much reduced stipules have an apex often twisted into a hook; the third leaf belongs to a different node separated from the first by a null internode, it has no opposite foliar organ, an extreme case of anisophylly, and its stipules are fused into a large conical hood, with an acuminate apex often curved into a hook. This stipular hood tightly shelters the inflorescence primordia which are invariably preformed even at the juvenile stage of the branch. Leaves of the stem and leaves of the branches of roughly similar dimensions. The lateral leaves of the branches differ by the asymmetry of the blade base; the other leaves are entirely symmetric. Petiole variable, sometimes absent, often short and thickened, rarely reaching 2 to 5 cm in length, with margins of the <<<---SECTION-BREAK--->>> channel ± foliate through decurrence of the blade. Blade variable ± obovate and attenuate in its lower half, reaching 50–140 × 15–50 cm or only 35 × 11 in the smallest flowering specimens of the trimerum form. Blade base highly variable, acute in var. magnificum, ± narrowly rounded or cordate in the other varieties; apex terminated by a brief acute acumen. Upper face glabrous; lower face very finely pubérulent or glabrous according to variety, frequently with scattered hairs on the nerves and on the margins. 18–40 pairs of secondary nerves; fine reticulation ± apparent. Inflorescences terminal on the lateral branches, formed of a capituliform group of 10–53 flowers with synchronous anthesis, entomogamous, sessile, juxtaposed without apparent order on a circular glabrous area 5–50 mm in diam., forming the apical plateau of the branch. The stipular hood persists, torn ± regularly. Calyces subprismatic through reciprocal pressure, 8–12 mm long, tubular for 2–6 mm, pubescent or rarely glabrous (f. Letestuanum), with small acute pubescent lobes.
