# *Scaphopetalum* Masters.

**Family**: [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Malvaceae]] s.l. — *Sterculiaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Authority**: Masters.
**Protologue**: Mast., Journ. Linn. Soc. 10:28 (1869)

## Diagnosis

Shrubs or small trees provided with stipules. Leaves alternate; shortly petiolate, with entire elliptic blade. Lateral veins pinnate with a basal pair.

Inflorescence few- or many-flowered, axillary or cauline, briefly contracted subfasciculate, or more or less elongately pedunculate, sometimes very slenderly so. Bracteoles small, acute; often 3 small prefolia on the pedicel. Floral bud globulose, angular or not, with calyx concealing the corolla.

Flower ∇ yellow, white, red or violaceous. Calyx with 5 variable sepals, sometimes more or less connate with each other. Petals cucullate dorsally convex, with more or less angular apex. Stamens connate in a wide membranous funnel or collar basally fused to the petals, with pentagonal outline, bearing at the angles 5 aggregates of sessile anthers; each aggregate is capped by a petal and ordinarily comprises 6 pollen sacs, i.e. 3 anthers; the collar is more or less dentate in short staminodes between the groups of anthers. Ovary superior oblong, sessile, often pubescent, with 5 locules few- or multiovulate; ovules biseriate sometimes superposed. Style single narrow, erect.

Fruit capsular dehiscent, adorned with ridges, pustules or horns.

Seeds provided with an aril, few in number in each locule.

The Scaphopetalum are forest understory plants of the dense forests of Africa. Their distribution reaches westward to Liberia, eastward to the Yangambi region in Belgian Congo, and southward to the limits of Mayombe. About twenty species have been described; this number could be reduced by more than a third.

Five species are known from Gabon. Sc. zenkeri K. Schum., only cited in the key, and known from Cameroon,

Gab., Mém. Soc. Bot.: 47 (1950-1951). Shrub reaching 2-4 m tall, often less, with plagiotropic branches. Extremities villous. Stipules subpersistent 3-13 mm long, acutely attenuate, somewhat pubescent. Petiole 3-8 mm long, pubescent. Blade glabrous or somewhat hairy, 11-25 cm long, by 4-10 cm wide, oblong, with base somewhat asymmetric and cordate, enlarged toward the apex and terminated by an acute acumen. Remarkable swelling of the blade toward the base, and on one side only of the midvein. 8-11 pairs of lateral veins; midvein clearly prominent on the upper surface of the blade. Flowers dark red, axillary fasciculate, on a glomérule or on a more or less contracted bracteolate axis. Pedicel slender 5-10 mm long, bearing below the articulation an involucre of 3 small ± acute bracteoles. Bud subglobose with pyramidal apex. Calyx with 5 lobes deeply emarginate 4-6 mm long, lanceolate, puberulent at least on the margins, glabrous or piliferous externally. 5 petals yellow, red or white almost as long or scarcely longer than the sepals, cucullate, with apex dominated by a small boss. Androecial collar pentagonal funnel-shaped; membrane margin sinuous between the stamen fascicles. Ovary short with fine puberulence at least at the apex, with 5 biovulate locules, with subulate style. Dry fruit 10-12 mm long by 12-20 mm wide, apex spatulate, contour 5-lobed, piliferous, tearing apically to release up to 9 seeds; calyx persistent at the base of the fruit. Seed hairy, rounded, approximately 6-7 mm, with sinuous aril. Type of the species at Brussels. Sc. thonneri var. klainei Pierre ex N. Hallé var. nov. This variety reaches only ca. half a meter tall; it moreover differs from the typical form by its smaller, thinner leaves with ciliate margins, and by its fruit in which each of the lobes is ornamented with a winged keel. Type of the variety: Klaine 2142 fr. (P.). The pocket at the blade base opens on the lower surface, either to the left or to the right of the midvein. Ants at different stages of development are observed there, and Coccids which the ants appear to exploit (E. Laurent).
