# *Dalbergia* L.f.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] (Papilionoideae)
**Authority**: L.f., nom. cons. — Suppl. pl.: 52 (1782)
**Synonym**: *Ecastaphyllum* P.Browne (1756), nom. rej.
**Treatment by**: O. Lachenaud

Twenty-seven species — the second largest genus in [[vol49]], and the one with
the greatest conservation profile in the entire corpus.

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or lianas, unarmed or spiny, **often bearing hooked prehensile
branchlets** — the climbing mechanism of the lianescent species and a reliable
generic character. Stipules generally small and caducous. Leaves imparipinnate,
unifoliolate in *D. ecastaphyllum* and *D. louisii*; stipels absent; leaflets
alternate or rarely subopposite, ± unequal, the upper generally larger.
Inflorescences axillary or terminal, in panicles of racemes or more rarely
simple racemes; bracts generally small. Flowers with two short bracteoles.

*D. adamii*, *D. adiantifolia*, *D. afzeliana*, *D. bakeri*, *D. crispa*,
*D. ealaensis*, *D. ecastaphyllum*, *D. fouilloyana*, *D. gilbertii*,
*D. gilletii*, *D. hostilis*, *D. lancistipula*, *D. lastoursvillensis*,
*D. librevillensis*, *D. louisii*, *D. mayumbensis*, *D. micheliana*,
*D. nervosa*, *D. ngounyensis*, *D. obliquifoliolata*, *D. pachycarpa*,
*D. rufa*, *D. saxatilis*, *D. semiapplanata*, *D. setifera*, *D. stenopetala*,
*D. xylocarpa* — 27 in all.

## Notes

***Dalbergia* is the rosewood genus, and the whole genus is CITES-listed.**
All species were placed on **CITES Appendix II** in 2017 in response to
large-scale illegal logging for the rosewood trade, principally to East Asian
furniture markets. That listing applies to the African species as well as the
better-known Malagasy and Asian ones, so every taxon on this page is regulated
in international trade. Several African species have been heavily exploited.

This makes *Dalbergia* the single most legally significant genus in this corpus
— the practical consequence of a determination here is different from that for
any other genus, and identifications may carry enforcement weight.

*D. ecastaphyllum* is a coastal shrub of strand and mangrove margins, widespread
on both sides of the Atlantic, and is one of the sources of Brazilian red
propolis.
