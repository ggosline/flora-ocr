Annual or perennial herbaceous plants, erect or more rarely prostrate, most often abundantly branched from the base. Stem unarmed or armed with stipular spines, which may be glabrous, furnished with prickles, and may present glandular puberulence or pubescence. Leaves alternate, evenly distributed on the stems, petiolate, stipulate, simple or most often palmately compound with 3-5-7- and in certain cases 9-12 leaflets linear to broadly elliptic or rhombic, with entire or sometimes vaguely dentate margins, sessile or shortly petiolulate, not articulate at the base. Petioles usually shorter than the leaflets, not articulate at the base. Stipules often rudimentary and glanduliform, sometimes transformed into spines (C. afrospina and certain American species). Hairs simple, pluricellular, terminated or not by a gland, spreading, generally of small size, sometimes transformed into prickles (C. afrospina and certain American species).

Inflorescences in terminal racemes or flowers axillary to the ordinary leaves. Bracts foliaceous or sometimes absent, simple or compound, early caducous or often long-persistent; bracteoles absent. Flowers zygomorphic, hermaphrodite, long pedicellate. Receptacle small, club-shaped. Sepals 4, open prefloration, free or connate at their base, equal or subequal, often subulate, caducous or sometimes marcescent. Petals 4, imbricate prefloration, thin and fragile, as long as or much longer than the sepals, subequal, most often ascending, caducous; petal limb oblong or obovate, basal claw narrow; the petals, glabrous or sometimes weakly puberulent on the lower surface, may be white, yellow, pink or dark purple, either all the same colour, or with an upper petal bearing a band or spot near the claw. Androgynophore either absent, or short or sometimes elongate, glabrous. Stamens 2(-6) or very numerous, either all fertile, or partly transformed into staminodes; filaments exsert or not, slender and fragile, rectilinear or more often inclined, free or sometimes connate at their base, caducous; anthers linear or oblong, slightly dorsifixed below the midpoint, versatile and curved at the apex with age, subsequently caducous; staminodes rare but present throughout the genus, appearing on the dorsal part of the flower, free from their base, much shorter than the fertile stamens; staminodial filaments often enlarged into a club at the apex or sometimes even terminated by minute rudimentary anthers; these filaments are caducous. Gynophore absent, short or more often elongate and slender, generally glabrous. Ovary linear or narrowly elliptic, unilocular, bicarpellate with 2 parietal placentas; ovules numerous in 2 rows on each placenta. Stigma sessile or terminating a filiform style, minute, capitate or truncate, with entire margin; style persistent.
