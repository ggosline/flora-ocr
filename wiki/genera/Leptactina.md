# *Leptactina* Hook. f.

**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Authority**: Hook. f.
**Protologue**: J. D. Hooker, Ic. Pl. II : 74 (1871)

## Diagnosis

Shrubs very rarely somewhat scandent, 2–5 m tall. Stipules broad, acute or not. Petiole channelled. Leaves glabrous or not, elliptic, generally acuminate, sometimes unequal: slight anisophylly. Domatia generally distinct and pubescent.

Inflorescences at the ends of lateral branchlets, few- to multiflowered, cymose, ± contracted, glabrous or pubescent. Flowers hermaphrodite, large. Calyx oval or subconical, sometimes longitudinally veined at the level of the ovary, with 5 approximately equal lobes, foliaceous, long, contorted in bud, erect at anthesis, persistent, striate with veins parallel when dry, furnished at the base with cereocysts in the form of hairs or small scales. Corolla white, tube long or very long, narrow, with 5 lobes attenuate acute, spreading, contorted in bud. 5 stamens sessile, inserted below the throat level, hidden or with apex exsert, fixed above the mid-length, often towards the upper third. Style generally furnished at the level of the anthers with ascending hairs collecting pollen before anthesis. Two stigmatic lobes ± linear, rarely exceeding the throat. Annular disc, small, fleshy. Ovary 2-locular; in the axis of each locule is inserted a fleshy multilobate placenta, ovoid or ± oblong.

Fruit ± globose to oblong-conical, moderately fleshy, green, yellow or orange. Seeds very numerous, c. 2 mm, smooth, shining, generally angular from mutual pressure (except L. Pynærtii), with a distinct punctiform hilum opening into a basal lacuna. Embryo straight.

Pollen 3–4 colporate, very small, not ornamented in L. densiflora Hook. f.

Type species: L. Mannii Hook. f., described from NW Gabon.

---

Nearly 25 species from tropical Africa, most from regions of dense forest. 2 species in West Africa; 3 in Angola; 6–8 in East Africa. 7 species at least from Gabon, plus 2 or 3 others insufficiently known: L. polyneura K. Krause and L. Tessmannii K. Krause.

The beauty of the floral bouquets is worth emphasizing for a possible horticultural use of these plants.

### KEY TO LEPTACTINA

1. Stipules gibbose-cucullate and refracted; sepals elliptic, c. 2–6 × 0.4–1 cm.

2. 7–11 pairs of secondary veins; pedicels most often less than 5 mm long..... 1. L. Mannii

2'. 12–25 pairs of secondary veins; pedicels most often more than 5–10 mm long..... 2. L. Arnoldiana

1'. Stipules not gibbose and rarely refracted; sepals variable.

3. Inflorescence multiflowered contracted; stipules much broader than the branchlet, twice or more towards the lower third.
