# *Didymosalpinx* Keay emend. N. Hallé

**Family**: [[Rubiaceae]]
**Authority**: Keay emend. N. Hallé
**Protologue**: R. W. J. Keay, Bull. J. B. Brux. 28 : 61 (1958)

## Diagnosis

Small shrubs entirely glabrous, erect or subsarmentose, with certain lateral branches modified into supra-axillary spines arranged at right angles, 0.5–2 cm long and very acute. Stipules weakly connate at the base, acute or acuminate subulate. Flowers axillary solitary, arranged in supra-axillary pairs, sometimes unilaterally subterninal. Pedicels

---

floral devoid of bracteoles. Calyx very shortly tubulous, with 5 erect small or foliaceous lobes ‡. Corolla with twisted prefloration, with thin and transparent limb (when dry or rehydrated), with large infundibuliform tube and strongly longitudinally nerved following the petalary axes. Anthers sessile entirely included, briefly apiculate, submedifixed. Pollen simple, tricolporate with very short sulci. Style thickened into a club with two apical lips. Ovary unilocular containing two opposing parietal placentae. Fruit globulous or fusiform with persistent calycinal limb, with wall becoming thin and crustaceous when dry. Seeds 8–16 per fruit, enveloped in a white, felted placental plectenchyma and together forming a compact mass that detaches easily from its parietal insertions. Seed irregularly oval lenticular c. 1 cm, with scarcely distinct pellicular testa. Albumen containing a straight embryo.

Type species: Didymosalpinx abbeokutæ (Hiern) Keay.

Tropical African genus of 4 species, of which two are Gabonese, D. parviflora Keay being excluded. A close genus, Monosalpinx N. Hallé, has been discovered in Côte d'Ivoire. It is very similar but nonetheless unarmed, strongly anisophyllous, with unilateral axillary flowers, and with pedicels bearing a pair of linear bracteoles; its stigma is not thickened, its style is puberulent (N. Hallé, loc. cit. 1968).
