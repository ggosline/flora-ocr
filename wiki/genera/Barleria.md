# *Barleria* L.

**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Linné, Sp. Pl. : 636 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Herbaceous plants, shrubs or even lianes, spiny or not. Leaves opposite, entire.

Flowers rather large, sometimes solitary or in pairs, generally united in spikes, racemes, cymes or capitula, provided with bracteoles. 4 opposite sepals arranged in pairs; the outer pair is much larger; anterior sepal often emarginate, bifid or bilobed, sometimes considered as formed of 2 fused sepals. Corolla with an elongated tube widened toward the apex, with 5 subequal lobes or with the anterior one extending from the tube lower than the others. 2 fertile stamens, exserted; 2–3 very small staminodes. Cupuliform disc; ovary two-locular; 2 ovules per locule, superposed; style long, subentire or slightly bifid.

Loculicidal capsule, with 2–4 seeds generally hairy, attached below the middle.

Type species: Barleria cristata L., from India.

There exist in Gabon no species of the sections Prionitis and Acanthoidea characterized by their spines. The two Gabonese species, non-spiny, belong to the sections Somalia (sp. 1) and Barleria (sp. 2).

According to C. B. Clarke the genus comprises 120 species mainly paleotropical; a few are neotropical. A more precise number cannot be given here; there exists no recent overall revision of this genus, which is one of the largest in the family. Approximately 80–90 species in Africa. The number of 250 given by Melchior (Syllab., ed. 12, 2: 459, 1964), is doubtless too high.
