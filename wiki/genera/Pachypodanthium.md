# *Pachypodanthium* Engler et Diels

**Family**: [[Annonaceae]]
**Authority**: Engler et Diels
**Protologue**: Notizbl.Bot.Gart.Berl.3 : 55 (19oo); Engl., Mon.Afr.Pflanzenf.6:32,tab.1I (1901).- R.E. Fries in Engler et Prantl, Nat. Pflanzenf.,ed. 2,17 a, 2 : 60 (1959)

## Diagnosis

Trees with an indumentum of stellate hairs. Flowers extra-axillary or axillary, arising on leafy or leafless branches, solitary or more often in very contracted, pauciflorous racemes. Pedicel thick, bearing a bract at the base and two opposite bracteoles towards the apex, completely enclosing the young bud, caducous. Floral buds ovoid-globose to globose. Sepals 3, valvate, free, coriaceous. Petals 6, in two whorls, imbricate, ovate, the inner ones slightly smaller and caudate at the apex. Stamens numerous, sessile or subsessile, thecae linear extrorse, connectives enlarged and truncate above the anthers. Carpels numerous, contiguous, with base ± included in the receptacle. Receptacle conical. Ovule 1, erect at the base of the ovarian locule.

Fruits pedicellate, globose to subglobose-ellipsoid, muricate; mericarps obpyramidal, contiguous or fused to one another in the lower 3/4, with base sunken into the receptacle, constituting a syncarp. Seeds numerous, ellipsoid, with smooth testa.

Type species: P. Staudtii (Engl. et Diels) Engl. et Diels (= Uvaria Staudtii).

Small genus from tropical Africa comprising 3 species and one variety.

The number of ovules per carpel seems to be disputed in the genus; Paiva (Mem. Soc. Brot. 19: 29, 1966) takes up the description of the genus from Hutchinson (Genera of flowering plants 1: 85, 1964), in which he mentions "numerous ovules". It appears that this can only be an error, the genus Pachypodanthium being perfectly characterized by the presence of a single ovule erect at the base of the ovarian locule, a character which is found very clearly in the presence of a single seed per mericarp in the fruit.

The fruit of Pachypodanthium has moreover never been clearly defined, owing to the coalescence or ± pronounced fusion of the mericarps. It is nevertheless truly a syncarp, in the sense generally admitted in the Annonaceae family and defined by Emberger (Traité de Botanique 1: 374, 1960): "fruits composed of elementary fruits, more or less intimately fused". If in the species Staudtii the mericarps are only contiguous, in the two species confine and Barteri, they are very evidently fused and present no difference from a syncarp of Annona.

As has already been remarked by Sinclair (Gardens Bull. Singapore 14: 167, 171, 1955), the genus Pachypodanthium belongs to a phylum derived from the Uvarieae but distinctly more advanced, the fruit having become syncarpic by the fusion of the free carpels in the flower. This evolution is manifested within the genus itself, where the fusion of the mericarps is ± advanced in the three species. This phylum would be constituted by the two African genera: Pachypodanthium and Letestudoxa and the American genus: Fusaea. Sinclair adjoins to it the genus Anonidium, according to the known descriptions of this African genus, but our observations have led us to rank it with certainty among the Unoneae.

## Key to the species

### I. Lower surface of leaves covered with very dense felt of white-yellowish stellate hairs.

- **2.** Flowers arising on leafless branches; floral bud 10-15 mm in diameter; leaves oblong-lanceolate, 20-40 cm long, 4-11 cm wide, attenuate toward the base, which is itself rounded and decurrent on the petiole → [[Pachypodanthium_confine\|*P. confine*]]
- **2′.** Flowers axillary on leafy branches; floral bud 5 mm in diameter; leaves smaller, 10-14 cm long, 2.5-3.5 cm wide, cuneiform at the base → 2. P. confine var. Sargosii I'. Lower surface of leaves sparsely covered with small stellate hairs.
  - **3.** Flowers extra-axillary; leaves narrowly oblong, shortly attenuate at both ends; petals white nacré; ovaries pubescent; stigmas obovoid; fruit with mericarps contiguous, detaching easily from one another → [[Pachypodanthium_staudtii\|*P. staudtii*]]
  - **3′.** Flowers axillary; leaves elliptic-lanceolate, attenuate toward each end from the middle part; petals red, becoming pink-yellow; ovaries puberulent; stigmas subtrigonous; fruit with mericarps fused to one another at the lower part → *P. barteri*
