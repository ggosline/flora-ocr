# *Ritchiea* R. Brown ex G. Don

**Family**: [[Brassicaceae]]
**Authority**: R. Brown ex G. Don
**Protologue**: Gen. Syst. 1: 276 (1831), «Richiea»; PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17b: 167 (1936); DE WOLF, Kirkia 1: 90 (1961); KERS, FCAM 29: 95 (1987)

## Diagnosis

Small or large shrubs, woody plants, sometimes lianes, unarmed, deciduous or evergreen, glabrous except on the inflorescences; trunk sometimes finely scabrous due to epidermal asperities, lenticels often numerous and clearly visible. Leaves alternate, arranged regularly or irregularly along the shoots, simple and shortly petiolate or else compound and long-petiolate, 1–5-foliolate. Blade entire, glabrous, papyraceous or coriaceous. Petiolules short, clearly articulate at the base. Petiole non-articulate or weakly articulate at the base. Stipules free, fine, very little visible, early withered.

Inflorescence a raceme with few or many flowers, often corymbiform at anthesis, terminal or subterminal on young branches or, rarely, a few flowers on condensed axillary shoots appearing on old wood; axes mostly with base leafless, with limited growth. Floral bracts very small, generally simple, sessile, stipulate lanceolate and early deciduous. Stipules very reduced, often marcescent. Flowers hermaphrodite, actinomorphic, often made evident by the long exsert staminal filaments. Pedicels clearly articulate at the base. Receptacle with prominent margins, vaguely conical, campanulate to cylindric, papyraceous, marcescent, crowned by the sepals; corona forming a fine and short rim, very little visible inside the base of the sepals and petals, near the margin of the receptacle. Sepals 4, valvate, equal or nearly so, on the margin of the receptacle, fine, deciduous. Petals 0 or 4, 8 or more numerous, on the margin of the receptacle, free from the base, entire or divided in the upper half, linear or narrow and lanceolate, fragile, longer than the sepals, sometimes clearly visible white or pale yellow, deciduous. Androgynophore short and thick, sometimes exceeding the receptacle after anthesis, glabrous. Stamens, indefinite in number ca. 50–110, all fertile (no staminodes), free from the base, arranged in 2 series on a distinct torus, wider than the gynophore; filaments fine and slender, irregularly coiled in the bud,

---

equal or nearly so, deciduous, leaving neat scars on the torus after their fall; anthers narrow and oblong 2–4 mm, basidorsifixed, versatile. Gynophore slender, often as long as the staminal filaments, glabrous, becoming thick and lignified in the fruit. Ovary unilocular with 2 or more rarely 3 or 4 parietal placentas, cylindric or ellipsoid, glabrous, smooth or variously ornamented with wrinkles or channeling, transverse section circular, with 4 obtuse angles or somewhat swollen on the flanks. Ovules numerous in 2 rows on each placenta. Stigma sessile or subsessile, truncate or swollen, capitate, generally reduced to entire margin or vaguely lobed.
