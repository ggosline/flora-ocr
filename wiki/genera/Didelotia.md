# *Didelotia* Baillon

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: Baillon
**Protologue**: H. Baillon, Adansonia 5 : 367, pl. 8 (1865); Adansonia 6 : 176 (1866); Hist. Pl. 2 : 113 (1870)

## Diagnosis

Leaves unifoliolate, unijugate or multijugate. Stipules connate into a bifid intrapetiolar blade, the base of which persists in the form of a fleshy scale.

Panicles elongate, pendulous, bearing short or very short branches. Two valvate bracteoles, enveloping the buds. Receptacle cupuliform, very short, fleshy, on the margins of which are inserted minute sepals, sometimes almost invisible, 5 white filiform petals, sometimes reduced to small subulate points or even absent, 5 fertile stamens, longer than the petals. Sometimes staminodes on the intrastaminal disc. Ovary subsessile.

Legumes in 2 woody valves, coiling upon themselves after dehiscence, marked with 1-2 longitudinal nerves sometimes not very distinct.

Type species: D. africana Baill. Gabon.

The reduction of the perianth parts is a very typical character of the genus. It is almost total in D. Letouzeyi where one can no longer distinguish either sepal or petal. The flowers of Toubaouate brevipaniculata are of the Didelotia type, but the reduction of the petals is not as extreme; if the 5 petals are always filiform they are as long or nearly as long as the stamens. Léonard and Oldeman placed Toubaouate in synonymy with Didelotia, because of the common structure of the flowers and similar fruits. Nevertheless it seemed preferable to me to maintain the separation. All species of Didelotia have in common very elongate, pendulous racemiform panicles, and connate stipules

---

forming an intrapetiolar blade whose base is distinctly persistent. These are practical and convenient identification characters. In Toubaouate brevipaniculata the connate stipules form a broad intrapetiolar blade, which is entirely caducous and which leaves an intrapetiolar scar that extends lengthily and very visibly on either side on the branch. Moreover the inflorescences are paniculate and not pendulous. Finally the young branches elongate from large scaly buds, whose scales very close together and very caducous leave at the base of the new branch a ring of annular scars very close together and very characteristic.

D. Morelii is very incompletely known. It is likely that another Gabonese species will yet be added to these, when it is better known. Indeed no. 2010 of Saint-Aubin studied by Oldeman (Blumea 12,2: 213 and fig. 5) which he had referred with doubt to Toubaouate brevipaniculata, is for us a species of Didelotia recognizable notably by its persistent intrapetiolar stipules, but is not Toubaouate (Didelotia) brevipaniculata of which it does not present either the close annular scars at the base of the branches. The comparison also made by Oldeman with no. 26679 Chevalier (Fleury) is probably acceptable, but this no. corresponds to a sterile specimen whose name that was cited by Chevalier, Brachystegia Fleuryana, was not accompanied by any valid diagnosis. Until new material is collected, it will be difficult to decide with complete certainty.
