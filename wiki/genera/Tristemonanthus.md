# *Tristemonanthus* Loesener

**Family**: [[Celastraceae]]
**Authority**: Loesener
**Protologue**: Repert. Spec. Nov. Regni Veg. 49 : 226 (1940) ; HALLÉ, Mém. IFAN 64 : 136 (1962)

## Diagnosis

Woody liane with rubber filaments. Leaves opposite, petiolate. Stipules extrapetiolar. Lamina entire.

Inflorescences axillary, in dichotomous cymes without supplementary flowers or additional branches; small bracts persistent. Bud globose, pedicellate. Sepals 5, short, subequal, quincuncial, imbricate laterally but little overlapping each other at the apex of the bud. Disc absent. Stamens 3, longer than the pistil, connate at the base, curved introrse; anthers transversely dehiscent; pollen in calmed tetrads of 4 tricolporate monads. Stigma subsessile, trilobed. Ovules biseriate, 4–8 per locule.

Fruit with 3 mericarps elliptic, dehiscent into 6 caducous valves.

Seeds winged (T. nigrisilvae).

TYPE SPECIES: Tristemonanthus mildbraedianus Loes., from Central Africa.

---

Genus of two species, the second of which, T. nigrisilvae (Hallé) Hallé, is localized to Liberia (Adam 20509, K!), Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana (Hall & Enti GC 36222, K!).

The etymology of the genus name (tri-stemon-anthus) emphasizes, in contrast to the closely related 5-staminate Campylostemon, the importance of the number of stamens in the flower.
