# *Vigna* Savi

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] (Papilionoideae)
**Authority**: Savi — Nuov. Giorn. Lett., sér. 3, 8: 113 (1834)
**Synonym**: *Voandzeia* Thouars (1806)
**Treatment with**: Rémy S. Pasquet

Sixteen species — **the most agriculturally important genus in the corpus.**

## Diagnosis

Herbs, mostly climbing and/or trailing, sometimes with a fusiform tuberous root.
Stipules persistent, generally prolonged below the point of insertion (except in
*V. comosa*).

## Species in region

*V. comosa*, *V. gracilis*, *V. juruana*, *V. laurentii*, *V. luteola*,
*V. multinervis*, *V. mungo*, *V. nigritia*, *V. oblongifolia*, *V. pubigera*,
*V. racemosa*, *V. radiata*, *V. reticulata*, *V. subterranea*,
*V. unguiculata*, *V. vexillata* — 16 in all.

## Notes

**Four of these sixteen species are crops, and two were domesticated in Africa.**

***V. unguiculata* is the cowpea** (niébé, black-eyed pea) — domesticated in
Africa and the most important indigenous grain legume of the continent, grown
across the savanna zone for seed, pods and fodder, and central to nitrogen
supply in smallholder rotations.

***V. subterranea* is the Bambara groundnut** — likewise African in origin, and
long treated as the separate genus *Voandzeia*, as this treatment's synonymy
records. It ripens its pods underground like the peanut, tolerates poor soils and
drought, and is a significant food crop that has attracted renewed attention as a
climate-resilient legume.

*V. mungo* (black gram) and *V. radiata* (mung bean) are Asian domesticates
present through cultivation.

Because the wild African *Vigna* are the crop wild relatives of cowpea and
Bambara groundnut, **the non-crop species on this page carry real breeding
value** — a point that gives a regional flora treatment direct agricultural
relevance. That the treatment was written with Rémy Pasquet, a *Vigna* crop-genetic
specialist, reflects this.
