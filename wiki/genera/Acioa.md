## Diagnosis

Small trees or shrubs, sometimes climbing. Leaves ordinarily with 2 glands toward the base of the lamina below, often with small glands under the acumen; lower surface glabrous or pubescent, rarely with white woolly tomentum; petiole without glands; stipules sometimes laterally inserted on the base of the petiole.

Inflorescences in simple or branched racemes or compound inflorescences with cymules; bracts on the peduncle often tricuspid; bracts and bracteoles devoid of glands or furnished with numerous stipitate glands, not concealing the flower buds, ± long-persistent (African species) or early caducous (American species), bracteoles alternate to opposite, ± distant from the articulation of the pedicel. Flowers hermaphrodite; receptacle longer than the remaining part of the pedicel on the disarticulated flower, sometimes equal, ordinarily elongate, tubular sometimes narrowly obconical, rarely campanulate (A. schultesii, American species), frequently gibbous at the base, hollow, glabrous or with scattered retrorsely directed hairs in its lower interior part, tomentose, pubescent or glabrous externally; sepals (calyx lobes) sub-

equal, oblong-ovate (African species) or unequal, rounded-orbicular (American species), tomentellous on the inner parts of the flower bud, very rarely glabrous internally (A. schultesii); petals very early caducous, sometimes fimbriate (American species); 10-75 stamens, with filaments connate ligulate over almost their entire length and inserted on the rim of the receptacle; staminodes opposite the stamens, generally in the form of denticulations on this rim; ovary inserted laterally on the opening of the receptacle, always surrounded by stiff hairs; carpel unilocular 2-ovulate, sometimes the second abortive.

Fruit inflorescence sparsely furnished; drupe hard or fleshy, ± ovoid flattened and distinctly pointed at the apex; epicarp often ferruginous tomentose mixed with hispid hairs, less often glabrous, exceptionally glabrous and rapidly deeply cracked (American species); mesocarp thin, except for American species; endocarp ordinarily hard but always thin, with rough outer surface and pubescent internally, without a germination plug; seed coat likewise pubescent externally; cotyledons woody (and with central cavity on dry specimens). (Mature fruits incompletely known for several species.) Hypogeal germination, first leaves opposite (at least in African species).
