# *Synandrodaphne* Gilg

**Family**: [[Thymeleaceae]]
**Authority**: Gilg
**Protologue**: Gilg, Bot. Jahrb. 53 : 362 (1915), nomen conservandum

## Diagnosis

≠ Synandrodaphne Meisner, in De Candolle, Prodr. 15 (1): 176 (1864), nomen rejiciendum. — Kostermans, Bibl. Laurac.: 1358 (1964).

= Gilgiodaphne Domke, Bibl. Bot. 111:119 (1934).

Woody plants of low stature, with erect branches, alternate distichous leaves. Flowers in inflorescences generally terminal, contracted into a very dense pseudocapitulum enveloped in pubescent bracts.

Hermaphroditic flowers typically tetramerous, with perianth reduced to a single dialypetalous whorl. Sepals 4, opposite in pairs, two inner, two outer. 4 stamens united by their filaments to an internal androecial tube, terminated by 4 very hairy lobes; filaments fused externally to this tube, at first shorter than it, then longer, alternating with the lobes. Ovary superior, sessile and somewhat fused to the androecial tube (which perhaps corresponds in part to the hypogynous disc of other Thymelaeaceae), bilocular, each locule with one ovule; style elongated, often prominent; stigma enlarged.

Fruit capsular, bivalvate, very small, less than 4 mm, each valve showing the trace of the two locules of the ovary; generally only one seed developed per capsule.

Type species: Synandrodaphne paradoxa Gilg, described from Cameroon (Mildbraed 4591).

Monotypic genus, known only from western equatorial Africa.

We have retained this genus, like the genus Octolepis, in the family Thymelaeaceae, although various characters appear aberrant, at least among the other African representatives. However, the presence of a stem furnished with numerous fibres, the instability of the floral structure (tetramery with transitions to pentamery), the absence of a stem, the presence of an androecial tube which could be the equivalent of the cycle of petaloid glands or of the hypogynous cupule, the existence of a fruit fairly comparable to that of African Octolepis or New Caledonian Solmsia, are criteria that prompt us to maintain the genus Synandrodaphne in this family.
