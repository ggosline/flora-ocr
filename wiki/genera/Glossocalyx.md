# *Glossocalyx* Benth.

**Family**: [[Monimiaceae]]
**Authority**: Benth.

## Diagnosis

Trees or shrubs; adult leaves alternate, but in the young stage opposite to a leaf reduced to the midrib forming an awl soon caducous; petiole short; limb entire or slightly dentate, acuminate. Flowers pedicellate, irregular, dioecious, sometimes monoecious (Letouzey), grouped in axillary fascicles pauciflorous (1-5 flowers). Male flowers: calyx campanulate subcharnose with 5 unequally developed segments except the outer one elongate into a ligule (whence the genus name) the others possibly being absent, reduced to a point on the calyx margin or attaining half the length of the large sepal; interior of the calyx partially closed by a membranous convex velum, with a wide opening; stamens numerous (10 to 25) with short, broad filaments, oval anthers dehiscent by 2 valves at the apex of the internal face. Female flowers: same perianth, fleshy velum with narrower opening letting through the styles; carpels numerous (5 to 8) free in locules arranged at variable heights; a single anatrope basilar ovule erect. Stigma curved, exsert.

Fruit subglobose more or less irregularly bosselated, subcharnose, formed by the carpels included in the persistent and accrescent calyx; seeds oblong or lanceolate.

The habit of these shrubs is characterized by a "bottlebrush" head (Letouzey) formed of horizontal branches, attaching perpendicularly to the stem by sorts of buttresses of parenchymatous tissue much reduced by desiccation. Moreover, these branches are caducous, separating probably by an abscission layer analogous to that of the leaf petioles for the scar left is very regular. According to N. Hallé, there is a

great analogy with the case of the Rubiaceae Schumanniophyton whose branches are equally caducous.

In creating the genus Glossocalyx, Bentham distinguished two species figured in the Icones: Gl. longicuspis and Gl. brevipes, the first from Fernando-Po and Gabon, the second from Cameroon, all collected by Mann in 1862. In 1898, Engler describes Gl. Staudtii from Cameroon. More recently (1943, Bot. Arch. Zeitsch. tome 44, 1st issue: 1, Vienna), Rudolph Wagner creates Gl. Zenker based on examination of Zenker specimens 1784-2482-3083-4678-4975, a species characterized by the presence of leaves reduced to a midrib 15-20 mm long, glabrous above and hairy below, more or less opposite to the normal leaves. Now, this character is rather generic than specific.

In the herbarium of the Paris Museum, the specimen Klaine 527 was described by Pierre as type of a Gl. Klainei remaining in manuscript, characterized by the receptacle with 6 longitudinal ribs and 3 developed sepals; these ribs originate from the presence of 6 carpels giving on the dried plant furrows as on the fruit, the presence of seeds is revealed from the outside by the bosselated surface of the pericarp. Two parts of a Zenker specimen without collection number from Mimfia (Cameroon) are noted: Gl. tricuspis K. Sch. species which appears characterized by its three developed sepals; the description has not been found.
