# *Copaifera* L.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Linné, Sp. Pl., ed. 2,1 : 557 (1762), nom. cons.; Gen. n. 542

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs, resiniferous subshrubs. Leaflets scattered with translucent dots. Bracteoles 2, small, not enveloping the bud. Flowers distichous, small, sessile or briefly pedicellate. Sepals 4, subvalvate. Petals or exceptionally minute. Stamens ± 10, free. Ovules 2 (-4). Fruits generally suborbicular to obliquely oblong, dehiscent in 2 valves, thin to thick. Seeds solitary, thick, furnished with a colored aril ± enveloping.

Type species: C. officinalis L., America.

The genus Copaífera (excluding species referred to the genus Guiboutia) is chiefly American with altogether about thirty species.

In Africa it is represented by a few species from the Guineo-Congolian forest: C. salikounda Heck. from Guinea to Ghana, C. Mildbraedii Harms from southern Nigeria to Congo, C. religiosa

---

Léonard exclusively from Gabon and Mayombé, and C. Baumiana subshrub from the southern clear forests.

The two species present in Gabon, but uncommon, are easily distinguished. C. religiosa is a very large tree, one of the tallest in the forest of Gabon and Mayumbé, remarkable even from a great distance by its red heartwood. The leaflets of the two species have the same form and the same type of venation; those of C. Mildbraedii are generally smaller and more numerous, 20-40, against 10-16 in C. religiosa.

### 1. Copaífera Mildbrædii Harms (Pl. 30, p. 135)

Andem-éviné

Harms, Notizbl. Bot. Gart. Mus. Berlin 8: 147 (1922). — Léonard, F.C.B. 3: 307 (1952). — Pellegrin, Lég. Gabon: 118 (1948). — de St-Aubin, For. Gabon: 85 (1963).

Trees. Leaves pinnate with leaflets alternate or subopposite, 10-20 pairs. Rachis channeled 15-25 cm in length, hairy then glabrous. Leaflets briefly but distinctly petiolulate (1-2 mm), oblong, with emarginate apex, with oblique, unequal, rounded base, the anterior side being more developed than the posterior side, 1.5-4 cm in length by 1-2 cm in width, glabrous below the midvein. Limb riddled with translucent punctuations. Numerous secondary veins, 14-20, traced to the margin, finely prominent below.

Inflorescences in the axil of terminal leaves, 10-30 cm in length, in racemes of short lateral spikes 2-5 cm in length, tawny pubescent. Bracts ovate-concave, caducous, 2-2.5 mm, hairy. Pedicels very short, 1 mm. Flowers white. Sepals 4, lanceolate, acute, 3-4 mm in length, 3 broad 1-1.5 mm, 1 broader (2.5 mm), tawny hairy on both faces. Petals 0. Stamens 9-10, free. Disk flat, hairy. Ovary yellow, sessile, hairy on the dorsal suture.

**2.** Copaifera religiosa Léonard (Pl. 30, p. 134) Anzem J. Léonard, Bull. Jard. Bot. Brux. 19: 398 (1949). — de St-Aubin, For. Gabon: 84 (1963). = Copaifera Salikounda non Heckel. — Pellegrin, Lég. Gabon: 118 (1948). — Heitz, For. Gabon: 109 (1943). Large trees. Leaves with 10–16 pairs of alternate leaflets of 2.5–6.5 cm in length × 1.5–3.5 cm in width. Rachis 15–20 cm long, finely canaliculate. Blade oblong or elliptic, rounded and emarginate at the apex, which is slightly apiculate in the extension of the midvein, rounded and unequal at the base, pitted with translucent dots, glabrous or glabrescent on the lower face, subcoriaceous. Numerous parallel secondary nerves, fine but well-visible below, most of them extending to a pronounced marginal nerve. Petiolules ± twisted, 1–5 mm long. Racemes of spikes in the axil of terminal leaves, with pubescent axes. Flowers subsessile, spreading, 8–9 mm in diameter. Sepals white, 4, 4 mm long, one of which 3 mm in width, the other 3 only 2 mm in width, hairy on both faces. Petals or reduced to small claws 1–2 mm long. Stamens 8–10, alternately short and long. Anthers oblong, versatile. Disk hirsute. Ovary subsessile, hairy at the base and along the margins; 2 ovules. Legumes suborbicular, blackish, flattened, with oblique base, 3–5 cm in length by 2–3.5 cm in width, glabrous to hairy, coriaceous, woody within, rugose and covered with droplets of resin. Seed solitary, surrounded by a red aril. Holotype: Heitz 39, Gabon (P). One of the largest and most beautiful trees of the Gabon and Mayombé forest, by its very tall trunk, red in colour, perfectly straight and cylindrical, bearing a broad crown spread in an umbrella shape. Base thickened, without buttresses. Bark smooth or finely scaly, with fibrous section, pale ochre, releasing a characteristic bitter-almond odour. The wood tender, light, of reddish-brown colour exudes a colourless resin, very sticky. This species exists from southern Cameroon to Gabon, in a very scattered state, and in Mayombé. Vernacular names: Olumi-rouge, Motombi, Anzem (Fang). GABONESE MATERIAL STUDIED: A. Chevalier: 34817, Sindara (fl. March). Heitz: 39, Noya-Kango. de St-Aubin: 2012, Monts de Cristal (fl. Jan.).
