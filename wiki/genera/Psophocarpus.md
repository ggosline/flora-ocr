# *Psophocarpus* Necker ex DC., nom. cons.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: Necker ex DC., nom. cons.
**Protologue**: Prodr. 2 : 403 (1825)

## Diagnosis

Herbs or climbing suffrutex. Stipules peltate. Leaves pinnate; stipels present; leaflets (1–)3, with entire or sublobate blade. Inflorescences in pseudoracemes axillary; rachis with solitary, geminate or fasciculate flowers on brachyblasts. Flowers: bracteoles 2, persistent; calyx bilabiate, upper lip entire to bilobate, lower lip with 3 lobes with the median one longer; petals white, blue or violet, unguiculate, standard largest, glabrous, with auriculate base, with 2 callosities on the ventral face, wings longer than keel, spurred, keel falcate-curved or erect-incurved, with short and obtuse rostrum; stamens 10, diadelphous, the vexillary one free at the base and fused higher up, anthers dimorphic; disc cylindric, lobed; ovary subsessile, ± winged, with 3 to several ovules; style thickened, curved, stigma terminal or on the inner face, penicillate. Pods tetragonous, ± winged at the angles, dehiscent. Seeds oblong to ovoid, with or without strophiole.

Genus with approximately 10 species, from tropical Africa and Asia. The species widely cultivated for the pod, seed and tuber, also in Gabon, P. tetragonolobus (L.) DC. (square pea, winged bean, Goa bean, Indian bean), is of uncertain origin, perhaps Asian. In Gabon, two spontaneous species that are fairly close to each other are found.

Notes: This genus is divided into two subgenera (subg. Psophocarpus and subg. Vignopsis (De Wild.) Wilczek). The two Gabonese species belong to the subgenus Psophocarpus.

B: Grubben (2004), Lewis et al. (2005), Pellegrin (1949), Verdcourt (1968, 1971c), Verdcourt & Halliday (1978), Wilczek (1954).
