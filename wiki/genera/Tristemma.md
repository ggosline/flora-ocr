# *Tristemma* Jussieu

**Family**: [[Melastomataceae]]
**Authority**: Jussieu
**Protologue**: Gen. : 329 (1789); JACQ.-FÉL., Bull. Mus. natn. Hist. nat., Paris, sér. 3, Bot., 28 : 137-207, (1976); Fl. Cam. 24 : 58 (1983)

## Diagnosis

Herbs perennial or subshrubs, ± setulose to hirsute, rarely glabrescens; branches 4-angular ± winged, or subangular. Leaves petiolate, elliptic-lanceolate or lanceolate, never cordate; 3-7-nerved; margins entire.

Glomerules terminal, subtended by one or several pairs of leaves; pluriflorous and compact, or reduced to one or a few flowers: bracts persistent, involucring ± the glomerule and each of the sessile or subsessile flowers. Flowers 5-merous; hypanthium cylindrical to campanulate, often furnished with one to several complete or discontinuous rows of setae, free or borne on a membranous emergence. Sepals triangular. Corolla poorly developed. Stamens concolorous, equal or subequal; anthers short, oblongues, rarely linear-subulate; podconnective short, with two anterior obtuse lobes. Ovary adherent over almost its entire height and staminous locules shallow or absent; apex convex or conic, often setulose, peristyle collar setulose, or reduced to a glabrous callus, or absent. Style short, straight, linear or thickened toward the apex; stigma punctiform to subcapitate; placentas protruberant, spongioid.

Fruit with persistent and reflexed sepals; berry-like by turgescence of the placentas. Seeds small, cochlear, nearly smooth, 0.3-0.5 mm.

TYPE SPECIES: Tristemma mauritianum J.-F. Gmel., Mauritius Island.

African genus of about fifteen species, one of which extends to Madagascar and the Mascarene Islands. Subforesrial and swampy habitats.

2'. Glomerule of one to a few flowers, poorly involucred by bracts not reaching the middle of the hypanthium; leaves elliptic; anthers 2 to 3 mm

1'. Hypanthium furnished with setae in one or several rows, complete or discontinuous; plants glabrescens to hirsute.

3. Setae ± slender and flexuous, borne on a membrane, at least on the upper rings; branches 4-winged or subangular.

4. Flowers numerous per glomerule; plants robust, normally erect.
