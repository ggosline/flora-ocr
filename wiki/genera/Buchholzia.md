# *Buchholzia* Engler

**Family**: [[Capparidaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Capparaceae]] — *Capparidaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Capparidaceae]]
**Authority**: Engler
**Protologue**: Bot. Jahrb. 7: 335 (1886); PAX & HOFFMANN, in ENGL., Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 2, 17 b: 192 (1936); RISSEEUW, Acta Bot. Neerland. 13: 161 (1964)

## Diagnosis

Large evergreen shrubs or small trees, entirely glabrous. Leaves simple, petiolate, alternate, evenly distributed along the shoots or clustered in a tuft at the apex of the branches; petioles not articulate at the base; stipules barely visible, broadly triangular, reaching 1 mm long, persistent. Axillary buds solitary or in series.

Inflorescences in simple or branched racemes, terminal, subterminal or axillary on the leafy branches. Flowers apetalous, actinomorphic, with open prefloration. Receptacle flat and small in size in the young bud, subsequently spreading and forming an annular disk, with rapid growth before anthesis, entire or divided into a small number of sections, fleshy, occasionally campanulate, reaching 4 mm high, margin crenate or dentate; disk persistent beneath the fruit. Sepals 4 arranged in 2 cycles, imbricate, free, opening very early, equal or nearly so, more rarely 5–6 (as a result of splitting of the primordia), concave internally, length reaching 5 mm, coriaceous, reflexed at anthesis, marcescent. Petals absent. Androgynophore poorly developed. Stamens numerous, approximately 40–45, free, all fertile (no staminodes); filaments erect in the bud, elongating rapidly before anthesis, reaching a length of 2.5 cm, remaining straight as they spread; anthers oblong 1.5–2 mm, basifixed, curved at the apex with age, early caducous. Gynophore erect in the bud, elongating rapidly, longer and more slender than the pedicel at the time of anthesis, thicker and lignified in the fruit. Ovary narrowly ellipsoid or cylindric, reaching 7 mm length at anthesis, containing 2 parietal placentas, uni- or sometimes becoming bilocular through fusion of the 2 placentas. Ovules 14–16 in 2 rows on each placenta. Stigma sessile, barely visible, truncate, with entire or vaguely lobed margin.

Fruit spherical or irregularly rounded, reaching 5 cm diameter; pericarp thick, coarsely lignified or coriaceous. Seeds 1–4 per fruit, spherical or furnished with rounded angles, large, filling the entire cavity of the fruit, often separated by fine false septa; large cotyledons sometimes partially connate; hypocotyl not elongating during germination.

TYPE SPECIES: Buchholzia coriacea Engler.

The genus Buchholzia comprises 2 closely related species restricted to the western regions of tropical Africa. Both species are present in Gabon: B. tholloniana is widely distributed and common there while B. coriacea has been collected there only once. This genus is well characterized by its actinomorphic and apetalous flower as well as by its fleshy and campanulate disk, formed from the inner face of the receptacle. It has never been recognized as having affinity with any other genus.

## Key to the species

- **1.** Lateral nerves 11-17, prominent on the upper surface of the lamina; leaves mostly 3-7 cm wide, elliptic to obovate; apex acute to acuminate. Inflorescences often terminal and branched → [[Buchholzia_tholloniana\|*B. tholloniana*]]
- **1′.** Lateral nerves ≤ 10, impressed on the upper surface of the lamina; leaves mostly 3-11 cm wide, broadly obovate; apex abruptly acuminate; inflorescence ordinarily simple, axillary or subterminal → [[Buchholzia_coriacea\|*B. coriacea*]]
