# *Aeschynomene* L.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Sp. pl. 2 : 713 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Herbs or shrubs 0.3–2.5(–8) m tall, sometimes spiny. Stipules mostly prolonged into a spur at the base. Leaves paripinnate, often glutinous; stipels absent; leaflets alternate. Inflorescences axillary or sometimes terminal, with 1 or 2 flowers or in pseudoracemes or panicles. Flowers: bracteoles 2, below the calyx; calyx deeply bilabiate, with 2 upper lobes and 3 lower lobes; standard oblong-obovate, shortly clawed, wings obovate-oblong, often ± falcate, equalling the standard, keel falcate and somewhat rostrate; stamens 10, monadelphous or rarely diadelphous with vexillary stamen free or united into two groups of 5, anthers uniform or rarely alternately large and small; ovary stipitate, multiovulate (in Gabon); style curved. Pod stipitate, ± oblong, slightly flattened, straight or slightly curved, with rectangular or ellipsoidal articles, separating at maturity, with 1 seed per article, with smooth or papillose or silky surface. Seeds oblong-reniform, flattened laterally, brown.

Genus of ± 180 species, half of which in Africa, including Madagascar, the others in the Neotropics, especially in Mexico and neighbouring regions, with four species distributed circumtropically by man; one species endemic to Asia and Australia. For the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Léonard (1954b) reported 35 species, and it is quite probable that more than the five confirmed to date are found in Gabon.

Notes: In flower, Aeschynomene resembles Sesbania, but the latter have calyces with 5 regular teeth, not bilabiate.

Thiombiano et al. (2012) treat Bakerophyton Hutch. as a distinct genus, but Lewis et al. (2005) and Mackinder et al. (2001) treat it as a subgenus of Aeschynomene following Léonard (1954a, 1954b). This latter opinion is followed here.

The presence of A. elaphroxylon (Guill. & Perr.) Taub. in Gabon remains to be confirmed, but is likely because of its presence in Cameroon, Republic of the Congo and Equatorial Guinea. The same applies to A. lateritia Harms (Bakerophyton lateritium (Harms) Hutch. ex Maheshw.), which is found in the Republic of the Congo.

The presence of A. crassicaulis Harms (a West African species) in Gabon was reported in the literature (based on Descoings 6297), but rests on a determination error.

Bibliography

b: Léonard (1954a, 1954b), Mackinder et al. (2001), Pellegrin (1949).
