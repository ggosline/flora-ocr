# *Nesogordonia* H. Baillon (1).

**Family**: [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Modern family**: [[Malvaceae]] s.l. — *Sterculiaceae* as circumscribed here is superseded; see [[Sterculiaceae]]
**Authority**: H. Baillon (1).
**Protologue**: BAILLON in Null. Soc. Linn. Paris I : 555 (1886)

## Diagnosis

Tall trees with very hard wood. Stipules acute caducous. Leaves petiolate alternate simple, entire. Venation of the blade often bearing small axillary pubescent domatia on the lower surface. Inflorescences axillary in few- or many-flowered cymes with long peduncles. Pedicel articulate. 5 sepals valvate. 5 petals with contorted prefloration, about as long as the sepals. 5 bundles opposite the sepals of 3 or 4 stamens barely fused to one another at the base of the filaments which are very short. Anthers with 2 elongate locules, extrorse, dehiscing longitudinally, with an acute prominent connective. 5 internal staminodes opposite the petals, laminate-lanceolate, exceeding the stamens. Ovary tomentose with 5 faces. Styles with stigmatiferous acute apices, briefly coherent to one another at the apex of the ovary. 5 locules opposite the sepals, each with 2 ovules. Fruit pentagonal capsular with loculicidal dehiscence. Valves thick and hard, ornamented with an axial basilar groove and a transverse subapical crest. 2 seeds per locule, ascending, with a fine membranous wing directed downward and with a very tenuous inferior nerve. Seed albuminous. Embryo with inferior radicle.

This genus comprises 21 species of which 13 are endemic to Madagascar. The 8 species from tropical Africa are very close to one another. In Gabon, 2 deciduous species.

The type species, N. bernieri Baillon, is from Madagascar.

Leaves almost glabrous or glabrous on the lower surface; petiole slender 15–50 mm long; margin of blade entire; valve of fruit with crest often spreading and often bilobed..... 1. N. papaverifera. Leaf densely pubescent on the lower surface; petiole 8–30 mm long; margin of blade sometimes undulate-denticulate toward the apex; valve of fruit with crest often erect, rounded or obtuse..... 2. N. fouassieri.

(1) Cf. addenda, p. 149.
