# *Haplocelum* Radlkofer

**Family**: [[Sapindaceae]]
**Authority**: Radlkofer
**Protologue**: Sitzungsber. Bayer. Akad. Wiss. München 8 : 336 (1878); in ENGL. & PRANTL, Nat. Pflanzenfam., ed. 1, 3 : (5) 327 (1895); in ENGL., Pflanzenreich, Sapindaceae : 881 (1932)

## Diagnosis

Shrub or tree with rounded branchlets, striate, ash-grey, ash-colored or ferruginous hairy when young, as are the petioles and rachis, soon glabrous; internodes short 0.5-2 (5) cm. Leaves alternate, clustered at the apex of branchlets; leaflets sometimes large, oblong, sometimes small, suborbicular or rhombic, subsessile, chartaceous to subcoriaceous, glabrous and glossy; cells secreting a substance resembling saponin giving translucent punctations.

Inflorescences in small cymes, at the axil of leaves or leaf scars, near the apex of branchlets; terminal flowers ♀; bracts similar to sepals, ferruginous hairy, glabrous inside; pedicel articulate at the base. Flowers monoecious, regular; calyx with 5–6 oblong sepals, membranous, tomentose outside or only at the apex, interior glabrous; petals absent; disc regular, hemispherical cushion-shaped, glabrous; androecium with 5–6 stamens inserted in foveoles of the disc at the periphery (♂ fl.); filaments 2–3 times longer than the sepals, filiform, glabrous; ovary (♀ fl.) ovate-fusiform, with 3 angles and 3 locules, glabrous most often; stigma 3-lobed. Fruit with thin sarcocarp, drying readily, ellipsoid in shape, briefly stipitate, apiculate at the apex; septa destroyed in such a way as to produce only a single locule with a single seed by abortion of the two other ovules; exocarp containing a substance resembling saponin, endocarp sclerified. Seed erect with hard, blackish tegument, thin aril enveloping to the apex, split on the back; embryo curved, radicle engaged in a tegumentary fold, cotyledons with starchy reserves and containing a substance frothing by agitation in water like the pericarps of the leaves (RADLKOFER).

### TYPE SPECIES: Haplocælum inopleum Radlk.

Genus remarkable for its fruit in which the disappearance of the septa leads to a single locule whence the name derived from the Greek: haplo-, simple and koilos, cavity. RADLKOFER mentions 6 species in Africa, 3 in the oriental region, 3 in the occidental and australo-central region. Currently only H. gallaense has been found in Cameroon.

1. Leaflets large, 4–12 cm; oriental species and one in Angola, with narrowly margined rachis, leaflets alternate, 2–3 on each side, lanceolate; apex obtuse (Angola). H. acuminatum Radlkofer.

1'. Leaflets small, 1–2 cm.

2. Leaves 4–7-jugate; leaflets suborbicular emarginate at the apex.. H. gallaense.

2'. Leaves 7–14-jugate; leaflets rhomboidal (Angola, Rhodesia)..... H. dekindtianum (Engler) Radlkofer.
