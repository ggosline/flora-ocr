# *Ruellia* L.

**Family**: [[Acanthaceae]]
**Authority**: L.
**Protologue**: Linné, Sp. Pl. 634 (1753)

## Diagnosis

Perennial herbs or shrublets, generally pubescent. Leaves opposite, entire or more rarely dentate, sometimes anisophyllous.

Flowers often large and showy, either axillary and solitary or fascicled, or in terminal panicles formed of cymes (not in Gabonese species), most often blue, violet or white in Africa, and sometimes red or yellow; bracteoles often small, more rarely oblong or lanceolate. Calyx with 5 sepals, these subequal or the posterior larger; the posterior sepals sometimes connate on the lower 2/3. Corolla hypocratériform or infundibuliform, with tube straight or more or less curved, generally narrow and somewhat widened toward the apex, with spreading zygomorphic or subregular limb, with 5 obtuse lobes twisted in the bud, equal, or the 2 posterior ones brought together into an upper lip or connate at the base. 4 didynamous stamens attached below the lobe, included or exsert; filaments united two by two at the base by a sort of membrane; anthers oblong-sagittate dorsifixed, with 2 parallel similar lobes not mucronate at the base. Disc generally indistinct. Ovary locules pluriovulate, with 2 or more ovules, ascending. Style with 2 unequal branches, one arched or coiled into a crozier, the other shorter or subnull.

Capsule oblong or claviform narrowed at the base into a long or short stipe, loculicidal, oligospermous; seeds compressed,

---

oval or orbicular, attached to the retinacula by their angles, covered with hygroscopic hairs which are especially concentrated toward the margins.

Type species: Ruellia tuberosus L., tropical America.

### Genus known from almost all warm countries of the globe.

The taxonomy of African species is very uncertain and requires critical revision. The only species of the section Dipteracanthus (Nees) C.B.Cl. represented in Gabon belongs to a group for which the name R. patula Jacq. has been used, up to 1963 (F.W.T.A.), in a fairly broad sense. The scanty and very young material, without fully opened flowers or fruits, studied from Gabon appears to have received a correct specific attribution, but the still imperfect knowledge of the genus in Africa does not allow one to be absolutely certain. The flowers are considerably smaller than those described by G. Lindau in his original description, but the character is doubtless due to the immature state; moreover, the dimensions of the floral organs are susceptible to varying individually in a rather considerable manner; in the present case, this in no way affects the conclusions or taxonomic delimitations.
