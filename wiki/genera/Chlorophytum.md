# *Chlorophytum* Ker-Gawl.

**Family**: [[Anthericaceae]]
**Authority**: Ker-Gawl.
**Protologue**: Bot. Mag . 27 : t. 1071 (1807)

## Diagnosis

(1857) nec Berk. & Broome (1861).

Anthericum sensu Baker, Fl. Trop. Afr. 7 : 477 (1898), pro parte .

Verdickia De Wild., Ann. Mus. Congo, Bot ., sér. 4, 1 : 7 (1902).

Plants of variable size and architecture, 5–250 cm in height; rhizome reduced or prominent, sometimes moniliform (composed of a chain of corms); roots with tubers.

Leaves in basal rosette or in 2 ranks, appearing with the inflorescence, linear to broadly lanceolate, sometimes tapering toward the base into a pseudo-petiole, glabrous. Peduncle without or with small leaves (bracts). Inflorescence spiciform or racemiform, with more than one bract per node, (1–)2–5 flowers per node. Flowers: pedicel with a distinct articulation; tepals spreading, stellate or urceolate, generally white or greenish-brown, this latter color often restricted to an external median line, outer tepals narrower than the inner ones; filaments filiform or fusiform, glabrous or papillose, longer than the anthers; ovary sessile or rarely shortly stipitate; style straight or curved, stigma punctate to slightly capitate. Fruits rounded, triangular to deeply 3-lobed in cross section. Seeds disciform, cupuliform or irregularly folded.

Genus of tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Southeast Asia and Australia, comprising approximately 150 species with a center of diversity in tropical Africa; in Gabon, six species.

---

Taxonomic notes: Two Gabonese species (C. alismatifolium and C. stoltzii) are distinguished by distichous leaves and a chromosome number 2n = 16. The four other species (C. filipendulum, C. orchidastrum, C. petrophilum and C. sparsiflorum), possessing leaves in rosette and a chromosome number 2n = 14, may in certain cases be difficult to delimit. Hybridization is probably not rare.

Another species, C. comosum (Thunb.) Jacques ('spider plant'), is often cultivated as an ornamental plant. It is close to the indigenous species, C. sparsiflorum Baker, both having a small plant at the node of the inflorescence (pseudo-vivipary). However, the former possesses ± linear leaves, the latter lanceolate leaves. C. comosum is native to South Africa; forms with variegated leaves are often found in cultivation.
