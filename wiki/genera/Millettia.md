# *Millettia* Wight & Arn.

**Family**: [[Leguminosae]] (Papilionoideae)
**Authority**: Wight & Arn., nom. cons. — Prodr. fl. Ind. orient. 1: 263 (1834)
**Synonyms**: *Pongamia* Adans. (1763); *Pongamia* Vent. (1803), nom. rej.

**The largest genus of Leguminosae in the region**, with 38 species — more than
any other genus in [[vol49]] and the largest in the family's three treatments.

## Diagnosis

Trees, shrubs or lianas reaching 20(–45) m. Leaves imparipinnate; petiole with a
pulvinus at the base; stipels present or absent; leaflets 5–19(–35), opposite or
nearly so, the blade coriaceous to membranous, reticulate and pinnately veined,
of medium size. Inflorescences in often ample panicles, or contracted into
unbranched pseudoracemes with 2–5 flowers at each flowering node
(**brachyblast**); bracts present. Flowers with bracteoles present; calyx
campanulate, truncate to lobed, the upper lobes often fused and the lower lobe
normally longest; corolla white to pink-purple or bright red, the petals
clawed, the standard circular to obovate, glabrous or pubescent on the back and
reflexed.

The **brachyblast** — a short lateral shoot bearing a cluster of flowers — is the
most useful field character separating *Millettia* from the other large
papilionoid liana genera in the region.

## Notes

***Millettia laurentii* is wenge**, one of the most valuable dark timbers
exported from Central Africa, used for flooring, furniture and musical
instruments. Heavy exploitation has made it a species of conservation concern
and its status is worth checking against current IUCN and CITES listings.
*M. versicolor* and *M. oyemensis* (**bokanga**) are also traded.

Several species contain **rotenone-type isoflavonoids** and are used as fish
poisons across the region — a property shared with *Lonchocarpus* and *Derris*,
to which *Millettia* is closely allied.

The genus is not monophyletic as traditionally circumscribed and has been the
subject of repeated segregation — *Afgekia*, *Callerya*, *Whitfordiodendron* and
others have been split off, mainly from the Asian species. The African species
remain the core of *Millettia* s.s., but generic limits here should be treated
as unsettled.
